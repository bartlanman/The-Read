# SYMBOL READ 002 — THRESHOLD

**Status:** First-pass researched symbolic interpretation · 2026-11-07  
**Protocol:** [Symbolic Masterwork](../../SYMBOLIC_MASTERWORK_PROTOCOL.md)

## I. Encounter — before the symbolic form
A body approaches an edge, entrance, boundary or change in conditions. There may be a step, door sill, shore, enclosure, or changing light. These are possible experiential bases, **not** recoverable evidence of the first human threshold symbol.

## II. Recognition
An entrance allows an observer to distinguish sides and imagine passage. Some passages change access, standing, ritual status or orientation; others merely change location. Crossing does **not** automatically imply transformation.

## III. Attestation and linguistic caution
The English word *threshold* is attested in Old English forms; its etymology is not securely reducible to the popular folk explanation about a sill holding back threshing-floor material. See [Oxford University Press, Anatoly Liberman on *threshold*](https://blog.oup.com/2015/02/threshold-word-origin-etymology/).

The Latin *limen* is a word for a sill or threshold and is the root of later technical language of *liminality*. Do not claim that Latin and Old English have identical historical origins.

Selected cultural encounters:
- **Roman Janus:** associated with gates, doors and beginnings; dual orientation is part of a specifically Roman religious framework. See [Encyclopaedia Britannica — Janus](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Janus-Roman-god).
- **Japanese torii:** a gate marking approach to a Shinto shrine and a distinction of sacred space; the cultural meaning must be understood in its particular tradition. See [Japan National Tourism Organization's shrine guide](https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/shrine-and-temple-traditions/).
- **Egyptian temple pylon:** a monumental gateway articulating movement into a temple precinct, often carrying distinct historical cosmological decoration. Specific archaeological dates and meanings require an identified monument; no universal meaning is inferred from form alone. See [Metropolitan Museum of Art — Egyptian art](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/egpt/hd_egpt.htm).
- **Rites of passage:** Arnold van Gennep's separation–transition–incorporation formulation and Victor Turner's subsequent treatment of liminality belong to modern anthropological analysis, not to proof of what every historical gateway meant. See [OpenStax Anthropology, Rituals of Transition](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-anthropology/pages/13-4-rituals-of-transition-and-conformity).

These are selected *different* uses and conceptual traditions, not a single evolutionary lineage or proof of the first threshold depiction.

## IV. Cultural lives
An architectural threshold may separate spaces; ritual transitions may change recognized status; modern speech calls a condition a *threshold* when a significant limit is met. These can be compared without assuming that all cultures share one metaphysical interpretation.

## V. There to Here
In contemporary art, design and technical language, a threshold may name an entrance, a transition, a triggering condition or a perceptual limit. These modern meanings must not be projected backward without evidence.

## VI. The Fundamental
**Form:** an edge, division or difference between states/regions, with an identifiable relation to passage.  
**Encounter:** orientation to sides, access and the possibility of crossing.  
**Proposed symbolic capacity:** **passage across a consequential distinction**. "Consequential" is contingent on circumstances and is a creative reading, not inherent in every doorway.

### What this Symbol Read can say
A threshold renders a distinction perceptible while also presenting the possibility of passage. The distinction may be architectural, social, ritual or conceptual, but no single historical tradition owns every use of the form. A crossing may change access without changing identity, and an altered condition may arise without passing through a literal doorway. The threshold therefore offers a contemporary symbolic language of conditional passage, with the significance of any particular crossing remaining open to the circumstances in which it occurs.
