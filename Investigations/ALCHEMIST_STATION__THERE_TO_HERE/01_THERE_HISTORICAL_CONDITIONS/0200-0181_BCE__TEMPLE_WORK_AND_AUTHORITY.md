# 200–181 BCE — Historical conditions: temples, production, and authority

**Evidence-first historical context for [Window 01](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/0200-0181_BCE__PTOLEMAIC_EGYPT.md).**

## Documented
1. Ptolemaic Egypt was administered through interacting royal and temple institutions. Greek and Egyptian administrative cultures overlapped; political control and relations with temple elites were under pressure during the revolts. [S01–S02]
2. The **196 BCE Memphis decree** documents fine linen manufactured in temples and discusses tax remission, temple donations, sacred objects, and ritual practices. [P01, Demotic lines 9–10, 17–18, 22–27]
3. A temple was more than a ritual building: redistribution, specialists, offices, and material requirements belonged to the institution. Royal favors to temples have a political and economic significance. [S02]
4. Related decrees in **186** and **185/184 BCE** document further interaction between clergy and monarchy after political conflict. [P02–P03]

## Warranted perception
Craft objects and crafted materials could have sacred and political value. A gold diadem, sacred textile or shrine functioned as more than an ordinary commodity *in the documented cult setting*. This is **not** evidence of a new transmutation technique. [P01]

## The necessary distinction
An **artisanal practice** can serve an institution without becoming an independently named profession; a **priestly office** is separately and explicitly named in the documents. We must not conflate temple priests, artisans, scribes, and later alchemists.
