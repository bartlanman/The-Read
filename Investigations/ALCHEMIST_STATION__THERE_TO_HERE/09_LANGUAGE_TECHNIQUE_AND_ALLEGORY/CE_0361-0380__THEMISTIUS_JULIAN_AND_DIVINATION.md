# Window 29 — Language, Technique, Public Metaphor and Access (361–380 CE)

**Method:** Separate what an original-period thinker says a technique could accomplish, what a practitioner actually demonstrably performed, what the state protects or prohibits, and what the surviving witnesses can verify. [Full Window 29](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0361-0380_CE__WINDOW.md) · [Source register W29](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0361-0380__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

## 1. Themistius's 366/367 comparison: social intelligibility is not demonstrated transmutation

In **Oration 7** (usually dated **367**, alternatively **366/367**), Themistius publicly praises **Valens's mercy** after the defeat of Procopius's revolt. He contrasts the emperor's political power to turn enemies into friends with fantastic transformations familiar from mythology (**Circe, Medea, Autolycus, Xerxes**) and with the hypothetical discovery of a procedure to **turn bronze into silver and silver into gold**. Olivier Dufault analyzes the relevant passage at **Or.7, 145.12–146.11**, particularly **145.26–146.11**, in [*Early Greek Alchemy, Patronage and Innovation in Late Antiquity* (2019), pp.100–102](https://escholarship.org/content/qt2ks0g83x/qt2ks0g83x.pdf).

This is **a positive named late-antique public witness of the concept**—a philosopher addressing the emperor expected the audience to find metal-changing imaginable as an extraordinary measure of transformation. It is NOT a recipe, experiment, demonstration, report of a successful workshop, public Alchemist appointment, or attestation that Themistius studied Zosimos. The original speech's **Greek wording belongs to an ancient text surviving in later manuscripts**; the common English presentation is a **modern scholarly rendering**, not a direct engraved 367 original. Date of delivery **366/367** is reconstructed, not a physically dated oration ticket.

**Critical transformation types remain distinct:**
1. *rhetorical/social transformation* — defeated political enemies made friends by mercy, the orator's operative comparison;
2. *legendary transformation* — Circe/Medea and other names evoke recognizable marvelous changes;
3. *hypothetical metallic transformation* — bronze→silver→gold described as the discovery of a technique **if** it existed;
4. *documented metalworking* — real coin-minting and alloy manipulation visible in surviving gold coinage, which does not establish (3).

Therefore: **public awareness of the claim is attested; authority, ability and professional social recognition for its performers are unverified**.

## 2. The disputed teacher: Julian 362 is about permission, not certified mastery

**CTh XIII.3.5**, legal dating **17 June 362**, requires `magistros studiorum doctoresque` to undergo municipal (`ordo`, `curiales`) approval with imperial review. This creates formal *teaching authorization* through a recognizable civic process. In a **different letter**, [Julian's explanatory Greek communication known as *Letter 36* (Wright), 61c (Bidez)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Works_of_the_Emperor_Julian/Letters/Letter_36), he argues instructors in the classical corpus must genuinely revere its traditional divine authors. His moral-theological test and the Latin decree are related but **not the same document** and not automatically written on one day.

Original issue (362) → **Theodosian Code compilation (438)** → later physical copies; Julian's literary letter → separate manuscript chain. This is not an original excavated 362 law on stone and it does NOT prove any alchemical teacher was appointed or refused office. Political/religious orthodoxy and proven practical competency are different grounds for authorizing a station.

## 3. Haruspicy versus harmful magic (371): legal category makes an ethical distinction

**CTh IX.16.9** dated **29 May 371** says `haruspicinam ego nullum cum maleficiorum causis habere consortium` and `nec haruspicinam reprehendimus, sed nocenter exerceri vetamus` — the state asserts that **haruspicy is not inherently identical to harmful magic**, though harmful practice is forbidden.

**Comparison to 357–358:** earlier law used `mathematici` (astrologers) and `magi` as condemned categories. The **371** distinction demonstrates that specific law, ruler, time, audience and type of alleged harm changed state classifications. It does **not** amount to general proof of universally safe/private prediction, or license a hypothetical Alchemist to practice under a ritual exemption.

**CTh IX.16.8** prohibiting astrological `tractatus` has a **date anomaly**: a transmitted **365** date sometimes critically revised to **370/373**; mark **CONTESTED** rather than create an artificial exact 371 shift. **CTh IX.16.10** (6 Dec 371) channels accusations of `maleficia` against senators through particular magistrates; the procedure is not proof of every defendant's guilt.

## 4. Political divination in practice: Ammianus's narrative of the Antioch trials

[Ammianus Marcellinus, *Res Gestae* XXIX.1–2](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Ammian/29%2A.html) recounts investigations of people accused of divining who would succeed Valens, involving a ritual **tripod/dish with letters and a suspended ring** reportedly forming `THEOD-`, with figures such as **Theodorus** and **Hilarius**. The narrative includes coercion, executions and condemnation of philosophical or ritual associations. This is **near-generation authored retrospective history, transmitted in later manuscripts**, not surviving original court minutes or a known excavated Antioch ritual device.

The usual dating is **371/372**, while **374** has also been advocated; avoid inventing a precise original day of consultation or execution. The story supplies **named claimed diviners and persecution**, but neither their techniques nor their accused acts are metal transmutation. An original bronze-looking device described by a historian is not a metallurgical laboratory, and `THEOD-` is not proof predictive power worked.

**Persecution requires a direct bridge:** the actual reported offense is alleged succession-divination and court intrigue. There is no named *Alchemist* convicted for turning metals, no laboratory confiscation inventory, and no securely evidenced concealment of chemistry as a consequence.

## 5. Gold as material of actual trade and coinage versus hypothetical gold creation

[**CJ IV.63.2**](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Corpus/CJ4.htm) transmits late-fourth-century imperial restrictions on exporting **gold** beyond the empire: `Non solum aurum barbaris minime praebeatur` and related limits on merchants' exchange. The code's editorial date is approximately **374?**, NOT securely 374 by original material subscription; actual customs enforcements require separate records.

A surviving [**Valens gold solidus, Berlin 18202344**](https://ikmk.smb.museum/object?id=18202344), dated **367–375** with a possible narrower later date, attests shaped/struck gold **already available** to an imperial mint. Neither legal refusal to export metal nor extant gold coinage is evidence that the emperor employed alchemists to manufacture precious metal from base substances.

The state thus operated with gold in two grounded senses: **precious-metal money** and **politically valuable controlled commodity**. Themistius's comparison operated with gold in a third, **hypothetical rhetorical sense**. **Do not conflate the three**.

## 6. Language provenance and false identification audit

| Term / reference | Precisely what source means | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| bronze→silver→gold in *Oration 7* | imagined/counterfactual technique, made intelligible by audience familiarity | active 367 chemist, successful transmutation, public licensed workshop |
| `magistri studiorum doctoresque` | teachers subject to municipal/imperial approval in CTh XIII.3.5 | chemistry professor or ancient occupational `Alchemist` |
| Homer/Hesiod/gods in Julian explanatory letter | religious authenticity of classical teachers | empirical test of technical capacity |
| `haruspicina` vs `maleficium` | Roman legal distinction (371) between divination and harmful acts | alchemists qualified for divination privilege |
| `mathematicorum tractatus` | astrological consultation in specific penal statute | normal geometry lessons uniformly outlawed |
| `THEOD-` | purported partial letters generated by Ammianus's divination story | physically verified oracle or chemical ingredient |
| `aurum barbaris` | gold export/retention in a later-codified trade law | proof state feared artificial gold creation |
| `TROBS` on a gold coin | Trier mint/gold standard or office according to museum numismatic reading | recipe code or naming of an Alchemist |

## 8. THERE → ENCOUNTER → HERE → REMAINDER

**THERE:** after 360, public Greco-Roman rhetoric could take the possibility of changing metals for granted as a meaningful hypothetical. The state separately regulates teaching, alleged divination and precious-metal flows.

**ENCOUNTER:** the scholar Themistius introduces the impossible-or-extraordinary metal-changing comparison to a courtly audience as a scale for Valens's political clemency; Julian subjects teachers to approval; Valens's environment punishes some succession-divination; Valentinian law differentiates ordinary haruspicy from malicious rites. **No historical proof these strands met in one named Alchemist.**

**HERE:** **the *concept* of transmutation now has a dated, identifiable, non-alchemical public witness**. That is a more secure result than assuming an alchemical profession existed whenever craft or priestly expertise existed. It is also a threshold between **an idea being culturally legible** and **a person being authorized to carry it out**.

**MOST PROFOUND:** *the social station cannot be inferred from the visibility of the idea*. Public mention and political fascination are insufficient to establish institutional practitioner recognition, much less material success.

**REMAINDER:** close Greek text collation of Oration 7 and manuscript chronology; possible 366 vs 367 dating; no named author/practitioner practicing bronze→silver→gold; no independent alchemical teaching office; timing of Antioch trials; exact gold export-law issue date; original named traders or workshop.
