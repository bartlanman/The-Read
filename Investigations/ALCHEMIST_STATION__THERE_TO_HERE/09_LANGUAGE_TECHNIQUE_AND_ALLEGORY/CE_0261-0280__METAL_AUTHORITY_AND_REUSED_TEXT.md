# Window 24 — Metal, Authority, Tooling and the Reused Literary Roll (261–280 CE)

**Status:** EVIDENCE-CONTROLLED LANGUAGE, MATERIAL-TECHNIQUE, ALLEGORY AND ACCESS DOSSIER (2026-10-18). **Contemporary period words ≠ later occupational identities.** [Window 24](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0261-0280_CE__WINDOW.md) · [Source register](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0261-0280__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

## 1. The three material transformations that cannot be conflated

1. **Coin making and political re-identification:** a billon alloy is prepared and struck in the Alexandrian mint under different political issuers. Original coins preserve texts/images claiming particular sovereignty, but not named artisan or method. [W24-O01–O03]
2. **Papyrus reuse:** the same *physical roll* first supports an earlier Greek literary discussion of Homer attributed to the actual scholar **Julius Africanus**, then a documentary will of **Hermogenes alias Eudaemon** in **276**. This is a change in *medium's function*, not a copying of secret metallurgical instructions by Hermogenes. [W24-P01]
3. **Post-striking alteration of a coin:** one surviving **Zenobia** specimen HCR32033 displays `L Δ`; the standard catalog's **272 year-five** type has `L E`. RPC identifies **this very specimen** and explicitly says its reverse **was tooled, the epsilon erased, and delta cut**, a material intervention at an **undocumented later time**. The altered witness cannot prove a separate ancient year-four issue. [W24-O02]

A modern observer could mistake **reworked coins**, **reused papyri**, **old literary attribution**, **official coin type**, and **original artisan activity** for one uninterrupted process. They are distinct transformations with different witnesses.

## 3. Alchemical method is more than silver-colored metal

- **Material craft actually attested in this window:** machine/mint preparation and striking of actual **billon**, a metal alloy with a silver component; it need not be described as production of elemental silver from another element. Specific individual operations at Alexandria cannot be read directly off the coin.
- **Appearance:** re-coloring, silvering, alloying and gilding can change what an object looks like; these are separable from its composition. A “gold-like” result, even technically sophisticated, would not prove changing atomic number.
- **Recipe horizon, not a securely dated 270 workshop:** Leiden X and Stockholm papyri contain numerous recipes for **coloring metals, stones and fibers**, often dated broadly to late third/fourth century. Exact copying **261–280** and actor biographies are **not securely established**. [W24-S04]
- **Modern coin terminology:** “billon,” “debased silver,” and “Antoninianus” are modern catalog classifications. Imperial legends use an ancient political vocabulary that should not automatically be turned into scientific self-description.
- **`XXI` / `KA` marks and Aurelian's c.274 monetary reform:** interpretations involving fineness ratio or denomination are debated; a coin mark is **not** a proven ancient ingredient formula and the dating/meaning needs issuer/type analysis. [W24-S05]

## 4. The crucial evidence gate: an object may change *after* its original historical event

The [RPC X 75758 specimen list](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/type/75758) identifies **Ashmolean HCR32033** as catalog specimen and annotates it **“rev. tooled, Ɛ erased and Δ cut in field to l.”** The [Ashmolean record](https://hcr.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coin/hcr32033) reports the present inscription `L Δ` and records **“tooled.”** These are the same specimen, not two independently issued types.

**Reconstruction bounded by evidence:**

1. Mint struck original Zenobia type in the year-five issue of **272 CE**; other specimens document `L E`.
2. At an **unknown later time**, someone modified the surface; the catalogue reports erasure of epsilon and cutting of delta.
3. Present-day records faithfully transcribe the modified surface as `L Δ`.
4. A historian reading only the present transcription could falsely infer a distinct historical year-four issue; the **comparative type catalog corrects this**.

This is an unusually concrete warning against conflating **what a surviving object visibly says now** with **what its maker caused it to say at its creation**. It has nothing to do with an alchemical alteration: the later tooling is a visible material/reception intervention, not a chemical transmutation or intentionally secret guild practice. **Exact tooling date and motive unknown.**

## 5. Papyrus: how a book becomes another kind of authority

[British Library Papyrus 2040](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002105408) has *recto* two surviving columns (35–36) of **Book 18** of Africanus's *Kestoi*, among themes of Odyssey textual authenticity. On the *verso* sits a **276 legal will** of **Hermogenes alias Eudaemon**, Oxyrhynchite councillor and officeholder. The writing of the will is inverted relative to the text of the book and runs **across the fibers**: this is positive physical evidence of reused medium, not simply a later reader's interpretive metaphor.

**Caution:** the exact ownership of the original *Kestoi* roll and the writer's motives for reusing it cannot be recovered just by identifying the testator. The will's use of existing written material does not prove a school, esoteric workshop, anti-alchemical persecution, disposal of banned manuals, contempt for technical knowledge or the destruction of an Alexandrian library. Earlier lettering can survive **because its material carrier is recycled for another purpose**. [W24-P01]

The geometrical/music ingredient substitution described in other transmitted *Kestoi* passages is NOT found in the physically surviving Book 18 portion. The missing key remains a separate copy-chain problem from this direct re-use.

## 7. The Outside, precisely bounded

**Political pressure:** Aurelian's accession and confrontation with Palmyrene power show up in original Egyptian coinage as **dual naming → Zenobia's Augusta title → Aurelian-alone minting**. The exact social response of mint employees is unknown. **Preserved continuity of a type of material operation** does not certify an unchanged workshop population.

**Material/political economy:** reduction of silver content and attempts at monetary reform c.274 are evidenced in numismatic syntheses. Coin appearance and metal composition concern *money*, not gold-transmutation authority. The debated `XXI` mark is a public administrative/numismatic sign, not automatically an alchemical formula. [W24-S05]

**Manuscript economy and reuse:** 276 Egyptian legal writing gains a practical documentary function on a previously written Greek literary roll, illustrating how material substrates can migrate across knowledge regimes. Motive (scarcity, convenience, previous ownership, status) remains uncertain.

**Historical narrative outside:** Aurelian's campaign is often blamed for Alexandria's entire Great Library destruction. [Robert Suski, 2018](https://bibliotekarzpodlaski.pl/index.php/bp/article/view/131) disputes that simple attribution. **A physical coin issue and a papyrus reuse cannot prove the date/cause of the Library's fate**, even if the period experienced violence.

## 8. THERE → HERE, and the unanswered question

**THERE:** paper/roll culture, practical alloys, claimants to rule, scribal copying and later learned technical attributions.

**ENCOUNTER:** competing authority finds tangible form on coin surfaces; one earlier text's physical carrier is repurposed as a private legal instrument. A later physical alteration can change what an original coin *appears* to date.

**HERE:** capacity to manufacture metal, legitimacy of public monetary issue, textual authority of Julius Africanus, legal standing of Hermogenes, and modern reconstruction of source identity can each be historically examined, **but none supplies a named 261–280 Alchemist**.

**REMAINDER:** who commissioned and worked in mint; true metallurgical recipes; original ownership of *Kestoi* roll; precise moment/motive of its disposal; date/actor of coin tooling; absence of any original original-period alchemical “guild” account; uncertain Maria biography, Zosimos chronology, and physical dating of later recipe papyri.
