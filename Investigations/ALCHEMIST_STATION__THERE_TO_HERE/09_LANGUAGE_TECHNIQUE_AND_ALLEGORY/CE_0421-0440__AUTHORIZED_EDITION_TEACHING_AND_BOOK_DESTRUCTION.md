# Window 32 — Authorization of Teachers and Authoritative Texts, 421–440 CE

**Evidence-control companion:** [Window 32](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0421-0440_CE__WINDOW.md) · [Sources](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0421-0440__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

## 1. Terms and real social authority

The **27 February 425** law **CTh XIV.9.3** recognizes specific public teaching positions and reserves public classrooms for teachers whose appointment government authorizes. Its `philosophiae arcana` means difficult/deep philosophy in its institutional context, **not** chemical cryptography or an Alchemist course. `Usurpantes sibi nomina magistrorum` characterizes unauthorized appropriation of *teacher* status, not fraudulently claiming gold production. Private teaching is differentiated from public recognized instruction. The **31** positions are a legal roster, not evidence of 31 real filled appointments.

In **CTh I.1.5 (429)** and **I.1.6 (435)** emperors authorize a textual compilation. The latter allows editors `demendi supervacanea verba et adiciendi necessaria et demutandi ambigua et emendandi incongrua`: subtracting unnecessary wording, adding needed text, changing ambiguities, correcting inconsistencies. **Editing is explicit authority**, not an invisible accidental error or deliberate cipher. The original enactment, authoritative excerpt, manuscript witness and modern translated paraphrase must each be dated separately.

The **3 August 435** doctrinal order **CTh XVI.5.66** explicitly requires the search for and public burning of condemned **Nestorius's** texts (`diligenti studio requiri ac publice conburi`); a Justinianic witness preserves a differing later date assignment. Here destruction of a specific text class is **legally attested as a command**, unlike unsupported claims that particular Egyptian alchemical volumes burned in 391. Actual enforcement and which physical books burned remain unproven by the law alone. A condemned theological book ≠ a metal recipe.

The **423** anti-sacrifice rules include protections against private assaults on peaceful pagans and Jews. Prohibited action, recognized profession and protected person are separable. No named goldsmith or `chēmeutēs` is accused in those clauses.

## 2. Material expertise and official standards

A surviving **`EXAGIVM SOLIDI`** weight (BM 1987,0703.24, c.425–430 in specialist cataloguing) with imperial busts and scales represents a public **gold coin weight standard**. The extant Theodosius II **gold solidus** (Berlin 18213856) and a separate **Alexandrian copper-alloy coin** (BM 1995,0201.83, 425–435) show existing precious/base-metal fabrication. None documents turning copper into gold. The catalogued weight's mass and a direct historical weighing of those exact specimens are not established by cited witnesses.

Here a real new comparative distinction emerges: **authorized measurement** may establish conformity of coin weight, while **hypothetical chrysopoeia** claims to change the substance. Political claims of standardization are not evidence of creation of an element.

## 3. Hypatia and manuscript afterlife

**Socrates Scholasticus**, completing *Ecclesiastical History* around **439–440**, narrates **Hypatia's murder in March 415**. A historian's later composition changes available **public memory and documentary authority**, not the event date or identity of victim. She remains a documented teacher of philosophy/mathematics, not an Alchemist. The surviving physical historian manuscripts date later still.

The alchemical texts attributed to **Olympiodorus** and **Synesius to Dioscorus** are not authenticated as 425–440 publications. A later heading naming a priestly authority is **a transmitted claim**, not a named appointment in 425.

## 5. What the stack now demonstrates

**THERE:** imperial control over public teacher credentials and textual legislation, and standards applied to precious-metal coinage.

**ENCOUNTER:** scarcity/status of teachers, changing legal provisions, contested Christian orthodoxy and monetary validity demand institutional responses.

**HERE:** contemporary government explicitly establishes **who may teach publicly**, which text gains **authorized legal authority**, and what theological text should be suppressed. The binding authority of the code can survive changes in editors and manuscripts. **No contemporaneous alchemical profession emerges from this evidence**.

**MOST PROFOUND:** *Textual custody itself becomes an explicitly governable station: society may authorize the editors who define which inherited statements count.* This gives a powerful structural comparison for later alchemical compilers, but not a proven connection to any 430 alchemical manuscript.

**REMAINDER:** professor appointments, code editors' actual micro-edits, which 435 theological books were destroyed, original exagium usage, real 420–440 alchemical teaching/technical documentation, contested ancient author identities.

**Next 441–460:** test original dated material and textual witnesses rather than assign Olympiodorus/Synesius to a century by prestige.
