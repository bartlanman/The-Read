# Window 26 — A Real Law for Goldworkers and Real Burned Scriptures: Language, Technique, Secrecy and Access (301–320 CE)

**Method:** original-period inscription → local legal category → material craft; vs near-contemporary **Christian eyewitness/literary sources** → religious book destruction; vs **later Suda chronicle** → contested alchemical book burning. **No inherited guild hypothesis can move across those sources without a new witnessed link.** [Ten-layer Window 26](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0301-0320_CE__WINDOW.md) · [Primary/scholarly source register](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0301-0320__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

## 1 — The hard documentary asymmetry

An **AD 301 physical inscription** survives on the **Civil Basilica at Aphrodisias**. The imperial **Edict on Maximum Prices** legislated **maximum prices and wage ceilings**, across c.1,400 listed goods/services in the combined surviving tradition. Reconstructed categories include raw gold, gold leaf, gold-worker labor, metallic thread-making, textiles, dyes and other specialized workmanship. **ATTESTED** public material/economic activity and imperial regulation. **NOT** named Alchemist credential, proof of a successful metallic transmutation, guarantee the full list was carved at this city, or a proof of exact quantities without consulting the composite critical edition. [Oxford Aphrodisias](https://aphrodisias.classics.ox.ac.uk/inscriptions.html), [PHI stone transcription](https://epigraphy.packhum.org/text/256955), [Crawford 2023 reviewed by Dickey](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2024/2024.07.21/).

**AD 303:** Two **near-contemporary Christian writers** recount **destruction of Christian churches and scriptures** during the persecution. [Lactantius, *DMP* 12](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Ante-Nicene_Fathers/Volume_VII/Lactantius/Of_the_Manner_in_Which_the_Persecutors_Died/Chap._XII), [Eusebius *HE* VIII.2](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nicene_and_Post-Nicene_Fathers%3A_Series_II/Volume_I/Church_History_of_Eusebius/Book_VIII/Chapter_2). These are **transmitted near-contemporary reports**, not physical 303 burned text fragments personally excavated by this investigation, and **their specifically described target is Christian worship and scripture**, not alchemical manuals. **ATTESTED as strongly source-supported persecution history**, but the source genre and interests remain explicit.

**AD 297–298, earlier Window 25:** later Byzantine [*Suda* Chi 280](https://cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/chi/280) claims that Diocletian burned old Egyptian books about preparing gold and silver to hinder future revolt, drawing on John of Antioch c.610–626. **Only retrospective literary attribution**, no verified Roman law of the 290s. It **does not become a 303 contemporary event** because a different category of books was actually reported burned in 303.

## 2 — Vocabulary provenance: the word that appears is not necessarily the person's occupation

| Term / expression | Who actually uses it | What can be concluded | Error to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| *pretium*, *merces* / categories of prices and wages | **301** Latin public-price inscription and composite scholarly text | imperial **maximum tariff** for **goods & crafts** | universal government professional licensure |
| **Aurum**, gold-working processes and wages | restored/reconstructed chapter(s) of 301 price edict | gold is recognized as **valued material**, specialist workers as **market actors** | government recognition of chrysopoeia or creation of gold |
| Greek `χημεία` (*chēmeia*) | **John of Antioch / Suda** late goldmaking-books episode, centuries after alleged event | a later vocabulary for reported **gold/silver preparation** | word appearing verbatim in an original **297 or 303** order |
| `βιβλία`, `ἔκαυσε` (books, burned) | later *Suda* story | narrative says ancient Egyptian **gold/silver books** were burned | proof specific books or practitioners destroyed |
| `scripturae` / sacred books | **Lactantius / Eusebius** descriptions of 303 Christian persecution | different, specifically **Christian** manuscript suppression | proof Diocletian destroyed alchemical manuals at that time |
| `conventicula` (meeting places/assemblies) | imperial grant as quoted in **Lactantius DMP 34 (311)** | conditional return of **Christian gatherings** | return of alchemical workshops |
| `cultum religionis` and **religious liberty** | **313 Licinius letter** Latin in Lactantius DMP 48 / Greek in Eusebius HE X.5 | return of ability to worship and some property | acceptance of chemical transmutation experiments |
| `magoi` in Zosimos' polemics | c.300 Greek author in later compilations | opponent characterization, **interested** | professionally verified guild, or label Zosimos claims for himself |
| `baphē / baphai` and material processing | later transmitted alchemical and dye texts | color/tincture practical vocabulary | evidence matter's elemental identity was changed |

**Actual category test:** the 301 law regulates a goldworker's **labor transaction**. Such a person might be expert at handling existing gold, making leaves or threads; that is a demonstrable economic station. To establish the **Alchemist** we would need evidence for distinctive transformative **claim**, textual or craft **qualification**, and **recognized audience/authority**. A conventional goldworker is not automatically a transmuter.

## 3 — Real technical operations and demonstrated outputs

The inscription anchors **economically recognizable categories** rather than original workshops excavated and directly assessed: beating thin gold into leaf; gold thread for textiles; conventional goldwork; dyeing/fiber work. They are **conceptually reproducible physical craft operations**. A price cap is an administrative rule, not evidence that *every* item was produced locally at Aphrodisias or that a particular method worked as described.

**Leiden X and Stockholm** are physical ancient **recipe manuscripts**, broadly late third/early fourth century. Techniques include imitation of gold/silver appearance, alloying, changing colors, dyes, and imitative gems. Approximate physical copying dates overlap **301–320** without locating any **307 operation**, and no manuscript here names a 311 licensed Alchemist. The presence of instructions is **counterevidence to universal secrecy**, not direct proof of universal open access.

**Zosimos:** presents material apparatus, furnaces and commentarial explanations, including allegorical *Visions*, in a **later-transmitted manuscript corpus**. His ability to discuss/interpret techniques demonstrates a **literary technical capacity**; the text alone does not independently certify a successful product of elemental goldmaking. Some passages addressed to **Theosebeia** suggest instruction and competitive patronage, but no text is precisely dated by an original **309 autograph**.

## 5 — 303, 311, 313: state can rescind a restriction on one station while leaving another untouched

**303:** Lactantius locates Nicomedian church demolition on **23 February 303**, while the **first edict's public posting** is usually **24 February 303**. Eusebius states edicts appeared in a month he calls **Dystrus**, often rendered March in old translation: **do not collapse** different regional calendars, reports and enactment/publication stages into a single uncontested universal event. [DMP 12–13] [Eusebius HE VIII.2].

**311:** **Galerius** in a transmitted Latin edict permits Christians to resume their religious association and meeting places, with a condition tied to public order and a duty to pray for the state. It is a significant **change of the church station's legal room for action**. It does not license Christian chemical recipes, abolish all local anti-Christian orders immediately, or name Greek alchemical studies.

**313:** **Constantine and Licinius** reached agreement at Milan, while **Licinius's posted circular at Nicomedia 13 June** is the actual transmitted Roman administrative act. Lactantius **chapter 48 Latin** and Eusebius **HE X.5 Greek translation** are two literary transmissions, not a securely preserved independent **“Edict of Milan” inscription**. The rights concern **religious practice and property restitution**, not a professional Alchemist.

## 6 — Patron, priest, author, worker: who is qualified?

- **Goldworker:** **economically counted** in a contemporaneous **301** public price list; even gold-leaf expertise is **no proof of chrysopoeia**.
- **Christian clergy:** institutionally recognizable status **threatened** in 303 reports and **conditionally restored** by policy in 311/313; real defined religious institutions and targeted texts.
- **Zosimos of Panopolis:** **recognized by modern source criticism as a named alchemical commentator**, and inside the letters a patron-client/student relationship with **Theosebeia** is invoked. Dufault (2019) notes he presented himself neither as `magos` nor `alchemist`. **No independent 307 government attestation to his teaching or craft occupation**.
- **Theosebeia:** respondent/patron within transmitted correspondence, not independently certified ancient school head under an official Alchemist title.
- **Neilos:** Zosimos's interested description of priestly rival, not independently corroborated as an actual 310 legally appointed alchemical instructor.
- **Original recipe copyists:** physically attest that texts circulated, **do not** identify composer, workshop, student or ability to produce gold.

## 7 — A potential new standard: when an Alchemist station finally becomes testable

There are **three distinct evidentiary thresholds** now visible in adjacent sources:

1. **A profession or valued technique is publicly categorized** (301 goldworkers in state price edict).
2. **A social/religious office is coerced or protected by name** (303–313 Christian clergy and congregations).
3. **A named individual claims authority to interpret specific texts and correct rivals** (Zosimos's c.300 transmitted alchemical commentary).

We are **not entitled** to combine these into a single “state-recognized alchemist who was persecuted.” That synthesis would require an **original or critically dateable bridge**: e.g. law explicitly about goldmaking texts, appointment of an alchemical teacher, documented audience recognizing him/her, or identified workshop with records connecting texts and results. **No such bridge established for 301–320**.

**THERE → HERE:** a genuine economic craft is regulated; a genuine religion's texts were targeted and status contested; alchemical *interpretation* becomes visible near this period as a scholarly persona. **REMAINDER:** no original 303 alchemical ban; unresolved manuscript biography, practices and effect; regulatory controls do not establish proficiency or transformative success. Next historical period **321–340 CE** should continue material/station tests rather than import Nicene Christian categories into alchemy without direct evidence.
