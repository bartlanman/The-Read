# 1–20 CE: Casting Money Is Not Making Gold

**Comparative method:** physical object, official decree, actual skill, reputational authority and retrospective text must be kept distinct.

## 1. 7 CE knife money: gold used, not gold made
A British Museum bronze knife-shaped coin (1996,0217.239), dated 7 CE, has gold inlay forming the legend 'one knife worth five thousand'. A metalsmith can inlay gold into bronze; no one therefore changed bronze's chemical identity into gold. **Metallurgical craftsmanship and claims of metal transmutation are different operations.**

## 2. Meaning created by authority
The phrase `一刀平五千` assigns **nominal** value, not measured mass of gold. New dynastic power decides which objects count as currency. This is closer to a **political-linguistic transformation** than a material-element transformation.

## 3. 9–14 CE: legal boundaries make one technique lawful or criminal
The National Museum of China documents reforms in 7,9,10 and 14 CE; a 20 CE restriction completes another step. *Hanshu* 24B uses `私鑄` for private/unauthorized coin casting and recounts draconian penalties. The material technique might resemble state minting, but the jurisdiction over it differs.

**Distinct station:** recognized caster/licensed mint worker vs unauthorized caster. Neither becomes a credentialed Alchemist just by doing metalwork.

## 4. Egypt: public sacred scripts vs claimed secret chemical code
Temples keep ancient Egyptian written and material rituals under Augustus and Tiberius. Roman official images/title are public statements. Neither the presence of hieroglyphs nor the existence of guarded ritual offices proves a laboratory cipher.

## 5. Why manuscript dating matters
The *Four Books* of pseudo-Democritus, arguably the earliest Greek alchemical recipe corpus, is **first-century CE broadly** but critically placed after mid-century. No responsible history should cite it as already circulating in the 1–20 CE window merely to fill a missing early period.

## Retained law
**Who has the authority to say what an object IS in a social system can differ from who has the capacity to make the object.** Nominal currency status, access to sacred office, and material capability are separate elements of THERE → HERE.
