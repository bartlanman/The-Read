# Language, technique and allegory

**Purpose:** research when practical procedures acquired names, riddles, coded labels, mystical images, legal obscurity and interpretative authority.

## The seven mechanisms
1. **Polysemy:** Greek *theion hydor* evokes sulfur/divinity; identifying the solution requires technical context [H02].
2. **Pseudonym:** Pseudo-Democritus borrows ancient philosophic standing [H03,H19].
3. **Revelatory story:** Isis to Horus and other claimed master narratives construct different authorities [H21].
4. **Allegorical technic:** Zosimos's Visions place practical and philosophical transformation into relation [H01].
5. **Lexical key:** medieval Arabic/Syriac dictionaries name hidden terms for materials and operations [H20].
6. **Translation:** Greek → Syriac/Arabic → Latin → vernacular can alter the text, not simply carry identical secrets [H03,H05].
7. **Emblem and sound:** Khunrath and Maier develop print/image/music as interpretative participation [H14,H15,H16].

## Foundational questions
- Did an expression describe an existing procedure or generate a new way to interpret nature?
- Is obscurity **deliberately produced**, or a consequence of lost lexicon/apparatus/copying?
- What evidence exists of **a key**: gloss, parallel recipe, chemical reconstruction, teacher's explanation?
- Who possessed the right/ability to decode and practice, and did that create a station?
- Which meanings are irreducible spiritual/philosophical readings, not secret replacements for reagent names?

## Proof standard
The documented language is the first datum; a reconstructed chemical decoding is a separately supported hypothesis. Never identify a metaphor as a particular compound without dated technical or manuscript corroboration.

## Working example: theion hydor
Martelli [H02] documents a Greek expression with both 'sulfur' and 'divine' semantic possibilities. It is **evidence of linguistically fertile technical terminology**, not, alone, proof of a persecuted author intentionally hiding an ingredient.

## Working example: Atalanta fugiens 1617
Maier's fifty emblems combine image, epigram, commentary and music. This is a *publicly printed complex interpretative structure*; complexity does not automatically imply prohibited knowledge. [H15,H16]
