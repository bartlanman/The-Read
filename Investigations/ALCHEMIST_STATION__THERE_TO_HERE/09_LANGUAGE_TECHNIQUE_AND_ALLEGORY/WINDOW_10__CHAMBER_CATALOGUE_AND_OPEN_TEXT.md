# Window 10 — Three Access Regimes: Chamber, Catalogue, Circulated Text

**Period:** 20–1 BCE. This is a comparative analysis; the Egyptian and Chinese case studies have no evidenced direct causal contact.

## 1. Dendur — physical custody of sacred practice
The small chamber in the rear wall of Dendur's sanctuary has an uncertain use, potentially storage for cult equipment. Its enclosure can restrict **physical access**, but this does not demonstrate deliberate encoding of material techniques in reliefs, or ritual secrecy motivated by metalwork danger. Augustus's hieroglyphic titles present visible royal ritual legitimacy.

## 2. Qilüe — categories govern what counts as knowledge
The lost late-BCE Qilüe, preserved in modified form in the later Hanshu Yiwenzhi, organized fangji among recognized classes of writing, including medical formulas and immortality arts. The **catalog** gives a field a place and a rank without warranting the field's underlying material claims.

## 3. Xiwangmu, 3 BCE — public writing claims personal power
The Hanshu reports a traveling written promise: 母告百姓，佩此書者不死. People need only *wear* the writing to claim access to extraordinary protection. Whatever they believed, this is not an actual laboratory recipe or proof of immortality. The message's **circulation**, not restricted transmission, is central.

## Cross-reading
| Axis | Dendur | Qilüe | 3 BCE movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restriction/access | Sacred spatial zones | Institutional classification and custody | Broad diffusion of tokens/writing |
| Authorization | Roman emperor in Egyptian cultic idiom | Court editor and library offices | Claimed divine authority without named court license |
| What is actually preserved | Stone architecture and relief | Later derivative catalog of lost original | Later historical quotation of circulating words |
| What is not proven | Encoded gold formula | Successful technical/immortality method | Deathlessness or skilled alchemical craft |

### There → Here
A station's authority can operate through material location, the ability to name a field of knowledge, or the spread of a claim of supernatural access. **None entails actual transmutation.** The archive must retain the distinction between *permitted access*, *understanding*, *performance* and *demonstrated results*.
