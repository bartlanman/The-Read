# Window 25 — Language, Technique, Access and the Suppression Story (281–300 CE)

**Status:** source-critical close study (2026-10-18), with a strict **narrated event / composition / earliest surviving witness** distinction. [Window 25](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0281-0300_CE__WINDOW.md) · [source audit](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0281-0300__SOURCE_REGISTER.md). We are testing whether this window **produces an Alchemist's station**, not assuming any goldmaking guild already existed.

## I. THREE CATEGORIES THAT SOUND SIMILAR BUT ARE NOT

**Contemporary sovereign status: 297.** Greek papyri in the reign of **Domitius Domitianus**, and contemporary Alexandrian coins, actually name a rival emperor. They reveal *political authority claimed* by living people during 297, not laboratory technique. [P.Isid.62 and related papyri](https://connectinglateantiquities.org/text.html?id=plre-1-domitianus-6) and [RPC X 76414](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/type/76414).

**Retrospective story of prohibited knowledge: narrated late 290s, text transmitted much later.** The **Suda** lexicon [Chi 280](https://cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/chi/280) identifies **`chēmeia`** and says that **Diocletian burned earlier Egyptian gold/silver-preparation books** lest prosperity fund future rebellion. The passage is repeated in [Suda Delta 1156](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/delta/1156), associated by Dufault with **John of Antioch fr.191 Mariev, *Chronography* c.610–626**. These are **related transmission**, not two independent 297 eyewitnesses. No surviving original late-290s edict, inventory of confiscated manuals, victim or workshop is independently supplied. What we can *observe* is **later narration of a fear of economically enabling knowledge**, not the burning itself.

**Nearly coeval alchemical scholarship around 300.** **Zosimos of Panopolis** is the first reliably identifiable author of commentaries on Greek alchemical texts according to [Olivier Dufault (2019)](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ks0g83x), and is generally assigned to **c.300 CE**. His actual surviving Greek passages are in **medieval compilations**, chiefly **Marcianus gr.299 c.1000**, **Parisinus gr.2325 13th century**, **Parisinus gr.2327 15th century**, rather than excavated 298 autographs. His letters and their internal social world are extremely important but do **not** document a specific year 281–300.

## II. GREEK WORDS AND LANGUAGE PROVENANCE

| Expression | Witness/time | Narrow meaning supported | Unwarranted leap |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ΔΟΜΙΤΙΑΝΟϹ ϹΕΒ` | original 297/298 Alexandria coin, RPC X 76414 | Domitianus called **Augustus**, political standing asserted | ruler actually trained in goldmaking |
| `L Β` | same original coin | regnal **year 2** in Egyptian notation | special ingredient sign or Egyptian guild initiation |
| `χ̣ημεία / χημεία` (`chēmeia`) | *Suda* Chi 280, medieval author drawing on earlier chronicle | word for **making/preparing gold and silver**, in account's vocabulary | automatically the *self-title* of 297 practitioners |
| `ἡ τοῦ ἀργύρου καὶ χρυσοῦ κατασκευή` | *Suda* Chi 280 | preparation/making of **silver and gold**, **lexicographer's gloss** | validated elemental transmutation by actual Egyptians |
| `βιβλία`, `ἔκαυσε` | *Suda* Chi 280 | **books; he burned**, a **later narrated act** | primary dated edict or independently verified fire |
| `χρημάτων ... περιουσίᾳ` | *Suda* Chi 280 | abundant **money/wealth** as *narrated imperial motive* | independently demonstrated economic cause of rebellion |
| `magoi` | Zosimos's **transmitted** polemical letters, c.300 broad | author labels opponents as threatening/wrongful sorcerers | neutral state registry of their occupations |
| `paideia` | Greek elite education, analytically stressed by Dufault | scholarly training and interpretive competence | universal formal Alchemist professional license |
| `baphai` (βαφαί) | Greek alchemical commentary context | dyeing/tinctures, **material-color change** | physically proved changing an element into gold |

No original 297 papyrus identifies a person as **`chēmeutēs`**. The *Suda's* definition is also **later** than the alleged event; a word's appearance in the later report cannot license assigning it to the people described centuries earlier.

### A second confusion in the same Suda

[Suda Delta 250, *deras* (“skin/fleece”)](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/delta/250) interprets the Golden Fleece as a writing on hide describing how to create gold through **chēmeia**. Its online critical note associates this mythological interpretation with **John of Antioch fr.15.3 FHG = 26.3 Roberto**. The story explains **a later writer's symbolic reinterpretation of a myth**, NOT a directly witnessed ancient Greek book of golden-fleece recipes. **No excavated fleece-text is demonstrated**. Its relation to the Diocletian passage is evidence for **literary reception**, not a real workshop.

## III. MATERIAL TECHNIQUE IS DIFFERENT FROM GOLDMAKING CLAIMS

- **Original objects:** Alexandria actually strikes dated *billon / debased-silver* coinage (e.g. **RPC X 76414 and 76417**, year two 297/298). An issued metal object permits analysis of coin appearance, mass, legends and metallurgy only when the object is examined; not the artisans' names or what kind of transformation theory they endorsed.
- **Leiden X / Stockholm:** ancient physical **Greek recipe papyri**, often estimated *late third century* or **late third/early fourth**, preserve procedures for **gold-like and silver-like products, metal coloring, imitative gems and dyes**. Cambridge's *Alchemy Reader* labels these **late third century** and estimates probably the same hand. But **this does not prove copies written 297**: an approximate palaeographic date range **overlaps** windows without giving a new dated action in each.
- **Actual practices:** gilding, metal coloring, alloying, silvering, glass/gem imitation and dyeing require real manipulation of matter; they are **not** proof of **elemental transmutation**. A formula may be literate and reproducible yet its literary promise of “gold” refer to appearance, market value or purification instead of a change in the metal's atomic identity.
- **Zosimos:** interprets earlier recipes, takes some procedures seriously and writes about **apparatus and furnaces** in his transmitted work; we have evidence of **technical authorship and commentary**, not an independently tested contemporary 299 finished gold specimen.
- **Alchemy vs chemistry vs pseudoscience:** the last two are inappropriate modern status labels if placed in 297 actors' mouths. The question is what **socially recognized authorization** attached to ancient words and practices, not how a modern discipline judges them retrospectively.

## IV. WHY THE 'SECRET' STORY IS NOT IN ITSELF A SECRECY PROOF

| Hypothesis | Source test | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| State **suppression of alchemical books** | medieval Suda/earlier 7th-c John of Antioch claims it | **ATTESTED RETROSPECTIVE CLAIM**; historical action **UNVERIFIED** |
| State **suppression of a political usurper** | multiple **original 297 papyri/coins** + later siege histories | **ATTESTED political change**, not alchemical targeting |
| **Fear of persecution** motivated recipe code | no original 297 practitioner statement names that fear | **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** |
| Imperial fear that goldmaking financed rebellion | alleged motive **explicit in Suda** | **RETROSPECTIVE CLAIM**, not directly observed 297 imperial psychology |
| **Economic trade monopoly** protected skills | specialized materials/operations often skill-intensive | **SOURCE-SUPPORTED GENERAL INFERENCE** but **no named guild rule** |
| **Dangerous reagents** withheld from novices | chemistry can carry risks | **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** this drove 297 concealment |
| **Learning capacity** controls competence | Zosimos appeals to education, manuscripts and rivalry | **SOURCE-SUPPORTED** around c.300, not dated to 297 |
| **Patronage** authorizes scholarship | Zosimos addresses **Theosebeia** and confronts rival priest **Neilos** in later-transmitted writings | **SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION**, not dated formal license |
| **Loss through manuscript transmission** | chiefly medieval copies remain, original Zosimos autographs missing | **ATTESTED LATER WITNESSES**; reason for loss undetermined |
| Public methods / **open recipe writing** | Leiden/Stockholm physical ancient recipe collections | **COUNTEREVIDENCE** to total concealment thesis |

**Suda caution:** it is easy to confuse imperial **religious** legislation, especially the Diocletian-era anti-Manichaean rescript (debated **297 vs 302**, known through *Collatio legum Mosaicarum et Romanarum* 15.3), with the **unindependently witnessed alchemical-book-burning story**. They are **different legal/narrative streams**. Also the **303 anti-Christian persecution** is **outside** this window and cannot be presented as an event before **300** simply because Suda's biography narrates both.

## V. THE PATRONAGE TURN: SOCIAL RECOGNITION WITHOUT ANCIENT JOB TITLE

Dufault identifies **Zosimos's letters to Theosebeia** and rivalry with **Neilos**, whom Zosimos calls **a priest associated with Theosebeia**, as the strongest internally narrated case of **Greek scholarly knowledge and alchemical techniques crossing**. Theosebeia's relationship, educational circle and aristocratic patronage are scholarly reconstructions based on interested writing; her exact dates, independent biography and literal kinship with Zosimos are uncertain.

**New threshold:** written correction and interpretive authority over **earlier material texts** becomes a social competitive claim, distinct from a simple craft recipe, a court magician stereotype or the success of transmutation. Zosimos, the **author**, may be real, his *opponents* are narrated in his transmitted writings, and a *patroness* is addressed. **None** is proven to occupy a state-certified occupation called “Alchemist,” or to have been subjected to an original 297 decree.

**Two tests of his authority:**
1. What named competence does **Zosimos claim**? Reading and interpreting earlier texts, distinguishing authentic natural processes from deceptive techniques, and moral/educational judgment.
2. Who **recognizes** it? Theosebeia is the addressed learned patroness, but independent household accounts, signed appointments, salaries or original-period book title pages **are not extant in this audited sample**. His critics are presented through his interested letters, so names and accusations must not be treated as disinterested testimony.

## VI. THERE → HERE AND THE OUTSIDE

**THERE:** publicly dated material money; real political revolt; earlier Greek metallurgical/dyeing recipe tradition and possible Egyptian temple craft influences.

**ENCOUNTER:** 297 rebellion displaced official titulature locally, while later literary writers presented the power of goldmaking books as a threat to regime security. A near-300 scholar (date broad) sought interpretive and patronage authority within the technical tradition.

**HERE:** independent **papyri and coins** make political station change unusually concrete, while the supposed anti-alchemical response remains a **late story**. The **Zosimos/Theosebeia** literary world offers a substantially more promising *alchemical textual station* than the anonymous officials and legendary founders previously encountered, without yet supplying a **281–300** original dated authorial attestation.

**REMAINDER:** whether any alchemy books were actually confiscated, whether money/bullion expertise affected the rebellion, Diocletian's motivations, earliest text of John of Antioch, exact identity/date of Theosebeia and Neilos, dates of Zosimos treatises, degree of chemical effectiveness of any purported goldmaking, lineage connecting Zosimos to contemporary artisans.

**NEXT:** 301–320 CE. Study genuine **303–305** laws/persecutions only where tied to actual witnesses; cross-check Zosimos's commentary and competing interpreters against manuscript dates and patronage, and search a primary record of institutional authorization before calling any person a licensed Alchemist.
