# Window 27 — Craft Capacity, Transmission, Civic Authorization and the Dead Emperor's Name (321–340 CE)

**Method:** each term is tied to a source's **composition/inscription date** and a different witness date if transmitted. The legal sources establish **civic/ritual and occupational authorization**, not a new Alchemist occupation. [Full Window 27](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0321-0340_CE__WINDOW.md) · [source register](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0321-0340__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

## 1. Four different stations, four different witnesses

- **Orkistos, 331 original inscribed reply:** city autonomy is granted/maintained after petition, and the **30 June 331** rescript addresses cult-related payments to neighboring Nacoleia. The dossier's earlier petitions are **324–326** and earlier decision **329/330**, not new 331 events. Civic station is a legal status, not proof of skilled metallurgy. [W27-P01]
- **Hispellum, c.333–337 original inscription:** annual **sacerdotes** continue to preside over local imperial-dynastic temple and performances, on condition against contaminating **superstitio**. An officially recognized priest or civic festival leader is **not automatically** a maker of gold. [W27-P02]
- **334 and 337 transmitted laws** (*CTh* 13.4.1–2, *CJ* 10.66.1): protect training of architects and listed artisans by exemption from certain **munera**, and provide learning allowance to architects. The specific goal of **training and teaching** is expressed in the text, without proof any individual received it.
- **335 original gold solidus:** actual existing gold was struck at Nicomedia under Constantine, bearing his imperial name; it gives a material demonstration of **mint output**, not changing another element into gold. [W27-O01]

## 3. The posthumous authorization problem in 337

*Codex Theodosianus* 13.4.2 is given as **2 August 337** under `Constantinus`, even though Constantine died **22 May 337**. That is an internally **incompatible date/issuer claim if read as a living monarch's signature**.

[R. W. Burgess, *The Summer of Blood* (2008)](https://constantinethegreatcoins.com/articles/Burgess_THE_SUMMER_OF_BLOOD_The_Great_Massacre_of_337_and_the_Promotion_of_the_Sons_of_Constantine.pdf) argues it was likely **promulgated in the dead emperor's name** during the transition, before the sons' formal promotion to Augustus. The code heading may also reflect later excerpting/reassignment, so the **original legal authorization mechanism is contested**. The law's text and date are still useful as transmitted; the actual writer or ruler's personal action on that August day is **NOT CONFIRMED**.

**Methodological consequence:** preserving an **authorizing name** after its original bearer is dead does not demonstrate that person acted then. This resembles a problem in alchemical tradition: Pseudo-Democritus, Hermes, Maria or a prestigious elder **may supply a remembered name**, but the name by itself cannot document the act, date, teacher or chemical result. **This is comparison, NOT an evidenced direct lineage or direct influence.**

## 4. Material transformations versus value/permission

**Mintcraft:** preparing and striking a gold solidus with existing metal is a verified operation, but cannot be used to infer *elemental conversion* from other elements. **Craft protections:** an imperial law may increase social freedom to acquire expertise without showing what an actual artisan produced. **Civic/religious privileges:** petitions and rescripts change permission to erect a temple or conduct a local festival without demonstrating a private ritual's efficacy. These three operations act on **matter, legal capacity and public perception**, respectively.

**Actual alchemical horizon:** Zosimos of Panopolis **c.300** has writings to Theosebeia surviving mainly in medieval manuscripts and displays learned interpretive rivalry, but there is no secure dated 330 workshop, signed teaching salary or imperial favor. Original Greek Leiden X and Stockholm papyri are **broadly dated late-third / early-fourth**, with dyeing, metal coloring, imitative gold/silver and other technique; overlap with this twenty-year span is not proof a new recipe or school began in 334.

## 6. THERE → HERE / the Outside

**THERE:** imperial government, skilled economic occupations, city status, priestly festivals, learned books and material gold money. **ENCOUNTER:** local communities petition for privileges; government uses relief and salaries to encourage teaching technical abilities; legal name outlasts emperor in 337 inscription-free transmitted text. **HERE:** recognized craft stations may include legally **protected capacity-building**—training, exemption, generational transmission—not merely workshop output. **REMAINDER:** no verified beneficiaries or named alchemical title, no date-fixed Zosimos school, no alchemical persecution, unresolved posthumous attribution and religious-clause meaning.

**NEXT 341–360:** The **344 law** extending privileges to mechanics, geometers and architects (*CTh* 13.4.3) must be treated as **Window 28**, not transplanted here. Seek an exact contemporary named Greek technical author/workshop before declaring the Alchemist a formally recognized profession.
