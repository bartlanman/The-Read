# 21–40 CE — When an Official Gets the Name and Workers Produce the Change

**Question:** Does the person recognized for technical progress need to be the person who invented or operated the instrument?

## Water power becomes furnace capacity
The Chinese historical account of **31 CE** says:
`造作水排，鑄為農器，用力少，見功多，百姓便之`.

The expression `水排` identifies a **practical bellows machine driven by water**, not the name of a hidden alchemical ingredient. The report points to a process with a physical mechanism: water can drive a blower, which increases the supply of air to a fuel-fed furnace used for metal casting.

The **named station** is `南陽太守`, Administrator of Nanyang, held by Du Shi. An office can make deployment, maintenance or credit possible; the actual furnace-making skill was distributed among workers whose names the later historian does not preserve.

**Warrant:** historical claim of useful mechanical metalwork.
**Unknown:** the original design, exact inventors, measured productivity, and continuity of the apparatus.

## The practical output changes how a claim can be judged
Compare with the earlier Liu Xiang failed gold-making narrative: `方不驗` says the proposed method was not borne out. Du Shi's account instead names an actual sort of implement and a proposed causal mechanism for lower effort.

**Caution:** these are both later literary testimonies, not modern trials. It would be incorrect to equate their different evidentiary strength with a simplistic change from pseudoscience to science.

## Recognition can travel separately from skill
A historian can preserve the official's name while omitting operators. This makes *lineage of public authority* easier to reconstruct than *lineage of hands-on technique*. Our station must preserve that difference.

## Unrelated civic labeling in 38 CE
Philo reports Roman-era Alexandria's anti-Jewish violence and legal/civic exclusion. This is historically consequential identity-making by officials and crowds, **but it is not a metaphorical account of alchemical censorship**. It belongs in the parallel register only with that restriction.

## Later text caution
The Greek *Four Books* attributed to Democritus has a broad first-century CE dating, but detailed work places the author likely later than 40 CE; no securely dated 31 CE pseudo-Democritean apparatus or writer can be inferred.
