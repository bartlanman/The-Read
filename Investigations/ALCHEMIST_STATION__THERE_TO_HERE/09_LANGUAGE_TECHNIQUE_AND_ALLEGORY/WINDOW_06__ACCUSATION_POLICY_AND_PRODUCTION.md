# Window 06 — Accusation, repentance, production and the use of historical language

**Purpose:** compare four distinct communicative acts before identifying any as 'alchemical allegory' or intentional concealment.

## 91 BCE — Accusation as an instrument of power
- *Wugu* (巫蠱) describes the feared harmful practices in the political investigation. It is not a general class for every specialist of methods.
- Wooden dolls/effigies and alleged curses appear in historical account, where accusations and coercive investigation make a ruler's suspicion consequential.
- **Station effect:** the authority to label a practice dangerous can be powerful without the charged practice having achieved its claimed result.

## 89 BCE — Selective historical storytelling
- The *Hanshu* preserves the *Luntai zhao*: criticisms of failing military expeditions, harmful burdens and divinatory failures.
- A later *Zizhi tongjian* passage dramatizes a remorseful emperor dismissing all immortality-seeking *fangshi*. Modern text criticism traces that addition to much later storytelling and contests its reliability.
- **Station effect:** an author centuries later can retell a prior social boundary as definitive. Narrative can manufacture a reputation retrospectively.

## 81 BCE — Public dispute about technique and jurisdiction
- The *Yantielun* makes salt and iron production subject to competing descriptions of what benefits subjects.
- Ministers discuss security, taxes and concentrated private wealth; literati discuss quality, cost, justice and agriculture. A real technological field becomes a subject of publicly organized political argument.
- **Station effect:** an authority claim once maintained through official command is now explicitly contested by a different elite audience.

## No 'hidden chemical key' has been found
The technical words **iron**, **salt**, **forgery**, **curse**, **royal policy**, **master**, **immortal** are neither interchangeable nor automatically cryptonyms. Difficulty reading old Chinese, and later rewriting of Han events, are different from deliberate encryption of a metalmaking recipe.

## Proposed crossing
Historical legitimacy can shift even when there is no new apparatus: the court changes **who judges**, **how criticism is recorded**, and **what kind of consequence matters**.

**Limit:** no alchemical profession is specifically proven to have changed in this window. The next tasks need artifacts of actual practice, not only court narrative and policy dialogue.
