# 120–101 BCE: When the 'secret' becomes a credential, bargain, and state instrument

**Status:** first primary-language source pass with interpretive test cases, NOT a proven history of actual encoded chemical formulae.

## Three types of withholding in one historical narrative

### 1. Specialist secrecy as status claim
When recommended to the ruler, Luan Da says he possesses knowledge from an extraordinary master (or access to it), but the beings who hold such knowledge do not trust socially inferior envoys. **Access depends on the station one holds.**

Original: 同師 (reportedly shares a master with Shaoweng), 黃金可成 (gold can be made), and 方士皆奄口 ('specialists would close their mouths'). Cf. Chapter 12 variants 掩口. [W05-P01–P02]

His demand for **guest rituals, royal kinship and seals** is not ancillary to the knowledge claim. He argues those public marks of rank are prerequisites to gaining knowledge from a superior realm.

### 2. Royal secrecy as reputation protection
Shaoweng allegedly feeds an ox a silk message, whose handwriting betrays him. The emperor orders execution and suppresses disclosure. The court protects its own reputation after having recognized an allegedly false expert.

Historical action as narrated: 於是誅文成將軍，隱之. **This is explicit concealment of the manner of punishment, not coded alchemy.** [W05-P01]

### 3. Sacred secrecy as sovereign practice
At Mount Tai in 110 BCE the emperor authorizes jade inscriptions (玉牒書) whose words are kept secret (書祕); participation in the summit ceremony is restricted and the ceremony is guarded (其事皆禁). **The actual tablets' words are unavailable**. A ritual protected by an institution is not automatically a technical recipe.

## Fourth possibility: secrecy creates a market for rivals
After Luan Da's elevation, other coast-dwelling experts reportedly say they too have **禁方** ('restricted methods'). *Secrecy becomes a claim that grants access to court markets and patrons.*

## Fifth possibility: verifying the wrong thing
A successful spectacle (objects moving), impressive inscriptions, expensive vestments, and seals are **not the same as a successful gold-making or immortality technique**.

When the emperor later secretly observed Luan's trip and found it false, perceived expert status collapsed. The court recognized and disciplined **claims** more than demonstrable technical competence.

## The key THERE → HERE transformation
The claimed secret no longer resides only in a person's alleged formula. It becomes:
- a negotiation over who may approach the source of knowledge,
- a condition for formal public honors,
- a source of income, titles and power,
- a reason to claim exposure to danger,
- a potential liability under later challenge.

**Discipline:** none of this proves intentional chemical allegory; the terms must be interpreted in Chinese historical context, and rhetoric about immortals can be sincere religious discourse, political performance, or deception.
