# Window 18 — Who May Be a Priest, Rent a Workshop or Practice Medicine?

**Span:** 141–160 CE. The distinction this window introduces is between **legal/regulatory station**, **productive place** and **professional skill**.

## 159/160 — The ritual station is subject to enforceable external rules
**BGU I 16**, a physical Greek papyrus from Fayum, concerns **priest Panephremmis** accused by **Pasis son of Neilos** of unacceptable hair and **woolen garment** practices. Government officers **Hierax**, strategos, and **Teimagenes**, royal scribe, require five senior Soknopaios priests to investigate under supervision of the **Idios Logos**. The statement's end is lost; neither the elders' full finding nor the investigation's end survives.

The governing authority is **not evaluating whether Panephremmis knows an elixir recipe**. It is enforcing a rule about observable **eligibility / ritual propriety**. **Dress is a sign of professional membership, not an encoded ingredient list.**

## 154/155 — The workshop has recognized legal standing without named technique
`P.Tebt. II 613` leases a house and **associated workshops** in Antinoopolis. The document can establish the **right to use space**, a rent and a property relationship. Its surviving content **does not certify its lessee as a metallurgist, chemist, dyer, or Alchemist**.

A site can be **licensed, leased or administered** while the capacity performed inside it remains invisible to the source.

## 157 — An expert's practice and standing emerge from teachers, patients and a job
Galen receives a recognized appointment as physician to gladiators in Pergamum after years studying philosophy and medicine and traveling to Alexandria. Unlike the anonymous alleged Alchemist, he can be attached to named teachers and a professional role.

**But** the central source is largely his own writings, not an original employment contract. This means there is substantial biographical continuity **at a lower level of independent documentary verification than the papyri**. His later anatomy demonstrations in Rome belong in **162+**.

## 144/145 — Public icon and political authority reside on metal
Alexandrian coinage from Antoninus Pius's year 8 (RPC IV.4 787) combines copper-alloy workmanship, imperial authority, local place name and Harpocrates iconography. Political/religious symbols are publicly legible or recognizable, **not an explanation of gold transmutation**.

## Secrecy audit
For any apparently opaque record ask:
- Is **recognition** of a practitioner being guarded or disciplined?
- Is **access to a site** being leased and managed?
- Is **hands-on knowledge** being trained, recorded, demonstrated or concealed?
- Is a divine image a **symbol of authority** rather than an encoded procedure?
- Is historical **silence** simply the original text being damaged?

These are five different answers. No source among the 141–160 examples demonstrates **deliberate chemical allegory**.

## The THERE → HERE law
**The authority to remain in a priestly role, the authority to use a productive workplace, and the capacity to heal or make an object are separate operations.** A later Alchemist may claim to unite ritual purity, workshop access and material competence, but we must not invent that union in this period.
