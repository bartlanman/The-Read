# Window 27 — Language, Technique and Access: The State Protects Learning, Not Proven Gold Transmutation (321–340 CE)

**Scope:** independently attested civic/ritual authorization and **late-coded** artisan teaching protections; source types matter. [Ten-layer Window 27](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0321-0340_CE__WINDOW.md) · [critical originals and code witnesses](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0321-0340__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

## 1 — Four original or documentarily transmitted operations

**(a) Civic standing made effective (Orkistos, 331).** An original engraved dossier preserves the **30 June 331** rescript confirming Orkistos's civic autonomy and removing Nacoleia's demand for `pecunia pro cultis`. The same inscribed dossier reproduces earlier petitions and rulings (**324–326 petition; 329/330 first grant**). **One monument is not multiple independently unearthed autographs**. It documents formal recognition of a city's station, with economic infrastructure and religious obligations at issue, not an alchemical workshop.

**(b) Conditional religious-cult permission (Hispellum, c.333–337).** The original **CIL XI 5265** rescript approves a new **Flavian** temple/festival arrangement, a city name `Flavia Constans`, and local annually appointed `sacerdotes`; it restricts contamination by `contagiosae superstitionis fraudibus`. Exact sacrificial acts encompassed by this phrase **CONTESTED**. This authorizes an identifiable religious/priestly event station, **not** an Alchemist or systematic prohibition of all pagan gatherings.

**(c) Protected opportunity to learn (334).** `Architectis quam plurimis opus est; sed quia non sunt`—the transmission of *Codex Theodosianus* **13.4.1** acknowledges a lack of architects in Africa, encourages trained youth to study, and exempts learners and parents from some civic duties. **The governing language concerns training and capacity**, but surviving text is **in a fifth-century code with later manuscripts**, not a 334 engraved training roster.

**(d) Exempt artisan craftwork to sustain sons' education (2 August 337).** `Artifices artium brevi subdito comprehensarum ... ab universis muneribus vacare praecipimus`: **CTh 13.4.2** / **CJ 10.66.1** grants specified craftspeople exemptions from `munera`, explicitly **`quo magis cupiant et ipsi peritiores fieri et suos filios erudire`**—to become more skillful and educate their sons. The **CJ** preserves an extensive list of goldsmiths, gilders, silversmiths, metal founders, bronze workers, glassworkers, stone-workers, physicians, painters, dyers/fullers and others. No named Alchemist appears. The actual named individual artisans protected are **not identified**.

## 2 — The posthumous name trap

The **CTh 13.4.2 / CJ 10.66.1** subscription dates the text **2 August 337**, after Constantine's documented **22 May 337 death**. The imperial heading attributes it to **Constantinus**. **Burgess (2008)** finds issuance in Constantine's name during the interregnum plausible; **Lenski (2016)** notes `idem augustus` could be editorial assimilation to the preceding constitution, and the precise issuer cannot be settled from this preserved heading alone.

**Three different facts must not be merged:**
1. A **dated legal-text tradition** calls for exempting specifically listed artisans to cultivate and pass on expertise.
2. A **later compilation** attributes the item to Constantine despite the post-death dated subscription; this is a **documentary/authority problem**.
3. Actual **exemption implementation**, personal recipients and success in training sons are not demonstrable from its formula.

Do **not** claim Constantine personally signed the 337 directive after death; do **not** claim its attribution is universally resolved. This is a documented example of **transmitted authority persisting through a name**, analogous at the method level to the danger of pseudonymous alchemical authority—not proof of a causal link.

**Translation discipline:** the legal compilation's list is not an original 337 signed artifact, and title/term ambiguity around `blattearii`/ `petalourgoi` must not be used to manufacture a specific alchemical operation without critical philology.

## 4 — A genuine gold product is not a goldmaking proof

The [Berlin Münzkabinett **18200650**](https://ikmk.smb.museum/object?id=18200650) catalogues an **original gold solidus**, Nicomedia **335**, **4.54 g**, with Constantine's jubilee/“Victory” imagery. The [British Museum's **1844,1015.310**](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1844-1015-310) offers another **335** gold solidus, related but *different detailed RIC type*. Actual physical coinage demonstrates gold handling, striking and monetary political authority. **No nucleic/atomic change, artificial gold manufacture, named technical gold-maker or initiated transformation priest is shown by these objects.**

It would be a category error to read Constantine's regnal year/anniversary title as a reagent cipher. Likewise gilding, gold-beating, silverwork and alloying are **real skilled capacities**, distinct from claims that one elemental substance became another.

**Leiden X / Stockholm:** papyri containing practical recipes for coloring metals, stones and dyes are broadly **late third/early fourth century** and cannot be assigned an exact copy date **331 or 337**. **Zosimos of Panopolis** is approximately c.300 and later written/commentarial voice; the preserved texts do not document that he received the above artisan protections, took a role in Orkistos or tutored particular people during 321–340.

## 6 — THERE / OUTSIDE / HERE / REMAINDER

**THERE:** practical monetary and precious-metal operations, Roman civic/rural political hierarchy, priestly cult roles, late antique technical training and book culture.

**THE OUTSIDE:** 331 fiscal pressure on newly restored municipality; c.333–337 imperial conditional response to civic request for festivals/temple; 334 scarcity of trained architects; 337 burdens that might inhibit technical skill and sons' instruction. These are **explicitly linked to named institutional subjects**. No comparable officially documented pressure on a named Alchemist.

**HERE:** legal authority can affirm civic and priestly status by petition; can protect trained artisanal capacity and its **transmission** even without acknowledging any theory of material transmutation. The inherited original documents and the more distantly copied law do **not** yield identical degrees of confidence about issuer, beneficiaries or implementation.

**THE DEEPEST IDEA:** **The law is now protecting the ability to become more capable and pass craft skills forward, not merely registering a valuable product.** That is a documented mechanism from **capacity → access to time → training → family transmission → socially recognized occupation**, though its execution remains unmeasured. It is the clearest comparator yet for a future *institutionally recognizable Alchemist*—precisely because the statutes **never name one**.

**REMAINDER:** 337 issuer, names of real exempt craftworkers, exact effects on sons' learning, wording of any local gold-related apprenticeships, alchemical commentators' dates, religious-cult `superstitio` scope, Greek–Chinese contact, lost alchemical manuscripts.
