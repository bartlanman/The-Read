# 121–140 CE — The Speaking Stone, the Created Citizen, and the Counterfeit Emperor's Voice

## 1. A sound is heard; a poet decides what it means
In November **130 CE**, Julia Balbilla carved four Greek verses on the Memnon colossus when Hadrian and Sabina visited Thebes. At least one passage describes a mysterious **sound**; the poem supplies a **heroic/mythical explanation**—Memnon answers from stone—and uses it to construct imperial favor.

**What survives:** actual poem carved on old sandstone, dated on one inscription **21 November 130**.
**What is asserted:** an ancient visitor's hearing and mythical reading of noise.
**What is not warranted:** proof that a god literally spoke, a proven 130 physical acoustic model or an alchemical formula.

Balbilla's **archaizing Aeolic Greek** signals education and literary links to Sappho. That specialized language lends a *literary station* to a named writer without encoding a technical chemical recipe. Her words are **public** inscriptions, not a hidden manual.

## 2. A city is founded; a social identity begins to operate
Hadrian founded Antinoopolis **30 October 130**, and original papyri from **April and May 133** indicate early settlement. The city incorporates selected residents into a new legal/civic arrangement. Political authorization **changes a person's juridical standing** without changing bodily matter.

**Do not retroject:** Later technical definitions of the Antinoites / `New Greeks` should be dated according to their own papyrus witness, not automatically assigned to a specific date in 130.

## 3. A bronze instrument is described; its insides remain lost
Han history assigns **132 CE** seismoscope to Zhang Heng, its eight dragon heads and bronze balls/tadpole-catching toads physically described while the internal causal mechanism is partly unspecified. **Original lost**; modern reconstructed mechanisms differ. The public **claim that an instrument registers a remote earthquake** is not identical to verified recovery of how the first device worked.

**Secrecy?** The mechanism being physically *inside* a bronze vessel does not establish a deliberate secret-text cipher or an exclusive initiation into alchemy.

## 4. The false 'Hadrian' speaks about crafts
`Historia Augusta` *Quadriga Tyrannorum* 8 contains a purported personal **letter from Hadrian** describing Alexandrian glassblowers, papyrus workers, linen makers and 'color-changing' cups gifted by a priest. The letter is **a late-antique forgery**, not a 130 CE emperor's own account. Source-critical scholars including Syme, Schmid and Schwartz show problems with its authenticity.

**Prohibitions:** No inference of Hadrian's actual purchase of magical glass, no verified alchemical cup, no 130 CE craft census, no claim this is the first Alchemist mentioned by a Roman ruler. The letter itself is evidence of **later writing under another's famous name**.

## 5. The four different thresholds
| Case | Actually attested? | Who confers standing? | What is missing? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Memnon stone 'voice' | Actual **130 CE inscription** attests poetic report | Poet, visiting imperial entourage, readers | Cause and divinity |
| Antinoopolis 'new citizen' | Foundation **130**, original papyri **133** | Emperor and legal/civic institutions | Complete early rights and people |
| Zhang device sensing tremors | Fifth-century history attests a **132 CE claim**, modern models | Official engineer and retrospective historians | Original mechanism and exact contemporary measurements |
| Hadrian's craft letter | Actual text is **late antique forgery** | Pseudonymous author impersonating emperor | Hadrian's authentic testimony; actual 130 cups |

**THERE → HERE:** A person may recognize or confer authority by publicly naming what a stone *means*, by creating new civic status, by claiming useful sensory apparatus or by falsifying a famous writer's voice. These are not equivalent kinds of warrant, and none creates an established 121–140 CE Alchemist profession.
