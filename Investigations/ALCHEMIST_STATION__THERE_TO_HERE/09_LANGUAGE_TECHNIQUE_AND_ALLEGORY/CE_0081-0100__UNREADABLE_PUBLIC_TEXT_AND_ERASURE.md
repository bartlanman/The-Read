# Window 15 — The Public Script No One Erased

**Period:** 81–100 CE. Case: Benevento Isis obelisks (original 88/89 CE) and the 96 CE erasure decree reported by Suetonius. Preserve actual dates of inscriptions and dates of later Roman literary testimony.

## 1. A sacred text that is public but inaccessible to most
At Beneventum in Italy, patron **Rutilius Lupus** dedicated two granite obelisks to Isis 'Lady of Benevento' honoring Domitian in **88/89 CE**. Original monuments have deeply incised Middle Egyptian hieroglyphs and the emperor's name in cartouches. The text was *displayed* at an Isis sanctuary, not buried or secretly hidden. Its language was not readable to most first-century Italians.

**Differentiated stations:**
- **Patron** authorizes and pays for monument.
- **Religious/linguistic specialist** probably composes the classical Middle Egyptian text (modern philological inference; individual unnamed).
- **Stoneworkers** carve signs, potentially in unfamiliar language.
- **Ritual audience** encounters the royal divine image without necessarily decoding every word.
- **Later epigrapher/Egyptologist** can translate an otherwise unreadable inscription.

The ability to sponsor, make, read and authorize meaning can be divided among people.

## 2. A script may protect memory without being intended as protection
After Domitian's assassination in **96 CE**, Suetonius says the Senate ordered his public inscriptions erased. Yet the Domitian cartouches on the Benevento obelisks survive.

**Evidence grades:**
- Original Egyptian text survives: **MATERIAL FACT**.
- Senate ordered erasure: **ANCIENT RETROSPECTIVE TEXTUAL REPORT**.
- Relative unreadability helped name escape: **MODERN INTERPRETATION**.
- Designers of 88/89 deliberately wrote hieroglyphs to defeat a future ban: **UNKNOWN / UNWARRANTED**.

The strongest idea is that **a difference in literacy or the material/cultural reach of an erasing institution can produce *unintended preservation***.

## 3. Distinguish secrecy from script-specialization
Written opacity can arise from:
- unfamiliar language or script;
- functional technical terminology;
- guarded institutional instruction;
- multi-stage translation and transcription;
- lost context;
- deliberate encryption, if evidenced.

These causes are NOT interchangeable. No evidence here maps a Benevento hieroglyph onto a hidden furnace ingredient.

## 4. Philosophy expulsions and the misuse of persecution as proof
Domitian's **c.93–94 CE** expulsion of philosophers documents political risk for intellectual communities. Modern source criticism finds date and scope disputed, with later sources including astrologers. Neither **philosopher**, **astrologer**, **Egyptian priest**, **stone-carver**, nor **Alchemist** is an equivalent professional category.

To claim 'alchemical works were secret because Domitian persecuted Alchemists' would require documents showing an Alchemist actually threatened under that policy. None is identified.

## 5. 97 CE attempted knowledge crossing, not a transmission result
Gan Ying is reported to have traveled toward **Da Qin** but not reached Rome in **97 CE**. The geographic horizon of a society can expand without any identifiable technical manuscript crossing between China and the Greek-speaking world.

## 6. The most profound THERE → HERE operation
**What is legible to a specialist need not be legible to a ruler, a hostile authority, an artisan, or the later public.** A station's language can outlive its patron because recognition is distributed unevenly across people and time.

This is a **model to test** against later alchemical manuscripts. It is **not proof** the Greek alchemical writers deliberately encrypted recipes to escape persecution.
