# 80–61 BCE — A King's Private Papers, Roman Translation and the Skilled Eye

**Status:** philological case study. Roman political and medical comparisons only; **not evidence of an alchemical code**.

## Pliny the Elder's text — an essential dating issue
Pliny writes in the **late first century CE**, reporting events around **63 BCE**. His voice, particularly his criticism of withholding knowledge, reflects his own Roman intellectual argument, *not necessarily Mithridates' beliefs or motives*.

### Station and knowledge custody
Pliny, *NH* 25.7:
- *scrinium commentationum* — case/archive of notes and observations.
- *in arcanis suis* — in his private/inner holdings.
- *transferre ea sermone nostro* — an order to transfer the content into Roman speech/Latin.

**Working reading:** royal collection → military conquest → new custodianship → translation into Latin. It is a **social-linguistic transfer** without demonstrated transfer of laboratory skill.

### Why secrecy matters
Pliny 25.1 criticizes those who hide discoveries and thereby make exclusive knowledge into a source of authority. This is explicit evidence for a Roman **first-century CE discourse about secrecy and prestige**, not direct evidence of the earlier Pontic monarch's intent.

### Why technique is not equivalent to text
Pliny 25.8 criticizes herbal illustrations because paints cannot reproduce all colors and copyists distort images; plants vary by season. This is a concrete description of **a failure of representation to supply identification skill**.

**THERE:** plant/poison expertise needs seeing, comparing, material contact.
**Text:** botanical description and possibly image.
**Pressure:** copying error, translation, lost practical setting.
**HERE:** readers can inherit *knowledge claims* without the full *capacity* to judge or enact them.

## Cicero — secrecy of political authority is not secrecy of matter
Cicero, *De lege agraria* 2.41–44 (63 BCE) speaks about the proposed hidden transfer of Egypt. Here public language describes a concealed legal transfer, not encrypted metallurgy.

## Han — rhetorical dialogue is not alchemical allegory
The *Yantielun* is composed under Emperor Xuan (74–49 BCE), based on 81 BCE debate. Its later literary speech-making should not be mistaken for an encoded experiment or exact original court transcript.

## Counterfactual tests
To prove an alchemical cipher we would need an extant glossary showing cover-name → procedure, a dated parallel recipe, an author stating intentional concealment of an ingredient, or a chemical reconstruction independently supported by text. We have none for this window.
