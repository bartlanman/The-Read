# 101–120 CE — The Name on Matter Is Not Necessarily the Maker

**Physical witnesses:** CIL XV 18 **110 CE** (Roman urban brick production), an original **116 CE** stamped brick preserved in Oxford's Ashmolean collection, and **P.Brem. 4 113–117 CE** original Greek petition to prefect Rutilius Lupus.

A brick's abbreviated owner/estate name and consular year communicate provenance and jurisdiction; **they do not identify the hands that made or fired it**. Greek `Ῥουτιλίωι Λούπωι τῶι κρατίστωι ἡγεμόνι` in a petition confers recognition on a provincial prefect; it does not establish practical mineral mastery.

**Competing identity:** Benevento's **88/89 CE** Isis obelisks name a `Rutilius Lupus`. Scholars debate whether he is **M. Rutilius Lupus** brick proprietor and later prefect. Similar names, offices, and dates invite a connected narrative, but matching strings cannot close a personal identity in history.

**Actual pressure and unrelated suffering:** anti-Jewish attacks, revolt and repression from **115–117** damaged Jewish communities. Family letters show fear and need for protection, not a persecuted class of alchemists. **Maria the Jewess** is remembered chiefly in Zosimos's much later texts and is not placed in 116 CE.

**THERE → HERE:** a source may make ownership/authority extremely visible while practical craft remains anonymous; language can create an illusion of a continuous named station where biography has not yet been verified.
