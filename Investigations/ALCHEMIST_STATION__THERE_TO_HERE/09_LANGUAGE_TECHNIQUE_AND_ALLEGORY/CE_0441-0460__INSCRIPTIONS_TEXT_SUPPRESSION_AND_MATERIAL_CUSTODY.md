# Window 33 — Original Names, Forbidden Writings and Material Custody

[Full ten-layer dossier](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0441-0460_CE__WINDOW.md) · [Source register](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0441-0460__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

**447 bilingual gate inscriptions:** Greek and Latin original public inscriptions name **Constantine** and commemorate a reconstruction in **sixty days**. Their separate languages and same project do not constitute two independent construction diaries. Later **Cyrus** attribution is contested. An authentic physical inscription is stronger for the official's identity than a later conflated biography, although the commemorated duration is not independently measured.

**448 suppression:** a state order targets writings of **Porphyry** and other specified religious authors. This is an explicit textual suppression rule, unlike presumed alchemical book burnings. There is no identified goldmaking book in the wording or original confiscation register. Porphyry was an earlier author, not living in 448.

**451 professional authorization:** council canons forbid purchasing ordained office. This is a regulation of social standing rather than a test of material craft. **Dioscorus the Christian bishop** who appears at Chalcedon is distinct from the **priest of Serapis named Dioscorus** in an undated alchemical dialogue. Neither name alone verifies chemical practice.

**458/459 civic material law:** a Western legal prohibition on unauthorized dismantling of monuments regulates who may take and reuse worked stone. Exact subscription and year remain **CONTESTED**; this does not mean physical material was chemically transmuted. Destroying a wall or conserving it is a station/ownership decision, not a reagent operation.
