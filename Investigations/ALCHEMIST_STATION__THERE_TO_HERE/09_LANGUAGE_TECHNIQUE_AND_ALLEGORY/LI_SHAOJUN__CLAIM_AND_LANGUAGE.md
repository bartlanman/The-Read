# Li Shaojun — from material technique to royal allegory? A falsifiable reading

**Date:** c.133 BCE narrative context; *Shiji* 28 written after reported event, surviving via later transmission.  
**Rule:** Do not call a ritual term a code-word merely because a modern reader cannot convert it into a recipe.

## The claimed sequence in Shiji 28
**Ritual stove (祠灶)** → **summoning beings (致物)** → **cinnabar (丹沙)** becomes **gold (黃金)** → **vessels for eating/drinking** → **longer life** → **Penglai immortals (蓬萊)** → **feng/shan rites** → **undying status**.

This is a historically recorded *chain of claimed causal and ritual conditions*, not an experimentally validated material sequence.

## Read each term on its own historical grounds
- **方士**: social category of specialists in methods, potentially encompassing many ritual, medical, prognostic and transmutational arts. Do not translate as a credentialed profession of Alchemist.
- **方**: the method or art Li claims; after his death it is allegedly received by a court agent. Content not preserved in the narrative.
- **丹沙**: cinnabar. It names a material rather than a demonstrated encrypted substitute.
- **黃金**: gold. The text states the intention to produce gold, not analytical proof that it happened.
- **蓬萊**: legendary island/realm; a narrative horizon for immortality, not a proven alias for a reagent.
- **匿其年及其生長**: concealed age/origins; explicit withheld *biography*, not an exact description of trade secrecy.
- **化去**: emperor's postmortem rereading. Narrative supplies a case where interpretation protects *claimed authority* against evidence of ordinary death.

## Allegory hypotheses still open
1. **Procedural metaphor?** Does a separate authenticated text link a ritual element to a laboratory operation?
2. **Sacred cosmology?** Does the writer present a sincere theory in which ritual and material transformation mutually enable each other?
3. **Prestige?** Does mystery of the practitioner's identity alter the audience's estimation of his capacity?
4. **Decoder audience?** Is there evidence of a specialized master-to-student explanation or restricted lexicon?
5. **Later reinterpretation?** Did subsequent Daoist or historiographical texts make the account more encoded than originally presented?

**Present verdict:** ritual and material language coexist. Conscious disguise of a chemical process through allegory remains *unverified*, whereas deliberate concealment of biographical details is stated in the *Shiji* account.

## Crucial station insight
A patron can protect a teaching's authority through **interpretation** even if the promised outcome does not occur. The emperor reportedly takes Li's actual death as evidence of transformation/departure and orders transmission of methods.

This is an extreme case of *perception shaping recognized authority*, not independent proof of immortalization.
