# Window 22 — The Missing Key: Technique, Learned Game, and Unintended Secrecy

**Period:** 221–240 CE. Primary focus: **Sextus Julius Africanus' `Kestoi`**, associated with the Severan court **222–235**, and how parts of its material/medical/natural procedures were represented through signs.

## 1. This is the first strong *named* author using ingredient substitutions in our research stack
Africanus organized a broad Greek corpus of techniques including dye-related procedures later quoted by alchemical authors, medicine, agriculture and military lore. In **several transmitted recipes**, ingredient names are **referred to indirectly through geometric forms and musical symbols**. They point to explanatory notices contained in an appendix.

**Scholarly finding:** *not writing the ingredient name directly* is a real deliberate *textual choice*. Source: Dufault, `Early Greek Alchemy`, pp.88–90, fragment references to F12.

## 2. But does symbolic substitution actually keep the knowledge secret?
**Dufault's counterargument is critical.** Although the study calls the method *rudimentary encryption*, it immediately says it was **unlikely intended to encrypt** against readers with Greek education. It may be a cultivated **puzzle, demonstration of paideia, or performance of specialized expertise**.

The **symbol→word appendix is missing**. This makes the text hard for *us* to decode completely. It does NOT prove that it was unreadable to a pupil or courtier in 230 CE, or that the author feared persecution.

| Cause of modern obscurity | Status for Africanus |
|---|---|
| Authorial geometric/musical substitution | **Supported in transmitted recipes** |
| Specific intended audience of Greek-educated readers | **Scholarly inference** |
| Formal guild access/initiatory oath | **No evidence** |
| Effective code to keep secrets from Greek speakers | **Unlikely, according to Dufault** |
| Original commercial monopoly | **Unproven** |
| Anti-persecution/safety cipher | **Unproven** |
| Appendix lost in copying | **Supported absence** |
| Original author completely known and every recipe authentic | **Unproven; variant transmission and later addition risk** |

## 3. Material witness vs symbolic fragments—DO NOT CONFLATE THEM
British Library **Papyrus 2040**, `P.Oxy III 412`, **really preserves part of Book 18** in ancient Greek on its front, including Africanus' *claim to restore* a Homeric necromantic passage; the back is a **276 CE legal will**. The physical literary papyrus copied **225–275/276** could be from our window but does not have an **exact 221–240 date**.

**This Book 18 papyrus is not the leaf containing the geometric ingredient code.** Those symbols are known through *other surviving fragments and later textual transmission*, not through a page of Papyrus 2040. Mixing these witnesses would overclaim provenance.

## 4. Textual authority and its claimant
Africanus' `Chronography` (c.221/222), **diplomatic representation of Emmaus** (traditional 221, another ~226), his letters to Origen critiquing a claimed biblical passage, and imperial dedication of *Kestoi* to **Severus Alexander** create a much more identifiable writer than Pseudo-Democritus.

The **Homeric necromancy** passage in Book 18 is not proved authentic to Homer merely because Africanus supplies it. Like an alchemical pseudonym, a **claim to restore an ancient text** is different from actual authorship.

## 5. What is this scholar's station?
**Known:** Christian chronographer, writer of technical/natural recipe collections, court-oriented intellectual and public envoy.
**Close to alchemical technical tradition:** dyes and material coloring procedures later cited by Greek alchemists.
**NOT demonstrated:** a formally titled working `alchemist`, state credential to turn copper to gold, a patron-funded operating metallurgical laboratory, an independently measured product.

Dufault considers **Zosimos (~300 CE)** the first known *alchemical commentator*. That designation and Africanus' authorial work belong to different literary stations, even though they can share forms of procedure.

## The THERE→HERE operation
**A text can be deliberately symbolized without being deliberately hidden. When the explanatory key is subsequently lost, a cultivated game may appear to later readers as a profound secret.**

The historical investigation must ask *who could read the signs then*, *why the signs were used*, *when the key was lost*, and *whether any work result was replicable*—not simply treat unusual geometry as evidence of persecuted ancient chemists.
