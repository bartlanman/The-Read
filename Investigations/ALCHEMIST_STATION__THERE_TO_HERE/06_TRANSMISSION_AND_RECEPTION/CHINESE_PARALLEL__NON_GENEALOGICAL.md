# Chinese alchemy: parallel historical line, not a claimed descent from Greek Egypt

**Status:** sourced comparative branch. Primary-language source reading still pending.

Fabrizio Pregadio's *Great Clarity* [H06] discusses the **144 BCE edict of Emperor Jing** against counterfeit gold, mid-second-century Han court transformations and rituals, and the now-lost *Hongbao yuanbi shu*, associated with the mid-second century BCE but known through later testimony.

## Difference that matters
- **Chinese court/station**: fangshi, royal court, ritual setting, transformation of substances, elixirs, ingestion, and political interdiction.
- **Greco-Egyptian station**: metals/dyes/gemstones, pseudonymous technical books, institutional religious setting, and later learned commentators.
- **Relationship**: **NO SPECIFIC DIRECT TRANSMISSION CLAIMED** between the traditions.

## Secrecy test
An edict against counterfeiting is not by itself a decree against *alchemy as a whole*. Separate law, market protection, spiritual legitimacy, personal ability, and dangerous substances.

## Language test
Chinese categories of waidan, fangshi, elixir and Daoist revelation require their own Chinese philology. They cannot simply be mapped onto Hermes, Democritus, or Western alchemical allegory.

## Important correction deposited in Window 03 (2026-10-18)

**The 144 BCE edict is not an edict prohibiting alchemy as such.** Pregadio's detailed reading of *Hanshu* Jingdi ji distinguishes private minting/counterfeit gold from alchemical elixir preparation and spiritual/ritual practices. Earlier readings by Dubs inferred an anti-alchemy ban; that inference is not supported by the statute's wording. **Li Shaojun's 133 BCE court episode belongs to the following 140–121 BCE window.** [Primary Chinese](https://ctext.org/han-shu/jing-di-ji/ens); [Pregadio 2006](https://doi.org/10.11126/stanford/9780804751773.003.0002); [Oxford project chronology](https://www.cabinet.ox.ac.uk/chinese-alchemy).

## Window 04 — Li Shaojun and emperor Wu (c.133 BCE)

The earliest relatively detailed historical narrative of a **named court-recognized transformative specialist** describes Li Shaojun as a **fangshi**. *Shiji* 28 (a work compiled later than the reported encounter) says he concealed his origins and age, drew gifts and patronage, connected stove rituals with changing cinnabar to gold and longevity, and was honored by the emperor. After Li's ordinary death, Emperor Wu allegedly viewed it as transformed departure and arranged transmission of the man's methods. Neither an executable original formula nor successful gold production survives in that account. **Allegory and ritual are not automatically encrypted chemistry.**

See [Window 04](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/FORMATION_BCE_0140-0121_BCE__WINDOW.md), [ST-004](../11_STATION_TRANSFORMATION_REGISTER/ST-004_HAN_LI_SHAOJUN__CIRCA_133_BCE.md), [language analysis](../09_LANGUAGE_TECHNIQUE_AND_ALLEGORY/LI_SHAOJUN__CLAIM_AND_LANGUAGE.md).


## Window 05 — Court danger, rank and restricted methods (c.113–110 BCE)

Shiji 28 reports that Luan Da argued other fangshi would fall silent if they met the execution suffered by Shaoweng. He demanded recognized guest status, seals and elite ties to become a credible envoy of extraordinary methods. Emperor Wu granted privileges, later investigated his claims and had him executed. After Luan's rise numerous people reportedly claimed secret arts. **This is evidence for a social and political *use of secrecy language*, not for verified transmutation.** See [Window 05](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/FORMATION_BCE_0120-0101_BCE__WINDOW.md) and [ST-007](../11_STATION_TRANSFORMATION_REGISTER/ST-007_HAN_LUAN_DA__113_112_BCE.md).


## Window 06 — 100–81 BCE: from extraordinary claim to public economic argument

The *Hanshu*'s 91 BCE witchcraft affair concerns court accusations and destruction, **not a general trial of alchemical workers**. The 89 BCE Luntai edict is about costly military policy; *Zizhi tongjian* later describes a broad purge of immortality-seeking *fangshi*, but this dramatic episode is contested by source critics. In **81 BCE** the court debates the state monopoly on **iron and salt**, a significant change in the public scrutiny of material productive authority. These are **three different kinds of outside pressure**. See [Window 06](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/FORMATION_BCE_0100-0081_BCE__WINDOW.md) and [sources](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/0100-0081_BCE__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).


## 60–41 BCE — The lost Huainan texts become an actual workshop commission

*Hanshu* 36 records **Liu Gengsheng / Xiang** presenting rare Huainan goldmaking material (the `枕中鴻寶苑祕書` titles are not unambiguously one book); Emperor Xuan assigns him to **Shangfang** craft/metal work, and after significant expense the method fails (`方不驗`). Officials accuse him of false gold, putting him at risk of execution; his sentence is reduced. Conventional dates c.60–56 BCE require further critical dating of the transmitted biography. Unlike the 144 BCE law, this is **a specific commissioned goldmaking attempt**. The `secret text → technical authority → test → failure → sanction` sequence should now be central to the Chinese parallel history. ## Window 10 — classification and distribution (20–1 BCE)

**Liu Xin** completed or advanced a court catalogue, Qilüe, broadly 7–1 BCE (often c.5 BCE), whose original is lost. The later Hanshu Yiwenzhi groups **fangji** into medical canons, recipes, bedchamber arts and **shenxian** immortality writings. Category is not scientific or craft validation. In **3 BCE**, Hanshu reports that Queen Mother of the West messages and tallies moved through communities and promised deathlessness to people who wore the message. This report is a counterexample to universal secrecy of immortality promises, but not a successful alchemical practice. [Window 10](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/FORMATION_BCE_0020-0001_BCE__WINDOW.md).


## Window 11 (1–20 CE) — The metal does not change when its legal value changes

Wang Mang's **7 CE bronze knife coin with gold inlay** and **14 CE copper-alloy huoquan** survive in the British Museum. Currency reforms in **7, 9, 10, 14 and 20 CE** redefined how money was named, valued, issued and restricted; `一刀平五千` assigned **face value**, not chemical composition. Later *Hanshu* describes penalties for `私鑄` unauthorized coin casting. **Craft capability, sovereign permission to issue, and true transmutation must be kept distinct.** No evidence of a licensed or persecuted Alchemist's profession follows from monetary regulation. [Window 11](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0001-0020_CE__WINDOW.md).


## Window 12 — 21–40 CE: real metallurgy, credited administrative station

Wang Mang's Xin rule ended in **23 CE**; Eastern Han was established in **25 CE**. A fifth-century `Hou Hanshu` account dates **Du Shi's governorship at Nanyang to 31 CE** and credits him with `水排`: a water-driven bellows for iron-foundry work producing farm tools. The report claims savings of human effort and higher benefit. **Named official ≠ proven sole machine inventor;** the identity of operating artisans is absent. No original mechanism or precisely measured output is recovered. This practical metal-fabrication story is **not** an elixir or gold transmutation and does not create an Egyptian genealogy. [Window 12](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0021-0040_CE__WINDOW.md) · [ST-031](../11_STATION_TRANSFORMATION_REGISTER/ST-031_DU_SHI__31_CE_WATER_BELLOWS.md).


## Window 15 — 97 CE: attempted contact with Da Qin, not demonstrated technical transmission

The *Hou Hanshu* (fifth-century compilation of earlier events) says **Ban Chao sent Gan Ying in 97 CE** to learn about **Da Qin**, normally read as the Roman world. He reached **Tiaozhi** near a sea, variously reconstructed in/around the Persian Gulf, but **never reached Rome**. This establishes a historical **geographical contact horizon** and mediation through western peoples. **It does not establish shared Chinese/Greek chemical recipes, traveling alchemical masters, direct institutional collaboration, or any actual apprenticeship**. Keep Han and Greco-Egyptian alchemical station formation **NON-GENEALOGICAL** until contemporary matching sources appear. [Window 15](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0081-0100_CE__WINDOW.md) · [ST-041](../11_STATION_TRANSFORMATION_REGISTER/ST-041_GAN_YING_97_CE__CONTACT_NOT_LINEAGE.md).


## Window 17 — 132 CE Zhang Heng seismoscope: technical station, no alchemical ancestry

**Zhang Heng** was credited by a much later `Hou Hanshu` narrative with creating a bronze `候風地動儀` earthquake-indicating device in **132 CE**. The original machine is missing; modern mechanical designs vary. Anecdotes of subsequent confirmation, often attached to a **134 CE Longxi earthquake**, must remain retrospective rather than an independently preserved operation log. This is a **Han technical authority** story, not a document showing Greek–Chinese alchemical teaching. [Window 17](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0121-0140_CE__WINDOW.md) · [ST-048](../11_STATION_TRANSFORMATION_REGISTER/ST-048_ZHANG_HENG_132__SEISMOSCOPE_DESCRIPTION_VS_OBJECT.md).


## Window 20 (181–200 CE) — no demonstrated Han–Greek technical bridge

Original Roman coins and Alexandrian provincial money demonstrate imperial political identity and the metalworkers needed to issue it. Galen's **192 CE** fire demonstrates fragility of medical tools, ingredients and copying networks; these events do **NOT** establish the arrival, destruction or transfer of Chinese `fangshi`, metallurgical recipes or an alchemical lineage in Rome/Egypt. A geographically and politically connected Roman world is not evidence of a named technical instructor or translated Han treatise. [Window 20](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0181-0200_CE__WINDOW.md).


## Window 22 — Julius Africanus' named Greek technical station is not a China-Greece transmission bridge

Africanus' *Kestoi* (dedicated to Severus Alexander **222–235**) organizes learned technical/natural recipes, including methods later cited in Greek alchemical writing. His use of Greek geometric/musical notation to refer to ingredients does not supply evidence of a Chinese teacher, Chinese manuscript translation or Han recipe origin. The third-century Oxyrhynchus **Book 18 papyrus dated 225–275/276** has no attested cross-Asian alchemical transmission. **Continue the Chinese and Greek lines separately until a matched original witness or demonstrable translation is found.** [Window 22](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0221-0240_CE__WINDOW.md).
