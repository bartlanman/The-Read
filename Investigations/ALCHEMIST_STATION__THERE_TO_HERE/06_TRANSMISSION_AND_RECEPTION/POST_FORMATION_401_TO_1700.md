# Post-formation transmission and reception — 401–1700 CE

This is a *first-pass source-anchored chronological scaffold*, not a completed 65-window critical edition. The detailed working windows are in [the master chronology](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/STACK_INDEX__200_BCE_TO_1700_CE.md).

### Intellectual transitions for special scrutiny
- Greek commentaries and Byzantine manuscripts: note dates and attributions.
- Arabic corpus: treat Jābirian collections as multi-stage, not a single-author event.
- Emerald Tablet: distinguish Arabic textual witnesses from supposed ancient Hermetic authorship.
- Twelfth-century Latin transmission: study translators and contested 1144 attribution.
- Thirteenth-century scholasticization: Albertus, Bacon, Pseudo-Geber.
- Fourteenth-century medicinal quintessence texts.
- Renaissance and early modern alchemy: Paracelsus; print and secrecy; multimedial symbol systems; laboratory chymistry and Newton.

**Key caution:** translations, manuscript copies and philosophical receptions are different events, sometimes centuries apart.
