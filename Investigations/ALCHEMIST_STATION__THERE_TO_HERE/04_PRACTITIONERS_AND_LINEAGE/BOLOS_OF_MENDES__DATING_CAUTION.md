# Bolos of Mendes — Open dating and identity problem

**Station:** natural-history / medico-magical author in Egypt, not securely a practicing 'alchemist'.  
**Window:** DO NOT assign to 200–181 BCE without stronger specific dating.  
**Date range:** Scholars variously place him in the third or second century BCE. Some traditions associate him with the third-century BCE poet Callimachus; others give later dates.

## The critical separation

1. **Bolos of Mendes**, an author associated with natural properties, marvels, pharmacology and texts attributed to Democritus, is represented principally through later testimonia and fragments. [S03]
2. **Pseudo-Democritus**, author of the alchemical *Four Books* on dyeing and metallic coloration, is a later, probably **first-century CE** figure, not to be collapsed with Bolos. [S04]
3. The historical linkage is a research question about textual conventions, names, authority, and later reception—not evidence for a directly witnessed teacher/student chain.

## Why it matters for station formation

The invocation of **Democritus** illustrates how a name might lend philosophical credibility to technical knowledge. That interpretive mechanism cannot prove when the actual professional station 'Alchemist' arose.

## Next research requirements
