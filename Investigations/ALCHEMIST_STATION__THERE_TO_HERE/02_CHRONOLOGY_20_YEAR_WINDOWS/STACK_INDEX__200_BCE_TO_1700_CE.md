# The Alchemist — Complete 20-year chronological stack (200 BCE–1700 CE)

**Status:** FIRST PASS, cross-period evidence scaffold. **95** consecutive non-overlapping inclusive windows, with no year zero.

- **200 BCE–400 CE:** 30 formation windows; the first 200–181 BCE has a separately researched long dossier preserved at its original path.
- **401–1700 CE:** 65 later reception windows; anchor-rich intervals are marked but less-studied periods remain explicitly OPEN.

The **nine-layer stack** is [defined here](../00_METHOD_AND_EVIDENCE/STACK_FIELD_PROTOCOL.md), with dedicated [secrecy](../03_PERCEPTION_AND_STATION/SECRECY__COMPETING_CAUSES.md) and [language/allegory](../09_LANGUAGE_TECHNIQUE_AND_ALLEGORY/README.md) investigations.

## Open / pending source checks
- All Greek first-century Pseudo-Democritean dating is broader than a window.
- The *Letter of Isis to Horus* is disputed; do not treat its legend of initiation as a documented human chain.
- Zosimos around 300 CE: resolve each treatise's textual witnesses and chronology separately.
- Claims of an earlier royal secrecy law from Zosimos and later Diocletian book-burning stories demand source-critical examination.
- Arabic Emerald Tablet witnesses: collate dated Arabic recensions, not mythical attribution to ancient Hermes.
- Jābirian collections cannot be given a single author or publication date.
- Latin Robert of Chester 1144 has disputed attribution/date despite its conventional historical position.
- In fifteenth-century and later emblem literature, allegory may deliberately **teach** as well as obscure.
- Missing-window text is a **research gap**, never a declaration of historical emptiness.
