# Window 17 — 121–140 CE: A Stone That 'Speaks', a City That Makes Citizens, and an Emperor's Letter That Was Forged

**Status:** SOURCE-CRITICAL TEN-LAYER WINDOW (2026-10-18). Three primary kinds of evidence: real 130 CE carved Greek poems, direct early-133 CE settlement papyri, and a later *purported* Hadrianic letter demonstrated by scholars to be a fabrication. The independent Eastern Han report of Zhang Heng's 132 CE mechanical seismoscope is a later-written dynastic history, not a recovered instrument. **No securely named practicing Greek-Egyptian Alchemist with a dated 121–140 recipe or workshop is established.**
**Span:** 121–140 CE, inclusive.
**Main question:** How do material phenomena, public literary interpretation, legal changes to civic standing and retrospective false attribution manufacture different sorts of knowledge authority?

## 01 — THERE: dated historical setting
- **Hadrian, emperor 117–138 CE:** travels Egypt in **130 CE** with Sabina and entourage; his companion **Antinous dies in the Nile** during the visit. The circumstances are disputed; don't substitute a story of occult sacrifice for documentation. [W17-E01,W17-E02]
- **30 October 130 CE:** Hadrian founds **Antinoopolis**, a new Greek-style polis in Middle Egypt named for Antinous. The city's later citizenship, city council and civic rights cannot all be projected without proof into the moment of foundation. **Actual 133 CE papyri** document early settlement and privilege developments. [W17-E02–E04]
- **20–21 November 130 CE:** at Egyptian Thebes, Hadrian/Sabina hear or visit the famously **'speaking' northern Colossus of Memnon**, originally a colossal figure of **Amenhotep III** damaged centuries before. **Julia Balbilla** records the journey in **four surviving Greek poems carved on the statue**. One has a **Hathyr 25 = 21 November 130 CE** date. These original stones are exceptionally strong contemporary evidence of a poet's claimed experience and the imperial audience's public commemoration. [W17-P01–P02]
- **132 CE:** Eastern Han astronomer, scholar and mechanical specialist **Zhang Heng** is credited in later *Hou Hanshu* with making a **bronze seismoscope** (`候風地動儀`) with dragons, toads and dropped balls to signal seismic direction. The original mechanism has **not survived**, later reconstructions differ, and the chronology of its claimed practical confirmation requires close checking. [W17-H01–H03]
- **135–138 CE** approaches end of Hadrian's reign; **138 CE** Antoninus Pius succeeds. These are political stations, not evidence of chemical transmutation.
- **Greek alchemical documentation:** an emerging pseudo-Democritean *Four Books* corpus is broadly assigned first century, possibly earlier than this period; original is lost. **Maria the Jewess** remains of uncertain lifetime and is mainly attested by late 3rd/early 4th-century citations (Zosimos), not a proven 130 CE practitioner. [W17-G01–G02]

## 02 — CAPACITY: what is actually observable?
**Stone and inscription:** the Memnon colossus and Balbilla's Greek words are surviving original period objects. The **carving** is physically verified. The poet's statement that the stone 'spoke' does not establish a literal divine voice. Sources report that a fracture after an ancient earthquake was associated with a dawn sound; heating/thermal expansion is a **modern physical hypothesis**, not a proven reconstruction of the original acoustic episode. [W17-P01–P02]
**City-building:** founding Antinoopolis and arranging Greek-style urban rights is a **legal and institutional productive act**. Foundation date and **133 CE** settlements stand on better chronological evidence than detailed imagined ceremonies or the identities of all builders. A Greek polis is not a chemical laboratory. [W17-E02–E04]
**Han technology:** `Hou Hanshu` relates an instrument which could potentially register tremors and their bearing. Historical testimony includes an anecdote in which a ball fell and a later message confirmed an earthquake in Longxi. No first-century instrument has survived; **internal sensing mechanism and precise calibration are unknown**. Modern functioning models demonstrate **possible reconstructions**, not identity with Zhang's actual 132 design. [W17-H01–H03]
**Alexandrian glass crafts:** an ostensibly vivid account of glassblowers, papyrus makers, textile work and **multicolored/changing cups** occurs in the letter supposedly from Hadrian to Servianus in the **`Historia Augusta`**, *Quadriga Tyrannorum* 8. This text is **a late antique fabrication**, not independent 130 CE workshop testimony. [W17-F01–F02]

## 03 — LANGUAGE, ALLEGORY AND ACCESS
**Stone sound → mythical voice:** Greek visitors called Amenhotep III's statue **'Memnon'**, the Trojan hero mourned by his mother Eos at dawn. The event of hearing a sound is **a claim of experience**; the poet gives it symbolic meaning as a divine salute to Hadrian. A first-century Egyptian royal monument thereby acquires new **Greek heroic and Roman imperial layers of interpretation**, without changing the stone's ancient origin. [W17-P01,W17-P02]
**Balbilla's Greek:** deliberately **archaizing Aeolic poetic diction**—evoking **Sappho**—is a marker of learned cultural authority and literary tradition, not a hidden recipe cipher. She invokes her own distinguished ancestry and praises imperial patrons. Her epigrams are public carved statements, **not** words preserved in a hidden initiatory manuscript.
**Antinoopolis:** the civic designation **'New Greeks'** (`Νέοι Ἕλληνες`, attested clearly in later documents) marks legally constructed group identity. **Early 133 CE enrollment/settlement papyri** connect personal/civic status to Hadrian's foundation, but the exact title usage in later papyri must not be backdated word for word without earliest witnesses. [W17-E03–E04]
**Zhang Heng's apparatus:** `候風地動儀` is the name of a described **earthquake-indicating instrument**; bronze dragon/toad ornament and cosmological vocabulary do not mean the device was an alchemical elixir. A hidden physical mechanism under an outer shell is different from an encrypted text.
**The forged Hadrian letter:** `Historia Augusta` gives Hadrian's voice to portray Alexandrian glassblowers, papyrus workers, linen weavers and color-changing cups. Its **pretended first-person 'I' is a pseudohistorical attribution** and cannot be converted into 130 CE testimony. The motive and actual late-antique author's sources remain research problems. [W17-F01–F02]

## 05 — PERCEPTION: whose reading counts?
**Ancient poet:** hears or reports a sound, names a divine hero, praises an emperor. **Ancient visitors:** leave hundreds of public inscriptions calling the stone 'Memnon' and interpreting sound as miraculous. **Modern physicist/historian:** can propose acoustic mechanisms, but not turn the poet's claim into a validated divine cause.
**Civic administrator:** can grant classification or residence privileges tied to Antinoopolis; Greek civic standing is a recognized status independent of inner physical change in the citizen.
**Technician:** Zhang Heng receives later historical credit for detecting quakes. **Modern reconstruction engineer:** can build one plausible device, but this does not mean the original was recovered.
**Late Roman pseudohistorian:** impersonates Hadrian in a supposed private letter; a credible named public speaker can be **created retrospectively** by a writer.

## 06 — STATION
**Attested:** Hadrian as imperial and city-founder; Sabina as empress; Balbilla as named author of surviving verses; new civic institutions of Antinoopolis; Zhang Heng as historical Han scholar with reported mechanical technology.
**Literary-only:** Hadrian allegedly writing to Servianus about color-changing cups; treat as LATE FORGERY / reception watch.
**Missing:** Greek-Egyptian professional Alchemist *independently identifiable by original dated 121–140 record*; a surviving 130 CE chemical manuscript; a dated teacher-student chain joining Maria, Ostanes, Democritus and imperial workers.

## 07 — LINEAGE AND DEPOSIT
- Amenhotep III original monument → Greek reidentification as Memnon → Balbilla's **130 CE** poem → later historians/readers: **physical object plus documented reinterpretation**, not a master/pupil chemistry lineage.
- Hadrian → Antinous memorial → founding Antinoopolis **130** → 133 papyri of early residents and status: **administrative and civic lineage**, with a new legal station and actual documentary trace.
- Zhang Heng **132** → fifth-century `Hou Hanshu` description → multiple modern functioning/contested mechanical reconstructions: **technical description and reengineering**, not physical recovery of original.
- Hadrian (historical ruler) → purported Hadrian letter **late `Historia Augusta`** → modern readers imagining 130 CE glasscraft: **FALSE RETROSPECTIVE DOCUMENTARY LINEAGE**.
- Maria the Jewess → Zosimos near **300 CE**: still a later textual testimony with uncertain historical lifetime; do not place in **121–140**.
- Egypt–Han alchemical teaching: no evidenced shared apparatus, translated manual or named contacts.

## 09 — HERE: what is established vs absent?
**Established:** Hadrianic imperial literary and civic authority can be placed at **actual material objects (130 CE poems and 133 CE papyri)**. A sound believed miraculous was transformed into an enduring public statement by a named poet; legal founding produced a new named community.
**Established with weaker temporal witness:** Han learned/mechanical credit for Zhang's seismoscope from later Chinese history.
**Excluded from contemporaneous evidence:** the alleged letter's descriptions of Egyptian glass and variegated cups, because its claimed Hadrianic authorship is **demonstrably spurious**.
**Not established:** miracle, elemental transmutation, professional Alchemist, 130 CE glass cup from the letter, Maria's 130 CE lifetime, a science-versus-pseudoscience verdict in second-century Roman society.

### Most profound historical realization
**A station can be made by a claim about matter, a public inscription that interprets matter, a political decree that creates a citizen, or a forged text that retroactively lends the name of an emperor to a trade. Only some of these are grounded in actual surviving contemporary testimony.**

This gives the Alchemist inquiry a powerful control: the apparent ancient authority of a text must be tested independently of its claimed speaker. The material permanence of a monument cannot establish the supernatural truth of what it says. A modern replica of a device cannot replace its original.
