# Window 20 — 181–200 CE: When the Practitioner Lives and the Repository Dies

**Status:** FULL TEN-LAYER SOURCE-CRITICAL PASS (2026-10-18). **Core event:** Rome's spring **192 CE** fire is documented by a named practitioner, **Galen**, in the treatise *Peri alypias* / *On Avoiding Distress*, composed **probably early 193 CE**, but preserved **only in a late medieval Greek manuscript rediscovered in 2005**. **Material counterpoint:** Commodus Hercules coin/medallion **191–192**, and Alexandrian Septimius Severus year-2 coins **193/194 CE**. Roman political crisis and later narrative eyewitnesses must not be recast as chemical or alchemical persecution.
**Interval:** 181–200 CE inclusive.
**Historical question:** When a practitioner retains learned capacity while manuscripts, instruments, drugs and trusted knowledge stores are destroyed, what truly survives of the station and what cannot be reconstructed even by its holder?

## 01 — THERE: documented conditions and sequence
- **180–192 CE:** Commodus governs after Marcus Aurelius. The Rome material market, learned medicine, temple storage, private books, professional instruments and imperial government coexist under a politically troubled reign. Galen continues medical and authorial activity; neither his role nor prior experience protects all his material belongings. [W20-G01]
- **c.189 CE:** Cassius Dio's later literary history reports a severe epidemic in Rome, with the claim that **2,000 died on many days**. He also recounts alleged killings using **poisoned needles**. These statements are **ancient testimony**, not a recovered mortality ledger, confirmed pathogen or verified organized poison conspiracy. Do not confuse with a documented class of secret alchemical poisoners. [W20-E01,W20-E02]
- **191–192 CE:** Commodus formally represents himself as **Hercules** on genuine Roman copper-alloy coins and medallions. The British Museum holds an **as R.15104** (dated 191–192; lion-skin bust and club) and a copper-alloy **medallion 1846,0910.247 dated specifically 192** with Hercules association. Museum documentation also describes medallions portraying his **symbolic refounding of Rome**. Physical material and political names are attested; the emperor's actual divinity is not. [W20-O01–O03]
- **Spring 192 CE:** a large fire destroys buildings along the **Sacred Way**, including the **Temple of Peace**, warehouses and **Palatine libraries**. Galen's entrusted holdings there are lost: personal/edited manuscripts, copies of rare books, drugs including compounds and precious antidotal ingredients, and tools of medical practice. **The fire was not an alchemy-directed book burning**, nor is a deliberate cause of the fire established by this testimony. [W20-G01–G05]
- **31 December 192 CE:** Commodus assassinated. **193 CE** becomes a disputed imperial succession year involving **Pertinax, Didius Julianus, Pescennius Niger, Clodius Albinus and Septimius Severus**. Some names are *claimants*, not consecutively accepted emperors in Rome: avoid false "five sequential rulers." Severus establishes control of Rome during **June 193**; conflict with Niger and Albinus continues into **194 and 197**. [W20-E03–E04]
- **Early 193 CE:** Galen likely composes *On Avoiding Distress* **after Commodus's death**, since its plain critical remarks about Commodus fit subsequent political circumstances. In an epistolary/ethical form Galen describes losses and argues that discipline and character allow him to avoid disabling grief. This is a **first-person source with self-presentational rhetoric**, not a neutral warehouse inventory or evidence all his work survived intact. [W20-G01–G04]
- **193/194 CE:** physically surviving **Alexandrian tetradrachm** catalogued **RPC V.3 type 65128**, Severus's **year 2** (`L Β`), shows his Greek imperial names and reverse **Athena**. It attests provincial minting and official presentation **after political transition**, not that every subject accepted the new ruler or a technical chemical process changed. [W20-O04]
- **c.200 CE:** no new independently dated original Greek-Egyptian text proving a professional Alchemist's appointment, named teacher–pupil school, direct foundry experiment or actual gold transmutation is verified by these sources. **Greek literary alchemical chronology and Maria the Jewess's lifetime remain disputed**.

## 02 — CAPACITY: instruments, substances, manuscripts and human practice
**Tools and drugs:** Galen reports **medical instruments**, prepared **simple and compound medicines**, antidotes/theriac and costly ingredients. These were *not merely ideas*: they were physically stored material requirements for practice. Loss made some work impossible or expensive to restore despite retaining medical understanding. [W20-G02–G04]
**Rare texts and exact editions:** Galen had copied and edited ancient authors from the Palatine libraries, seeking unusually careful text correction. His fire-era losses included **rare books unavailable elsewhere**, *his* versions of some works, and duplicates which were ironically kept in repositories that all burned. He could replace some common items via others but described other texts as **irrecoverable**. **Do not assume that every Galenic work or library in the empire burned**. [W20-G03–G05]
**Material symbol:** a 192 Roman emperor portrayed as Hercules on actual copper-alloy medallions/coins; technical mint competence is independent of the ruler's self-declared godly persona. [W20-O01–O03]
**Post-crisis minted authority:** **193/194 Alexandria** produces money bearing Severus's Greek name and year 2, a directly surviving demonstration that new recognition can be applied materially. It is *legal-political change*, not elemental conversion. [W20-O04]
**Disease:** Dio's plague and needle-poison stories attest ancient representation of public dread and harmful agents, **not** reconstructable evidence that widespread toxin attacks actually occurred. [W20-E01]

## 03 — LANGUAGE / ALLEGORY: what survives a name and what does not?
- **`Περὶ ἀλυπίας` (*Peri alypias*)** = *On Avoiding Distress*; a philosophical/ethical text, most likely written **early 193**. Its title describes emotional discipline after professional and private loss, not an initiatory alchemical formula.
- **`ἀλυπία` (*alypia*)**, freedom from grief/distress, is Galen's **self-proclaimed practiced philosophical capacity**. Source has rhetorical strategies including a friend's admiring report. **Cannot infer Galen was literally unaffected in every private moment**. [W20-G01–G04]
- **`theriakē` / theriac:** a known specialized multi-ingredient medicinal antidote, present among Galen's medicinal holdings. The loss of an actual batch is different from loss of its **written formula** and from loss of the **learned judgment to recreate it**. Avoid treating it as a proof of goldmaking.
- **Textual witness:** the *193-authored text* was known by title, quotation and later transmission, but the **surviving Greek text appears in the 15th-century codex Vlatadon 14**, recognized in **2005**. **Event time ≠ work's composition time ≠ surviving manuscript time ≠ rediscovery time**. [W20-G01,W20-G06]
- **Commodus as Hercules:** genuine 191–192 medallions bear lion-skin/club symbolism; the artistic representation claims mythic authority for a sovereign. **No material change makes Commodus literally Hercules**.
- **Severus's `L Β`:** Egyptian regnal year **2 = 193/194 CE** on Alexandria issue; a state time-marker. The coin's alloy is documented; no encoded secret chemical recipe.
- **Secret poisons in Dio:** charged narrative of needle poisoning is **hostile and dramatic literary reporting**; it must not be converted into a reproducible or socially authenticated technique attributed to alchemists.

## 04 — SECRECY, ACCESS, PROTECTION, VALUE, DANGER
| Possible cause | Historical instance | Evidence limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge stored in institutions** | Palatine libraries, Temple of Peace and Sacred Way warehouses hold texts/tools | The destruction is **accidental/material loss in testimony**, not a purge of Alchemists |
| **Trust in protective custody** | Galen says repositories were considered safe, then burned | Institutional prestige and perceived safety do not guarantee actual resilience |
| **Craft competence/tacit knowledge** | Galen personally knows remedies, anatomy and medical procedures | Can't work identically without instruments, drugs or unique original readings |
| **Economic value and scarcity** | Medicines, rare ingredients, precise editions, expensive objects | Valuation is real in Galen's rhetoric, not proof of chemically creating gold |
| **Danger** | City fire and large-scale disease, literary poison tales | Real physical risk ≠ evidence that alchemists encrypted formulas for safety |
| **Political/personal danger** | Commodus' autocracy, rapid succession 192–193 | Not an attested statute persecuting Alchemists |
| **Language lost by time** | Vlatadon 14 copied centuries later; once-lost Greek treatise recovered in 2005 | Delayed rediscovery ≠ deliberate secrecy in Galen's original work |
| **Attribution and self-presentation** | Galen narrates his serenity after losses; Commodus portrays himself as Hercules | Both authorial and political recognition must be compared with independent witnesses |

## 05 — PERCEPTION: who reports what?
- **Galen as first-person author:** authentic ancient physician concerned with losses, recovery and moral response. The narrative is shaped by the literary genre of practical ethics and his claimed capacity to resist grief. Not a neutral third-party log. [W20-G02–G04]
- **Friends, colleagues and dependents:** mentioned in Galen's narrated letter, but source does not preserve their independent testimony. Other physicians might later have lent Galen surviving copies; quantity of rescue/reconstruction remains partial.
- **Librarians/repositories:** Galen describes placing scarce items where many trusted them; library and storehouse collapse exposed a **common mode of failure** affecting his supposed safeguards.
- **Commodus and Roman audience:** copper-alloy medallion materially communicates a ruler's appropriation of Hercules, without needing the viewer to accept actual divine embodiment. [W20-O01]
- **Dio as historian:** has retrospective Rome-wide epidemic and poisonous-needle stories; treat as testimony of social narrative and potentially exaggerated severity, not epidemiological proof. [W20-E01]
- **Alexandrian coin users, 193/194:** encounter Severus as an officially dated, minted emperor; coin attests issued recognition, not uniform local loyalty or expertise.

## 06 — STATION: identity/ability/custody
**Named practitioner:** **Galen**, a physician and author with actual public demonstrations in prior Window 19, compound preparation and writing; his professional tools and book collection are vulnerable to institutional fire.
**Political station:** **Commodus** self-styles Hercules through material state media; **Pertinax / Julianus / Niger / Albinus / Severus** contest the imperial name in 193; a material Severan coin preserves a successful public issuing authority.
**Knowledge station:** librarians, copyists, editors, medical compounders and collectors can transmit knowledge despite not being original discoverers. A text may be irreplaceable due to **unique readings, annotations or lost exemplars** even if the general medical craft persists.
**Still not attested:** named **181–200 CE Alchemist**, a licensed Egyptian transmutation workshop, a period original Greek alchemical notebook, a targeted fire destroying one alchemical tradition, or goldmaking as successful chemical achievement.

## 07 — TRANSMISSION / RECEPTION / NON-LINEAGE
1. **Galen's original medical practice → owned instruments / compound medicines / curated copies → fire in 192:** strong direct testimony of material interruption. **The person retains some capacity, but its tools and exact records do not remain equally recoverable.**
2. **Galen's early 193 `On Avoiding Distress` → later readers / occasional medieval quotations → 15th-century Greek codex `Vlatadon 14` → 2005 identification → modern editions**: traceable literary **survival through manuscript copying**, not revival of the actual books lost in 192. [W20-G01,W20-G06]
3. **Commodus' 192 Hercules portraits → death 31 Dec → contested 193 authority → Septimius 193/194 Alexandria coins:** different successive names on durable state-issued material, not proof technical workers changed with each emperor.
4. **Possible Greek alchemical texts `Four Books`, `Isis to Horus`, and named Maria → later Zosimos:** source dates unresolved; cannot insert a named 192–193 victim or manuscript into the Roman fire simply because both involve ancient learning.
5. **Han China → Roman/Greek medical knowledge:** no contemporary recipe, teacher or translation contact proved.

## 09 — HERE: what this window shows, doesn't show, and leaves unknown
**Shows:** Galen is direct testimony that a practicing scholar can **lose manuscripts, devices, medicines and rare exemplars** all at once because several stores share one disastrous external vulnerability. His professional identity and some know-how survive, but some specific text and material capacities may be gone. Later copying can preserve **a text about that loss** far beyond the copies destroyed in the event. Roman coinage demonstrates how political identities are conveyed in material objects, while rival names make public authority contingent.
**Doesn't show:** every Galen book was burned, the original 192 fire destroyed Greek alchemy, that all rare recipes were actively secret, that Galen's mental discipline is a verified psychological measurement, that a poisoned-needle epidemic caused the deaths, or that imperial self-divinization changed the ruler's substance.
**Unknown:** identities of all lost books and their physical originals, how much Galen reconstructed, which tools/materiae were replaced, which works were copied outside Rome, actual cause of fire, impact on any contemporaneous Egyptian chemical workers, epidemiological precision for 189.

## 10 — THE OUTSIDE: trigger → station response → remainder
| Outside force | Actor or node affected | Response and evidence | What cannot yet be restored? |
|---|---|---|---|

| Public legitimation crisis | Commodus in 191–192 | Physical coins/medallions declare Hercules identity and symbolic refoundation | Whether any viewer accepted divine meaning |
| **Spring 192 city fire** | Galen, libraries, repositories | Books, unique texts, drugs, tools destroyed by shared disaster | Some rare works lost without surviving comparanda |
| **Political assassination** 31 Dec 192 | Imperial station | Commodus dies; multiple claimants 193 | Universal agreement over transition/loyalties |
| **Early 193 composition** | Galen as interpreter of own adversity | Writes *On Avoiding Distress* to explain equanimity and material losses | Subjective truth of his emotional claims |
| **193/194 mint legitimacy** | Severan administration in Egypt | Original tetradrachm shows emperor and year 2 | Full degree of social acceptance, mint-worker biographies |
| **Long-run manuscript selection** | Galenic reception, scribes and custodians | `Vlatadon 14` survives and is noticed in **2005** | Original 193 autograph and all burnt originals, neither recreated by find |

### Single most important emerging idea
**A station is not simply the expertise contained in a person's mind. It depends on material reproducibility, the continued availability of tools/ingredients and the custodial infrastructure that allows knowledge to be copied, corrected and used. Institutional protection can become the common point of failure.**

This is a **source-grounded model to test** on later alchemical recipe traditions. It is **NOT** evidence that the Alchemist's actual station was destroyed in 192, nor that such books were intentionally hidden from persecution.
