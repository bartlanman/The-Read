# 1781–1800 CE — Station under Outside Pressure

**Status:** TWO DATED TEXTUAL MILESTONES. 
**Historical range:** exactly 20 inclusive calendar years.  
**Program:** [The Outside research protocol](../10_OUTSIDE_PRESSURES/README.md) and [pressure audit](../00_METHOD_AND_EVIDENCE/OUTSIDE_PRESSURE_AUDIT.md).

## 01 — THERE: inherited station
Chemical arts, their differing professional roles, natural philosophy, public and private techniques, and past claims of transmutation. Do not automatically project today's opposition between chemistry and pseudoscience backward into this interval.

## 02 — Material technique and apparatus
Distinguish what actual operations are recorded from hypothetical or retrospectively attributed transformations.

## 03 — Language and allegory
Look for exact terms in the period: alchemy, chymistry, chemistry, transmutation, elements, later pseudoscience. Which actor used each label and why? Compare natural-language formulas with methods of empirical description.

## 04 — Secrecy and access
Did experiments depend on privately held craft skills, institutional resources, open publication, legally restricted material, laboratory credentials or contested trustworthiness? Danger is not automatically secrecy.

## 05 — Perception
Identify whether contemporaries took the relevant activity as serious experimental inquiry, legitimate craft, private speculation, fraud, esotericism, or entertainment—and who judged.

## 06 — Station
Track named offices, schools, paid work, societies, professional qualifications and the right to speak publicly as a competent interpreter of material change.

## 07 — Lineage and reclassification
A retained material technique or adapted apparatus can pass into chemistry without a teacher-to-student *alchemist* lineage. Conversely, an artistic or esoteric tradition may inherit symbols without claiming the same laboratory basis.

## 08 — Texts and anchors
- 1787: new chemical nomenclature authored by Lavoisier and colleagues.
- 1789: Lavoisier's Traité élémentaire de chimie articulates a public, systematic account of substances and transformations.

**References:** [OP06]
Source key: [Outside pressure register](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/OUTSIDE_PRESSURE__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

## 09 — HERE and remainder
**What can be inferred:** The external pressure above potentially changes how material expertise is recognized or organized. Its magnitude, causal effect and actor's response require contemporary text evidence beyond date placement alone.

**What cannot be inferred:** That scientific chemistry began on a single date, that *all* prior work was pseudoscience, that historical chemical transmutation succeeded, or that every symbolic work was an encrypted recipe.

## 10 — OUTSIDE PRESSURE → STATION RESPONSE
**Pressure:** Reformulation of vocabulary and evidence in publicly teachable chemical theory.

**Affected claim, practice or role:** subject of investigation; distinguish method, reputation and profession.  
**Who applies pressure:** verify actual institutions, publishers, publics, participants, theorists or regulators.  
**Evidence for acceptance/resistance:** Selected scholarly sources [OP06]  
**Knowledge continued vs rejected:** to be verified per text or experiment.  
**New social station or relabeling:** to be demonstrated, not assumed.  
**REMAINDER:** establish dates of texts, original language, causality and counterevidence.
