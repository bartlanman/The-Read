# Window 26 — 301–320 CE: A Public Law for Goldworkers, a Persecution of Scriptures, and Zosimos's Unfixed Date

**Status:** TEN-LAYER SOURCE-CRITICAL DEEP PASS (2026-10-18). **Conclusion:** **301** has a preserved **original imperial economic inscription** specifying and valuing skilled craft (including goldworking in the reconstructed edict); **303** has near-contemporary literary testimony to state destruction of **Christian** scriptures/churches; **311** and **313** have transmitted legal reversals toward Christian assemblies/property. **None explicitly names a practitioner of Greek alchemy as its target, beneficiary or state-recognized occupation.** Zosimos of Panopolis remains a substantive named **alchemical commentator c.300**, with **no secure date of activity within 301–320**. **No independently dated, professionally licensed 301–320 Alchemist established.**

**Geography and scope:** 301 inscription at **Aphrodisias in Caria**, imperial law affecting markets broadly; **Nicomedia**, Caesarea/Palestine, Roman imperial ecclesiastical policy; Greek Egyptian alchemical commentary as **cross-window** context. Chinese and other traditions remain independent absent witnessed transmission. ## 01 — THERE: original conditions and dated encounters (301–320)

- **AD 301**: Diocletian and co-emperors issue **Edict of Maximum Prices**. A **surviving original-period Latin inscription** on the **Civil Basilica at Aphrodisias** contains a substantial local copy. The **combined text** lists c.**1,400 maximum prices/wages** for goods and labor; the surviving Aphrodisias portion preserves c.**40%**, and more than **40 fragmentary copies** survive in the eastern empire, including some Greek price-list translations. Distinguish the edict's *original public promulgation* from a **2023 editor's integrated reconstruction**. [W26-O01]
- **Around 301**: same site bears a distinct **Currency Dossier**, concerning imperial monetary change, not an alchemical handbook. Some modern monetary reconstructions place denomination-revaluation on **1 September 301**; particular dates/formulas require reading the reconstructed inscription. [W26-O02]
- **23 February 303**, according to near-contemporary **Lactantius, *De mortibus persecutorum* 12**: officials enter the **Christian church at Nicomedia**, burn Christian scriptures and level the building. **24 February** is Lactantius's following day for posting the first restrictive edict (**chapter 13**). His own descriptions are Christian near-contemporary narratives surviving only in later copies, not stone copies of the order. [W26-T01]
- **Early persecution 303**: **Eusebius *Ecclesiastical History* VIII.2** reports demolition of churches, public burning of **sacred books**, and expulsion/loss of standing for specified Christians, with increased attention to church leaders. His month **Dystrus** should not be made identical to Lactantius's February Nicomedia chronology without calendar/source control. Eusebius, too, wrote/revised in the early fourth century and is preserved in later manuscripts. [W26-T02]
- **7 June 303**, in Eusebius's *Martyrs of Palestine*, **Procopius** is depicted being brought before officials at Caesarea and killed after refusing expected sacrificial speech/acts. A named **Christian**, not a named metalworker or Alchemist, and the quoted courtroom dialogue is not extant independently as official minutes. [W26-T03]
- **305**: Diocletian and Maximian abdicate. Roman administration continues under successive emperors and regional persecution policies; the date does not create an alchemist credential.
- **30 April 311**: **Galerius's toleration order**, transmitted by **Lactantius DMP 34**, posting date recorded **in chapter 35**, with a Greek version in **Eusebius HE VIII.17**. Christians obtain permission to reconstitute worship assemblies subject to maintaining public order, asked to pray for state well-being. **No original inscribed 311 copy is inspected.** [W26-T04]
- **Early 313 and 13 June 313**: Constantine and Licinius meet at Milan; **Licinius's letter/circular posted at Nicomedia 13 June 313** is transmitted in **Lactantius DMP 48** and **Eusebius HE X.5**, proclaiming wide worship choice and restoration of Christian property. The expression **“Edict of Milan”** is customary shorthand; the transmitted document is **not an independent original Milan stone decree**. [W26-T05]
- **301–320 exact alchemical horizon:** the physical Leiden X/Stockholm recipes are broadly dated late third/early fourth century; Zosimos is placed **c.300** by Dufault and others, but neither a dated 308 autograph nor a legally appointed Alchemist in 311 has been identified. **No new event should be manufactured by overlapping a palaeographic century range with 301–320.** [W26-S01–S03]

## 02 — MATERIAL TECHNIQUE: gold and other crafts as regulated work, not metal creation

**A real state schedule of work and value:** the 301 price edict's reconstructed gold category/sections and occupational tariff framework recognize **gold**, gold leaf preparation and goldworking labor, alongside metals, dyed materials, textiles and other trades. This demonstrates **economic recognizability and potentially remunerated competence**, not that Aphrodisias had *every* workshop listed or that a named alchemist created gold. Before assigning a specific numeral or wage to an Aphrodisian worker one must collate the original inscription with Crawford's **2023** edition and variant fragments. [W26-O01]

**Coinage:** the associated Currency Dossier concerns stated government monetary value, distinct from changes in atomic substance. A value or denomination doubled by law does not mean the substance doubled. Roman coins made from gold, silver or alloy reflect minting and existing material craft, not a documented procedure to turn one element into another. [W26-O02]

**Written recipe techniques:** Leiden and Stockholm broadly ancient papyri include methods for gilding/metal coloring, imitative precious stone finishes, dyeing and other craft; exact period of copying in **301–320** is **NOT SECURE**, and recipes may describe an *appearance* rather than the element's conversion. Zosimos's treatises as later preserved discuss apparatus/furnaces and alternative interpretations; **no independent original early-fourth-century specimen tied to him proves chemical conversion**. [W26-S01–S03]

**Book material:** Lactantius describes burned **scriptures** at Nicomedia, Eusebius also describes Christian books burned. They do **not** identify a recipe with named alchemical ingredients or prove the contents of any allegedly destroyed goldmaking manual. The *Suda* gold/silver-book story belongs to John of Antioch (seventh century) and is not a near-contemporary 303 physical object. [W26-T01–T02; W25-T01]

## 03 — LANGUAGE, ALLEGORY, SOURCE VOICE AND ANACHRONISM

The **AD 301 tariff** publicly classifies trades, wages, prices and desirable physical goods. **`aurum`** refers to gold as economic material, and the fact that workers can be classified is not the appearance of `chēmeutēs` as an official **Alchemist** title. [W26-O01]

**Lactantius DMP 12**, Latin `scripturae repertae incenduntur`: his narrative says **scriptures were found and burned** at a Christian church. The noun and local context forbid substituting **alchemical books**. **DMP 13** describes loss of public dignity/legal standing for Christians. **Eusebius HE VIII.2** gives a separately transmitted Christian account with different calendrical cues; both authors had interested religious purposes. [W26-T01–T02]

**313 legal label:** “Edict of Milan” is retrospective convention; **Licinius's Nicomedian letter** is transmitted, not an archaeological original decree. **Galerius's 311 permission** is distinct, not one document with the 313 letter. [W26-T04–T05]

**`chēmeia`** in the alleged Diocletian gold-book-burning passage is transmitted through John of Antioch c.610–626 and *Suda* (tenth century), not securely on a 303 Roman state inscription. **`magoi`** is Zosimos's rhetorical category for opponents in later-transmitted texts, not a contemporary register of whose workshops were burned. His symbolism, furnaces and visions operate on distinct levels: literary allegory, technique, criticism, and attributed mystical experience. [W26-S01–S02]

**Important actual legal contrast:** an imperial state publicly regulates **goldworker wages** (a specialized economic station), while another policy in 303 threatens **the Christian institution and its scriptures**. We cannot combine the two as a persecution of an alchemical guild.

## 05 — PERCEPTION: PUBLIC AUTHORITY / PROFESSIONAL ABILITY / TEXTUAL VOICE

**Price-edict issuer:** the state perceived craft goods and services as economically measurable, legitimate items for tariff ceilings. The inscription does not certify a worker actually created elemental gold.

**Christian witnesses:** Lactantius and Eusebius perceived attack on religious books and worship structures as an attack on Christian identity and institutional power. The narrative voice is near-period and potentially informed, but not official minutes or a chemical bibliography.

**Government reversals:** 311/313 rulers viewed toleration and restitution as useful for public order/peace. This evidences re-recognition of Christians as an institution, not a newly granted alchemist's status.

**Alchemical commentary:** Zosimos criticized rival practices and interpreted inherited recipes in correspondence attributed to him, including an addressee Theosebeia. If he was active around 300, this may overlap 301–320; **a date span alone does not show he wrote a specific treatise under the 303 persecution**, survived a purge or received an official appointment. [W26-S01–S02]

## 06 — THE STATION: WHAT IS ACTUALLY RECOGNIZED?

1. **Goldworkers/dyers/other artisans:** publicly legible **trade** and possible labor rates in a real 301 inscription; capacity is distinct from issuer's approval of any philosopher's view of transformation. **Not titled Alchemists** by this source.
2. **Christian church, clergy, readers and proprietors:** recognizable social/religious station subject to attacks on buildings/books and changing legal status. Actual **Outside pressure → reaction/restoration** is documented here.
3. **Zosimos of Panopolis:** a genuinely nameable alchemical **commentary author** broadly **c.300**, describing patronage, education and competition with priestly colleagues; not a documented 307 wage worker, temple officeholder licensed for alchemy, or a successful gold transmuter.
4. **Theosebeia/Neilos:** relations are transmitted through Zosimos's interested textual witness. No **301–320** independent city register or contract has been established.
5. **Leiden/Stockholm scribe(s):** original ancient recipe papyri physically show technical writing but exact copying year and authorship unknown. No formal Alchemist title.

**Window station verdict:** **NO SPECIFIC ORIGINAL 301–320 ALCHYMIST PROFESSION IDENTIFIED**. The **named alchemical commentator** is a positive cross-window scholarly status, retained from ST-085 without fabricating a second independently dated event.

## 07 — LINEAGE AND WITNESS CHAIN: NO FALSE IDENTITIES

- **301 imperial edict** → **original Latin inscribed at Aphrodisias Civil Basilica** (partial surviving stones, modern integrated edition Crawford 2023) → modern economic scholarship. This is a **material source contemporary with its intended operation**, not a later narrator's claim.
- **303 Nicomedia actions** → **Lactantius's near-contemporary literary account** (DMP 12–13) and **Eusebius's similar but not identical report** (HE VIII.2) → later Christian manuscripts and modern editions. These are **literary report chains**, **not original destruction orders** in autograph.
- **311 Galerius law** → **Lactantius DMP 34/35 Latin** + **Eusebius HE VIII.17 Greek** → much later physical copies; date **30 Apr 311** comes from Lactantius's posting notice.
- **313 religious settlement** → **meeting in Milan** → **Licinius letter posted 13 Jun at Nicomedia**, transmitted in **Lactantius DMP 48** and **Eusebius HE X.5**; distinguish policy history, document posting and manuscript copy.
- **Zosimos c.300** → later Byzantine compendia **Marcianus gr.299 c.1000; Parisinus gr.2325 (13th c), Parisinus gr.2327 (15th c)** and other translations → modern source criticism; no original signed 309 document or proven personal instruction in 303.
- **Greek versus Chinese lineages:** no original 301–320 teacher/citation translation chain established.

## 09 — HERE: warranted changes, failures, remainder

**WARRANTED:** **real public craft/economic recognition** in an original stone law of 301; **near-contemporary and mutually reinforcing reports of explicit destruction of Christian sacred texts** in 303; transmitted **legal shift to toleration** in 311 and 313; broad near-300 formation of named **alchemical scholarship** in Zosimos's preserved corpus.

**NOT SHOWN:** any 303 original law targeting alchemical manuals, goldmaking as success of elemental conversion, 301 tariff converting goldworkers into an alchemical profession, Zosimos convicted/tortured for alchemy or Christianity, precise 306 alchemical school around Theosebeia, 311/313 alchemical legalization, proof that an ancient manuscript was purposely encrypted to evade Diocletian.

**UNKNOWN:** exact transcription/price for every damaged goldworking line in local Aphrodisias stone vs composite 2023 edition, relationship of other city copies to this text; whether alchemical writers personally experienced coercion; Theosebeia's actual independently attested status and date; Zosimos's exact lifetime and manuscript genealogy; how skill was credentialed within separate workshops.

**The most profound result:** **The same emperor's regime could publicly regulate trades using precious metals and subsequently suppress texts of an entirely different institution.** Physical stone attests the former; closely situated religious literary testimonies describe the latter. **An authentic repression of books is NOT historical permission to move the repression onto whatever body of specialized knowledge modern readers find mysterious.** Meanwhile a named alchemical commentator's authority emerges near this date without a secure proof of state recognition.
