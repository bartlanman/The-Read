# Window 23 — 241–260 CE: Certified Ritual, Imperial Metal, and the Missing Alchemist

**Status:** TEN-LAYER SOURCE-CRITICAL DEEP PASS (2026-10-18); **NO SECURE NAMED ALCHEMIST IDENTIFIED IN 241–260 CE**. The three most informative evidence classes are an original official metal object (248/249), independently preserved original ritual certificates (June 250), and near-contemporary Christian testimony about coercion (257–258) preserved by later authors. **Station finding:** practical metal production, authorized ritual certification, literary theology, and alchemical textual authority remain distinguishable; their convergence into an authenticated Alchemist profession is NOT shown.

**Geography:** principally Roman Egypt (Alexandria, Oxyrhynchus, Theadelphia) with comparative Carthage and Roman imperial center. **Date rule:** years inclusive; broad late-third-century / fourth-century witnesses are comparative horizons, not automatically products of 241–260. **Continuation:** [Window 22](CE_0221-0240_CE__WINDOW.md) · [source register](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0241-0260__SOURCE_REGISTER.md) · [language/access dossier](../09_LANGUAGE_TECHNIQUE_AND_ALLEGORY/CE_0241-0260__LANGUAGE_TECHNIQUE_AND_ACCESS.md).

## Evidentiary scale and central result

**ATTESTED:** original surviving documents and coins whose issue/date is established, including material objects, not a demonstration of supernatural efficacy. **RETROSPECTIVE:** a later writer's narrative of an earlier episode. **SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION:** an explicit inference distinguishable from source wording. **CONTESTED:** disputed meaning, original circumstances or dating. **NO SECURE EVIDENCE:** not shown by the witnesses examined, not proof of nonexistence.

**Central result:** In 250 a person could submit a *libellus* stating that he and his children had publicly sacrificed and tasted offerings, and two officials could attest that they witnessed the sacrifice. We can identify the people, performance claim, named official witnesses, and calendar date, but **not** convert this evidence for state-certified religious conformity into an alchemical credential. Meanwhile a genuine minted metallic object displays issuer, calendar and debased-silver composition but does **not** display its artisans, a recipe, or conversion of elements. [W23-P01–P03]

## 01 — THERE: conditions and actual events

- **241–249:** Greek-writing Roman Egypt sustains documentation, markets, crafts, coinage and religious institutions during third-century imperial instability. Continuity of these *systems* is demonstrable through coin issue and dated papyri; a named secret alchemical association is not. Earlier **Africanus' Kestoi**, dedicated to Severus Alexander (222–235), remains a textual predecessor. **P.Oxy. III 412** has a broad original copy date 225–275/276 and a will on verso dated 276: it *might* have been copied in this window, but the copy **cannot** be assigned precisely here. [W23-S01; W22-P01]
- **248/249:** Alexandrian mint issues **Philip I year 6**, **RPC VIII 3329**, debased-silver tetradrachm, Greek imperial legend and regnal-year mark LϚ; eagle holding wreath on reverse. **ATTESTED object**, not alchemical text. [W23-O01]
- **249:** Decius replaces Philip I. Late imperial political instability has consequences for the names on officially issued objects, but no 249 anti-alchemy legislation has been established. [W23-S02]
- **14 June 250:** Oxyrhynchus **P.Oxy. IV 658** = **Yale P.CtYBR inv. 65**, one surviving original Greek sacrifice certificate. The adult applicant's name is partly broken; his children are identified as Aurelius Dioskoros and Aurelia Lais. He reports sacrificial acts before administrators and requests their endorsement. The document is *not* an alchemy license, commercial recipe, or trial record of a goldmaker. [W23-P01]
- **17 June 250:** Theadelphia **P.Mich. III 157**, an independently preserved document, states that **Aurelius Sakis**, with children Aion and Heras, offered sacrifice, poured libations and tasted sacrifices; **Aurelius Serenos and Aurelius Hermas** endorse as witnesses. This is not the same papyrus as P.Oxy. IV 658. [W23-P02]
- **250–251:** persecution of Christians and the requirement for public sacrifice are supported by dated original Egyptian certificates and Christian literary witnesses. The full original empire-wide edict itself does **not** survive; neither its exact publication date nor every locality's administrative implementation can be inferred from two Egyptian records. [W23-P01–P02; W23-S03]
- **c.250:** Eusebius, writing later (early fourth century), reports Origen's confinement and abuse during the Decian persecution; this is testimony regarding a named **Christian intellectual**, not a record of a persecuted alchemical technician. [W23-T01]
- **257–258:** Valerian-era measures restrict Christian clergy. **Cyprian, Ep. 80.1–2** reports a rescript against bishops, presbyters and deacons and sanctions on Christian rank/property; a near-contemporary letter in a later manuscript tradition, **not an extant original imperial rescript**. [W23-T02]
- **c.257–258:** **Dionysius of Alexandria**, in a letter *against Germanus* quoted in Eusebius **HE 7.11.3–11**, describes being examined by the prefect Aemilianus, refusing to abandon Christian worship and being sent to Cephro. His **actual station is bishop**, not Alchemist. His reported dialogue is preserved through Eusebius's later selection. [W23-T03]
- **260:** Valerian captured by Sasanian forces; Gallienus alone governs the Roman empire. Eusebius **HE 7.13** preserves a Greek version of a rescript attributed to Gallienus restoring bishops' use of worship sites. Its precise promulgation timing, authenticity in wording and material effects must be separately assessed; the passage has no reference to an alchemical craft. [W23-T04]
- **Across the window:** the date of *composition* of Greek texts attributed to Maria the Jewess, the exact dates of Zosimos's life and the physical copies of Leiden X and Stockholm are **not** safely fixed at 241–260. [W23-S01, W23-S04]

## 02 — MATERIAL TECHNIQUE: what can actually be observed?

**Minted metal:** RPC VIII 3329 is a debased-silver tetradrachm, recorded diameter ca. 24 mm and average 12.66 g across cataloged specimens. An alloy is an actual material outcome. Whether workers used specific smelting, assaying, refining or deliberate monetary adjustment is **not established by this coin type alone**. It would be a category error to infer chrysopoeia from a precious-metal appearance, or to treat alloy/debasement as elemental conversion. [W23-O01]

**Ritual material actions:** both June 250 certificates enumerate sacrificing, libations and consuming offerings; these are procedures upon physical materials under officials' eyes, but **neither metalworking nor pharmaceutical nor alchemical recipes**. P.Mich. explicitly contains officials' witness formula, whereas P.Oxy. should not be credited with named endorsers absent reading of its particular breaks. [W23-P01–P02]

**Inheritable craft horizon:** metals coloring/alloying; preparing lookalike gold or silver; coloring stones or glass; and textile dyeing occur in the later transmitted Greco-Egyptian recipe tradition. Martelli examines its relationship to workshops and vessels; the relevant early recipe texts, however, are *not physically datable to this exact period*, so their techniques are **cross-period parallels** rather than excavated 250 CE operations. [W23-S01, W23-S04]

**What is NOT measured:** recovered 250 CE alchemist-associated crucible, named alchemist's own workshop, eyewitness trial resulting in gold, a 250 metallurgical process record tied to a particular titled practitioner, or elemental analysis proving transmutation. **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** in the examined witnesses.

## 03 — LANGUAGE, ALLEGORY AND ATTRIBUTION

- **Visible, nonsecret certification:** **τοῖς ἐπὶ τῶν θυσιῶν ᾑρημένοις** (to those selected over sacrifices) in P.Mich. III 157 names recognized officials, not an initiatory society. Its verbs **ἐθύσαμεν** (we sacrificed), **ἐσπείσαμεν** (we poured libations), **ἐγευσάμεθα** (we tasted) are precise public-performance claims. **εἴδαμεν ὑμᾶς θυσιάζοντας** marks observers' attestation. [W23-P02]
- **Regnal and civic signs:** **LϚ** (= regnal year six) and the Greek emperor legend on RPC VIII 3329 communicate public official standing. They do not function as an ingredient code. [W23-O01]
- **The inherited lost key:** Africanus's geometrical/music ingredient substitutions come from *other transmitted fragments*, not the Oxyrhynchus Book 18 papyrus. The missing appendix creates a transmission difficulty; Dufault argues it was probably a learned game rather than effective concealment. Nothing demonstrates new use of this code in **241–260**. [W23-S01; Window 22]
- **Magi as hostile labeling:** Dionysius' polemical material transmitted in **Eusebius HE 7.10.4–6** blames an Egyptian circle of “Magi,” connected with the figure Macrianus, for Valerian's turn against Christians. The theological allegations of sacrifice/sorcery **are accusations in a partisan narrative**; **not** independent evidence identifying a 250s alchemical practitioner, a metallurgical rite or actual causal control over imperial policy. [W23-T05]
- **Later terms:** Dufault cautions that **chēmeia / chēmeutēs** as identifiable categorial vocabulary is principally visible in substantially later authors; do not ascribe “licensed alchemist” to a 250 document whose own words do not name one. [W23-S01]

## 04 — SECRECY AND ACCESS: seven separately rated possibilities

| Mechanism | This window's witness | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional coercion and permitted ritual | June 250 *libelli* addressed to sacrificial commissioners, with named testimonial formula | **ATTESTED** for ritual compliance, not alchemy |
| Fear of punishment / self-protection | Cyprian reports sanctions; Dionysius reports compelled confession, exile | **ATTESTED/RETROSPECTIVE** for Christian station; **NO SECURE ALCHEMY LINK** |
| Commercial craft monopoly / protection of secret formula | No securely dated workshop contract or artisan oath tied to an alchemist here | **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** |
| Competence and trained access | Actual craft requires skill; later alchemical vessels imply operation | **SOURCE-SUPPORTED COMPARISON**, no named trained 250 technician |
| Hazard-based restrictions | Possible hazards of ancient materials in general | **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** secrecy motivated by hazard in this window |
| Prestige, polemic, naming | Egyptian “Magi” charged by Dionysius/Eusebius; patrons and critics police labels | **RETROSPECTIVE POLEMICAL WITNESS**, not proven actual guild |
| Loss of textual key and manuscript gap | Africanus appendix missing; Zosimos and recipe manuscript chronology later | **ATTESTED TRANSMISSION PROBLEM**, not proven 250 intentional concealment |

For religious certificates: who can claim performance (named inhabitants), who judges it (appointed commissioners), and what legitimates the documentary claim (endorsement) are observable. There is **no evidence** these commissioners judged chemistry, instructed metalworkers, or issued alchemical occupational credentials.

## 05 — PERCEPTION: who saw what?

**Mint users:** imperial images, regnal dating and debased-silver currency signaled monetary and political authority; the type does not record a goldmaking claim or user belief in alchemy. **Sacrificial commissioners:** witnessed or certified publicly described ritual; a certificate records an administrative assessment, not the person's private religious convictions. **Christian bishop:** Dionysius viewed coercion as persecution and reports theological conflict, filtered through Eusebius. **Christian polemicist:** labeling hostile “Magi” can delegitimize rivals without verifying their skills, equipment or professional identity. **Later alchemical readers:** invoked revered names and reinterpreted material recipes; their retrospective authority cannot be transferred to a precisely 250 CE individual.

**Verdict: NO SECURE ALCHEMIST IDENTIFIED IN THIS WINDOW.** A positive contrast case is warranted: a 250 state can visibly impose an evidentiary requirement on a ritual role, while there remains no corresponding attested alchemist's state license.

## 07 — LINEAGE AND WITNESS CHAINS

1. **Africanus Kestoi** dedicated **222–235** → a *Book 18* physical witness broadly **225–275/276** → **276** reverse-side will; this is a continuity *horizon*, NOT proof it was copied between 241 and 260.
2. **P.Oxy IV 658 / Yale P.CtYBR 65** and **P.Mich III 157** are **two distinct original papyrus objects** independently evidencing the common June 250 bureaucratic form, not versions of the same document.
3. **Dionysius' letters / material about Valerian**, written in the relevant aftermath → **Eusebius HE 7.10–13** (early fourth century) → modern editions/translations. This is a **quotation chain**, not a new contemporary papyrus.
4. **Cyprian Ep. 80**, near-contemporary Latin letter (258) → manuscript transmission and 1996 Diercks critical edition; **not identical to the original senatorial rescript**.
5. **Maria** in Zosimos's commentary citations → surviving much later Greek compilation manuscripts (e.g. Marcianus gr. 299 c.1000, others later); these prove reception *not* a named teacher–student meeting in 250.
6. **China:** no new securely identified Chinese–Greek transmission in these years; parallels remain independent.

## 09 — HERE AND REMAINDER: change without fabricated founding

**HERE—demonstrated:** political authority minted material objects with clear dates; religious obligations became recordable and certifiable through appointed eyewitnesses; near-contemporary clergy protested imperial sanctions and reported concrete changed access. **SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION:** an emerging Alchemist's eventual station cannot be established by the existence of recipes alone; we must find evidence of *who could attest an operation and who recognized the practitioner*. These institutions illustrate what an evidence trail for authorization looks like, **without being its alchemical origin**.

**DOES NOT SHOW:** exact ancient metallurgical recipe used for RPC coin; physical gold transmutation; imperial alchemist persecution in 250 or 258; secret alchemical guild behind “Magi”; Maria alive in mid-century; Zosimos teaching by 260; a Diocletian order to burn alchemical books in this earlier window; physical Leiden/Stockholm papyrus produced in 250.

**UNKNOWN:** whether material-specialist networks in Egyptian cities had protected occupational pathways now lost; which recipes in later papyri predate the physical copies and by how much; relationship, if any, of Zosimos to a historical Maria; how widely ritual certification occurred outside surviving Egyptian documents; content of alleged original Decian edict; degree of exact fidelity in Eusebius's quotations.

## 10 — THE OUTSIDE: dated pressure, tested encounter, evidenced response

| External force | Attested encounter and date | Response evidenced | Alchemical limit / survival |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imperial monetary legitimacy | Alexandrian year 6 coin **248/249**, RPC VIII 3329 | Mint issues official debased-silver design with emperor's titles | Coincraft and money survive, but no named Alchemist or method |
| Decian religious requirement | Oxyrhynchus **14 Jun 250**, Theadelphia **17 Jun 250** | Families petition, perform/report sacrifice; officials validate | **Direct institutional pressure**, but upon ritual conformity—not alchemical operation |
| Valerian anti-Christian policy | Cyprian **Ep.80 (258)**; Dionysius recalled in HE 7.11 | Sanctions, reported examination, bishop sent away | **Real pressure on bishop**, not proven metal-tech specialist |
| Polemical struggle over intellectual legitimacy | Dionysius via Eusebius **HE 7.10** depicts Egyptian “Magi” negatively | Accusation constructs suspect rival authority | Writer's hostility is evidence of **perception**, not expertise or imperial causal mechanism |
| Post-Valerian political reversal | Gallienus' restituent rescript via HE 7.13 (date at **260/after**, not pinned to month) | Public permission for bishops reportedly restored | Religious station changes; alchemical station response **UNVERIFIED** |
| Workshop transmission into commentary | Later alchemical recipe texts / Zosimos | Later scholars read and comment on recipes | **Cross-window horizon** only; no dated 250 response established |

**Counterexample that limits a persecution thesis:** two **publicly dated original ritual certificates** exist. They show administrative transparency for compulsory *sacrifice*, not state suppression of an alchemical recipe, and the most vivid reference to “Magi” is a hostile **later-transmitted** theological explanation. We cannot make evidence for one institution stand in for another.

## The most profound finding

**The public ability to certify a performance can be better documented than the specialized ability to perform a transformation.** A 250 Egyptian certificate tells us an applicant, ritual action, state certifier, place and date. The alchemical source horizon tells us techniques and famous attributed authors, often without coeval documentation of who practiced or authorized them. The difference is not proof that Alchemists were absent: it defines **the archival threshold the later investigation must meet to call an Alchemist a socially authenticated station**.
