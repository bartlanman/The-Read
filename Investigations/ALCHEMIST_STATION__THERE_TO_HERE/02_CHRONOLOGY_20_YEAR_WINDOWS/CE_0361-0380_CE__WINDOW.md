# Window 29 — 361–380 CE: Themistius Makes Metal Transformation Publicly Intelligible Without Producing an Alchemist

**Status:** TEN-LAYER SOURCE-CRITICAL DEEP INVESTIGATION (2026-10-18), after replacing existing FIRST-PASS SOURCED LEADS scaffold. **Most important positive result:** a **named fourth-century public orator Themistius** invokes a hypothetical **bronze→silver→gold** technology in a speech about Emperor Valens's mercy toward political enemies, usually dated **367** (possible **366–367**). **This does not demonstrate a successful procedure, a professional occupation or state authorization to practice alchemy.** No securely identified **361–380 CE Alchemist** with dated documented laboratory success, recognized office or actual persecution is established.

## 01 — THERE: original conditions, dates and outside forces

- **17 June 362 — Julian and teachers:** **CTh XIII.3.5**, via later Theodosian Code (438), says **teachers and instructors** need local council approval and imperial review. His **separate explanatory Greek letter** requiring congruity between a classical instructor's faith and the poets they interpret belongs to a **different literary witness**, not the precise wording of the Latin statute. This is an example of political/religious gatekeeping of a **teaching station**, not ancient official licensing for goldmaking. [W29-T01]
- **361–363 — Julian:** his religious and institutional policies altered who might be eligible to explain authoritative Greek literature, but there is **NO dated Alchemist exposed, hired or dismissed** under the teacher law. Julian dies **363**, not part of the 367 speech.
- **366 — Procopius's rebellion against Valens defeated.** Themistius's later public praise takes this event as political context. Do not label 366 rebellion evidence of any metallic laboratory.
- **366/367 (commonly 367) — Themistius's *Oration 7*:** in a courtly speech about Valens's merciful treatment of defeated political opponents, he compares changing enemies to friends to legendary transformations by **Circe, Medea, Xerxes and Autolycus**, and imagines a process to transform **bronze into silver, and silver into gold**. **This is counterfactual rhetoric**, not testimony that an actual 367 practitioner made gold. Dufault 2019, pp.100–102 cites **Or.7 145.12–146.11**. [W29-T02]
- **9 September 364 — CTh IX.16.7:** forbids harmful nocturnal magical/sacrificial acts in a transmitted late Roman law. Divinatory/sacrificial regulation does not cover an identified chemical workshop by implication.
- **12 December 365? / 370? / 373? — CTh IX.16.8:** treats astrological `mathematicorum tractatus` as forbidden, with **CONTESTED transmitted date/correction**. No automatic causal story of alchemists escaping a single exact edict.
- **29 May 371 — CTh IX.16.9:** explicitly distinguishes `haruspicina` from `maleficium`: the emperor says ordinary entrail-divination is not inherently harmful magic, while harmful exercise remains forbidden. **This legal permission is qualified and not equivalent to a protected chemical profession**. [W29-T03]
- **6 December 371 — CTh IX.16.10:** addresses procedure for senatorial accused of harmful magical acts, not a named Alchemist's conviction. [W29-T03]
- **c.371–372, with alternatives including 374 — Antioch succession-divination trials**, recounted by **Ammianus Marcellinus, *Res Gestae* XXIX.1–2** (composed later in fourth century). Figures **Theodorus, Hilarius and Patricius** are associated with alleged oracular inquiries about Valens's successor using a suspended ring over alphabet letters; severe interrogations and punishments follow in the narrative. **Date debated; original trial minutes/device not available; not a transmutation case.** [W29-T04]
- **c.374? — CJ IV.63.2**, preserved in later sixth-century Code, restricts gold transactions/supply outside imperial territory. **Original precise 374 date CONTESTED/EDITORIAL**. Policy expresses geopolitical/economic value of actual **gold commodity**, not verified artificial-gold production. [W29-T05]
- **367–375 original Valens solidus**, Berlin museum **18202344**, struck from actual gold, Trier mint, **4.22g, 21mm**; date broad, possibly narrower mid-373–375. This **is a physical gold object**, not a source attesting hypothetical bronze→gold procedure. [W29-O01]
- **Beyond 380 excluded:** Olympiodorus/Synesius uncertain chronology and later text witnesses; exact Zosimos floruit broadly c.300; original Leiden X/Stockholm manuscript dates broad. Do not give them invented **367** activity.

## 02 — MATERIAL TECHNIQUE: products, claims, measurements and operations

**Themistius's procedure does not exist as an inspected procedure.** His reference to hypothetical **bronze-to-silver / silver-to-gold** is a **rhetorical supposition** rather than a written material recipe. There is no raw-material ratio, retort, controlled heat treatment, ore assay, specimen or named goldmaking author attached to the speech. The public audience's intelligibility of the idea is positive evidence for **perception**, not **successful capacity**.

**Real working of metals** is independently evidenced by a struck gold **Valens solidus**, not proof of **new elemental gold** from existing base metals. Color, gilding, dye, alloying and imitative products in earlier physical papyrus recipes can change perceived appearance without converting elemental atomic identity; their authorship and date cannot be assigned to 367 from a broad later manuscript range.

**Law regulates actual gold**: the attempted restriction on precious-metal export concerns available **commodities**, traders and imperial resources, not patent rights or occult manufacture. No named workshop whose activity changes in response is documented.

**Divination kit** in Ammianus: a suspended-ring/letter device is a reported instrument within a **retrospective political-ritual account**, not an independently excavated apparatus for chemical process. Divination and physical alchemy are not interchangeable.

## 03 — LANGUAGE, ALLEGORY AND ATTRIBUTION

**Themistius's form is analogy/counterfactual:** it is the **hypothetical discovery of material transmutation** set against **what Valens actually did politically**: transforming opponents into friends by clemency. Circe and Medea function as literary figures, not literal workshop teachers or documented ancestral alchemists. *A public metaphor based on a chemical-sounding transformation* is not documentary evidence of a professional transmuter. Oration's composition/delivery usually **367**, possible **366/367**, preserved in later manuscripts/editions. [W29-T02]

**Julian's `magistri studiorum doctoresque`** are instructors admitted by municipal and imperial institutions. The separate explanatory Greek letter is not another original 362 Latin edict and does not list chemical teacher candidates. [W29-T01]

**`Haruspicina` / `maleficium`** in the **371** law are legally differentiated. `Mathematicorum tractatus` names astrological consultancy in another contested-date law, not 344 government-protected practical geometry. A claimed magical divinatory apparatus does not prove its prognostic capacity, and Ammianus's reported sequence **THEOD-** is neither scientific measurement nor encoded metal recipe. [W29-T03–T04]

**`Non solum aurum barbaris minime praebeatur`** in CJ IV.63.2 concerns exchange/export of **gold** to outsiders; it does not express fear of new laboratory gold, establish an Alchemist license or name a guild.

## 05 — PERCEPTION: audiences, belief and claimed effects

**Themistius's court audience (c.367):** metaphor suggests that turning bronze into silver and silver into gold would be extraordinary, while Valens's mercy is comparably astonishing and preferable. **The audience must understand the imagined comparison**, but need not believe the method exists. This is a significant **public, dated and named witness for cultural recognition of the possibility of metal-change**.

**Julian's teachers:** imperial authorities judge moral/theological fitness for interpreting classical authors; this tells us about curricular/cultural legitimacy, not ability to convert matter.

**Ammianus's narrated political defendants:** alleged ability to foresee succession could bring deadly attention; this is an official and historian's perception of risk, not proof of supernatural capacity or links to metallurgy.

**Gold merchants and mint:** state monetary importance and actual struck gold are documented separately from any perception of practical alchemical ability.

## 06 — STATION: who is recognizable as what?

- **Themistius:** named philosopher/public orator, rhetorical witness to a **hypothetical transformation**, **not a professional Alchemist**.
- **Julian's sanctioned teachers:** generic literary/educational station with a legal gate; no separately documented 362 Alchemist.
- **Haruspices/astrologers/magi named in laws:** ritual/divinatory categories, qualified/restricted by specific rulers and acts; no documented 367 chrysopoeia worker.
- **Theodorus/Hilarius in Ammianus:** named alleged political diviners, not identified transmutation specialists.
- **Valens's gold coin issuer, mint and traders:** real state monetary/material roles, people who handle gold, not those proved able to create it from other metals.
- **Zosimos of Panopolis c.300:** historically identifiable earlier technical commentary/patronage claimant, but **not securely active in 361–380**.

**Conclusion:** the concept of transmutation enters **a recognizable public discourse**, while the station of an actual **alchemically authorized practitioner** is still not independently demonstrated for these years.

## 09 — HERE, FAILURE CONDITIONS, REMAINDER

**WARRANTED NEW INSIGHT:** metal-to-metal transmutation is an idea publicly presented by an identifiable non-alchemical **fourth-century court intellectual**, with a reasonably located speech (**c.367**). It is specifically framed as a **hypothetical extraordinary achievement**, not a procedure. Political/legal authorities distinctly grant or condition scholarly-teaching legitimacy, tolerate some ritual claims while prohibiting harmful ones, repress politically consequential alleged divination, and regulate trade in real precious metal. No documentary bridge between these streams and one practicing Alchemist is established.

**NOT SHOWN:** complete contemporary Greek laboratory recipe, named 367 Alchemist, empirical sample from successful bronze→silver→gold conversion, Zosimos/Themistius teacher relation, institution accepting chrysopoeia as officially credentialed service, named alchemy victim in Antioch trials, alleged successful oracle, proven identical dating of gold-embargo and oration.

**UNKNOWN:** precise Themistius speech delivery date; possible cultural origin of his metallic comparison and its audience reaction; availability of practical artificial-gold methods; prosopographical links between philosophers/artisans; exact trial chronology and imperial procedure; actual enforcement of export ban; social identity of local metalworkers.

**MOST PROFOUND:** **An idea can gain a public witness before any practitioner acquires a socially warranted station.** Themistius gives us stronger positive evidence of **cultural perception of alchemical-style transformation** than of actual **alchemical capacity**. This stops the project from confusing *public knowability of a claim* with *recognition of the claimant*.
