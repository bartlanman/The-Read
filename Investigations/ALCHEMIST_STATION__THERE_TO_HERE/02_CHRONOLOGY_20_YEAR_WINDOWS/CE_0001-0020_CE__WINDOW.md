# Window 11 — 1–20 CE: The State Names the Value of Metal; Institutions Keep Their Crafts

**Status:** SOURCE-ANCHORED TEN-LAYER HISTORICAL PASS, 2026-10-18. **No secure individual Alchemist's station within this exact window.**  
**Span:** 1–20 CE inclusive; no year zero between 1 BCE and 1 CE.  
**Separate trajectories:** Wang Mang's Western Han / Xin China and Augustan–Tiberian Roman Egypt. There is no evidenced direct transmission between them.  
**Investigative question:** When political authority changes the nominal value and legal circulation of metal, how does that differ from technical capacity to transform the material? How does a change of sovereign alter Egyptian temple stations and their material support?

## 01 — THERE: inherited setting
**China:** Wang Mang consolidated power in the Western Han regency and proclaimed the **Xin dynasty in 9 CE**, ruling through **23 CE** (his death and dynasty's collapse **not** in our 1–20 CE window). Chinese money/craft work already involved state oversight, and the earlier histories show law against counterfeit gold **144 BCE** and Liu Xiang's unsuccessful **c.60–56 BCE** imperial goldmaking attempt. Neither event proves continuity of a unified alchemist guild through the Xin. [W11-C01–C03]
**Egypt:** Roman Egypt under Augustus until **14 CE**, followed by Tiberius. Existing temples, priestly titles, language and craft traditions interacted with Roman prefectural taxation and cult policy. Large temple sites (Dendera, Edfu, Kom Ombo, Philae) are **multi-century** building histories; a Tiberian cartouche (14–37 CE) cannot automatically be assigned to the first six years of his reign in this window. [W11-E01–E03]

## 02 — CAPACITY: observable manufacturing and material technique
- **7 CE — physical coin:** British Museum 1996,0217.239 is a **bronze Wang Mang knife-shaped coin with an inlaid gold inscription**, inscribed "one knife worth five thousand". The archaeological object demonstrates metallurgical craft and legally claimed face value; it does **not** demonstrate changing bronze chemically into gold. [W11-O01]
- **14 CE — physical coin:** British Museum 1908,0212.15 is a **copper-alloy huoquan** coin assigned to 14 CE. Metallurgical work persists while the government's money system changes. [W11-O02]
- **Reforms 7,9,10,14 CE:** National Museum of China presents rounds/knife/spade coins, gold and silver, turtles and cowries in a heavily altered currency system. **20 CE** cessation of large denomination circulation belongs inside this window, not to the coming 21–40 period. [W11-C01]
- **Actual craft vs nominal value:** 7 CE gold inlay is observable physical gold applied to bronze. "5000" is a governmental valuation, not an assay showing the object contains 5,000 times the bronze. The legal issuing authority and the physical capacity to make a coin are separate.
- **Egypt:** Egyptian religious/cult buildings and ornate reliefs demonstrate physical craft under Roman rule in broad dates. A particular alchemical furnace, technical recipe or name of an alchemist in 1–20 CE **has not been established** by these sources.

## 03 — LANGUAGE, FORM, SYMBOL AND ALLEGORY
**China:**
- **一刀平五千** ("one knife valued at five thousand"): numismatic **declaration of value** in characters, with gilded/inlaid characters physically visible on a coin. A publicly readable monetary promise, not a secret chemical cipher. [W11-O01]
- **貨泉** (*huoquan*) and **貨布** (*huobu*) are changed monetary forms/names, not names of goldmaking ingredients. Date their issuance to the relevant reform: 14 CE. [W11-C01–C02]
- **Five materials/six currency names/twenty-eight denominations:** the 10 CE court classification connects material to *legal equivalence*. The ruler can change *nominal* value without changing the properties of copper, gold, silver or shells. [W11-C01]
- **鑄錢** (*zhu qian*, coin-casting) and **私鑄** (*si zhu*, unauthorized/private casting) in historical account describe production under licensing/legal status, not material transmutation. Severe penalties make the distinction consequential. [W11-P01]
**Egypt:** cartouches, imperial titulature and Egyptian temple images position Roman Augustus/Tiberius in earlier pharaonic sacred roles. These are **ritual legitimation** and architectural production, not verified goldmaking recipes. [W11-E01]

### Greek alchemical anticipation — prevent premature dating
Matteo Martelli's critical *Four Books of Pseudo-Democritus* places the Greek alchemical corpus **in the first century CE**, with detailed dating arguments pointing **after its first half, possibly in Nero's reign (54–68 CE)**. These writings are **not evidence of an Alchemist stationed in 1–20 CE**. Bolos of Mendes (earlier centuries BCE) should not be equated with this later pseudonymous Greek author. Treat this as a **later window research lead**, not a contemporary event. [W11-G01–G02]

## 04 — SECRECY, ACCESS, PERSECUTION, CAPACITY AND DANGER
| Mechanism | Evidence in 1–20 CE | What it does *not* mean |
|---|---|---|
| **Institutional control** | Wang Mang regulates issuance, valuation, possession and casting of currency; temple estates under Roman administrative negotiation | Not a ban on every alchemist or secret metallurgy school |
| **Persecution/punishment** | Laws and reported severe sanctions for illicit coin production and non-compliant money use | Punishment for *currency crimes* ≠ prosecution for successful transmutation or ritually making elixirs |
| **Economic value** | Government overstates some new coin nominal values relative to materials; public resists | The coin does not contain gold equal to its face value |
| **Competence** | Cast copper alloy, bronze blade, finely inlay gold and cut character shapes | A craftsman possessing this ability is not automatically legally authorized to mint |
| **Hazard** | Economic loss and judicial danger to illicit producers; no source here alleges secret chemical safety restriction | Cannot infer symbolic language arose to avert chemical poisoning |
| **Prestige** | New dynasty chooses archaizing knife/spade forms and traditional metal/commodity classifications | Archaic appearance not necessarily deliberate secret code |
| **Archive / retrospective voice** | *Hanshu* compiled after events; modern museums preserve coins as physical evidence | Later moralizing narrative cannot independently establish ancient actors' every motive |

### Egyptian temple-confiscation correction
Older narratives assert Roman rule confiscated temple land *en masse*, producing early institutional collapse. **Andrew Connor (2022)** rereads documentary papyri including P.Tebt. II 302 and argues that a localized property conflict was turned into an unwarranted story of empire-wide seizure. This is a **scholarly dispute over interpretation**, not the conclusion that Rome exerted zero pressure. Do NOT repeat the older thesis as a verified universal cause of alchemical station decline. [W11-E02]
The existence of temple rites in Roman Egypt does not prove secret chemistry, and restrictions on priestly resources are not identical to an alchemical knowledge monopoly.

## 05 — PERCEPTION
- **Government of Xin:** can present currency names, images, forms and equivalences as legally binding, while threatening opponents of the new system.
- **Coin users / later historian:** money reforms create exchange distress and encourage unapproved private casting, according to historical reconstruction. Material utility and legal legitimacy can diverge.
- **Egyptian priests and subjects:** continuity and institutional change coexist; the actual distribution of local property was contested, not resolved by an easy story of total confiscation. **No demonstrated named Alchemist directly involved.**
- **Modern historian:** Martelli's dating of Greek pseudonymous alchemical treatises questions an imagined single unbroken Greek alchemical order extending backward through early Roman Egypt.

## 06 — STATION
**Attested:** Regent/new Xin emperor Wang Mang; minting/monetary administration and actual manufacturers evidenced by numismatic objects; Roman rulers and Egyptian temple-priestly infrastructure.
**Not attested:** a formally designated Alchemist office in 1–20 CE; a verified worker at a 1–20 CE Egyptian goldmaking apparatus; independent archaeological demonstration of the Chinese ruler employing alchemists to transmute elements.
**Station pressure:** legitimacy to *make a money-shaped object* does not equal legal permission to *issue currency*. The same technology is rewarded as mint work and punished as forgery depending on authorization.

## 07 — PEOPLE, GENEALOGY AND FALSE TRANSMISSION
- **Wang Mang / Xin institutions → artisans and coin users**: legal/economic hierarchy and material production; not an apprenticeship chain of alchemists.
- **Earlier Han catalogues (Liu Xiang/Liu Xin) → Wang Mang's classicizing political ideology**: possible intellectual institutional backdrop; any **specific** exchange or technical lineage demands documents not yet found.
- **Roman rulers → Egyptian temple institution**: external administration and ritual continuity, not a demonstrated transfer of alchemical secrets.
- **Bolos → later pseudo-Democritus:** earlier attribution has been challenged by modern critical textual scholarship; do not assert identical authorship.
- **China ↔ Egypt:** unproven teacher/text/formula connections in this window.

## 09 — HERE: WARRANTS AND REMAINDER
**Warranted:**
1. Gold-inlaid bronze and copper coin artifacts attest technical artisanship, but their declared numerical values come from political authorization.
2. A state can change the **accepted identity, denomination and legality** of a metal object without altering its underlying material.
3. Economic pressure can lead to punitive jurisdiction over craftspeople; this is not alchemical persecution.
4. Egyptian temple cultural stations were not simply annihilated by Roman conquest; a sweeping older confiscation thesis is contested by close papyrological study.
5. A well-known first-century Greek alchemical text must **not** be falsely anchored in 1–20 CE when critical dating points later.

**Unknown:** identities of coin casters, mint oversight methods, exact archaeological workshops, how illicit casting actually occurred, 1–20 CE Egyptian named experimental chemists, Egyptian priestly training under Augustus/Tiberius, early Greek authors of pseudonymous alchemical recipes.

### Most profound historical idea
**The ability to transform a material and the authority to change what that material means are different powers.** The same gold-inlaid coin can be a brilliant workshop object and an economically contested legal claim. A ruler defines its currency status; an artisan physically realizes its form. That tension is a historical model for why claimed alchemical capacity, perceived value, and institutional authority must remain distinct.
