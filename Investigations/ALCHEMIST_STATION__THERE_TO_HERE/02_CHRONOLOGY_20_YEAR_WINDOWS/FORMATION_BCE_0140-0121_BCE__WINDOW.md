# Window 04 — 140–121 BCE: A Practitioner Recognized at Court, and a Patronage Network of Knowledge

**Status:** SOURCE-GROUNDED FIRST PRIMARY-TEXT PASS (Shiji 28), with modern specialist secondary controls; still open for critical-edition and date collation.  
**Window:** 140–121 BCE inclusive.  
**Geographical distinction:** Western Han China is a **separate early Chinese alchemical history**, not an asserted ancestor of Greco-Egyptian alchemy. Ptolemaic Egypt is tracked separately as a continuing background; no securely attributable 140–121 BCE Egyptian alchemical practitioner/text has yet been identified in this pass.  
**Central question:** When a ruler sponsors a person who claims to transform matter, does that recognition create a station? How do secrecy, demonstration, narrative and continuation of methods sustain it?

## 01 — THERE: historical conditions
- **141 BCE:** Emperor Wu begins his reign (141–87 BCE). His court becomes a setting for competing methods and legitimacy claims: ritual experts, literary advisors, Confucian and Huang-Lao discourses, and practitioners described as **fangshi** (*masters/specialists of methods*). The native term is **not** a precise professional title equivalent to the modern Alchemist. [W04-P01, W04-S01, W04-S03]
- **Earlier background:** The 144 BCE counterfeit-gold law belonged to Window 03. It did not explicitly ban elixir alchemy. No source demonstrates that it caused Li Shaojun to go underground. [W03-C01–C03]
- **139 BCE (common conventional date, debated in source criticism):** Prince Liu An's scholarly entourage and the *Huainanzi*. Early court histories report sponsorship by Liu An of writers and method specialists. Scholarly debate remains over precise composition, presentation and Liu An's individual authorship. **Do not treat the Huainanzi itself as a surviving operative goldmaking manual.** [W04-S04–S06]
- **122 BCE:** Liu An dies after accusations of treason and his princely domain is absorbed by central power, illustrating political vulnerability of a patron's station—not demonstrated persecution for alchemical study. [W04-S04, W04-S05]

## 02 — TECHNIQUE: what was claimed versus what was shown
### Li Shaojun, often placed around 133 BCE
The *Shiji*, 28 (*Fengshan shu*), names Li Shaojun and reports that he came before Emperor Wu as a practitioner of stove-related ritual, dietary techniques and methods for resisting old age. He claimed a staged possibility:
1. Perform ritual at the stove (祠灶, *ci zao*).
2. Attract beings/phenomena (致物, *zhi wu*): exact semantics require critical commentary.
3. Transform **cinnabar** (丹沙, *dan sha*) into **yellow gold** (黃金, *huang jin*).
4. Make eating and drinking vessels from the gold.
5. Through their use, prolong life, encounter Penglai immortals, and perform the *feng* and *shan* sacrifices leading to deathlessness. [W04-P01, lines corresponding to paragraphs 56–58; W04-S01]

**Attested as narration:** claims, imperial worship at the stove, sea expeditions, and attempts to make gold.  
**NOT established:** actual successful transmutation, enhanced longevity, immortals, independently recovered gold vessels, an explicit working recipe, or the material capability claimed. [W04-P01]

That makes the proposed procedure *more than an isolated metallurgy trick*: the described transformation is embedded in ritual, sovereignty, substances, longevity and royal legitimation. This is what the historical actor reportedly *claimed*, not a demonstration that his cosmology worked.

## 03 — LANGUAGE AND ALLEGORY: the mechanism of apparent authority
| Chinese expression | Working reading | Why it matters | Warrant limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 方士 *fangshi* | specialist/master of methods | Indigenous category within a broader court ecology | Not a literal translation of an alchemist profession |
| 方 *fang* | methods, recipes, arts | Methods can be conveyed, acquired, recognized | Does not imply secret chemical formula alone |
| 祠灶 *ci zao* | sacrifice to stove | Ritual action is the first link in the claimed material process | Not simply instruction to use a laboratory furnace |
| 丹沙 *dan sha* | cinnabar | Materially identifiable substance in transformation claim | No evidence it is a deliberate code-name for some other reagent here |
| 黃金 *huang jin* | yellow gold | Valued result and utensil material | Claim of achievement is not recovered laboratory evidence |
| 蓬萊 *Penglai* | legendary immortals' realm | Sacred/revelatory dimension linked to bodily longevity | Not a concealed reagent without separate key |
| 化去 *hua qu* | departed/transformed | Emperor's interpretation after Li's death | Not actual demonstration Li escaped death |
| 匿其年及其生長 | concealed age and biographical origins | **Explicit textual report of intentional withholding** | Concealment concerns identity, not demonstrably ingredient names |

**Finding:** We observe **ritual-technical sequencing and exceptional self-presentation**, rather than a verified allegorical cipher that hides an executable laboratory recipe.

An ancient bronze vessel episode reportedly confirms Li's knowledge of its origins. Its inscription was said to match Li's identification, prompting observers to imagine he was centuries old. This story concerns the *social perception of capacity*. We have not verified an independent contemporary record of that demonstration. [W04-P01]

## 04 — SECRECY, ACCESS, HAZARD AND QUALIFICATION
| Hypothesis | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional monopoly | Emperor sponsors ritual and attempts; no evidence of a special royal license required for *all* fangshi | **Not established** |
| Practitioner fear/persecution | Li concealed his age and birthplace; narrative says the gifts and prestige grew due in part to uncertainty about his origin | **Concealment documented in narrative; fear of persecution NOT evidenced** |
| Economic value | Patrons bestowed gifts, enabling Li to live without visible trade | **Attested in Shiji narrative** |
| Capacity/apprenticeship | Li's extraordinary knowledge of an antique vessel was interpreted as exceptional mastery; evidence of apprenticeship or actual reproducible chemical skill absent | **Perception attested, demonstrated transmutation NOT evidenced** |
| Danger | Cinnabar is a material with mercury compounds and can be hazardous in certain circumstances; no text here ties secrecy to safety | **Physical hazard context; protective secrecy NOT evidenced** |
| Prestige/authority | Concealed biography + astonishing identification + imperial honor + claimed immortality sequence | **Strong evidence of an authority-making narrative** |
| Loss vs deliberate code | No Li Shaojun manual or specific original encoded recipe survives | **Textual absence not proof of encryption** |

**Negative evidence:** Li is not presented as a member of a persecuted underground guild. The named court supports, rewards and attempts to preserve his methods.

## 05 — PERCEPTION: how observers assigned extraordinary capacity
- **Peers/patrons:** The *Shiji* says visitors gave Li wealth and trusted his exceptional claims despite not knowing his origins.
- **Palace audience:** His identification of a bronze vessel allegedly impressed the court, whose members viewed him as extraordinarily ancient.
- **Emperor:** Supported Li's ritual and material program, then refused to understand his death as ordinary death.
- **Historian:** Sima Qian reports that Li became ill and died; the emperor instead thought he had *transformed away*. This explicit **difference in interpretation** is precious evidence for our station model. [W04-P01]

There is a strong contrast between an **observer's verified biography** and an **audience's projected exceptional station**. The latter could persist even when a predicted capacity was not demonstrated.

## 06 — STATION: what was actually recognized?
**Warranted:** a named **fangshi / specialist in methods** with preexisting patronage who received access, gifts and imperial honor. An explicit ruler/practitioner relationship appears in a relatively early surviving historical work.

**Not warranted:** Li was titled 'Alchemist' as a distinct Han office; he received a formal chemical credential; he could successfully make elemental gold; he founded a permanent guild; all peers belonged to one initiatory succession.

**Interpretation:** A *court-supported transformative-knowledge station* is visible as a **social relation**, even though proven technical capability and a dedicated official title are not.

## 07 — PEOPLE, PATRONS AND LINEAGE
- **Li Shaojun (李少君):** named method specialist; reported former retainer of Marquis of Shenze, then imperial patronage under Wu. **~133 BCE is conventional, not an exact date established by the quoted Shiji narrative.** [W04-P01, W04-S01–S02]
- **Emperor Wu:** patron whose support, reinterpretation of death and order to transmit Li's methods explicitly changed the social trajectory of the teachings. [W04-P01]
- **Person ordered to receive the methods:** The source says the emperor arranged for them to be received after Li's death. The transmitted Chinese string **黃錘史寬舒** has disputed segmentation in later notes; do not confidently claim a specific number of named recipients without critical collation. [W04-P01, W04-S07]
- **Other coastal fangshi:** more appeared in the court narrative afterward. This is *competition and influx*, not proof of direct apprenticeship from Li. [W04-P01]
- **Liu An:** Prince of Huainan and patron of scholarly/method specialists; the Huainanzi is evidence for broad philosophical court writing, while several **lost** technical/elixir books were subsequently associated with his circle. Authorship and manuscript histories remain disputed. [W04-S04–S06]

No link to Greek alchemy is inferred.

## 08 — TEXTS AND MANUSCRIPTS: what manifested?
| Text | Alleged historical setting | Witness / composition | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shiji 28, Fengshan shu** | Imperial activity under Wu (Li often c133 BCE) | Written late 2nd/early 1st century BCE by Sima Qian, *later than described encounter*; surviving copies much later | Named expert, claims, ruler's actions, patronage, criticism, reported method transfer [W04-P01] |
| **Shiji 12, Xiaowu benji** | Similar Wu episodes | Textually close parallel; its own transmission/compilation issues; **not an independent eyewitness confirmation** | Comparison of variants in transmitted history [W04-S07] |
| **Hanshu 25A, Jiao si zhi** | Early Han rituals | First/second-century CE compilation and later manuscript history; follows earlier accounts closely | Later history and terminology, not a 133 BCE eyewitness [W04-S07] |
| **Huainanzi** | Liu An's court; presentation conventionally 139 BCE | Compilation and authorship contested; extant transmitted philosophical compendium | Actual intellectual patronage and cosmological argument; **not itself a procedural alchemical recipe book** [W04-S04–S06] |
| **Hongbao yuanbi shu / associated secret collection** | Broad mid-second-century BCE affiliation in later bibliographies | **LOST**; titles/authorship/reporting vary; no original recipes available to collate | Existence as historical textual tradition reported by later sources; exact 140–121 date/contents not secure [W04-S01, W04-S06] |

## 09 — HERE: warranted transformation and unresolved remainder
**There → Here (source-supported):**
1. A *named specialist* claims a sequence linking the manipulation of matter, health, immortality and royal ritual. This is more directly relevant to an alchemical station than previous royal or monetary laws.
2. Patrons respond to perceived exceptional capacity by awarding material support and access.
3. The emperor funds or authorizes attempts associated with Li's methods.
4. **After the practitioner dies, the emperor attempts to preserve and transmit those methods.** An expertise can therefore acquire custodial continuity through a patron *even without evidence that the claimed outcome succeeded*.
5. A prince simultaneously supports broad cosmological, philosophical and specialist activity in another court; literary and practical authority are distinct.

**UNKNOWNS:** original recipe; transmutation outcomes; laboratory vessels; instruction relationships; what passed to successors; cause of self-concealment; whether Li's performative knowledge was intentional deceit, sincere ritual belief, technically informed skill, or a combination.

## 10 — THE OUTSIDE: pressure and station response
**For Li:** Pressure includes imperial demand for exceptional longevity, ritual legitimacy and prestige, and competition among court specialists. Li's claim integrates technical, ritual and historical assertions into one attractive offer. At his death the station could have vanished, yet the emperor's response sustained the *claimed knowledge* by commissioning its transfer, while further coastal specialists entered the patronage field. [W04-P01]

**For Liu An:** Liu An's princely scholarly position supported collective textual work and circulation, but centralization and charges of treason culminated in 122 BCE death and loss of political station. That is not demonstrated persecution of the Huainanzi's cosmology, the alchemical art or his authors as such. [W04-S04–S06]

**For Egypt:** No source presently shows a securely dated 140–121 BCE Greco-Egyptian alchemical station pressured or transformed. Separate chronology and provenance from the independent Chinese case.

## Most profound distinction
**A claimed capacity can become institutionally consequential because a patron credits it, attempts to act on it, and preserves its methods—even when the original actor dies and the claimed achievement remains unverified.**

This supports **a station of publicly recognized exceptional expertise**, not confirmed goldmaking or a documented professional alchemist title.
