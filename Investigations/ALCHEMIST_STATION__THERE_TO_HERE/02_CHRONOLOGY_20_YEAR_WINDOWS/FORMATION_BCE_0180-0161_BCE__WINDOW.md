# Window 02 — 180–161 BCE: Authority Reaffirmed, Royal Names Erased

**Status:** SECOND DETAILED HISTORICAL PASS, SOURCE-GROUNDED BUT OPEN  
**Scope:** Ptolemaic Egypt, with comparative attention to actual inscription, named stations, craft evidence, and access to technical knowledge.  
**Dates:** inclusive 180–161 BCE = twenty consecutive years.  
**Crucial distinction:** the attested **priestly station** is not an attested **alchemical station**.

## 01 — THERE: Historical conditions
Ptolemy VI Philometor succeeded his father Ptolemy V in 180 BCE, initially under the regency of his mother, Cleopatra I. The monarch's authority was mediated by court factions and Egyptian temple institutions. Regnal disputes, a triple kingship with Ptolemy VIII and Cleopatra II, and the Sixth Syrian War (170–168 BCE) culminated in a period of foreign invasion, divided rulership and contests over legitimate sovereignty. Ptolemy VI was expelled by his brother in 164 BCE and restored in 163 BCE. [W02-S01, W02-S02]

**Research condition:** a governing institution could remain intact while the occupants of royal station changed, split or were re-legitimized. Do not infer specific outcomes for craftworkers without documentary evidence.

## 02 — Technique / material practice
The British Museum catalogs coins under Ptolemy VI in copper alloy and gold, with royal and cultic imagery. **Dating caution:** coins given a production range of **180–145 BCE** do not prove that a particular surviving specimen was struck inside 180–161 BCE. They are evidence of metalworking/minting in the broader reign, not alchemical transmutation. [W02-S03]

Archaeological investigation of a colored inlay workshop associated with the Soknebtynis sanctuary at Tebtunis demonstrates that Ptolemaic temples could be productive sites as well as ritual centers. **Dating caution:** the investigated workshop is Ptolemaic, but the source does not assign it securely to 180–161 BCE; use as period-level background only. [W02-S04]

Neither temple inlay nor royal coinage establishes that a separate professional alchemist existed in this window.

## 03 — Language, script and allegory
The 161 BCE **Memphite Sacerdotal Decree**, Cairo CG 22184, survives in **hieroglyphic** form. Its damaged state does not establish that it was intentionally coded. The modern editors caution that one cannot know whether the original monument also included Greek and Demotic, unlike certain earlier decrees. [W02-P01]

**Language operation actually visible:** authority is displayed through an Egyptian royal-priestly inscription and royal names in cartouches. The later **erasure of royal cartouches** is a separate physical alteration to a public inscription, not evidence that a workshop formula was allegorically encrypted.

**Interpretative comparison (NOT source fact):** a symbolic or sacred script, a public royal title, an erased name, and a deliberate technical cipher would all act differently on access and authority. Our stack must not collapse them under the word 'secret'.

## 04 — Secrecy, access, danger and capacity
- **Institutional protection:** temples and royalty manage ceremonial recognition; no evidence yet of a ban on an alchemical art in this window.
- **Persecution:** political rivalry and coups are evidenced; the claim that alchemists were persecuted here is **NO SECURE EVIDENCE**.
- **Economic value:** royal coinage and sacred objects suggest precious-material value as context, but no direct demonstration of concealed craft formula.
- **Competence:** minting, inlay, inscription and writing involve specialized skills; these material operations cannot simply be equated with alchemical initiation.
- **Physical danger:** no specific ancient text in this window has been shown to restrict access to a hazardous chemical preparation.
- **Prestige and legitimacy:** a priestly decree's invocation of royal legitimacy is documented; any analogy to secret alchemical title-making is interpretive only.
- **Loss or concealment?** Later **erasure of cartouches** demonstrates deliberate alteration of publicly visible named authority; it does **not** prove original concealment of knowledge.

## 05 — Perception
Ptolemaic state and temple actors displayed royal legitimacy through ceremonial titles, monuments and decrees. The Memphite decree's 161 BCE date suggests a reaffirmation of Ptolemy VI's status after his restoration, though a coronation/re-coronation is the interpretation offered by modern editors, not an undisputed fact derived from a complete inscription. [W02-P01, W02-S05]

## 06 — Station
**Attested:** royal office, royal cult and priestly assembly, hieroglyphic inscription, royal names and titles, elite temple actors.
**Not attested:** a self-identified person holding the separate office of Alchemist, a named alchemical master, or an identifiable teacher–student alchemical curriculum.

A recurring ritual-station text does not imply that a transmutational station was created.

## 07 — Practitioners and lineage
- **Ptolemy VI Philometor:** reign and competing royal authority.
- **Cleopatra I, Cleopatra II, Ptolemy VIII:** dynastic legitimacy, religious and court institutions, not practitioners of alchemy in this evidence.
- **Anonymous priests at Memphis:** the issuing group of the 161 decree; no identifiable alchemical authorship.
- **Bolos of Mendes:** still not securely located in this window; avoid forcing a later-alchemical genealogy.

No continuous personal genealogy from 196 BCE priestly authors to the early Greek alchemists is warranted.

## 08 — Texts and textual manifestations
| Work or witness | Date and authorship | What is observable | What is not proven |
|---|---|---|---|
| Memphite Sacerdotal Decree, Cairo CG 22184 (TM 89794) | 161 BCE priestly assembly | Hieroglyphic monument, royal recognition, later cartouche erasure | An alchemical text; original Greek/Demotic translations |

**Important source-critical correction:** Panov and Lanciers note that the **161 BCE decree is not merely a copy or simple update** of the 196/185 BCE decrees, despite structural similarities. Its partly erased names and deviations demand separate textual interpretation. [W02-S05]

## 09 — HERE: What the new window changes
**Warranted transition in institutional recognition:** the previous window (200–181 BCE) showed a public mechanism through which priestly title, royal cult and written authority were formalized. The new window documents how royal station could be **contested, recertified and subsequently attacked through erasure in the very medium that made it visible**.

This matters for the broader Alchemist inquiry because it gives us a comparative model: expertise and recognition can be contingent, institutionally granted, and politically vulnerable. It is **not** yet direct evidence for why alchemical practitioners hid a technique.

### Strongest question carried forward
**At what point can someone retain recognized competence or a lineage of practical knowledge when the political or institutional station authorizing it is challenged, removed or erased?**

## Remainder / required verification
- Consult complete critical edition of the 161 BCE decree: exact hieroglyphic lines, lacunae, damage chronology, and alternative interpretations of the erasure.
- Establish a specific 180–161 BCE workshop contract, craft tax or apprenticeship record before claiming an actual chain of craft-specific authorization.
- Do not assign a single Ptolemy VI coin whose museum range is 180–145 BCE to this narrow twenty-year interval.
- Temple workshop finds are broad Ptolemaic evidence unless a narrower archaeological date is justified.
- No specifically dated alchemical treatise, allegorical code or persecuted practitioner has been found in the reviewed evidence for this window.
