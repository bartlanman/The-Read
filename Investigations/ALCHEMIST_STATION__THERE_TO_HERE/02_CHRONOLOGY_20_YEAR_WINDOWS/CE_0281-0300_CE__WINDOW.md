# Window 25 — 281–300 CE: Real Political Revolt, Later Story of Burned Gold Books, and Zosimos at the Threshold

**Status:** TEN-LAYER SOURCE-CRITICAL DEEP PASS (2026-10-18), original-period papyri/coins vs later literary testimony controlled. **The 297–298 Egyptian imperial rebellion is evidenced. Diocletian's alleged destruction of books on alchemical gold/silver preparation is a MUCH LATER NARRATIVE, not an independently evidenced 297 edict.** **Zosimos of Panopolis, c.300 CE, is the first identifiable Greek alchemical commentator; his exact activity within the calendar 281–300 is not securely established.** Neither an officially credentialed Alchemist nor ancient gold transmutation is verified here.

**Period:** 281–300 CE inclusive; region Roman Egypt/Alexandria, with learned Greco-Egyptian literature; separate comparative lineages remain separate. **Previous:** [Window 24](CE_0261-0280_CE__WINDOW.md). **Source register:** [Window 25](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0281-0300__SOURCE_REGISTER.md). **Language and access:** [Source-critical dossier](../09_LANGUAGE_TECHNIQUE_AND_ALLEGORY/CE_0281-0300__LANGUAGE_BOOKS_AND_AUTHORITY.md). **Station tests:** [ST-082–ST-085](../11_STATION_TRANSFORMATION_REGISTER/REGISTER.md).

## 01 — THERE: politics, religious/institutional conditions, economy and chronology

- **284 CE:** Diocletian succeeds to Roman imperial power. The empire's later tetrarchic reforms change public naming, military governance, taxation and administrative authority. **Chronological constraint:** features of the later system developed over years; they must not all be assigned to 284.
- **286–293:** Maximian becomes co-Augustus (286), then in 293 the tetrarchic arrangement includes Caesars Constantius and Galerius. This is a development in **recognized public office**, not a new Alchemist charter.
- **Late 280s–290s:** Roman monetary reforms alter weight standards and forms of coinage. **Sutherland 1955** gives differing dates for gold-standard changes and later lower denominations; avoid claiming one single 294 day or a proved motive for subsequent revolt. [W25-S06]
- **297 CE original documents:** The rebel **Lucius Domitius Domitianus** appears as emperor in original Egyptian Greek documentary dating formulas. Prosopography indexes **P.Isid.139** (24/28 August, first regnal year, specific day uncertain), **P.Isid.99** (29 August), **P.Isid.62** (5 September), **P.Thead.26** (13 September), additional petitions and leases into November, and **P.Michael.24** (2 December). This is original-period naming, not retrospective legend. [W25-P01,P02]
- **297/298 original metallic objects:** **RPC X ID 76414** shows **Domitianus Augustus** named `ΔΟΜΙΤΙΑΝΟϹ ϹΕΒ` on debased-silver/billon Alexandrian year-two tetradrachm, `L Β` and Nike on the reverse. Compare **RPC X 76417**, a different year-two type. These are minted **political claims and real worked metal**, not gold produced from copper. [W25-O01]
- **297–298:** later literary historians often put rebel **Aurelius Achilleus** at the center of the revolt or Alexandria's defense. Actual papyri instead identify **Domitianus as emperor**, and in some readings Achilleus as a `corrector`, suggesting separable persons/roles. Reconstructions of when the revolt began, precise duration of Alexandria's siege, and local consequences are disputed. The event should not be compressed to “Diocletian punished alchemists.” [W25-P01, S03]
- **c.298:** Diocletian's military reconquest of Alexandria is generally accepted from literary/numismatic/papyrological synthesis, but precise siege dates are debated. The evidence supports **suppression of a political revolt**, not a named alchemical-book confiscation. [W25-S03]
- **281–300 alchemical horizon:** Papyri Leiden X and Stockholm are generally dated broadly to the *late third/early fourth century* or by some critical anthologies to late third, but without a day-dated 297 colophon. **Zosimos of Panopolis** is often placed around **300 CE**, hence a candidate crossing this and the next window; **do not assert he published in 299**. [W25-S01–S02,S05]
- **Horizon excluded:** **303–305** Diocletianic anti-Christian persecution belongs to **Window 26 (301–320)**, even if a medieval Suda passage recounts it in the same entry as alleged Egyptian book burnings. Certain anti-Manichaean rescript dates **297 vs 302** are contested and must be kept cross-window, not treated as an established 297 alchemical edict. [W25-S04]

## 02 — MATERIAL TECHNIQUE: coins, recipes and competence claims

**ATTESTED object:** minting original **297/298** billon/debased-silver tetradrachms with Domitianus's public titles and regnal year. RPC X 76414 is a **tetradrachm type** with sample average c.**6.66 g**; minted metal is genuine craftsmanship, not a chemical recipe. No named mint-worker appears. The object cannot verify actual ability to manufacture gold or silver from another chemical element. [W25-O01]

**ATTESTED broadly dated technical-writing horizon:** Leiden X and Stockholm papyri contain **material-coloring recipes, precious-metal imitation, dyes, glass/gem effects**. Cambridge's *Alchemy Reader* dates them approximately the **end of the third century**, probably scribally related, and explicitly contrasts practical recipe prose with allegory in Zosimos. Neither physical papyrus bears a precisely datable **297** legal/colophon date, nor identifies its author or patron. A copying date broadly overlapping the window is not a 281–300-specific technical event. [W25-S05]

**Zosimos's documented authored interests:** alchemical manuscripts, apparatus and furnaces, interpretation of earlier writings and competition over correct interpretations; commentary is **an intellectual technique** layered above practical recipes. Actual exact year of writing and independent test of a finished gold sample remain **UNVERIFIED**. [W25-S01,S02]

**Categories:** alloying, gilding, silvering, coloring, dyeing and imitating precious stones are **real material processes** but not proven elemental transmutation. Metal fraud/coin counterfeiting, if alleged by a later source, needs an independently connected witness to become a 297 historical case.

## 03 — LANGUAGE AND ALLEGORY: `chēmeia` and who said it when

**The primary control:** Suda [Chi 280 `chēmeia`](https://cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/chi/280), and [Delta 1156 `Dioklētianos`](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/delta/1156), are **Byzantine lexicon passages**, compiled in the **tenth century**, drawing (according to Dufault pp.94–95 and notes) on **John of Antioch's *Chronography* c.610–626**, fragment **191 Mariev**. The transmission chain is **narrated Egyptian rebellion during Diocletian's reign → 7th-century chronography → 10th-century lexicon → modern translation**. It is **NOT** 297 documentary attestation.

Suda glosses `χημεία` as **`ἡ τοῦ ἀργύρου καὶ χρυσοῦ κατασκευή`**, the making/preparing of **silver and gold**, and alleges Diocletian sought old Egyptian `βιβλία` (books) and `ἔκαυσε` (burned them) to deny Egyptians wealth that would finance revolt. **We learn what the later writer said about technology, money and state risk**, not that Diocletian actually undertook the suppression or that artisans achieved actual gold conversion. [W25-T01]

**Contrast:** 297 original coin's `ΔΟΜΙΤΙΑΝΟϹ ϹΕΒ` and `L Β` are *contemporary* Greek public words. They tell us a political claimant styled himself Augustus, not the profession of a chemical operator.

**Golden Fleece narrative:** Suda [Delta 250](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/delta/250) retrospectively says Jason's Golden Fleece was an ancient treatise on obtaining gold via chēmeia. This is a **later exegetical myth**, not a recovered ancient chemical manuscript, and cannot serve as either an ancient author's autograph or a confirmed tutor's lineage.

**Earlier pseudonymous recipes and Zosimos:** texts attributed to Democritus, Hermes, Maria and other revered names cannot be treated as signed biography of the supposed author. In Zosimos's later-transmitted writing, **`magoi`** and cultic accusations are **interested polemic** against competitors, not independently verified scientific or occupational labels. [W25-S01]

## 05 — PERCEPTION: audiences and interested voices

**297 Egyptian administrators/contract parties:** dating in the reign of Domitianus is direct evidence that local documentary business counted him as emperor for dating purposes. It is not a referendum or universal legitimacy.

**Minting authority:** the coin depicts claimant's title and personifies victory; this reveals **publicly asserted authority**, not actual controlled outcomes of military victory or alchemical metal creation.

**Later Byzantine historiographer:** the Diocletian book-burning narrative places wealth-creating knowledge in a politically dangerous category. It **portrays** suppressive imperial logic, but may reflect much later cultural perceptions of alchemy; its exact earlier historical referent remains unverified.

**Zosimos and Theosebeia:** near-300 alchemical-text reading moves within the educated patronage field. Dufault interprets Theosebeia as wealthy patroness and learned interlocutor; a rival priest called **Neilos** is castigated in Zosimos's letters. **These are voices in interested transmitted treatises**, not independently audited contemporary witnesses to named professional guild roles.

## 06 — THE STATION: what is recognizable, and how far?

| Person/institution | Warranted role | Missing link to recognized Alchemist |
|---|---|---|
| Diocletian / tetrarchic rule | independently attested emperorship and military/political power | actual 297 anti-alchemy decree or targeted specialist |
| Domitius Domitianus | original 297 Egyptian document/coin **imperial claimant** | practical gold preparation, alchemist title, sustained universally accepted rule |
| Aurelius Achilleus | rebel figure in later accounts; some papyri indicate subordinate **corrector** role | direct technical role, conflation with Domitianus |
| Alexandrian mint | makes actual metal money bearing political names | identifiable craftsmen and personal alchemy claims |
| Copyist of Leiden X/Stockholm | physical transmitted practical recipe scribe, broadly late third/early fourth century | specific 297 named expert, authorship or guild |
| **Zosimos of Panopolis** | identifiable **alchemical commentator**, c.300, addressing a patroness and criticizing rivals | precise **281–300** publication or 297 association; job title recognized by a city/emperor; demonstrated gold sample |
| Theosebeia / Neilos | patroness and competitor/priest **as portrayed in Zosimos** | independent dated household/office attestations and actual techniques |

**Most consequential threshold:** We may now describe a **named writer** taking *alchemical textual expertise* into an educative, patronage-mediated competition. This is more specifically alchemical than Window 24's mint/legal comparisons. Yet **“first named commentator near 300” is NOT “first officially recognized professional Alchemist in 299.”**

## 07 — LINEAGE, SOURCE SEPARATION AND TRANSMISSION

**Suda book-burning:** reconstructed narrated late 290s event → **John of Antioch c.610–626** → **Suda 10th century Delta 1156 and Chi 280** → online modern critical edition. **Same textual family**, not multiple independent evidence of suppression. Contemporary decree/inventory/identified burnt book: **NONE located**.

**Rebellion:** 297 date-bearing **P.Isid** family and **P.Michael.24** (original Egyptian contracts/petitions) + 297/298 **RPC X coins** are distinct kinds of period witnesses. Later historians mainly describe **Achilleus**; original material names **Domitianus**. Do not “correct” all ancient accounts to one biography or automatically equate them.

**Zosimos:** likely authentic named early alchemical commentator, broadly c.300 → transmitted later through **Marcianus gr.299 c.1000, Parisinus gr.2325 13th century, Parisinus gr.2327 15th century**, other Syriac/Arabic traditions → modern text-critical editions and Dufault. **No original 297 letter, dated teacher appointment, or signed gold-transmutation certificate.**

**Maria/Pseudo-Democritus → Zosimos:** citations of named previous textual authorities **ARE textual reception**, not physical evidence these people were alive in 297 or personally trained Zosimos. China remains an independent study with no documented Greek–Chinese teacher/citation transfer identified.

## 09 — HERE: what changed, what did not, what we do not know

**SHOWN:** An Egyptian rebellion produced public alternate political dates/titles on both documents and coins. A later author **imagined or reported** rulers responding to potentially wealth-producing alchemical books with suppression; no secure original source demonstrates the alleged order. Meanwhile the **first reliably named commentator on earlier Greek alchemical texts** emerges around the turn of the fourth century in **Zosimos** (broad date), with intellectually competing claims over how material recipes should be understood and interpreted.

**NOT SHOWN:** any identified actual author of burned 297 books, Diocletian's authenticated law against goldmaking, successful elemental transmutation, named target punished for being an alchemist, goldmaking as causal finance of the rebellion, a formal credential or legally recognized alchemist station, individual Zosimos alive/teaching in **299** as a day-year fact, Theosebeia's public office confirmed independently, Leiden X physically copied in 297.

**UNKNOWN:** degree of state interest in particular recipes, when the suppression anecdote first arose, whether John of Antioch was adapted by Suda, whether any actual books were burnt, nature of Zosimos's early sponsors and rivals outside his letters, precise material practice in a 297 Egypt shop, biographies of alleged older masters, the effects of later imperial policy on actual recipe manuscripts.

**Single deepest finding:** **A story about the suppression of knowledge is itself evidence of what later people believed knowledge could do—but not evidence that the suppression occurred.** The more historically promising formation event is Zosimos's **alchemical interpretive authority** under patronage, not the dramatic book-burning tale. The social authority to **read, correct, teach and judge** recipes may matter more to the emerging station than any one demonstration of coloring metal.
