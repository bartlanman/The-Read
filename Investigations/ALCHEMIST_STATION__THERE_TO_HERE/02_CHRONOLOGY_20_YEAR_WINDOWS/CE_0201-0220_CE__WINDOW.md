# Window 21 — 201–220 CE: The Law That Grants Standing and the Order That Removes Access

**Status:** TEN-LAYER SOURCE-CRITICAL DEEP PASS (2026-10-18). **Strong period objects:** the Roman Forum Arch of Septimius Severus (dedicated **203 CE**, Geta name subsequently cut away); **P.Giss. 40**, a **c.215 CE Greek papyrus** preserving a Greek version of Caracalla's citizenship grant, plus an Alexandrian exclusion order. **Contemporary intellectual witness:** Clement of Alexandria, `Stromata` VI.4, broadly c.198–203 CE; his Egyptian-priestly book list is a *reported scheme*, not original surviving 42 books. **Retrospective:** Cassius Dio's account of Caracalla's abuse of Alexandrian Aristotelians. **No reliably attested practicing Greek-Egyptian Alchemist of 201–220 CE.**

## 01 — THERE: dated events, documents and outside pressures
- **Early third century, composition broad c.198–203:** Clement's `Stromata` VI.4 describes a procession of Egyptian temple experts (singer, astrologer, scribe, `stolistes`, `prophetes`) and **42 books attributed to Hermes**, of which **36** form priestly learning and **six** are medical materials assigned to `pastophoroi`. It is a Christian intellectual's **secondhand representation** of Egyptian religious knowledge; not a dated, directly excavated Egyptian curriculum or a goldmaking text. Some of its likely composition range is **before 201 CE**, so it is cross-window context, not safely a newly authored 201 event. [W21-C01–C03]
- **203 CE:** Roman Senate dedicates the **Arch of Septimius Severus**, honoring emperor and sons Caracalla and Geta. The original inscription was set in bronze letters; traces remain on real monument. After Caracalla murdered Geta **December 211 CE**, Geta's name was physically **chiseled away and replaced** with honorific language. This is a documented intervention in a **public material record**, not destruction of alchemical literature. [W21-A01–A03]
- **202–203 CE:** persecutions of Christians, including Carthaginian martyrs **Perpetua and Felicitas (203)**, are historically attested, but an **empire-wide Severan ban on conversion** depends on later/problematic source traditions such as `Historia Augusta`. Newer legal scholarship finds a universal specifically anti-Christian edict **cannot securely be sustained**. **No evidence a chemical Alchemist was targeted under such a law.** [W21-R01]
- **211 CE:** Severus dies; Caracalla and Geta briefly share imperial power; **December 211**, Caracalla has Geta killed and his public image erased. [W21-A01]
- **212 or 213 CE:** Caracalla issues `Constitutio Antoniniana` extending **Roman citizenship to almost all free residents** of the empire. Exception wording, exact day/date, and legal reach remain disputed; do not claim *everyone including enslaved people* was made a citizen. The `dediticii` exclusion is an **editorially restored, contested** reading. [W21-P01–P05]
- **c.215 CE:** the actual surviving papyrus **P.Giss. 40** is copied in Greek from the Latin imperial material. Its **left column** contains the damaged citizenship grant, and its **right side** contains other imperial texts including a Caracallan **instruction excluding categories of Egyptian migrants/rural residents from Alexandria**, exempting certain useful workers such as **river boatmen, pig traders, and reed suppliers for baths**. The usual interpretation situates this restriction in **215 CE**. Some modern scholarship argues the papyrus should not be described uncritically as *three separate edicts*; an order to a prefect is a different genre. [W21-P01–P05]
- **215/216 CE:** Caracalla's visit to Alexandria leads to violence and punishments according to Cassius Dio and Herodian; Dio says the emperor removed **common meals and other privileges of Alexandrian Aristotelian philosophers**, motivated by his worship of Alexander and supposed resentment of Aristotle. This is **retrospective authorial testimony** about philosophy's institutional privileges, not an original 215 law prohibiting Alchemists. Expulsions in P.Giss 40 have **direct original period written support**, but do not prove the full details of Dio's reported massacre. [W21-D01,W21-P03]
- **217 CE:** Caracalla assassinated; Macrinus becomes emperor. **218 CE:** Elagabalus rises; imperial political and religious claims change again. **No dated Alchemist certification** follows solely from the succession.

## 02 — MATERIAL / TECHNICAL CAPACITY
**Arch:** craftsmen can carve marble, fasten bronze letters, remove an original name and recut the same physical surface. The result is **both an altered public claim and a surviving physical trace of alteration**. Producing stone lettering does not prove the ruler's actual moral or divine fitness.
**P.Giss. 40:** scribes translate, copy and compile **public law** on papyrus, and state actors classify people by legal status, geography, rural/urban origin and occupation. The physical papyrus was copied **c.215**, distinct from the **212/213** legal enactment it preserves. Original Latin state document **not extant**.
**Clement:** describes physicians/medicine among temple knowledge custodians and a learned division of texts. **No medical papyrus containing the precise six books survives here**, and none demonstrates conversion of base metals into gold.
**Alchemy:** Greek Pseudo-Democritus remains a broadly dated earlier anonymous textual authority; **Maria the Jewess** is known later through Zosimos ~300, not direct 201–220 eyewitness proof.

## 03 — LANGUAGE, TRANSLATION, ALLEGORY
- `civitas Romana`, Greek legal citizenship terms: **juridical classification**, not automatic right to live in every quarter of Alexandria or capacity to produce matter.
- The citizenship papyrus has **missing portions**. The often-cited `dediticii` exception derives partly from **contested restoration**; never reproduce it as an intact ancient phrase without marking uncertainty. [W21-P04–P05]
- The Alexandrian restriction language sorts Egyptian `agroikoi` (rural people) from categories permitted because they **supply needed goods/services**. Occupational classification is a condition for *urban access*, **not professional alchemical initiation**. The order's rhetoric disparages migrants and must not be adopted as our neutral ethnographic description.
- The arch's erased **Geta** name is a material **rewriting**, not a manuscript copying error: original letter-fixing holes and changes in marble level remain. This means **an attempt at erasure can itself become evidence**. [W21-A01–A03]
- Clement's `Hermes` is a religious/textual attribution inside his reported Egyptian priestly learning. **It is not itself evidence the named author 'Hermes Trismegistus' personally produced a surviving set of 42 original Egyptian books**, or that a `pastophoros` was certified a goldmaking Alchemist. [W21-C01]
- Greek/Latin official texts, hieroglyphic cult writing and specialized medical vocabulary represent **different literacies**, not one intentionally encrypted chemical code.

## 04 — SECRECY, VALUE, INSTITUTION, FEAR AND HAZARD
| Candidate cause | This window's evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| State control of recognized status | 212/213 citizenship decree, copied c.215 | **Legal station is conferred**, not material skill |
| State control of movement | c.215 Egyptian-rural expulsion/restriction order | **Place-based access restricted** despite broader citizenship change |
| Profession-specific exemption | River boatmen, pig traders, reed suppliers excused in order | **Utility-based occupational carve-outs**, not craft guild initiation |
| Hierarchical temple curriculum | Clement lists priestly offices and medical books | **Reported learning distribution**, not an extant original rule or Alchemist exam |
| Politically motivated textual erasure | Geta name cut away after 211 | **Public memory targeted**; not secret chemistry or original book lost by accident |
| Philosophical exclusion | Dio reports Aristotelian professors' benefits removed | Retrospective interpretation, **not a ban on Alchemists** |
| Persecution for religion | 203 Christian martyrdoms; disputed imperial general-edict claim | Cannot attribute alchemical secrecy to a poorly attested religion law |
| Profit, hazard, initiation | No original 201–220 recipe or licensing syllabus | **UNVERIFIED** |

## 05 — PERCEPTION: maker, recipient and reader
**Free resident:** a legal transformation of citizenship may change rights and tax relationships; **what particular rights followed** for each group is contested. Being a new Roman citizen does not reveal their workshop skills.
**Egyptian rural migrant:** in an original-period order, the state may deny residence in Alexandria, while certain supply occupations are excepted; the order speaks from imperial administrative interests, **not the perspective of displaced Egyptians**.
**Monument reader:** after 211, sees only surviving revised titles where Geta was once displayed. A modern epigrapher can read marks left by the effort to delete him.
**Temple literary reader:** hears Clement's sophisticated portrait of prescribed specialist books; cannot infer every Egyptian temple actually owned his exact scheme.
**Roman intellectual:** according to Dio, Alexandrian Aristotelians lose privileges. What social consequences followed and how long remain undocumented by a dated period record.

## 06 — STATION: what is attested?
- **Original materially attested:** Roman emperor as **legal grantor**; provincial **scribal transmission**; differentiated categories of civic status and urban workers; **Geta** erased from **203 arch** after 211; patronizing Roman state.
- **Reported intellectual stations:** Egyptian priestly specialists and medical `pastophoroi` in Clement's learned secondhand description; Alexandrian Aristotelian philosophers with privileges in Dio's Roman history.
- **NOT ATTESTED:** a first-hand **201–220 CE individual office titled Alchemist**, temple license to make gold, a contemporary gold-transmutation result, apprentice ledger, Greek-Egyptian secret-recipe code or a direct China-to-Egypt teacher.

## 07 — LINEAGE AND TRANSMISSION: real versus imaginary chains
1. **Latin legal decree 212/213 → Greek `P.Giss. 40` copy c.215 → modern catalog**: documented **translation and administration** with textual loss and contested restorations.
2. **203 arch → after 211 physical name erasure → surviving altered monument**: material evidence of **political forgetting that could not eliminate all traces**.
3. **Egyptian temple professional ideals → Clement's narrative c.198–203 → later Christian and Hermetic readers**: documented **literary description**, NOT the 42 physical books nor a demonstrable initiation genealogy.
4. **Alexandrian philosophical institutions → Dio's early third-century-history narrative about 215/216 punitive action**: retrospective status report, not original philosopher expulsion list.
5. **Earlier Pseudo-Democritus + later Zosimos/Maria**: literary reception spans intervals and cannot be filled with a fictitious 212 chemical worker.

## 09 — HERE: what the stack shows and cannot show
**Shows:** original-period written evidence for an empire-wide **expansion of legal citizenship** and a roughly contemporaneous **place-based exclusion** of Egyptian rural migrants, with exceptions tied to needed occupations. A physical monumental inscription was **rewritten** after murder, yet could not erase its own traces. A learned Alexandrian Christian near the turn of the century describes a hierarchical temple **text-based knowledge station**, including medical books.
**Does NOT show:** that citizenship automatically granted Alexandria residence, that *everyone* became citizen regardless of free/slave status, that the `dediticii` reading is intact, that Clement physically examined 42 surviving volumes, that temple medicine was licensed alchemical goldmaking, or that any persecution in 202/215 targeted a professional Alchemist.
**Unknown:** precise social impact of citizenship on different Egyptian populations, reconstructed wording and exceptions, actual on-the-ground compliance with the 215 order, real professional knowledge of Alexandrian Egyptian rural workers, existence/contents of the forty-two alleged books, and early alchemist's precise institutional date.

## 10 — THE OUTSIDE: pressure → station response → remainder
| Pressure | Station touched | Documented or reported response | Unresolved remainder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Need for universalizing citizenship / fiscal sovereignty | Emperor → free subjects | **212/213 citizenship grant**, c.215 Greek copy | Dated application and debated exclusion classes |
| Governance of dense Alexandria and rural migration | Egyptians, traders, rural workers | **c.215 exclusion order** with utility-driven trade exceptions | Actual deportations, full population affected |
| Violent dynastic struggle | Geta's imperial status | **Post-211 erased monumental name** on 203 arch | How effectively memory was suppressed elsewhere |
| Defense and classification of learned temple knowledge | Clement's literary Egyptian priestly offices | 36 + 6 medical Hermes books **in his report** | Authentic Egyptian original curricula and hands-on technique |
| Roman philosopher–emperor antagonism | Alexandria Aristotelians | Dio reports lost public meals and privileges | Independent administrative record absent |
| Religious polarization | Christian converts and provincial officials | Actual 203 martyrdoms; supposed blanket law disputed | Local versus empire-wide enforcement and motive |

### Most profound historical result
**The same institution may make a person legally eligible to belong to the empire and restrict that person's ability to enter or remain in a city. Neither operation tells us whether the person can perform a particular craft. A public inscription can revoke a person's name while leaving physical traces of that revocation.**

The important separation for the Alchemist is now **capacity ≠ citizenship ≠ local permission ≠ official recognition ≠ interpretive authority**. A temple's described medical curriculum is a **serious lead about knowledge custody**, but it is not an alchemical station proved by this window.
