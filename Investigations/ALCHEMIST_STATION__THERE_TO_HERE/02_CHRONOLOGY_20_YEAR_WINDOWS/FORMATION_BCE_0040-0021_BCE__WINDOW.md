# Window 09 — 40–21 BCE: Conquest, Translation of Authority and the Return of Liu Xiang

**Status:** FIRST SOURCE-GROUNDED TEN-LAYER PASS; specialist epigraphic study, Greek primary testimony and Han annals examined at key passages. Precise manuscript and administrative reconstruction remains OPEN.  
**Interval:** 40–21 BCE (20 years, inclusive).  
**Independent traditions:** Ptolemaic-to-Roman Egypt / Western Han China. **No demonstrated direct connection of alchemical teacher lineages.**  
**Main test:** Does a knowledge-bearing station disappear, change its sponsor, change its language, or survive in another form when an outside institution takes control?

## 01 — THERE: historical conditions
### Egypt and Rome
- **31 BCE:** Octavian defeats Antony and Cleopatra's alliance at Actium.
- **30 BCE:** Cleopatra VII and Antony die; Octavian annexes Egypt and places it under a Roman prefect. The Ptolemaic *royal station ends*; the country does not cease to have temples, priests, workshops or learning. Augustus later memorializes the annexation in his *Res Gestae* 27, composed as a late retrospective self-presentation. [W09-R01–R02]
- **30 BCE:** C. Cornelius Gallus becomes first Roman prefect of Egypt; new prefectural administration interacts with long-established Egyptian and Greek cultural institutions. [W09-R02, W09-R03]
- **29 BCE:** Gallus sets up the **trilingual Philae stele**, commemorating military and political acts in **Latin, Greek and Egyptian hieroglyphs**. His claim to a new Roman office appears in a material monument, while traditional Egyptian iconographic and textual modes are reused. Crucially the Egyptian section is **not simply a literal rendering** of the Latin/Greek text. [W09-P01, W09-R03]
- **c.27/26 BCE:** Gallus falls from Roman favor and dies by suicide; the relative dating of his death as 27 or 26 BCE differs in later testimonies. The *newly instituted prefect's individual station is revocable by the Roman ruler*. [W09-R04]
- **25–24 BCE:** Strabo travels in Egypt, including a journey with prefect **Aelius Gallus** (NOT the earlier prefect Cornelius Gallus). His *Geography* was written and revised much later, although these passages claim travel experience. [W09-P02, W09-R05]

### China: same person, different capacity and official role
- **26 BCE (Emperor Cheng, Heping year 3, autumn/eighth month):** the *Hanshu*, Emperor Cheng annals and Bibliographic Treatise, records a court decision to recover and collate dispersed books: envoy **Chen Nong** searched for missing works; **Liu Xiang** (formerly Liu Gengsheng) was commissioned to collate *classics, philosophical masters and poetry*. [W09-H01–H02]
- **Division of textual labor:** other appointees received different categories: **Ren Hong** military writings; **Yin Xian** divination/calculation texts; **physician Li Zhuguo** *fangji* texts of recipes/medical/technical arts. Do **not** attribute direct editorial responsibility for *fangji* to Liu Xiang solely because he previously claimed knowledge of gold-making. [W09-H02]
- Liu Xiang's earlier **failed gold-making commission under Emperor Xuan** belongs to Window 08. His new scholarly role in 26 BCE shows that the *same person could be credited with textual competence after an earlier failed technical claim*. **No evidence proves that the failure itself caused the later role or that he now proved transmutation.** [W08-P01, W09-H01–H02]

## 02 — TECHNIQUE, CAPACITY, MATERIAL TEST
**Egypt:** Gallus's stele is real skilled carved inscription and state/temple visual technology, but not gold-making. The conquered country maintained substantial agriculture, crafts, Nile engineering and temples; site-by-site continuity of specialized workshop training requires dated papyri and excavations. **No person is securely attested performing a novel alchemical transmutation in 40–21 BCE.**

**Strabo:** the Greek writer describes the workings of Alexandria's Mouseion, a scholarly community with common property and a priest, not a named metal-transmutation laboratory. He describes visits to Heliopolis, where past learned priests were associated with philosophy/astronomy; the older local learned organization no longer appeared to him. Do not infer all practical astronomy or ritual capability vanished from Egypt. [W09-P02]

**China:** collation involves collecting texts, comparing variants, grouping categories, drawing up summaries and advising court. This is expertise *in textual transmission*, not empirical confirmation of the *Hongbao* recipe from the earlier gold-making case. The records classify *fangji* as a separate area of textual knowledge; their specific contents and experimental validity require independent evidence. [W09-H02]

## 03 — LANGUAGE, SYMBOL AND ALLEGORY
1. **Gallus / Philae, 29 BCE:** three scripts, one royal-roman governing claim, with different cultural inflections. Latin and Greek versions foreground a Roman military achievement; Egyptian hieroglyphs adapt an indigenous monumental language and differ in content. **Different audience-specific meanings are evidenced; secret coded reagent instructions are not.** [W09-P01]
2. **Strabo, Alexandria:** *Geography* 17.1.8 says the Mouseion priest was formerly appointed by the kings but is now appointed by 'Caesar' (Augustus). A single office persists with a new authorizer. *Caesar* should not be mistaken for the assassinated Julius Caesar in this passage. [W09-P02]
3. **Strabo, Heliopolis:** *Geography* 17.1.29 uses Greek `μυστικούς` ('secretive' in context), `δυσμεταδότους` ('reluctant/difficult to share'), and `ἀπεκρύψαντο` ('they concealed') about **priests in the reported ancient Plato/Eudoxus story**. This is not Strabo's first-hand witnessing of an alchemical prohibition. It concerns **astronomy and philosophical instruction** and is presented in a Greek narrative of what earlier scholars learned. **The reported ancient master-pupil story needs separate source criticism.** [W09-P02]
4. **China:** `校中祕書` / `秘府` refer to imperial collections of internal/guarded texts; `校` is collation, not a spell or coded furnace act. `方技` designates a class of technical/medical arts titles. The category creates a *bibliographic* station of expertise, not necessarily a practicing goldmaker. [W09-H01–H02]
5. **Cleopatra name-watch:** nothing about Egyptian conquest validates the *later* Greek alchemical compositions attributed to a 'Cleopatra' as authored by Queen Cleopatra VII. The distinction from Window 08 persists.

## 04 — SECRECY, ACCESS, VALUE, CAPACITY, DANGER
| Secrecy mechanism | Source-grounded evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional control | Augustus appoints new prefect; according to Strabo he now appoints the Mouseion's head priest | **AUTHORITY TRANSFER DOCUMENTED**; no institution-wide alchemical secrecy demonstrated |
| Public/multilingual representation | 29 BCE Philae inscription in three scripts with significant version differences | **PUBLIC POLITICAL ADAPTATION**, not alchemical encryption |
| Prestige and interpretative exclusion | Strabo depicts earlier Egyptian astronomer-priests as slow to impart knowledge and attributes some Greek learning to translated priestly records | **LATER GREEK NARRATIVE** about earlier learned priests; motive and reality unverified |
| Persecution | Cornelius Gallus disgraced c.27/26 due to conduct and claims to glory in later Roman narrative | **POLITICAL SANCTION**, no general anti-alchemy purge |
| Economic value | Roman prefectural rule, taxes and Egyptian production | **STATE ECONOMIC INTEREST**, no demonstrated chemical secrecy |
| Capacity threshold | Strabo's historical narrative of instruction/translation and contemporary Han book comparison | **KNOWLEDGE-TRANSFER PROBLEM**; no surviving technical apprenticeships verified |
| Danger | Political danger to new appointee; no chemical-safety motive for restriction attested | **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** of hazard-based alchemical secrecy |

## 05 — PERCEPTION
- **Roman ruler:** Augustus's own *Res Gestae* frames 30 BCE annexation as an achievement. A political justification is not a neutral material history. [W09-R01]
- **New prefect:** Gallus uses multilingual monumentality to publicize a new governing identity; later Roman accounts portray self-advertisement as reason for disgrace. Contemporary stone and later historians should be treated as different source layers. [W09-P01,W09-R04]
- **Greek traveler Strabo:** contrasts Alexandria's functioning learned Mouseion with the older Heliopolis astronomy school that his visit did not encounter, while narrating long-past priestly secrecy. He is an observer with his own cultural and imperial standpoint. [W09-P02, W09-R05]
- **Han emperor Cheng:** values recoverable textual heritage and commission-based professional classification; evidence of personal alchemical belief in Liu's old formula is absent. [W09-H01–H02]

## 06 — STATION
**Actual ancient positions recognized in surviving witnesses:**
- Egyptian monarchic station **terminated** when Rome annexed Egypt (30 BCE).
- First Roman prefect of Egypt: **Cornelius Gallus** (30 BCE); its personal occupant later deprived of legitimacy (c.27/26 BCE).
- **Mouseion head priest** continues but appointing authority passes Ptolemaic king → Caesar/Augustus (attested in Strabo's later text describing Augustan Egypt).
- Temple priests and learned astronomers: Strabo sees ritual personnel at Heliopolis, does not find the older school there; he does not prove universal elimination of Egyptian learning.
- **Han literary collator Liu Xiang** assigned texts in 26 BCE; distinct officials collate other categories of technical/medical writing.

**No source here securely identifies the separate social office or title of Alchemist in Roman Egypt.**

## 07 — PEOPLE, CONNECTIONS, LINEAGE
- **Cleopatra VII → Octavian/Augustus → Cornelius Gallus → Aelius Gallus:** succession of **authority over Egypt**, not a genealogy of individual craft teachers.
- **Ptolemaic kings → Mouseion priest → Caesar:** documented *continuation of office under a different appointer*, not proof of a transferred chemical secret.
- **Heliopolis astronomer-priests → Greek students/translators (as Strabo tells it):** reported *literary narrative of knowledge movement* with significant historical uncertainty. Distinguish from contemporary Strabo's visit.
- **Liu Gengsheng/Xiang → failed Shangfang commission (earlier) → 26 BCE scholarly book collation:** *same individual across different recognized capacities*; neither event proves the other caused it.
- **Han book collation labor** differentiates expert roles: Liu Xiang classics/poetry/philosophers, Ren Hong military, Yin Xian numerical/divinatory, Li Zhuguo *fangji*. **No one should be called a licensed alchemist simply for editing books about technical arts**.

## 09 — HERE: what actually crosses?
**Egypt:** sovereign kingship disappears. Some learned and ritual stations continue, but now answer to a different authority. Official multilingual monumental language adapts quickly to new status; different texts can inhabit the same stone without saying identical things. A classical writer reports both the survival of scholarly institution in Alexandria and perceived absence of one particular old school at Heliopolis.

**China:** a previously named goldmaking claimant is later entrusted with a different role in textual heritage. His *later* intellectual authority does not validate the goldmaking he had failed to deliver. **Textual skill, craft skill and political recognition are three separable capacities.**

**Most profound working finding:** **What gives a station legitimacy can move from the original patron to a new institution without a corresponding transfer of demonstrable technical mastery.** Likewise a person may lose one claim to exceptional ability and later occupy a recognized station for a different ability.

**Unknown:** whether any early Roman conquest destroyed a specific Egyptian alchemical workshop, whether a Greek alchemical recipe passed through the Mouseion after 30 BCE, or whether Han editorial categories preserved Liu's supposed goldmaking book.

## Remainder — next source tests

2. Review actual 30–21 BCE papyri documenting changes to temple, workshop, mineral and dye production.
3. Verify source lineage of Strabo's story about Plato/Eudoxus and Heliopolis; identify what the reporter observed personally and what he repeated.
4. Obtain *Hanshu* 10 Chengdi annals and 30 *Yiwenzhi* editions, and separate LIU XIANG's authorized classics/text collation from LI ZHUGUO's *fangji* task. Identify if Huainan *Hongbao* book appears in surviving categories.
