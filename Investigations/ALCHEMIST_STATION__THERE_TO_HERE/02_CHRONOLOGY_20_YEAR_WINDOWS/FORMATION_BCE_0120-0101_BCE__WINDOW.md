# Window 05 — 120–101 BCE: Court Recognition, Persecution, Failed Methods and Sealed Rites

**Status:** SOURCE-GROUNDED HISTORICAL PASS; PHILOLOGICAL AND CHRONOLOGICAL QUESTIONS OPEN.  
**Time:** exactly 120–101 BCE inclusive.  
**Independent contexts:** Han China, Ptolemaic Egypt. There is no demonstrated exchange of an alchemical lineage.  
**Central question:** What happens when the court creates an exceptional station for transformative expertise, then subjects the practitioner to verification, possible punishment, secrecy, and competition?

## 01 — THERE: inherited conditions
### China — court after Li Shaojun
Emperor Wu continued recruiting specialists of methods (*fangshi*, 方士), promising contact with immortal beings, longevity, creation of gold and other practical or ritual results. The *Shiji* reports many such specialists circulating among patrons; the emperor supported ritual innovations while often losing patience when promised results did not appear. These are reported *court actions and perceptions*, not proof that gold-making or immortality actually worked. [W05-P01, W05-S01]

**Chronology:** Shaoweng's audience and execution precede Luan Da. Their exact date must be derived from neighboring regnal sequences in critical annotations rather than guessed. Luan Da's rise in **113 BCE** and execution **usually dated to 112 BCE** are later narrated as events within the present window. Emperor Wu's *feng/shan* ceremony in **110 BCE** is a separately dated act of state ritual. [W05-P01–P02, W05-S01]

### Egypt — Ptolemaic institutional stations (no linked Chinese transmission)
Cleopatra III's dynastic regime moved from ruling with Ptolemy IX after 116 BCE to replacing him with Ptolemy X in 107 BCE; she served personally in the eponymous priesthood of the royal cult c.105/104 BCE, and died in 101 BCE. The change in who was able to hold the office is **documented institutional station change**, not evidence of an Egyptian alchemist at this date. [W05-E01–E03]

## 02 — TECHNIQUE: material promise vs verified operation
- **Shaoweng:** ritual techniques to make visible the likeness of a deceased royal consort, court reconstruction of sacred settings, and a fraud involving a silk message supposedly found inside an ox. The court reportedly recognized his handwriting; no independently verified supernatural operation is established. [W05-P01]
- **Luan Da:** claimed that gold could be produced, a breached Yellow River repaired, an immortality medicine acquired, and immortals summoned. A demonstration of little objects in apparent combat was reported; *the described motion is not an independently documented method for transmuting gold*. [W05-P01]
- **Material pressure:** the emperor's concern over a destructive Yellow River breach and disappointment that gold conversion had not occurred helped motivate a patronage offer. [W05-P01]
- **Gongsun Qing:** omens, accounts of traces left by supernatural beings, and appeals to rituals and ancient texts; **not a demonstrated material gold-making operation**. [W05-P01]
- **Egypt:** royal cult documentation and ongoing crafts; no identified alchemical technical manuscript securely dated 120–101 BCE from the reviewed evidence. [W05-E01–E03]

## 03 — LANGUAGE AND ALLEGORY: who authorizes the claimed capacity?
| Source wording / image | Historical function | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **方士** (*fangshi*) | General category of specialists in methods | Not a single professionally licensed occupation 'Alchemist' |
| **方** (*fang*) | The arts or methods claimed, performed or not yet fully taught | Not always an extant recipe or encoded formula |
| **同師** (*tong shi*) | Shaoweng and Luan Da reportedly studied under one master | Reported master link, not independent documentation of an extant school |
| **黃金可成** | Luan Da invokes promise that gold can be made | No successful element transmutation demonstrated |
| **方士皆奄口** (textual variant also rendered 掩口) | Luan Da says experts would close their mouths from fear | Bargaining utterance does not prove all practitioners sincerely feared persecution |
| **客禮 / 信印** | Luan Da demands guest treatment and authority seals to legitimize his mediation | Not a demonstrated chemical qualification |
| **禁方** (*jin fang*) | Secret/restricted arts that new practitioners claimed to possess following Luan's elevation | Not evidence of a single technical recipe or a unified guild |
| **書祕 / 其事皆禁** | Sealed/secret writing and ceremony at Mount Tai in 110 BCE | **Actual court ritual secrecy**, not proof of chemically encrypted alchemical text |
| **神人 / 仙人** | Claimed spiritual interlocutors whose nonappearance can be reinterpreted | Not names of chemical reagents absent evidence |

**Translation caveat:** original Chapter 28 has 奄口 in one witness and Chapter 12 has 掩口 in another; compare editions. Some online English editions call the apparent small-item demonstration 'chess-piece banners'; Chinese texts use 鬬棋; do not force a specific apparatus from an ambiguous term. [W05-P01, W05-P02]

## 04 — SECRECY, ACCESS, PERSECUTION AND CAPACITY
**We now have direct narrative evidence for a strategic persecution claim.**
1. Shaoweng was made an honored general but, after his manufactured ox-message was recognized, was reportedly **executed**, and the emperor kept the matter secret: 於是誅文成將軍，隱之. [W05-P01]
2. In front of Emperor Wu, **Luan Da explicitly invoked Shaoweng's fate** as a reason other *fangshi* would not speak of their methods: 然臣恐效文成，則方士皆奄口，惡敢言方哉. [W05-P01]
3. The emperor denied the execution, offering a claim of death from horse liver instead. This is **royal suppression of politically sensitive information** in the narrative, **not proof of an anti-alchemy law**. [W05-P01]
4. Luan negotiated public office, guest status, imperial kinship, seals and privileges as preconditions for contacting purported immortals. **Restriction of access creates leverage**. [W05-P01]
5. Later Luan was followed secretly and executed when his promises failed (c.112 BCE). [W05-P01]
6. In 110 BCE the emperor's Mount Tai rites placed writings under the altar and kept aspects of performance secret—**institutional ritual secrecy**, not a universal hidden laboratory. [W05-P01]

**Testing the six mechanisms:**
- **Institutional control:** strong evidence of control of titles, rites, seals, public narrative, official favor and penalties.
- **Fear of persecution:** explicit *reported rhetorical invocation* of execution's chilling effect; no evidence of systematic prosecution of scientifically competent transmuters.
- **Value:** very strong—gold, public works, longevity, court favors, princess and elite titles.
- **Competence:** emperor tested a small art and later used agents to audit conduct, yet apparent proof did not establish transmutation.
- **Hazard:** executions are real political danger *in the narrative*, not evidence that cinnabar chemistry was safeguarded against toxicity.
- **Prestige/authority:** exceptionally strong—public honor, many seals, rank, estate, elite intermarriage, and other experts claiming secret arts after recognition.
- **Loss/opacity:** methods themselves not extant; reported shared master remains unverifiable.

## 05 — PERCEPTION
**Court's perspective:** claims about immortality, rare arts, gold, river repair, and spirits could justify extraordinary resources; failure could convert a favored expert into a dangerous deceiver.

**Rival practitioners:** Shaoweng/Luan's purported common training acted as an authority claim. Others on the coast claimed they too held restricted arts after seeing Luan's rise.

**Historian's perspective:** the *Shiji* repeatedly makes explicit the mismatch between claims and demonstrable results. It shows Gongsun Qing remaining effective at supplying stories of signs and delayed verification even after publicized failures.

**Limit:** the textual voice is retrospective and crafted; not an audio transcript of court dialogue. Chapters 28 and 12 overlap and cannot be counted as independent witnesses.

## 06 — STATION: what was actually conferred and removed?
### Shaoweng
- Presented as a *fangshi*, appointed **General of Accomplished Literature** (文成將軍), rewarded and received as a guest.
- Died by imperial order after a forged-sign episode, with official concealment. [W05-P01]

### Luan Da
- Reported former royal workshop director (*shangfang* 尚方) at Jiaodong and pupil of the same master as Shaoweng.
- Presented to the emperor via court family and Marquis of Lecheng.
- Named **General of Five Advantages** (五利將軍), received several military/spiritual titles and seals, title of marquis, an estate, attendants, and an imperial marriage.
- Demonstrably **granted a powerful official status within the narrative**. This is stronger proof of *station recognized by others* than a standalone secret recipe.
- Executed after the emperor used agents to check his supposed journey to his master. [W05-P01]

### Gongsun Qing
- Obtained audience through an allegedly inherited bamboo text and Yellow Emperor legend; received a lesser official position and survived by postponing proof.
- No secure evidence of successful alchemical metallurgy. [W05-P01]

**Conclusion:** there is evidence that the state could give specialized method practitioners striking official standing. This is **not** proof of one distinct formal office called Alchemist across China.

## 07 — PEOPLE, PATRONS AND LINEAGE
- **Shaoweng ↔ Luan Da:** source reports an actual common master (同師). Independent verification of teacher identity, taught content and direct instruction remains absent; **reported person-to-person educational claim**, a better lineage lead than mere pseudonymity.
- **Luan Da → emperor:** direct patronage, titles, gifts, proof demands and execution as narrated.
- **Marquis of Lecheng / Jiaodong queen → Luan Da:** documented *social referral within account*, showing how a specialist gained access.
- **Gongsun Qing → Shengong 申公 (claimed):** a transmission of Yellow Emperor knowledge alleged through an earlier authority and a written text; the original chain not verified.
- **Coastal fangshi:** many reportedly asserted possession of secret methods following Luan's success. These are **imitation and competition**, not proven pupils.

## 08 — TEXTS THAT MANIFESTED FROM THE STATION
| Text/inscription | Attributed origin or setting | Witness and dating | Warrant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alleged silk writing in ox | Shaoweng claimed strange omen | Recounted in *Shiji* 28; **no extant silk recovered** | Deliberately created textual prestige / alleged deception, not a preserved revelation |
| Luan Da's 'master's secret arts' | Supposed teacher at sea, 113 BCE | Only report in historical narrative | Authority claim; no surviving instructions |
| Gongsun Qing's bamboo-slip Yellow Emperor text | Presented at court in narrated episode | Only transmitted passage; not original slip | A text could offer legitimacy and ancient lineage |
| **110 BCE secret jade tablets** | Emperor Wu's *feng/shan* ritual | *Shiji* narration; tablets' wording **not preserved** | Deliberate *ritual* secrecy, not verified chemical allegory |
| *Shiji* 28 封禪書 | Sima Qian historical composition late second/early first century BCE | Later transmitted editions; closest major narrative source | Criticism, patronage, titles, results, secrecy, ritual |
| *Shiji* 12 孝武本紀 | Later replacement of lost original annals, largely drawn from *Shiji* 28 | Later reconstruction/commentary tradition | **Not independent eyewitness**; cross-check only |
| *Hanshu* ritual treatises, *Hou Hanshu* fangshu biographies | Later retrospective historical compilations | First–second century CE and later | Later reception and labels, not immediate contemporary alchemical manual |

## 09 — HERE: Station transformation and remainder
1. **Ascent:** claims plus royal patronage produce concrete seals, office, wealth and recognition.
2. **Boundary:** the institution now requires some acceptable performance and may secretly investigate the specialist.
3. **Dissolution:** failure and deception may destroy personal official standing and even the practitioner.
4. **Continuation:** emperor and later colleagues continue exploring extraordinary arts; practitioners adapt stories and seek other forms of credibility rather than the entire domain ending.
5. **Language:** claimed master's restricted arts, seals, spirit mediation, and prestige create a vocabulary of legitimate access; **deliberate coded chemical recipe not demonstrated**.
6. **Remainder:** actual formulae, devices, master identity and whether any legitimate material innovations emerged are unknown.

### Thesis supported but bounded
**Recognized station may be created by a patron's investment in claims and removed by that patron's changed perception of the claims, independently of established material mastery.** This is a Han specialist case, not the genealogical origin of Western alchemy.

## 10 — THE OUTSIDE: pressure → station response
| Pressure | Concrete witness | Response | What survives / what breaks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imperial desire for longevity | Emperor calls practitioners, builds ritual sites | Offices, seals, material rewards | Specialist networks grow |
| Material inability to make gold | Court remains dissatisfied; new applicants promise results | Further sponsorship alongside scrutiny | Belief in possibility, not proof |
| Flooded Yellow River | Demand for practical river repair accompanies spiritual promises | Luan Da offers comprehensive solution | No demonstrated flood repair by Luan |
| Detection of fraud | Ox writing and monitoring of Luan | Executions and official concealment | Individuals lose station; demand persists |
| Threat of losing access to secrets | Luan uses fear that experts will fall silent | Patron elevates envoy's authority | Secrecy claim becomes bargaining leverage |
| Scholarly competition | Confucian ritual experts disagree with fangshi | Emperor dismisses certain scholarly advisers | Court determines which experts count |
| Imperial ritual sovereignty 110 BCE | Secret jade text and limited attendance | Royal controls witness/publicity | Ritual secrecy, no proved recipe encryption |
| **Egypt, 116–101 BCE** | Cleopatra III changes royal/co-regent and eponymous priestly role | New station bearer and unprecedented role occupancy | Demonstrable priestly/royal change; **not** Egyptian alchemical birth |

## Independent Egypt control
The Ptolemaic royal court witnesses **116 BCE succession**, **107 BCE replacement** of Ptolemy IX with Ptolemy X, **c.105/104 BCE** Cleopatra III's personal taking of the eponymous priesthood, and **101 BCE death**. These are actual institutional station changes within the Greek/Egyptian court environment, though no direct alchemical practitioner is attested. [W05-E01–E03]

## Next questions
- Did the court shift from verbal prestige to requirements of reproducible demonstration, or was scrutiny ad hoc and political?
- Did executions **really cause technical secrecy**, or was risk used strategically by applicants to secure privileges?
- Did reported common teacher transmit an actual technique? Can any independent manuscript identify or corroborate the relationship?
- How do the Egyptian and Chinese examples differ in whose capacity a station protects?
- Where can we find contemporaneous hands-on procedures, rather than an elite historian's account of their alleged results?
