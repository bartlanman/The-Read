# Window 28 — 341–360 CE: The Protected Geometer and the Prohibited Astrologer

**Status:** TEN-LAYER SOURCE-CRITICAL DEEP PASS (2026-10-18), researched legal text, language, material and Outside-pressure evidence; **NOT A CROSSING-VERIFIED ALCHEMIST STATION**. **Verdict: NO SECURE NAMED 341–360 PROFESSIONAL ALCHEMIST OR EVIDENCED ALCHEMY-SPECIFIC PERSECUTION.** Rome protects teachers of applied measurement while issuing threats against ritual divination. **A named specialist's function and audience matter more than modern resemblance between words.**

**Sources and preparation:** [W28 source register](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0341-0360__SOURCE_REGISTER.md) · [language/technique/secrecy](../09_LANGUAGE_TECHNIQUE_AND_ALLEGORY/CE_0341-0360__GEOMETRAS_MATHEMATICI_AND_LEGAL_ACCESS.md) · [ST-095–ST-099](../11_STATION_TRANSFORMATION_REGISTER/REGISTER.md). Prior [Window 27](CE_0321-0340_CE__WINDOW.md). Place/time Roman Empire, with direct ancient craft object from **Alexandria**; literary alchemical genealogy kept independent until documentary bridge.

## 01 — THERE: actual late Roman conditions and dates

- **341 — CTh XVI.10.2:** a law to **Madalianus** states `Cesset superstitio, sacrificiorum aboleatur insania`, demanding cessation of sacrifice and threatening legal sanction. **341 is the receipt/consular date**, with exact day/month not secured. Normative statute, not an original 341 execution docket, and not an alchemy book prohibition. [W28-T02]
- **1 November 346 [or critically corrected 342] — CTh XVI.10.3:** imperial order that selected temples **outside city walls**, tied to games and public festivals, remain intact (`intactae incorruptaeque`). Its preservation of certain buildings complicates any simplistic image of universal sanctuary destruction. **Specific original-year attribution CONTESTED.** [W28-T03]
- **6 July 344 — CTh XIII.4.3**, under **Constantius II and Constans**, directs policy encouraging **mechanics, geometers, architects and aqueduct leveling** and exempts qualified trainers. Distinguish **the enacted instruction (344 as subscribed)** from **physical copy in the Theodosian compilation 438 and later manuscript editions**. Training program is **prescribed**, no identifiable class/person shown teaching it. [W28-T01]
- **1 December 346 [or 354?] — CTh XVI.10.4:** orders temples closed and sacrifice prohibited, with death/confiscation threats; official address to Taurus. **CONTESTED original date** and practical enforcement. Keep alongside, rather than erase, the earlier/later protection of some festival buildings. [W28-T03]
- **23 November 353 — CTh XVI.10.5:** nocturnal sacrifices are outlawed; text attributes previous permission to the rebel Magnentius. That is the **imperial speaker's retrospective assertion**, not a recovered original Magnentius permission edict. [W28-T04]
- **19 February 356 — CTh XVI.10.6:** threatened **capital punishment** against demonstrated sacrificial practices or honoring images. A prescribed penalty does **not** demonstrate one identified local execution or explain secrecy by an alchemical specialist. [W28-T04]
- **25 January 357 — CTh IX.16.4:** Constantius II (and Julian Caesar in heading) prohibits consulting **haruspices** (entrail-readers), **`mathematici`** (here ASTROLOGERS), **harioli** (soothsayers) and other `Chaldaei`, `magi`, `malefici`. Defines the issue as illicit inquiry into the future (`divinandi curiositas`), not teaching of structural geometry. [W28-T05]
- **4 December 357 [?] — CTh IX.16.5:** condemned `magicis artibus` said to `elementa turbare` and harm individuals. The **law's allegation is not independent proof** of disturbing physical nature, changing elemental identity or manufacturing gold. Subscription's exact chronology has editorial uncertainty. [W28-T05]
- **5 July 358 — CTh IX.16.6:** coerces alleged sorcerers/diviners especially in the political sphere around **the imperial court**, announcing torture even in ways meant to overcome rank privilege. Court-centered threat matters; legal text is not evidence that a named Egyptian chemical writer was convicted. [W28-T05]
- **351–355 (original material):** [British Museum B.13591](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_B-13591) **Alexandria copper-alloy nummus**, **1.41 g, 18 mm**, portraying Constantius **Gallus** Caesar; museum also lists Constantius II as ruling authority. This is ancient coin minting, not bronze-to-gold conversion. [W28-O01]
- **No secure dated Alchemist:** Zosimos c.300, Leiden/Stockholm late 3rd/early 4th century, and later Olympiodorus attribution remain **broad/other**. **Themistius's metallic transmutation reference belongs to 367 in the NEXT window**, not to 350. [W28-S02]

## 02 — MATERIAL TECHNIQUE, INSTRUMENT, RECIPE, TEST OF CAPACITY

**344 technical work described by law:** division, cutting or marking structures, **measurement**, appropriate fabrication and **water-course surveying/leveling for aqueducts**. These are specific demonstrable *classes of operations* the legislators believe technical experts can teach. **The enactment does not contain construction diagrams**, a list of named aqueduct engineers, test results, nor a state laboratory recognized to change metals. [W28-T01]

**Actual physical object in the window:** struck Egyptian **copper-alloy nummus 351–355** (BM B.13591). The mint evidently formed, stamped and issued copper alloy. The specific worker, alloy formula and materials preparation process cannot be recovered from the museum record alone. **The object is not proof of chrysopoeia.** [W28-O01]

**Practical recipe horizon, separately marked:** Leiden X/Stockholm papyri record dyes, surface-metal operations, imitations and other techniques; broad manuscript date is not a 354 copying event. A gold appearance can follow gilding/alloying/chemical coloring without having changed a base element to gold.

**`Elementa` in the 357 law:** an accusatory phrase about magical disturbance of nature, death/spirits and life injury, **not an ancient detailed recipe to create elements**, with no reported analytical result. The conceptually modern atomic test is a caution against equating appearance and substance, not an ancient measurement supplied by the law.

## 05 — PERCEPTION: WHO THOUGHT WHAT?

**Emperor's protected technical audience (344):** state treats trained engineering and aqueduct surveying as public benefit; qualified people should teach others. No named trainee or curriculum survives within law.

**Imperial legal view (341–358):** some practices categorized as sacrifices, astrologers, diviners or `malefici` threaten political/religious order. **Accusation is not confirmation of supernatural efficacy**, a real chemical operation, or the title the accused preferred.

**Temple users:** competing instructions to preserve certain festival temples and close/restrict others show how **legal legitimacy varies by act, site and date**, without a universal demonstrated outcome.

**Money users:** 351–355 coin represents emperor/caesar and military ideology in public material, not some artisan's goldmaking station.

**Alchemical manuscript audiences:** known from commentary near c.300 and later transmission; not securely observed responding to particular 344/357 laws.

## 06 — STATION: RECOGNIZED ENGINEER, PROSCRIBED CONSULTANT, UNFOUND ALCHEMIST

**Legally protected:** `mechanicus`, `geometra`, `architectus`, water surveyor/teacher—**specific, publicly valued technical roles** with prescribed immunity conditional on skilled teaching, not attested appointment of a named 350s individual.

**Legally threatened:** divination consultants/haruspices/astrologers (`mathematici`)/`magi`/malefic practitioners and sacrificers under given legal contexts. **Different roles**, sometimes names overlap in rhetoric, but this code does not establish their work as alchemy.

**Coin issuer and craftworker:** Constantius Gallus appears on struck Alexandria money; the mint worker is unidentified and not an “Alchemist.”

**Historically identified alchemical commentator:** **Zosimos of Panopolis c.300** (older ST-085); no direct **341–360** dating or official professional status. No securely dated “Olympiodorus Alchemist” active 345.

**Result:** **NO VERIFIED Alchemist whose social station changes 341–360**. These are comparative institutional changes with no proven crossover case.

## 07 — LINEAGE, COPYING, CODE COMPILATION

1. **344 transmitted edict → 438 Theodosian code → later MSS/editions**. The legal text's issue subscription may be controlled; **original 344 document not physically present**.
2. **341–356 sacrifice/temple regulations → Theodosian code XVI.10**, with **conflicting scholarly year assignments** for paragraphs .3/.4. Multiple laws within one 438 codification **do not independently prove separate archaeological temple closures**.
3. **357/358 anti-divination edicts → Theodosian code IX.16**, and some later interpretative texts. There are statutes, **not excavated criminal convictions** of named Alexandrian specialists.
4. **351–355 nummus originally struck → excavated/collected physical object → British Museum catalog**. The coin is an independent original period artifact but does not witness the laws’ effects.
5. **Alchemical recipes and Zosimos** remain a separate earlier/broad manuscript tradition; author-to-teacher chain, witness dates and claimed expertise **not** demonstrated to enter a 357 court case.
6. Chinese traditions remain independent from Greek/Roman sources absent actual translation/citation evidence.

## 09 — HERE: WARRANTED DIFFERENCES AND REMAINDER

**What evidence shows:** The state can explicitly distinguish publicly beneficial scientific/engineering measurement with skilled teaching privileges from divinatory consultation and politically threatening ritual practice under increasingly severe statutes. A preserved coin attests the actual production of a material object by mint craft. Specific provisions may protect some festival temple buildings while restricting rituals, illustrating complex religious governance rather than a single undifferentiated ban.

**What it does NOT show:** any validated technique turning base metal into gold, physical alchemical experimental record dated 350, formally authorized 344 Alchemist, chemical repression under CTh IX.16, a named prosecuted alchemist, or direct evidence that secret/allegorical recipes arose from fear of these laws. **A legal prescription is not its enforcement**, and a later compilation is not an original contemporary inscription.

**Known unknowns:** exactly how empire-wide laws were implemented locally; number and names of protected teachers, accused diviners and penalized ritualists; exact dates of XVI.10.3/.4; whether any identifiable specialist crossed both worlds; Zosimos manuscript chronology and later commentator attribution; independent gold-transmutation capacity.

**Most profound finding:** **The state distinguished what a specialist claimed to know, what they actually did and for whom they acted—protecting skilled public measurements while prohibiting perceived dangerous prediction.** A common modern label *mathematics* can conceal entirely different contemporary social stations: 344 **`geometra`** is an engineering educator; 357 **`mathematicus`** is an astrologer within a divination statute. **The historical station rests on a social authorization relationship as well as a technique, not on modern word resemblance.**
