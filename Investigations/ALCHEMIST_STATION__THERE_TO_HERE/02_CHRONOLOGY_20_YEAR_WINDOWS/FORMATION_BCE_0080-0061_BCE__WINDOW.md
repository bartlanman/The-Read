# Window 07 — 80–61 BCE: When Sovereignty Fails, Who Keeps the Knowledge?

**Status:** SOURCED FIRST DEEP PASS, with dated primary testimonia and explicit cross-century witness controls. **Not** a reconstructed complete practitioner genealogy.  
**Window:** 80–61 BCE inclusive, exactly twenty years.  
**Independent and comparative tracks:** Ptolemaic Egypt; Roman Republic and Pontic kingdom; Western Han China. None of these is automatically the historical ancestor of the others or evidence of a single Alchemist station.

## 01 — THERE: inherited historical conditions

### Egypt: accession and external dispute
- **80 BCE:** Following the death of Ptolemy IX about the turn of 81/80 BCE, **Berenice III** assumed a short-lived queenship. **Ptolemy XI Alexander II** arrived as her husband/co-ruler, had her killed, and was himself murdered by an Alexandrian crowd after roughly 18–19 days. **Ptolemy XII Auletes** succeeded, approximately by April 80 BCE. The ancient record and later chronologies differ on details; these are not events of alchemy. [W07-E01–E02]
- **76 BCE:** Ptolemy XII was crowned at Memphis; Ptolemaic royal legitimacy depended on established Egyptian cult and priestly structures. [W07-E02]
- **65 BCE:** Roman politicians debated whether to annex Egypt. This is a threat to the *sovereign station and the right to dispose of the kingdom*. It did not result in annexation within this window. [W07-E03]
- **63 BCE:** Cicero's speech *De lege agraria* 2.41–44 disputes a purported royal will handing Egypt to Rome and attacks secretive or dubious transfer of the kingdom to appointed commissioners; this is **public political rhetoric**, not cryptic metallurgical technique. [W07-P01]

### Pontus: royal defeat and pharmacological knowledge
- **63 BCE:** Mithridates VI died amid defeat by Roman forces; his successor Pharnaces II received a restricted kingship in the Bosporan region. [W07-M01]
- **Later testimony:** Pliny (*Naturalis historia* 25.5–7, written about 77 CE) claims Mithridates curated medicinal studies and records, and that after the royal spoils passed to Pompey these materials were **translated into Latin by Pompey's freedman Lenaeus**. That is evidence for *a Roman writer's story of confiscation and translation*, with the exact date of the translation and original documents **unverified**. [W07-P02, W07-M02]
- The king's surviving fame for poisons and antidotes makes this a **comparative medical-technical station**. No evidence makes him a Greco-Egyptian transmutational Alchemist.

### China: contested administrative stations
- **74 BCE:** Emperor Zhao dies; the short-lived monarch Liu He is deposed; Emperor Xuan begins ruling while powerful regent **Huo Guang** still dominates appointments. [W07-H01]
- **68 BCE:** Huo Guang dies. **66 BCE:** members of the Huo family are accused and punished in a reported conspiracy, weakening the inherited power network while the imperial institution remains. [W07-H01]
- **During Xuan's reign (74–49 BCE)** Huan Kuan compiles or elaborates the *Yantielun*, a literary reworking of the **81 BCE salt–iron discussion**. The exact date of writing is not established within **80–61 BCE** specifically; it might belong to the next window. [W07-H02–H03]

## 02 — TECHNIQUE: actual production, claimed mastery, and material constraints
- **Han:** iron smelting, cast-metal tools and salt production are actual craft-industrial techniques under disputed state jurisdiction. The *Yantielun* communicates political arguments about expertise, cost and benefits, **not** a secret chemical recipe. Archaeological and institutional accounts distinguish blast furnaces, cast iron and manufactured products. [W07-H02–H04]
- **Pontus:** Pliny names plant remedies, effects of medicines and alleged prophylaxis against poison. This testifies to *pharmacological expertise claimed and preserved in literary tradition*, not independently recovered experiments or proven universal immunity. The king's original medicinal notes are not available for direct chemical reanalysis. [W07-P02]
- **Egypt:** royal coinage, temple crafts and priestly institutions continue; **no securely dated operative alchemical text or named transmutational practitioner** has been established in the present pass.

## 03 — LANGUAGE, TECHNIQUE, ALLEGORY AND TRANSLATION
Four distinct transformations of language occur:
1. **Royal title and succession (Egypt):** Ptolemaic priestly names and dynastic iconography define the bearer of political legitimacy, **not a secret chemical process**.
2. **Public controversy over 'hidden' power (Rome):** Cicero describes the alleged transfer of Egypt using terms including *occulte* and *furtim* (De lege agraria 2.41). **The thing concealed is political disposition of a kingdom**, not an ingredient. [W07-P01]
3. **Private medical notes crossing language (Pontus → Rome):** Pliny's *scrinium commentationum* and *in arcanis suis* describe Mithridates' personal dossier; *transferre...sermone nostro* signals the reported move into Latin. **Private custody is not equivalent to encoded pharmacology**. [W07-P02]
4. **Later public teaching of industrial controversy (China):** *Yantielun* (鹽鐵論) means *Discourses on Salt and Iron*; a late Western Han redactor recasts an earlier debate as an authoritative text. Literary dialogue is not a verbatim stenographic record or a chemical cipher. [W07-H02–H03]

**Later intellectual witness, NOT contemporary 63 BCE voice:** Pliny 25.1 argues that withholding remedies can create *authority over knowledge*, and 25.8 cautions that drawings of plants lose reliability through inaccurate copying and seasonal differences. These lines provide a highly relevant **first-century CE interpretation of secrets, capacity and representation**, not proof of Mithridates' personal motivation. [W07-P02]

## 04 — SECRECY, ACCESS, PERSECUTION, DANGER AND VALUE
| Hypothesis | Evidence | Appropriate verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Institutional custody** | Mithridates' documents reportedly in royal private collection; imperial Han industrial administration; Egyptian royal sovereignty contested | **Custody evident in later account / political records; alchemical monopoly unproven** |
| **Persecution of outsiders** | Dynastic violence, siege and punishment in Egypt/Pontus/Han | **Political danger established; not an anti-alchemy persecution program** |
| **Economic value** | Salt, iron, Egyptian revenue and medicinal knowledge implicated in political power | **Strong contextual evidence; motive for hiding individual recipes unknown** |
| **Competence threshold** | Practical iron/medicine require know-how; Pliny later warns a botanical image does not fully identify a changing plant | **Interpretation backed by later statement; no special alchemical initiation documented** |
| **Physical danger** | Toxic plants and poisons in Mithridatic medical lore | **Potential dangers real; no contemporaneous safety-secrecy rule documented** |
| **Prestige / authority** | Kingship, ancient claims over Egypt, medicinal achievements and official status | **Significant but not proof of special alchemical rank** |
| **Loss, translation, copying** | Mithridatic records are lost in original; translation known via Pliny; *Yantielun* later redaction | **Transmission filters visible; full mechanisms uncertain** |

## 05 — PERCEPTION: which audience recognizes what?
- **Alexandrian population:** violently rejected Ptolemy XI; Ptolemy XII became successor. That is a case of **political status being granted and revoked**, not an adjudication of transmutation.
- **Roman orators/senators:** disagreement over whether a claimed will and legal title would allow Rome to claim Egypt. **Authority to interpret ownership of a station** shifts outside Egypt's borders, even before Roman annexation. [W07-P01,W07-E03]
- **Pliny's Roman audience (about 77 CE):** king Mithridates appears as a collector and specialist in medicinal botany, while Rome emerges as the translator/distributor of foreign knowledge. This is a **later Roman literary perspective**, not an intact Pontic personal archive. [W07-P02,W07-M02]
- **Han literati and regency:** the aftermath of material-industry debate is framed for moral and governmental instruction; authority depends in part on who writes the history of prior decisions. [W07-H02–H03]

## 06 — STATION: was there a new 'Alchemist'?
**Documented political and professional stations:** Ptolemaic royal titles and coronation; Roman legal oratorial office; Pontic royal library/medicinal patronage; Han imperial, regental and industrial administrative stations; Huan Kuan's literary/official position.

**Not documented:** a named professional *Alchemist* during 80–61 BCE, an alchemical guild, an Egypt–Han line of instruction, or a primary gold-transmutation recipe assigned to the interval.

**Specific crossing insight:** Control of a technical collection and legitimate use of it may pass to *someone who has not performed the originating craft*, via conquest, interpretation, translation and institutional possession.

## 07 — PEOPLE AND CONNECTIONS: actual and false lineages
- **Ptolemy XI → Berenice III → Ptolemy XII:** *political* succession and violence, not technical apprenticeship. [W07-E01–E02]
- **Rome ↔ Ptolemy XII:** political contestation over ownership, legitimacy and sovereignty, not a teaching relationship. [W07-P01,W07-E03]
- **Mithridates → (reported) private medicinal records → Pompey → Lenaeus → Pliny:** **reported custody and translation chain**, with significant gaps in date and direct access. *Not* proof Lenaeus became Mithridates' personal student or that Pliny tested the formulas. [W07-P02,W07-M02]
- **81 BCE Han consultation → Huan Kuan's later compilation:** a **document-to-commentary lineage**, not an alchemist masterline. Source dating is broad: 74–49 BCE and must not be narrowed artificially. [W07-H02–H03]
- **Huo Guang → Emperor Xuan / Huo clan:** royal-administrative custody and contested appointments, no special gold-making expertise demonstrated. [W07-H01]

## 09 — HERE: warranted station transformation and remainder
**Warranted by source testimony (with date caveats):**
1. A political station can be transferred or contested *without a change in technical capability* (Egypt, Han).
2. An archive may change custodians through conquest; a later author reports it being translated, creating readers distinct from the originating king's subjects (Pontus → Rome).
3. Rewriting a practical policy debate into a durable text can make the later interpreter important to how future societies understand legitimate material production.
4. Textual opacity has multiple causes: deliberate withholding, loss, multilingual mediation, pictures that mislead, or lack of hands-on training. No single "alchemical code" explains all.

**Not established:** successful antidotal immunity under modern tests, actual alchemical metal transmutation, a private secret deliberately concealed by Mithridates to prevent harm, an identifiable Greek-Egyptian alchemist's professional station, or a demonstrable East–West practitioner lineage.

## Most profound finding
**A specialized practice can persist after a station's political disappearance because techniques are copied, seized, translated or absorbed by a rival institution. But the institution inheriting the words may not inherit the capacity to perform the technique.**

That is a **warranted conceptual inference from disparate evidence**, not a demonstrated historical origin for the Alchemist.

## Remainder and next move
- Collate Ptolemy XII accession/coronation against dated papyri rather than only later chronologies.
- Seek Mithridates original-language prescriptions or identifiable later translations; Pliny's account of Lenaeus is a later literary testimony.
- Examine how illustration, botanical description and actual apprenticeship relate to the ability to recognize a plant species in practice.
- Date Huan Kuan's *Yantielun* more tightly; do not place it in the window merely because it overlaps.
- Locate a source attesting technical transformation and a practitioner claiming a distinct station in precisely 80–61 BCE.
- Next window **60–41 BCE**: renewed Roman coercion in Egypt, Ptolemy XII's exile/restoration, transition to Cleopatra VII, and late republican knowledge/patronage controversies. No events after 61 BCE should be projected backward into this window.
