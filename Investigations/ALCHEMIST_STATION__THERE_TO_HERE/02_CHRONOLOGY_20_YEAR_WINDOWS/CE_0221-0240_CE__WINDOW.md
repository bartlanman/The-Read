# Window 22 — 221–240 CE: Julius Africanus, the Named Technical Author, and the Symbolic Recipe Key

**Status:** TEN-LAYER SOURCE-CRITICAL DEEP PASS (2026-10-18). Crucial transition from **pseudonymous Greek recipe authority** toward a **historically nameable scholar** who wrote natural/technical recipes including material-coloring methods later cited in alchemical literature. **NOT YET a proven professional station called 'Alchemist'.**
**Twenty-year interval:** 221–240 CE inclusive. **Main region:** Greek-writing Roman Near East, Egypt, and Severan imperial court; China remains an independent comparative stream.
**Strongest object:** British Library **Papyrus 2040 = P.Oxy. III 412**, third-century Greek copy of part of Africanus' *Kestoi*, original-period manuscript with **later verso will dated 276 CE**. British Library gives broad manuscript **225–276**; Kyprianos gives **225–275** for first use. **Copy might date to 221–240 but cannot be proven to do so.** [W22-P01–02]
**Core hypothesis:** The named specialist who **assembles, writes, qualifies and makes selective symbolic substitutions for technical knowledge** is closer to an emerging recognizable material-intellectual station than purely legendary founders. **He is not demonstrably a successful gold-transmuter or formally recognized alchemist.**

## 01 — THERE: historical conditions, 221–240
- **c.221/222 CE:** Sextus Julius Africanus completes a Christian *Chronography* running through Elagabalus' last year (a source offers **221**, Dufault **shortly after 222**); date of completion disputed. He is well enough known to participate in Greek Christian scholarly debates and writes letters discussing disputed biblical textual authenticity. [W22-S01–S04]
- **c.221 (some traditions 226):** an embassy by Africanus helps **Emmaus** achieve renewed Roman civic status, later called **Nicopolis**. Eusebian chronographic tradition supplies the date, not an actual contemporary original envoy receipt; historical sources disagree on year and precise emperor. **Do not treat 221 as an independently day-dated decree.** [W22-S01,W22-S04]
- **222–235:** **Severus Alexander** governs. Africanus dedicates the **`Kestoi`** (also *Cesti*, 'Embroideries') to the emperor. The corpus spans practical medicine, cosmetics, military techniques, agriculture, marvels and special effects. Exact completion year within 222–235 **unknown**; it cannot be presumed a fully stable edition published in 223. The surviving corpus's length and contents differ by later author: commonly **24 books**, but later Syncellus refers to **nine books**; not proof one historical edition always comprised a fixed number of books. [W22-S01,W22-S02]
- Africanus was involved with **scholarly patronage**: possible earlier association with king Abgar at Edessa, mission from Emmaus, and his **own claim** to have overseen a library for the emperor by the **Pantheon**. The library's precise location, administrative function and what books it contained are **not directly demonstrated by a foundation papyrus**. [W22-S01–S03]
- **225–276** (material-copy date range): in Oxyrhynchus, Egypt, the beginning/end of Africanus' *Kestoi* **Book 18** was copied on a papyrus roll. It recounts a purported 'lost' necromantic portion of Homer's *Odyssey* and evaluates its authenticity. The same roll's reverse later holds a **will dated 276**. Date overlap with this window **possible**, but exactly 225, 230 or 239 unverified. [W22-P01–P02]
- **229:** Roman historian **Cassius Dio** attains a second consulship under Severus Alexander, a comparative recognized author/official station, not the original author of material transformation manuals. [W22-S05]
- **234/235:** original Alexandrian **RPC VI 10643** type of **debased silver tetradrachm**, year **14 (L ΙΔ)** of Severus Alexander, depicts imperial name and **Helios/Selene**. Actual manufactured metallic object and public temporal claim, **not the production of gold**. [W22-O01]
- **235:** Severus Alexander is killed and **Maximinus Thrax** rises; the patron named in Africanus' dedication disappears from office. No evidence that the *Kestoi* or its author was thereby suppressed.
- **238:** several competing emperors—**Maximinus, Gordian I, Gordian II, Pupienus, Balbinus and Gordian III**—occupy or claim imperial recognition over a short period. Historians debate the exact days; locally dated Egyptian papyri and coins are especially important chronological checks. **Six imperial names does not mean six universally recognized uninterrupted individual terms.** [W22-S06]

## 02 — MATERIAL TECHNIQUE AND PRACTITIONER CAPACITY
- **Africanus' *Kestoi*:** medical recipes, cosmetics, methods affecting **hair color**, dyes, healing, military machines or stratagems, methods claiming extraordinary changes. Some **dye recipes later cited by Greek alchemical authors** suggest continuity of recipe types. **Later citations** may have undergone copying, addition or reassignment and **do not show Africanus personally carried out every procedure**. [W22-S01,W22-S02]
- **Book 7:** some text survives through military writings and mentions military/medical animal management; **Book 18 P.Oxy. III 412** *physically survives* but features a discussion of claimed Homeric necromancy, **NOT a preserved original period metal-transmutation protocol or the symbol key itself**. [W22-P01,W22-S01]
- **Natural powers / craft:** Africanus claims special ability to draw on properties of materials and natural procedures. We possess **descriptions and literary technical knowledge**, not contemporaneous apparatus bearing his name, named apprentices or a verified produced gold sample.
- **Coins:** Alexandrian debased-silver stamped metal, labeled official year 14, is demonstrably produced material but **not alchemical transmutation**. [W22-O01]
- **Later Leiden X / Stockholm papyri** are normally dated **late third to early fourth century** in modern scholarship; even though their recipe genres are relevant, **we cannot assign those physical manuscripts to 221–240**. [W22-S07]

## 03 — LANGUAGE, SYMBOLS, ALLEGORY AND FALSE COMPLETENESS
- **`Kestoi`** is Greek for 'embroideries' / elaborate woven adornment, associated by Africanus with **Aphrodite's decorated band** in Homer. The title frames the corpus as skilful, varied and pleasingly composed, not a generic laboratory guild name.
- In several transmitted recipes, **animal or plant names are NOT written in ordinary words**. They are **replaced by references to geometrical figures marked by musical signs**. An **explanatory appendix/key**, invoked by the text, is **lost**. This is positive scholarly evidence for deliberate **symbolic substitution in a technical literary artifact**; exact unavailable substitutions must **not be reconstructed as if known**. [W22-S01, pp.88–90]
- **Important caveat:** Dufault describes this as a **rudimentary encryption-like method** but **explicitly argues it was *unlikely intended as effective encryption***, because Greek education might suffice to decipher it; it could be an educated reader's **learned puzzle or display of `paideia`**, not an anti-authority secret. An absent appendix can make a once-soluble puzzle *modernly opaque*.
- **Homeric restoration claim:** Africanus presents the *Odyssey* necromancy passage in *Kestoi* Book 18 as a literary/philological supplement to what he says earlier editors/Homer left out. **That is Africanus' literary claim**, not independent proof that Homer wrote hidden lines or that a working rite existed. Physically surviving papyrus shows it circulated to readers. [W22-P01,W22-S01]
- **Scholar vs `magos`:** his technique and unusual content could evoke an educated 'learned sorcerer' stereotype, yet Africanus generally **explained marvelous techniques through nature**, not as evidence he himself claimed a licensed magical or alchemical priestly office. [W22-S01]

## 04 — SECRECY AUDIT: COMPETING EXPLANATIONS, NOT A SINGLE MOTIVE
| Hypothesis | What this window provides | Evidentiary status |
|---|---|---|
| **Deliberate symbolic substitution** | Geometry + musical signs used instead of ingredient names in transmitted *Kestoi* | **YES — textual feature described by critical scholar** |
| **Strong cryptographic exclusion** | Learned notation appears interpretable with educated Greek competence; missing key | **NOT PROVEN; Dufault suggests unlikely** |
| **Show of specialized education / prestige** | Technical references and Homeric restoration fitted to `paideia` | **Supported interpretation** |
| **Patron-protecting commercial monopoly** | Dedicated to imperial patron and court experience | Patronage plausible, **exclusive business motive unknown** |
| **Personal fear of persecution** | No actual documented Africanus anti-technical-procedure prosecution | **UNSUPPORTED** |
| **Safety protection** | Some transmitted recipes mention potentially toxic acts | **No evidence cipher invented as safety rule** |
| **Practical trained capacity** | Technical processes need materials, technique and judgment | **Textual techniques, not witnessed successful performance** |

| **Pseudonymous authority** | Genuine known named author vs his contested Homer 'recovered' verses | **Two distinct attribution questions** |

## 05 — PERCEPTION, PATRONAGE AND COMPETING READERS
**Imperial court:** a book of useful/natural military and healing recipes can confer prestige on an educated client even if elite readers question some marvels. Dedication to Severus Alexander **claims a relationship**, not necessarily a confirmed state salary or title 'Alchemist'.
**Greek educated reader:** musical and geometric references are a demonstration of literacy and intellectual play, rather than an impenetrable security technology.
**Later alchemical reader:** some dyestuff procedures appear within a later alchemical citation stream; it is risky to call all content chemical/alchemical in the original context.
**Modern historian:** original third-century papyrus confirms a *version of Book 18 circulated*, but **does not itself prove an exact recipe's effectiveness** or date of the whole 24-book work.
**Political/numismatic readers:** 234/235 coin names current emperor even though his government will fall in 235; institutional public name may outlive holder.

## 06 — THE STATION: WHAT HAS CHANGED?
**NEW POSITIVE CANDIDATE:** **Sextus Julius Africanus** is **historically nameable**, has a literary technical corpus dedicated in **222–235**, participates in diplomatic/authorial work, and **is attested by third-century papyrus circulation**. This is stronger *named technical author evidence* than the inherited master-story of Pseudo-Democritus.
**But:** Africanus was an **author/client scholar**, not demonstrably trained/titled **`chēmeutēs`**, a licensed alchemical official or personally verified gold transmuter. His surviving works are mixed-genre and their manuscript layers differ.
**Technical-dye crossing:** his recipes were later part of the material referenced by alchemical authors. This is **textual proximity to alchemy**, not proof he founded a distinct alchemical institution.
**No new securely titled contemporary professional Alchemist** in **221–240**.

## 07 — LINEAGE AND TRANSMISSION: WHAT REALLY CROSSES?
- **Africanus as author c.222–235 → 3rd-c copy of Book 18 P.Oxy III 412 → 276 re-used verso will → British Library catalog**: first historically strong **named technical-work + material manuscript** chain. The physical recto may date **after 240**.
- **Africanus → recipes quoted in later Greek alchemy**: identifiable *later citation* links with textual uncertainty; not a demonstrated named teacher–pupil chain.
- **Africanus → claim to commission/administer a Pantheon-area library**: literary self-report in his transmitted writings, not an independent surviving architectural inscription. He invokes patronage, but court relationship not independently fully confirmed.
- **Emmaus embassy in 221 or 226 → Nicopolis**: civic access/administrative role, reported by later sources, not a direct original grant found in 221.
- **Musical/geometric references → missing appendix**: once-implied cross-reference becomes unreadable to later readers, not necessarily historically secret to contemporary scholars.
- **Independent Han and Egypt/Greek lines:** no newly established technical translation or teacher.

## 09 — HERE: WHAT THE STACK SHOWS, DOES NOT SHOW AND LEAVES OPEN
**Shows:** A named early-third-century author deliberately used **symbolic notation in recipes** and operated in a court-oriented educated culture; a fragment of his work has an actual third-century Oxyrhynchus manuscript witness, with a firm **276 terminus ante quem** from reuse. This offers unusually strong evidence on the *access to text versus interpretive key* question. It also shows that court-patronized technical writing and the later institutionally recognized alchemy of Zosimos are not automatically the same category.
**Does not show:** first Alchemist's license, actual intention to hide chemicals from persecution, everyone being unable to decode his signs, living Africanus having personally performed every recipe, a functional poison or successful transmutation, or Book 18 original composed/copied 225 rather than 270.
**Unknown:** date and authorial layers of *Kestoi*, actual contents of missing appendix, recipients' ability to decode, whether original patron supported a real laboratory, whether some later alchemical citations preserve actual Africanus recipes without addition.

### Most profound result
**We have finally located a named practitioner of *technical writing* whose transmission includes demonstrable symbolic ingredient substitutions, yet the historical evidence warns us that those substitutions may have been a learned literary game, not a lock against outsiders. A missing key can make a once-accessible text look secret centuries later.**

This is a **positive third-century threshold toward the station**, not a proven date when 'the Alchemist' became a formal profession.

**Next:** 241–260 CE, a high-priority bridge toward the **later 3rd-century physical chemical recipe papyri**, changing imperial administration and competing material authority. Track Zosimos first only when there is actual independent dating.
