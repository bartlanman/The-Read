# Window 10 — Historical Source Register (20–1 BCE)
**Review:** 2026-10-18. Surviving monument vs later historian vs modern scholarship clearly separated.

## [W10-E01] The Met, Temple of Dendur origins
https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/essays/temple-of-dendur-gift-to-the-met
Diana Craig Patch: construction c.15–10 BCE, completed 10 BCE, Augustus's sponsorship, local cult under Philae influence.
**Quality note:** some dynamic Met art-result labels say '10 CE' even on pages whose curator writes '10 BCE'; independent MetKids/JSTOR collection listings use '10 B.C.' Resolve in print catalog before claiming greater precision.

## [W10-E02] The Met, Architecture and Ritual
https://www.metmuseum.org/de/essays/temple-of-dendur-architecture-and-ritual
Egyptologists Arnold/Oppenheim: rooms, sculptural and ritual layout, small *secret chamber* in rear sanctuary wall with unknown function, perhaps cult equipment. NOT chemical secrecy evidence.

## [W10-E03] The Met, Temple Cult and Decoration
https://www.metmuseum.org/de/essays/temple-of-dendur-cult-and-decoration
Egyptologist Stünkel: Augustus as pharaoh, cartouches Pharaoh/Autokrator/Kaisaros, Egyptian sacred plant and solar motifs; not proof of technical cipher.

## [W10-C01] Hong Kong University Press, Yang Yuanzheng (2022)
https://hkupress.hku.hk/image/catalog/pdf-preview/9789888805662.pdf
Intro discusses Liu Xiang/Liu Xin and **Qilüe c.5 BCE**; lost original, later reproduction in abbreviated Hanshu.

## [W10-C02] Hur-Li Lee and W.-C. Lan, Knowledge Organization 38(1), 25–42 (2011)
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/290055586_Proclaiming_Intellectual_Authority_Through_Classification_The_Case_of_the_Seven_Epitomes
Completion sometime **7–1 BCE**; classification expresses classicist intellectual hierarchies. Liu Xiang death dated differently (6 or 8 BCE); do not overstate precision.

## [W10-C03] ISKO encyclopedia, Library classification
https://www.isko.org/cyclo/library_classification
Late-Western-Han catalogs, lost originals, six classes and an introduction, reconstruction from Hanshu; no direct evidence of practicing goldmakers.

## [W10-C04] Tian and Dong, medical categories in Hanshu Yiwenzhi (2021)
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34645124/
**fangji** class comprises 医经 medical, 经方 prescriptions, 房中 bedchamber, 神仙 immortality/transcendence. Their classification reflects literature, not tested efficacy.

## [W10-H01] Hanshu, Emperor Ai annals 建平四年 (3 BCE)
https://ctext.org/han-shu/ai-di-ji/zh
`四年春，大旱。關東民傳行西王母籌，經歷郡國，西入關至京師。`
History describes drought and traveling Xiwangmu tallies/tokens; manuscript source compiled **first/second century CE**, not surviving 3 BCE original.

## [W10-H02] Hanshu, Five-Elements treatise, same phenomenon
https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=en&node=63983&show=parallel
`母告百姓，佩此書者不死。不信我言，視門樞下，當有白髮。`
History reports publicly distributed protective writing, local gatherings, songs/dance, alarm. **This and the annals are parts of the SAME compiled history**, not two independent eyewitness records. An immortality claim is not an actual chemical finding.

## [W10-P03] Hanshu Yiwenzhi, Book 30
https://ctext.org/han-shu/yi-wen-zhi
First/second-century CE compilation inheriting (with change) lost late-BCE catalog. Separates literature, computation/divination, and fangji technical arts. Never backdate every wording to 5 BCE without source collation.
