# WINDOW 28 — 341–360 CE — SOURCE REGISTER

**Research status:** sourced deep investigation, 2026-10-18. **Controls:** SOURCE/INSTITUTION/WITNESS EVENT DATE ≠ original copy date ≠ later codification ≠ reconstructed law date. **Main point:** the preserved imperial legal collections distinguish legitimate engineering/geometrical skill from prohibited divinatory consultation; **they do not mention an alchemical guild or a named 341–360 transmuter**. Roman legislation survives as selections compiled into the **Theodosian Code (438 CE)** and later manuscript copies, **not original 344 or 357 engraved autographs**.

**Evidentiary grades:** ATTESTED (quoted ancient transmitted legislation / original surviving artifacts); SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION (what statutes purport to do); CONTESTED (date, reading, jurisdiction, enforcement); NO SECURE EVIDENCE (positive connection to an identifiable Alchemist). A law's penalty is a legal prescription, **not evidence anyone was executed**.

## W28-T01 — CTh XIII.4.3, engineers/geometras/architects, July 6 344

- **Original legal act as transmitted:** `Impp. Constantius et Constans aa. ad Leontium praefectum praetorio.` [Latin text *Codex Theodosianus* XIII.4.3](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Codex_Theodosianus/Liber_XIII); [critical transcribed Roman-law collection](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh13.html); [translation with source section](https://readancienttexts.com/theodosius/liber_xiii/1).
- **Date in transmitted subscription:** `Dat. prid. non. iul. Leontio et Sallustio conss.` = **6 July 344**. **Original date claimed by law**, not date of modern webpage and not the original fifth-century compilation.
- `mechanicos et geometras et architectos` (*mechanics, geometers and architects*), describing divisions, incisions and measurements used for fabricating structures and finding aqueduct gradients, are explicitly asked to **teach and learn**: `in par studium docendi adque discendi`; skilled instructors should take students: `suscipiant docendos qui docere sufficiunt`. `inmunitatibus gaudeant`: exemption/immunity from duties. **This is official encouragement for skills of mensuration/engineering, not recognition of `chēmeia`**.
- **Witness separation:** emperor-era original **lost**, preserved in **438 Theodosian Code** and later copied/printed text; no named actual engineering teacher, patron, student or 344 alchemy laboratory independently attested. [ST-095]

## W28-T02 — CTh XVI.10.2, suppression of sacrifice, 341

- [Latin *Codex Theodosianus* XVI.10.2](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh16.html); [another Latin editorial copy](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Codex_Theodosianus/Liber_XVI).
- `Imp. Constantius a. ad Madalianum agentem vicem praefectorum praetorio` = Constantius to an acting praetorian official. `Cesset superstitio, sacrificiorum aboleatur insania` (*let superstition cease and the madness of sacrifices be abolished*); `competens ... vindicta` threatening a suitable penalty for anyone offering sacrifices despite prior regulations.
- **Dated by receipt, not issue date:** `Accepta Marcellino et Probino conss.` => **341 CE**, with **no preserved exact day/month**. Cites alleged earlier prohibition by deceased father Constantine without preserving that earlier law in this paragraph. Do not invent a 341 prosecution or say all temple craft immediately ceased.
- **Source grade:** DATABLE LEGISLATIVE PRESCRIPTION in later compilation, not an actual inscribed 341 enforcement record. **Sacrificers specifically targeted; alchemical transmuters not named.** [ST-096]

## W28-T03 — CTh XVI.10.3 and XVI.10.4, protection versus closure of temples; disputed dates

- [Full Latin text and divergent dates](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh16.html).
- **XVI.10.3** Constantius & Constans to **Catullinus**, Urban Prefect: despite a stated preference that all `superstitio` be removed, temples **outside the city walls** involved in games, circus or contests are to remain **`intactae incorruptaeque`** (intact and unspoiled). Subscription: `Dat. kal. nov. Constantio IIII et Constante III aa. conss.` conventionally **1 Nov 346**, but critical dating often annotated **[342]**. **CONTESTED**; securely in Window 28 regardless.
- **XVI.10.4** to **Taurus** praetorian prefect: orders temples closed everywhere, prohibits sacrifices, threatens death and confiscation and sanctions negligent governors; subscription `Dat. kal. dec. Constantio IIII et Constante III aa. conss.` conventionally **1 Dec 346**, sometimes critically corrected **[354?]**. **CONTESTED** original date and relation to contemporaneous temple practice. Its stated law is not proof all temples were physically sealed.
- The two provisions are **differently addressed policies within the same transmitted compilation**. They can reflect circumstance, region, date errors, or differentiated purposes. Don't simply claim universal complete closure in 346 and ignore protection of some shrines. **No alchemical temple/person linked.** [ST-097]

## W28-T04 — CTh XVI.10.5 (353) and XVI.10.6 (356), further sacrifice rules

- [CTh XVI.10.5–6 Latin](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh16.html).
- **XVI.10.5**, Constantius to Cerialis, Urban Prefect, **23 November 353**: `Abol[e]antur sacrificia nocturna Magnentio auctore permissa`—nocturnal sacrifices claimed to have been permitted under Magnentius are to be abolished. **What Magnentius permitted is inferred from the imperial counterclaim**, not an original independent Magnentian decree.
- **XVI.10.6**, Constantius and Julian Caesar, **19 February 356** at Milan: orders **capital punishment** for those demonstrated engaging in sacrifices or honoring statues (`poena capitis`). Neither passage identifies an alchemical practitioner. **A law with severe penalty ≠ observed execution of a goldmaker.** [ST-097, contextual]

## W28-T05 — CTh IX.16.4–6: 357 and 358 divination penalties, lexical `mathematicus`

- [Latin Codex IX.16.4–6](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh09.html).
- **IX.16.4**, Constantius II with Julian Caesar, **25 January 357** (subscription `Dat. VIII kal. Feb.`): `Nemo haruspicem consulat aut mathematicum, nemo hariolum`; forbids consulting an **entrail-diviner, astrologer (`mathematicus` in this legal context), soothsayer**, then names `Chaldaei ac magi` and `malefici`; suppresses `divinandi curiositas` and threatens execution. **The word `mathematicus` here is NOT interchangeable with a 344 `geometra` protected for measuring building dimensions**.
- **IX.16.5**, **4 December 357** (editorial date sometimes **[?]**): alleges practitioners of `magicis artibus` disturb **`elementa`** and endanger lives, invokes dead spirits and harmful arts; this is a **criminalizing charge**, not experimentally measured *elemental transmutation*. Rhetorical “disturb elements” may concern winds/weather or nature, not production of gold from copper.
- **IX.16.6**, **5 July 358**, to praetorian prefect **Taurus**: all `magi` branded enemies of humanity, diviners, `mathematici`, dream interpreters etc. accused **at imperial court** face torture regardless of status. **Court setting expressly relevant; avoid converting it into verified universal enforcement against every workshop**.
- **Witness type:** legal pronouncements **as preserved in 438 code and later manuscript copies**; no 357 trial dossier of a named Alchemist excavated. Actual punitive actions require separate case evidence. [ST-098]

## W28-O01 — Alexandrian copper coin, 351–355 original metal comparator

- [British Museum B.13591](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_B-13591), original **copper-alloy nummus** minted at **Alexandria**, museum **351–355 CE**, **1.41 g / 18 mm**. The portrait is associated with **Constantius Gallus** (Caesar), reverse soldier spearing fallen horseman. Museum ruler field includes **Constantius II**, which must not be mistaken for a claim that Caesar and emperor were the same person.
- **Materially ATTESTED** actual worked copper alloy and imperial military iconography, not a chemical recipe, produced elemental gold, named worker, religious sacrifice, divination or alchemical guild. An image of military victory does not validate a specific real battle or metal-creating technique. [ST-099]

## W28-S01 — Context and actual effectiveness of restrictions

- [Christopher P. Jones, “Three Temples in Libanius and the Theodosian Code,” *Classical Quarterly* 2013](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/classical-quarterly/article/abs/three-temples-in-libanius-and-the-theodosian-code/F72CAF2AB5424F01CCCC3D4697A15B8B): scholarship problematizes using laws as a universal census of temple destruction. [Libanius *Oration 30* *Pro Templis* translation](https://ccel.org/ccel/pearse/morefathers/files/libanius_pro_templis_02_trans.htm) is a **later (c.380s/early 390s)** rhetorical account of temple damage under several rulers; **not a contemporary 346 record**.
- Critical inference: enforcement was likely uneven and source-dependent; **no locality-specific 346–358 temple raid or alchemical persecution demonstrated in this study**. Keep distinctions among **legislated pressure / received text / actual incident / later advocate's memory**.

## W28-S02 — Earlier alchemical commentary horizon and date guardrails

- [Olivier Dufault (2019) *Early Greek Alchemy, Patronage and Innovation in Late Antiquity*](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ks0g83x): Zosimos of Panopolis **c.300**, Theosebeia patronage and interpretation in later Greek compilations; **no secure 344 or 357 activity in a dated original source**. Greek alchemical traditions of Pseudo-Democritus and Maria have attribution/dating uncertainties.
- Leiden X and Stockholm papyri **late-third/early-fourth-century broad copy horizon**; copying range overlap **not** license to assign a 350 recipe writer or alchemical persecution. Olympiodorus's alchemical commentary and Themistius **367** transmutation mention belong to later/uncertain horizons, **not 341–360**.

## Explicit source-grade verdict

**Secure period dates for transmitted laws:** 341 receipt of sacrifice ban, 344 technical teaching, 353 nocturnal sacrifice measure, 356 sacrifice prohibition, 357 anti-divination edicts and 358 court-sorcery edict. **Disputed period dates:** paired 342/346 temple conservation and 346/354 temple closure laws. **Direct original period object:** 351–355 Alexandrian copper nummus. **No named Alchemist officially licensed, protected, persecuted or caught in 341–360** identified; no proof of elemental transmutation.
