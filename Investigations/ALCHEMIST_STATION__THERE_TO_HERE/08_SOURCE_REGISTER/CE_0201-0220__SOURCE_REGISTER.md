# Window 21 source register — 201–220 CE

**Audit date:** 2026-10-18. Source hierarchy: **actual Roman Forum arch** and **P.Giss.40** with distinct enactment/copy dates > Clement near-period testimony of Egyptian traditions > retrospective Dio and highly problematic Historia Augusta. No directly attested Alchemist found.

## [W21-P01] Justus Liebig University Giessen, original P.Giss. 40 historical introduction
https://www.constitutio.de/en/papyrus/introduction
- A physical **Greek papyrus copied c.215 CE**, not Caracalla's autograph from 212.
- **LEFT column:** damaged Greek citizenship decree `Constitutio Antoniniana` originally **212/213 CE**.
- **RIGHT column:** further imperial material including **c.215 restriction/expulsion of Egyptian migrants/rural people from Alexandria**; also amnesty. Contains differences of terminology and source classifications.
- **Not:** proof all three texts composed on the same day, and not a text written by an Alchemist.

## [W21-P02] University Giessen, Constitutio Antoniniana introduction/history
https://www.constitutio.de/en/constitutio-antoniniana/introduction
https://www.constitutio.de/en/papyrus/known-history-of-the-constitutio-antoniniana
- Grant to **almost all free inhabitants** of Roman world, **212 or 213 CE**; Latin original not recovered, physical **c.215 Greek copy**.
- Damage and scholarly reconstructions require separation of **legible papyrus** from **editorial supplements**.
- Giessen materials treat **2017 UNESCO Memory of World recognition** of papyrus, not evidence of any 212 manuscript other than copy.

## [W21-P03] Original Greek Alexandrian expulsion instruction, P.Giss 40 col II 16–29
https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/Reditu_aegyptiorum_DDBDP.gr.html
English ancient legal source edition: https://attalus.org/docs/select2/p215.html
- **c.215 CE**, order excludes Egyptian **rural migrants** in Alexandria. Exceptions include **pig traders**, **river boatmen**, and **reed suppliers for heating public baths**, plus visitors/festival participation in some cases.
- State's derogatory wording about nonurban Egyptian farmers must be recognized as *administrative viewpoint*, **not neutral modern assessment**. Occupation matters in exemption, but no chemical craft listed.
- **Do not assert:** all free rural Egyptian citizens were expelled everywhere or that an alchemist profession was specifically targeted.

## [W21-P04] Peter van Minnen, 'Three Edicts of Caracalla? A New Reading of P.Giss. 40', Chiron 46 (2016), published online 2020
https://publications.dainst.org/journals/chiron/article/view/2393
DOI https://doi.org/10.34780/6243-495a
- Revises longstanding belief `P.Giss.40` comprises *three separate edicts*, suggesting **one imperial citizenship edict plus separate instruction to prefect** and arguing for further Latin textual parallels, possible additional Greek papyrus. Need retain genre debate explicitly.
- **Do not assert** copy constitutes an undamaged exhaustive modern legislative code, nor quote missing words as intact.

## [W21-P05] Scholarly dispute over `dediticii` exception
https://www.iris.unict.it/handle/20.500.11769/498849
- Academic 2020 reinterpretation challenges conventional restored Greek exception of `dediticii` and proposes a different category of noncitizens. Interpretations contested.
- The broad grant to free persons has high historical support, **exact excluded classes and social consequences disputed**.

## [W21-A01] Official Parco archeologico del Colosseo — Arch of Septimius Severus
https://colosseo.it/en/event/the-arch-of-septimius-severus-tells-its-story-special-maintenance-and-repair-work-underway/
- **ACTUAL 203 CE monument** of marble/travertine in Roman Forum dedicated to Severus, Caracalla, Geta; visibly modified after **Geta murdered December 211**.
- Original dedication bronze letters lost, **sockets and recut marble attest revised inscription**. Not a chemical manuscript.

## [W21-A03] Hülsen, `The Roman Forum` 1906, physical correction description
https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/Places/Europe/Italy/Lazio/Roma/Rome/Forum_Romanum/_Texts/Huelsen%2A/2/10.html

- A material **erasure leaving evidence** is robust, despite unknown exact day re-cut.

## [W21-C01] Clement of Alexandria, `Stromata` VI.4, priestly book procession
https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/02106.htm
- Primary transmitted late second/early third-century Christian intellectual passage: priestly singer, astrologer, sacred scribe, stolistes and prophet, **42 books attributed to Hermes**, with **36** priestly subjects and **6 medical books** read by `pastophoroi` covering body, diseases, instruments, drugs, eyes, women.
- Broad `Stromata` date **c.198–203** straddles previous window. **Not a catalog of extant original Egyptian manuscripts or written proof all temple practitioners complied**.
- **Not:** scientific proof Hermes wrote them, or claim priestly medical expert = Alchemist.

## [W21-C02] Academic source dating caution for early Christian text
https://www.thestromata.com/texts/the-stromata-book-vi
- Date c.198–203 **approximate**, based on literary authorship and historical research, not independently dated original 201 book or day.
- Translation is from later manuscripts and modern editions. Independent eyewitness access to temple archives is NOT established.

## [W21-D01] Cassius Dio, Roman History 78.7 and 78.23
https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/78%2A.html
- Dio relates Caracalla's antipathy toward Aristotelian philosophers, withdrawal of Alexandrian common-meal privileges and reported violence when visiting Alexandria. Event usual **215/216**; Dio's account **not same genre** as original papyrus. He reports Caracalla's alleged logic about Aristotle and Alexander; do not make it neutral imperial policy statement.
- **No documented anti-alchemical expulsion**, even if philosophical communities harmed.

## [W21-R01] Legal-historical scholarship on doubtful Severan anti-conversion edict
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-roman-studies/article/legislation-and-christians/4314F4F8BDE191C3201E0D7C187674D5
- `Historia Augusta` Severus 17.1 claims severe penalties for conversion to Judaism and Christianity around 202. Recent critical reassessment says **a universally specific anti-Christian law cannot be sustained**, though individual local martyrdoms (Perpetua and Felicitas 203) are real historical data.
- **Never infer** the alleged law targeted Greek Alchemists or compelled recipe disguise.
