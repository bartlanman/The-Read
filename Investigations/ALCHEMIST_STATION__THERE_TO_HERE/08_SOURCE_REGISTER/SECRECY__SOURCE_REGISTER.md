# Secrecy and the emerging station — source register

**Date reviewed:** 2026-10-18. **Status:** sourced inquiry, not a settled historical verdict.

## S-SEC-01 — Olivier Dufault (2019)
*Early Greek Alchemy, Patronage and Innovation in Late Antiquity*, California Classical Studies 5.
URL: https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ks0g83x
**What it supports:** Zosimos's patronage, the social and textual competition among scholarly interpreters, the distinction between later labels and ancient self-identification; his accounts of Egyptian royal control and alchemical secrecy.
**Important limitation:** Zosimos's reconstruction of *older* priestly and royal practices is a late-antique testimony, not automatically contemporary evidence for Ptolemaic Egypt.

## S-SEC-02 — Lawrence M. Principe (2013)
"The Secrets of Alchemy," *Science History Institute*, 30 January 2013.
URL: https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/the-secrets-of-alchemy/
**What it supports:** mixture of practical craft and philosophical frameworks; extent of surviving Zosimos texts; variation between coded writing and practical apparatus instructions; mercury and arsenic vapors; later examples of deliberately omitted recipe details.
**Important limitation:** twentieth/twenty-first-century chemical interpretation and early-modern secrecy cannot simply be assigned to second-century BCE Egyptian craftspeople.

## S-SEC-03 — Matteo Martelli (2017/online 2018)
"Translating Ancient Alchemy: Fragments of Graeco-Egyptian Alchemy in Arabic Compendia," *Ambix* 64(4), 326–342.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/00026980.2017.1412137
**What it supports:** pseudonymous authorial authority; narratives about hidden Egyptian and Persian writings; late antique Greek, Syriac, and Arabic transmission; Democritus/Ostanes discovery stories as textual claims.
**Important limitation:** the stories of hidden tablets/books are reported claims of alchemical authors, not archaeological proof of actual royal archives of alchemy.

## S-SEC-04 — Pamela O. Long (2001)
*Openness, Secrecy, Authorship: Technical Arts and the Culture of Knowledge from Antiquity to the Renaissance*. Johns Hopkins University Press.
Bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books?id=x_k91r2u6NgC
**What it supports:** use of separate analytic categories for technical secrets, ownership, craft transmission, authorship, and esotericism; especially chapters 2 "Secrecy and Esoteric Knowledge in Late Antiquity" and 3 "Handing Down Craft Knowledge."
**Important limitation:** full text not fully reviewed here. Do not attribute particular wording or conclusions to specific pages until consulted.

## S-SEC-05 — Science History Institute (2014)
"Gold, Secrecy, and Prestige."
URL: https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/gold-secrecy-and-prestige/
**What it supports:** how craft secrecy, protection of prestige and wealth, and threats of counterfeiting/prosecution figure in the long history of alchemy.
**Important limitation:** broad survey; claims from later medieval or early-modern settings cannot prove ancient Egyptian cases.

## S-SEC-06 — Matteo Martelli and colleagues (2022)
"Exploring the ancient chemistry of mercury," *PNAS*, open full text:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9214491/
**What it supports:** surviving evidence for extraction and manipulation of mercury from cinnabar, reconstruction of Greek/Latin/Syriac technical processes; study observes hazards of fumes and ancient awareness in particular traditions.
**Important limitation:** modern experimental danger must not be treated as evidence that an ancient text's secrecy was *motivated* by safety.

## S-SEC-07 — Lynn Thorndike (1923)
*A History of Magic and Experimental Science*, vol. I, ch. 5, pp. 193–195.
Digitization: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/67792
Read online: https://readingroo.ms/6/7/7/9/67792/old/67792-h/67792-h.htm
**What it supports:** source-critical caution about the Diocletian book-burning story, first attested in much later literary sources; also notes secrecy and publication coexist.
**Important limitation:** older scholarship; consult Dufault 2019 and primary John of Antioch / *Suda* before drawing firmer conclusions.

## S-SEC-08 — Anonymous recipes, Leiden and Stockholm Papyri
Modern orientation: Cambridge *The Alchemy Reader*, ch. 6 "From Leyden Papyrus X and the Stockholm Papyrus":
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/alchemy-reader/anonymous-late-third-century-ad-from-leyden-papyrus-x-and-the-stockholm-papyrus/B9648991C8B475894FB7F48982B151F4
**What it supports:** explicit procedural recipe literature coexists with allegorical alchemical texts, showing that secrecy is not universal or binary.
**Important limitation:** papyrus copy dates, recipe composition dates, and later textual classifications must be distinguished.

## S-SEC-09 — Didier Kahn (2016)
"Alchemical interpretations of ancient Mysteries" (study).
Academic text: https://pergamos.lib.uoa.gr/uoa/dl/object/2944049/file.pdf
**What it supports:** interpretation of initiation in Pseudo-Democritean literature, with an account of an unfinished initiation and concealed formula in a column.
**Important limitation:** an initiatory narrative is not proof of an ancient historical apprenticeship.

## Open primary-text verification tasks
- Obtain a critical edition and exact Greek/Syriac text of Zosimos's *Final Account*, including the passage on Egyptian kings, training and restricted practice.
- Collate *Suda* entry on Diocletian and John of Antioch fragments with dates of witnesses.
- Compare technical secrecy in alchemical recipe texts against overt instructions in Leiden and Stockholm papyri.
- Check whether sources distinguish legal threat to counterfeiters, unauthorized specialists, magicians, or all practitioners.

**Method:** every future claim must tie to an exact textual witness, or receive the label INTERPRETATION / CONTESTED.
