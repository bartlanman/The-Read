# Window 06 Source Register — 100–81 BCE

**Checked:** 2026-10-18. **Evidence class:** transmitted Chinese original wording (Han histories, later *Yantielun*), a retrospective Song chronicle, modern institutional synopsis, modern epigraphic chronological source. **Not** a fully collated critical edition.

## [W06-H01] *Hanshu* (Book of Han), chapter 45, biography of Jiang Chong (蒯伍江息夫傳)
- **Transmission:** Later Han compilation by Ban Gu and others, first/second century CE, describing 91 BCE events, surviving through later copies.
- **Chinese text:** https://ctext.org/han-shu/kuai-wu-jiang-xi-fu-zhuan/zhs
- **Relevant account:** emperor fears curse arts; Jiang Chong invokes 巫蠱 and investigates; nonconsensual/coerced confessions, wooden effigies, crown prince's downfall; some reports refer to forged proof and the emperor's retrospective recognition of fraud.
- **Warrants:** a specific court-political crisis with punishment for accusations related to harmful ritual; institutional empowerment and later discrediting of investigator.
- **Does not warrant:** universal alchemist persecution, physical transmutation success, equating all fangshi with witchcraft suspects.
- **Additional academic article:** Chang Hui-lin, "The Calamity Caused by Witchcraft and the Writing of History" (2018), CityUHK scholar record https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/%E5%B7%AB%E8%9B%8A%E4%B9%8B%E7%A5%B8%E4%B8%8E%E5%8E%86%E5%8F%B2%E4%B9%A6%E5%86%99/
- **Historian's caution:** *Shiji*, *Hanshu* and *Zizhi tongjian* convey different levels of detail and their own moral frame.

## [W06-H02] *Hanshu* 96B, 西域傳下 (Xiyu zhuan, Western Regions) — Luntai edict
- **Claimed historical date:** 89 BCE.
- **Text:** https://skqs.dazhishi.com/show_qpywaazlyv.html
- **Parallel fragments:** https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=68141&show=parallel
- **Warrants:** rejection of proposed military/agricultural colonies in Luntai; grief over defeated soldiers; critique of omens and divinations in military strategy; priority to curb oppressive levies and support farming.
- **Does not warrant:** clear universal dismissal of all alchemical experts or an edict criminalizing goldmaking.
- **Important transmitted phrase:** 當今務在禁苛暴，止擅賦，力本農 (contemporary policy rhetoric as transmitted), **not** a license to infer a wholesale immediate transformation to modern scientific skepticism.

## [W06-H03] *Yantielun* (Discourses on Salt and Iron): 81 BCE consultation, later compiled by Huan Kuan
- **Chinese original, collection:** https://ctext.org/yan-tie-lun/juan-yi
- **Ch. 1 "Original Debate":** https://ctext.org/yan-tie-lun/ben-yi
- **Ch. 6 "Return to Antiquity":** https://ctext.org/yan-tie-lun/fu-gu
- **Ch. 9 "Criticizing Power":** https://ctext.org/yan-tie-lun/ci-quan/zh
- **Date distinction:** 81 BCE is the court gathering; the elaborate dialogues are written/redacted later during Emperor Xuan's reign, not stenographic transcripts.
- **Warrants:** contested authority over iron manufacture/salt extraction, material quality and access, and competing public/private rationale.
- **Does not warrant:** a chemical recipe for turning base metals to gold, uniform historical opposition to all alchemy, exact wording of every interlocutor's speech at the original meeting.

## [W06-L01] *Zizhi tongjian* 資治通鑑, Emperor Wu year 89 BCE (Sima Guang, 11th c CE)
- **Transcription:** https://www.xuoda.com/ls/zztj/0297.htm
- **Reported story:** after a tour and late repentance, Tian Qianqiu asks emperor to dismiss practitioners claiming contact with immortals, and emperor agrees; includes phrase 悉罷諸方士候神人者.
- **DATING CRITICAL:** this is eleventh-century narrative about an alleged 89 BCE event, **not** a direct contemporary 89 BCE report.
- **Does not warrant:** a securely dated mass dismissal of early Han alchemists.
- **Source-criticism:** historian Xin Deyong's work as summarized by StoryStudio https://storystudio.tw/article/gushi/%E6%98%AF%E8%AA%B0%E3%80%8C%E8%A3%BD%E9%80%A0%E3%80%8D%E4%BA%86%E6%BC%A2%E6%AD%A6%E5%B8%9D%EF%BC%9F/
- **Disputed source ancestry:** *Han Wu gushi* may underlie this repentance/discharge story, not the earlier *Hanshu*. Modern historians disagree about its value. Mark the event CONTESTED.

## [W06-S02] Xin Deyong, *The Making of Emperor Wu* / 漢武帝晚年政治取向與司馬光的重構
- **Accessible overview:** https://storystudio.tw/article/gushi/%E6%98%AF%E8%AA%B0%E3%80%8C%E8%A3%BD%E9%80%A0%E3%80%8D%E4%BA%86%E6%BC%A2%E6%AD%A6%E5%B8%9D%EF%BC%9F/
- **Main contention:** the story in the *Tongjian* of universal late regret and dismissal of fangshi relies in part on doubtful late anecdotal literature; the earlier *Hanshu* Luntai text is narrower, centered on strategic decisions.
- **Opposing view:** other historians, including Tian Yuqing, have understood the edict as a larger ethical/policy turn. Do not resolve an active scholarly dispute through confident assertion.

## [W06-E01] Chris Bennett/Trismegistos, Ptolemy IX
- **Access:** https://www.trismegistos.org/calendar/Bennett/ptolemies/ptolemy_ix.htm
- **Date evidence:** second return to power probably May 88 BCE, alternate reconstructions extend earlier/later. Last dated reference Dec 81 BCE, death roughly Dec 81–March 80 BCE, so **death cannot be assigned securely to this window alone**.
- **Warrants:** ancient papyrus and coin evidence for dynastic position; no alchemical practitioner role.

## [W06-E02] Chris Bennett/Trismegistos, Ptolemy X
- **Access:** https://www.trismegistos.org/calendar/Bennett/ptolemies/ptolemy_x.htm
- **Claim supported:** expulsion during 88 BCE and death during late 88 or 87, regnal dating contested by sources.
- **Does not warrant:** professional station of an Egyptian alchemist.
