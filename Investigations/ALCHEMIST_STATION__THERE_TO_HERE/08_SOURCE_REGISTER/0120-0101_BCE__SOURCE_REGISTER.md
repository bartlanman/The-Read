# Window 05 source register — 120–101 BCE

**Research status:** Primary-language *Shiji* 28 first close pass; modern explanatory translation of overlapping later version; independent Egypt academic chronology; chapter provenance explicitly audited. No original manuscript of Luan's master, Shaoweng's message or Mount Tai jade wording is extant in these sources.

## [W05-P01] Sima Qian, Shiji 28 封禪書
- **Chinese original:** https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E5%8D%B7028
- **Composition:** late second/early first century BCE; events in present window. **Transmitted textual witness survives through later copying.**
- **Passage locations in online transcription:** section with 少翁 (Shaoweng) and ox message; 欒大 (Luan Da), 113 BCE; 寶鼎 and 公孫卿; later execution and 110 BCE rites, around lines 253–281.
- **Evidence:** emperor rewards and executes Shaoweng for alleged forged writing and hides it; Luan invokes fear of persecution and an alleged common teacher, negotiates seals and access, enjoys many titles then is executed; Gongsun Qing offers texts and delayed signs; ritual documents at Mount Tai kept secret.
- **Original language cues:** 同師 / 奄口 (variant 掩口) / 方士 / 禁方 / 黃金可成 / 食馬肝死 / 印 / 書祕 / 其事皆禁.
- **Does not show:** actual successful goldmaking, master identity verified outside narrative, physical original silk, original laboratory cipher or complete jade text.

## [W05-P02] Shiji 12 孝武本紀 — later reconstructed annals
- **Bilingual transcription and editorial paraphrases:** https://warringstates.day/library/shiji/12-xiao-wu-ben-ji
- **Primary Chinese commentary:** https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98%E4%B8%89%E5%AE%B6%E8%A8%BB/%E5%8D%B7012
- **Critical issue:** Later *Shiji* 12 replaces an earlier lost/reworked annal, substantially using *Shiji* 28; some translators transcribe differing variants or split events differently. **Do not count 12 and 28 as independent eyewitness testimony**.
- **Relevant narrated episodes:** Shaoweng (around paragraphs 198–206 on site), Luan (275–309), execution (406–412), Gongsun (423–430), imperial 110 BCE rites (site's Mount Tai section). 
- **Does not establish:** scientific proof or independent corroboration simply by duplication.

## [W05-S03] "The Significance of Feathers in Early and Medieval China" (2025), *Bulletin of SOAS*
- **Access:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-school-of-oriental-and-african-studies/article/significance-of-feathers-in-early-and-medieval-china/A9CBD109AA1974D32AC6FEC90A82941C
- **Warrant:** feathered robes and named title of Luan Da are historical costume/symbolic markers; later Yan Shigu commentary interprets flight symbolism.
- **Limit:** robe symbolism is **not** a coded reagent or laboratory procedure.

## [W05-S04] Leaking Rulers and Confidential Officials (2024), *Journal of Chinese History*
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1017/jch.2023.14
- **Warrant:** historical conceptual distinction between rulers' secrecy and later officials' confidential offices in early China.
- **Limit:** broader Chinese secrecy tradition does not prove Li/Luan were personally taught coded chemistry.

## Interpretative safeguards
- The *Shiji* narrator's reporting of immortal apparitions is **a source claim** about audience and belief, not an independent description of supernatural events.
- Fear of execution appears in **Luan's bargaining speech**; the later emperor's sentence appears in source narrative. Distinguish rhetorical claim, remembered speech and documented broader repression.
- Prince and court *fangshi* roles remain multifaceted; avoid converting all to one stable licensed professional station called 'Alchemist'.
- Court craft office 尚方 may mean artisan/royal workshop role; its remit for Luan requires technical historical lexicon, not generic 'alchemist'.
