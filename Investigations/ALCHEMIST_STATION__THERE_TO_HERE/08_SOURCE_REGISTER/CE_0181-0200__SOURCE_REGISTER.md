# Window 20 historical source register — 181–200 CE

**Established:** Original Rome imperial coins and medallions from 191–192, original Alexandrian tetradrachm issues **193/194 CE**, Galen's self-authored early-193 report of the 192 fire in a *later* manuscript, and Roman historians of the violent late Commodan and 193 politics. **Distinct witness dates are mandatory**.

## [W20-G01] Galen, *On Avoiding Distress* / `Περὶ ἀλυπίας`, modern critical introduction (2023)
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK613949/
- 192 fire on Palatine / Temple of Peace, libraries and Sacred Way stores. Work **likely composed early 193 CE**, after Commodus's death **31 December 192**, inferred from explicit criticisms of that reign. **Epistolary literary/ethical form**, including reported friend's admiration of Galen's emotional restraint.
- **Text surviving in** Vlatadon monastery Greek **MS Vlatadon 14**, fifteenth-century copy, noticed in **2005** by Antoine Pietrobelli. This is NOT an extant 193 autograph.

## [W20-G02] Original ancient Galen text, modern English translation with facing Greek (Polemis & Xenophontos, 2023)
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK613950/
- Galen's account begins with burned **manuscripts**, **simple/compound medicines**, **medical instruments**, valuable drugs and property placed in Sacred Way warehouses. The friend reports his apparent lack of grief.
- Later discussions differentiate rare original writings from common works needing accurate versions. Verify each exact quote against this new edition rather than reproducing manuscripts lost in 192.

## [W20-G03] Contemporary historical scholar Vivian Nutton, 'Galen's Library' (Cambridge, 2010)
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/galen-and-the-world-of-knowledge/galens-library/0118A55364AF65B0719F8F50B2D697E7
- Most of Galen's stored personal library, rare books and own copies perished spring 192; for some works reconstruction impossible; depended on other people's surviving copies.
- **Caution:** 'Most of stored library' ≠ every book he ever owned, all his authored works, or entire Roman Empire's libraries.

## [W20-G04] Sophia Xenophontos, *Medicine and Practical Ethics in Galen*, 'Avoiding Distress' (2023)
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK612009/
- Rhetorical account of supposedly secure **Temple of Peace** deposits; Galen claims rare editions, instruments, medicine/antidotes and individual loss. Ethical *performance* of serenity shapes the narration.
- **Warrant:** source shows Galen's own evaluation of irreplaceable texts and the fragility of common storage; not independent proof of exactly how he felt.

## [W20-G05] *Medical History* (Cambridge, 2014), 'Psychotherapy and Moralising Rhetoric in Galen's Newly Discovered Avoiding Distress'
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/medical-history/article/psychotherapy-and-moralising-rhetoric-in-galens-newly-discovered-avoiding-distress-peri-alypias/DDBE367E94DE54D1394A7C9293070FFD
- Lost precisely annotated texts and rare copies; rhetoric of the grieving recipient, composition as philosophical resilience. Stored duplicates destroyed *together*.
- Separate Galen's genre and claims from objectively verified psychological outcomes.

## [W20-G06] Vlatadon 14 and rediscovery / edition
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK613955/
https://www.persee.fr/doc/crai_0065-0536_2005_num_149_2_22868
- Greek treatise unknown as a complete work to scholarship until 2005 recognition of **15th-c Vlatadon 14**, despite medieval lists and quotations (including Arabic and Hebrew). New critical Greek and English translation **2023**, editors I. Polemis / S. Xenophontos.
- **This does NOT mean the 192 library was physically restored in 2005**; a separate copy-chain preserved a different work in another time.

## [W20-E01] Cassius Dio, *Roman History* 73.14 (Commodus plague and needle poisoning)
https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/73%2A.html
- Dio wrote after the described **c.189 CE** epidemic; says **2,000 died on some days** in Rome and reports poison-needle criminals. One may cite his **claim**, not an independently verified demographic count or an established mass poisoning operation.
- **No source calls these alleged people Alchemists or supplies demonstrated techniques.**

## [W20-E02] Quantitative epidemiology study revisiting Dio
https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0313684
- Researchers assess 189 CE estimate and early Antonine plague accounts; figure depends on ancient narrative and assumptions about population/pathogen. **Does not verify poison needle tales or smallpox identification**.

## [W20-O01] British Museum, Commodus Hercules coin **R.15104**
https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_R-15104
- Original **copper-alloy as**, **191–192 CE**, Rome mint. Lion-skin-bedecked Commodus portrait, club. Material fact of political association with Hercules; not embodiment of a god.

## [W20-O02] British Museum, Commodus Hercules medallion **1846,0910.247**
https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1846-0910-247
- Original **192 CE** copper-alloy medallion, Emperor Commodus represented as Hercules, lion skin, club. Not chemical experimentation.

## [W20-O03] British Museum, Roman Money Gallery, narrative guide (2020/2021)
https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2021-05/Money_Gallery_LPG_2020_Room_68.pdf
- Commodus/Hercules medallions dated **192** and symbolic **refounding of Rome**; contemporary political propaganda preserved in metal. Self-representation ≠ realized divinity.

## [W20-E03] Herodian, *History of the Empire since the Death of Marcus*, Books 1.14–17, 2.1–15, 3.1–7
https://www.livius.org/sources/content/herodian-s-roman-history/
- Herodian wrote **early third century CE**, near but after events of 192–197. Reports major 192 fire and Commodus killing **31 Dec 192**; 193 saw multiple rival claimants **Pertinax, Didius Julianus, Pescennius Niger, Clodius Albinus, Septimius Severus**. **Not five sequential universally recognized emperors**.
- Civil war continues through **197 CE**, still within 181–200. Political account not proof of alchemical persecution.

## [W20-E04] J. Lendering, Herodian 2.13 / imperial chronology
https://www.livius.org/sources/content/herodian-s-roman-history/herodian-2.13/
- Describes Septimius in Rome around **June 193 CE**; late-2nd-century transition. Modern publication of ancient translation not independent ancient witness.

## [W20-O04] Roman Provincial Coinage (Oxford et al.), **RPC V.3 type 65128**
https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/type/65128
- **Alexandria, Egypt**, Septimius Severus, **regnal year 2 (L Β) = 193/194 CE**, **debased silver tetradrachm**, reverse **Athena seated**. Scholarly museum numismatic catalog lists **18 specimens**. Actual period manufactured Roman-Egyptian state-issued coins.
- Warrant: publicly recognizable minted authority, regnal year, craft and material. **NOT** a conclusion about every Egyptian's loyalty or precious-metal transmutation.

## [W20-G07] Galen chronology in Oxford Handbook (2024)
https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/57517/chapter/467334321
- Dates fire **192 CE**, his late Commodus writings and aftermath. His **192 text loss is not evidence Galen died in 192**.

## Open verification questions
1. Collate *Peri alypias* exact Greek passages describing destruction, 'safe' repositories, and language about **irreplaceable** manuscripts, especially restorations from damaged late copy. Avoid quoted English beyond copyright.
2. Test extent/causal path of fire in archaeological material compared to Galen, Herodian and other Roman writers; fire's physical cause is not secured.
3. Determine precisely which lost books from Galen were copied elsewhere and eventually recovered; **the 2005 find of *On Avoiding Distress* does not recover the lost 192 exemplars**.
4. Compare Commodus medallion imagery with politically revised Severan coin legends as **material identity claims**, not laboratory chemistry.
5. Find an original 181–200 dateable Greek alchemical practitioner, recipe or teacher line (NONE verified).
