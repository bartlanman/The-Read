# Window 22 Historical Source Register — 221–240 CE

**Audit 2026-10-18.** Main methodological novelty: a **known early-third-century scholarly writer**, a genuinely surviving **third-century Egyptian papyrus of his work**, and **documented symbolic substitutions for ingredients**—but NOT an independently titled Alchemist or verified precious-metal transmutation.
**Special caution:** The original British Library papyrus's **first use 225–275/276**, **dated verso 276**, means **the physical copy might be later than this precise 221–240 window**. Critical fragments about recipe notation DO NOT occur in that particular papyrus leaf.

## [W22-S01] Olivier Dufault, *Early Greek Alchemy, Patronage and Innovation in Late Antiquity* (2019), pp.84–91
https://escholarship.org/content/qt2ks0g83x/qt2ks0g83x_noSplash_bd08d6316153f6cf32b8279c7ebfa658.pdf
- Main book openly hosted University of California Press / California Classical Studies. Chapter **4.3 Julius Africanus**, especially **pp.84–90**.
- Chronography completed probably soon after **222 CE**; patronage at Edessa and perhaps Alexander Severus, `Kestoi` emperor dedication; Africanus self-claims overseeing an imperial library and embassy Emmaus traditionally dated **221** (other traditions ~226).
- *Kestoi* contains technical/medical/natural/military/cosmetic procedures. Later Greek alchemical authors cite **dye recipes**, but these are not necessarily securely all in the original authorship.
- **CRITICAL CAVEAT ON 'ENCRYPTION':** Dufault **p.89** calls geometric-musical sign substitutions a **rudimentary encryption method** but immediately explains **it is unlikely Africanus used them as encryption**, since ordinary learned Greek education could solve the system; perhaps a **learned game** or social marker.
- An explanatory **appendix with coded referents is lost**. This does NOT establish deliberate persecution avoidance, safety secrecy or universal incomprehensibility.
- **Consequence:** material-coloring techniques now attach to a named learned writer, but **Africanus is not a proven Alchemist station**. Dufault holds Zosimos as first identified Greek **alchemical commentator**.

## [W22-P01] British Library Papyrus 2040, `P.Oxy. III 412` (recto), `P.Oxy. VI 907` (verso)
https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002105408
- Museum-cataloged original Greek **papyrus roll fragment** found Oxyrhynchus; front has **Book 18 conclusion of Sextus Julius Africanus' `Kestoi`**, including purported omitted Odyssey ritual lines, a discussion of their authenticity, and **end title**.
- Back side written later (upside down, across fibers) contains a **will of Hermogenes alias Eudaemon dated 276 CE**. The BL overall manuscript catalog **225–276 CE**.
- The legal will mentions urban/rural property, family, enslaved persons and inheritance, **not** a chemical recipe. This is **reuse** after scholarly text; it gives a **firm later bound**, not a 230 CE exact copying date.
- **LIMITS:** no independent confirmation original Africanus wrote every line of the 'lost Homer' incantation, that the rite worked, or that the papyrus displays the geometric recipe notation (IT DOES NOT).

## [W22-P02] University of Würzburg, Kyprianos manuscripts DB, M759
https://www.coptic-magic.phil.uni-wuerzburg.de/index.php/manuscript/kyp-m759/
- `PGM XXIII`, `P.Oxy III 412` / British Library Papyrus 2040: categorizes front **literary/magical-formulary**, verso **legal**.
- Palaeography gives **225–275**, explains **`Kestoi` front after 221** and later reused **will 25 June–24 July 276**.
- Independent metadata clarifies actual witness date different from date of any original composition under Alexander Severus.

## [W22-S02] Dufault p.85–90, reported edition of *Kestoi*, Michael Psellus' later content summary, Syncellus' assessment
Same as W22-S01.
- Syncellus (around 800 CE) mentions **nine-book** `Kestoi` concerning medical, natural, agricultural and chemistry-related properties; a **24-book** scope is customary from other transmission evidence. **Do not claim all witnesses refer to one identical edition**.
- Psellus (11th c) later lists recipes with hair coloring and other topics, some possibly added over centuries.
- Book 7 in military anthology, Book 18 third-century Oxyrhynchus fragment, cross-sources dye fragments cited by alchemists.
- Specific **ingredient code** references geometries, music-note signs and absent explanatory appendix. Some examples involve dangerous toxins, **do not reproduce recipes**.

## [W22-S03] William Adler, 'Sextus Julius Africanus and the Roman Near East in the Third Century', *JTS* 55 (2004)
https://academic.oup.com/jts/article-abstract/55/2/520/1666515
- Explores Africanus' Romanized name, identity, relations with Edessa/Emmaus/Nicopolis, his Roman imperial patronage and **claim** to have designed an emperor's library near Pantheon.
- Earlier embassy typically 221; **date and identity of emperor debated**; physical original architectural building plan of imperial library remains uncertain.
- Patron/client status warrants literary analysis but not conclusion Africanus held a royal alchemist job.

## [W22-S04] Eusebius and Jerome, Africanus biographical/embassy testimony
https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0614.htm
- Africanus' Chronography / writings transmitted by later authors; some records give **221**, others **226** for Emmaus' Nicopolis restoration. Do not treat a late chronographic note as a surviving 221 grant document.
- Africanus corresponded with Origen about authenticity of Susanna passage in Daniel, showing an identifiable textual-critical intellectual rather than unbroken master goldmaking guild.

## [W22-S05] *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, Cassius Dio
https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/dio-cassius/
- Dio, known Roman author/official, serves consul again in **229 CE** under Alexander Severus; his identifiable author-station distinct from Africanus recipe scholarship.

## [W22-O01] Roman Provincial Coinage VI 10643 (temporary), Alexandria, **234/235 CE**
https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coins/6/10643
- Actual Roman-Egyptian **debased-silver tetradrachms** with Severus Alexander, **`L ΙΔ` regnal year 14**, reverse **Helios + Selene**. **Three extant specimen records** in catalog; proof of dated metal product and official current authority, **not making gold**.
- Mint iconography/imperial names do not validate every claim of patron's special knowledge.

## [W22-S06] Roman emperors and rapidly contested statuses in **238 CE**
https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/pupi.htm
- Scholarly retrospective online ancient-ruler encyclopedia: after Maximinus, **Gordian I & II**, **Pupienus & Balbinus**, and **Gordian III** compete/rule. Precise chronology controversial; Egypt's primary papyri often have dated imperial formulas that can constrain it.
- **Cannot** count as six consecutive equally accepted solo reigns or use ruler change to infer loss of *Kestoi* manuscripts.

## [W22-S07] Greek alchemical recipe papyri comparative, late third century
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352409X20305332
- Modern experimental archaeological study on **alum-based fake silver** recipes in *Leiden*/*Stockholm* texts dated broadly **late third/early fourth century** in scholarship.
- The test illustrates **surface/silver alloy resemblance vs elemental silver production**, but the original papyri MUST **not** be backdated to 221–240; see later future windows.
