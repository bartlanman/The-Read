# Window 27 — Critical Source Register, 321–340 CE

**Audit date:** 2026-10-18. **Evidence categories:** ATTESTED ORIGINAL OBJECT/INSCRIPTION; ANCIENT DOCUMENT IN LATER COMPILATION; SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION; CONTESTED; NO SECURE EVIDENCE. A surviving imperial **law in a fifth-/sixth-century code** is not an original **fourth-century physical inscription**. The **text's legal attribution** must not be treated as pristine proof of who signed it, especially when a date and emperor's death conflict. This dossier studies **Rome/Asia Minor/Italy/Africa**, not an independently dated Roman Egyptian alchemical workshop.

## W27-P01 — Orkistos, original stone civic dossier, confirmation dated 30 June 331

- **Ancient inscription:** CIL III 352 + 7000 = **ILS 6091** = **MAMA VII 305**; a **single physical stone-epigraphic dossier** containing several **distinct documents** (petitions/imperial rescripts), not four independent findspots. Full reproduced Latin with damage/restorations at [Treccani Enciclopedia Costantiniana, Constantinian Epigraphy](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/l-epigrafia-costantiniana-la-figura-di-costantino-e-la-propaganda-imperiale_%28Enciclopedia-Costantiniana%29/).
- **Timeline strictly differentiated:** first civic petition **324–326**; imperial approval/restoration to `civitas` generally placed **329/330**; **second original inscribed imperial reply specifically dated 30 June 331** recognizes Orkistos's restored city rank and forbids neighboring Nacoleia to demand money for **civic cults** (`pecuniam quam pro cultis ante solebatis inferre`). The **inscription dossier was likely carved around 331–332**, but exact engraving date does not coincide necessarily with all antecedent petitions. [André Chastagnol, *Ktema* 6 (1981)](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ktema_0221-5896_1981_num_6_1_1858); [additional Chastagnol study](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bsnaf_0081-1181_1982_num_1980_1_8857).
- **Original public recognition:** local inhabitants petitioned for reauthorization of autonomous **municipal station**; official ruling altered tax and cult contribution obligations. The petitioners reportedly claimed Christian identity; this is relevant to local reasoning, not proof they included alchemical practitioners. Inscriptions emphasize roads, **mills / aquimolina**, water, public buildings, commercial infrastructure, and civic offices—not experimental transmutation.
- **Grade:** ORIGINAL INSCRIPTION for the **text of the dated 331 authorization**. Earlier decisions as quoted in the same stone are **earlier embedded documents**, not independently surviving autographs. **No Alchemist, recipe, original laboratory or successful gold-conversion** appears in identified text.
- **Separate status from** Spello/Hispellum below: geographically distinct petitions with sometimes contrasting religious concessions. [ST-090]

## W27-P02 — Hispellum / Spello, original imperial-cult rescript, probably 333–337

- **Original inscribed Latin rescript:** **CIL XI 5265**, now Spello, found **1733**. [University of Grenoble Roman Law original Latin diplomatic text](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/Hispellates_CIL.htm); [Treccani Constantine epigraphy synthesis and cautious date](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/l-epigrafia-costantiniana-la-figura-di-costantino-e-la-propaganda-imperiale_%28Enciclopedia-Costantiniana%29/).
- **Dating:** usually **333–337 CE**, not exactly 335 without additional justification. The named imperial sons/titulature allow the range, but missing honorifics create contested more precise chronologies. This is the original **LOCAL inscribed copy** of imperial rescript, not an autograph written by Constantine.
- **Content:** Spello's petition described annual **priests** (`sacerdotes`) and theatrical and gladiatorial **shows** previously organized at Volsinii; requests local performance, permission to build a temple dedicated to the **Flavian dynasty**, and new civic name **Flavia Constans**. The rescript consents to local observances and temple **provided it is not polluted by the deceits of an objectionable `superstitio`** (`ne aedis ... contagiosae superstitionis fraudibus polluatur`), **while the Volsinii festival must continue**.
- **Scholarly interpretation of `superstitio`:** some read restriction of sacrificial practice under a Christian emperor; exact prohibited acts **not listed exhaustively** in the inscription. Gladiatorial-show permission appears, so do **not** claim Constantine eliminated the games in the text. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/l-epigrafia-costantiniana-la-figura-di-costantino-e-la-propaganda-imperiale_%28Enciclopedia-Costantiniana%29/).
- **Grade:** ORIGINAL C.333–337 PUBLIC PETITION/GRANT: local religious office, imperial memory and ritual access. **Not** a permit for an alchemical priest or evidence of chemical magic suppression. [ST-091]

## W27-T01 — CTh 13.4.1: Constantine's architects' training law, Africa, 334

- **Ancient legal text in LATER CODE:** [*Codex Theodosianus*, book XIII, 13.4.1, Latin](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Codex_Theodosianus/Liber_XIII); [translated paragraph with subscription](https://readancienttexts.com/theodosius/liber_xiii/1).
- **Legal attribution:** *Imp. Constantinus a. ad Felicem*. Proclaims shortage of architects in **African provinces**: `Architectis quam plurimis opus est; sed quia non sunt`. Encourages youths with some **liberal-letter education** (about eighteen years old) to study architecture; exempts them **and their parents** from certain burdens and orders appropriate financial allowance for learners. Published **VI Kal. Sep.** under **Optatus and Paulinus**, conventionally **27 August 334** (some editions mark date's reconstruction with question marks).
- **Witness dates:** purported **334 edict**; extant **438 Theodosian compilation** and later manuscript copies/printed editions, **NOT excavated 334 tablet**. Training explicitly recognized by government, **architect role**, not a certified Alchemist.
- **Grade:** transmitted dated ancient regulation; applied scope/general effects and numbers of trained students cannot be independently established by the legal text alone. [ST-092]

## W27-T02 — CTh 13.4.2 = CJ 10.66.1, a dated 2 August 337 crafts exemption with posthumous attribution problem

- **Theodosian Latin, 13.4.2:** [Book XIII](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Codex_Theodosianus/Liber_XIII), emperor cited by editorial `Idem a.` (same Augustus as preceding law), addressed `ad Maximum praefectum praetorio`, **`Artifices artium brevi subdito comprehensarum ... ab universis muneribus vacare praecipimus`** (artisans on appended list should be free from public burdens) **`quo magis cupiant et ipsi peritiores fieri et suos filios erudire`** (so they may improve and teach sons). Subscription **`IIII Non. Aug. Feliciano et Titiano conss.` = 2 August 337**.
- **Justinianic re-transmission and actual list:** [*Codex Justinianus* 10.66.1 (Krueger edition)](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Corpus/CJ10.htm), preserving craft names including **`aurifices` (goldsmiths), `deauratores` (gilders), `argentarii` (silversmiths), `fusores` (casters), `aerarii` (bronze workers), `vitrearii` (glassworkers), `fullones` (fullers), `blattearii` (gold-leaf/gold-beating specialists)** among many others; **NO `chēmeutai` / Alchemist** appears. These are categories of artisans, not certificates of success in transmutation.
- **Critical historical contradiction:** Constantine died **22 May 337**; yet laws **CTh 13.4.2 / CJ 10.66.1** attribute this **2 August 337** measure to `Constantinus`. **Do NOT say Constantine personally signed it 2 August**. Historian **R.W. Burgess, *Dumbarton Oaks Papers* 62 (2008), 5–51, pp.28–30**, argues likely issued in the dead emperor's name during succession delay: [Burgess original scholarly article PDF](https://constantinethegreatcoins.com/articles/Burgess_THE_SUMMER_OF_BLOOD_The_Great_Massacre_of_337_and_the_Promotion_of_the_Sons_of_Constantine.pdf). **Noel Lenski, *Constantine and the Cities* (2016), chapter on 337**, cautions `idem Augustus` heading only follows preceding law in codex; it is **ambiguous evidence** for personal posthumous issuance, not incontestable proof of a surviving "ghost emperor" appointment. [Relevant cited discussion](https://studylib.net/doc/27889103/-empire-and-after--noel-lenski---constantine-and-the-citi...).
- **Evidentiary statuses:** transmitted **legal directive and craft roster** are present in separate later legal compilations (**NOT independent original 337 inscriptions**); **subscription** confidently says August 337 in standard editions; **attribution to living Constantine is impossible** if date preserved; hypotheses include posthumous imperial naming, misplaced header, revised/subscription date or copying/editorial error. Distinguish fact of legal-preserved text from exactly who issued it and how widely it was implemented.
- **Term `munera`:** civic obligations/compulsory service, not all taxes in every circumstance; ability to train and teach is government **purpose** in the text, not independently confirmed training results. [ST-093]

## W27-O01 — Original gold solidus of Constantine, Nicomedia, 335 CE

- [Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, Münzkabinett **18200650**](https://ikmk.smb.museum/object?id=18200650): **struck gold solidus**, **4.54 grams**, **22 mm**, mint **Nicomedia**, **335 CE**, RIC VII Nicomedia **179**. Imperial bust, reverse `VICTORIA CONSTANTINI AVG` and round shield `VOT / XXX` celebrating the thirty-year jubilee, with figure Victory and Genius.
- [British Museum 1844,1015.310](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1844-1015-310) is another **real struck gold solidus**, Nicomedia **335**, ~**4.56 g**, iconographically related but **RIC VII 177**, a different detailed catalog type, not falsely called the same type.
- **Original physical metal artifact** attests imperial mint's possession, forming and striking of **existing gold**, not any base-metal-to-gold operation, engineer's identity, named guild, or actual policy response by a local alchemist. The gold is an observable *metallic product*, not attested laboratory synthesis. [ST-094]

## W27-S01 — Constantine's Orkistos and Hispellum petitions: contrasting legal recognition

- [Treccani, Constantinian epigraphy](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/l-epigrafia-costantiniana-la-figura-di-costantino-e-la-propaganda-imperiale_%28Enciclopedia-Costantiniana%29/), works through original Latin texts, details **Orkistos 331** exemption from *pecunia pro cultis* and **Hispellum c.333–337** local dynastic temple permission subject to a superstition clause.
- [Chastagnol 1981, Orkistos](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ktema_0221-5896_1981_num_6_1_1858), distinguishes prior petitions 324–326, first approval 329–330 and second response 30 June 331; broader chronological span DOES NOT convert all events into 331.
- [JRS article *A legal exemption for the temple cults of Rome and Alexandria in the fourth century* (2025)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-roman-studies/article/legal-exemption-for-the-temple-cults-of-rome-and-alexandria-in-the-fourth-century/8FCF6B2789837BED8D95874E77605DC5) discusses the two 330s rescripts as evidence of **different city/religion interactions**, not uniform blanket suppression of every pagan ritual group. **Do not backdate the 2025 analysis or claim an alchemist was targeted**.

## W27-S02 — Boundary checks in alchemical text chronology

- [Dufault, *Early Greek Alchemy, Patronage and Innovation in Late Antiquity* (2019)](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ks0g83x): **Zosimos of Panopolis c.300**, a named alchemical **commentator** who addressed Theosebeia in much later transmitted writings; 330s public legal title/payroll does **not** identify him or document a 334 teaching session.
- Leiden X/Stockholm original Greek recipe papyri **broadly late third/early fourth century** rather than precisely 330; material technique such as metal coloring/gilding differs from elemental transmutation. **Maria** and **Pseudo-Democritus** names are earlier source traditions with attribution/date caveats.
- A later commentator called **Olympiodorus the Alchemist**, associated with Zosimos, has heavily debated late antique dating and namesake confusion: [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Olympiodorus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/olympiodorus/). It is NOT warranted to put Olympiodorus's known commentary into 321–340.
- **No securely identified 321–340 Egyptian original Alchemist workshop, named teaching contract, chemical manuscript with certain 334 copy date, record of a gold-making success or alchemical prosecution in the sources audited here.**

## Explicit remainder

1. Legal text **CTh 13.4.2**: exact issuer of 2 August 337 and whether `Idem Augustus` preserves authentic heading vs editorial harmonization. Critical issue must remain **CONTESTED** and noted in register.
2. Hispellum's precise year and what `superstitio` prohibited; do not equate all traditional religious activity with legally persecuted magic.
3. Orkistos's original inscription *carving date* and first petition history vs secure **331** second imperial response.
4. Which actual precious-metal workers received `munera` exemptions; surviving law expresses permission/intent, not lists of individual beneficiaries.
5. Exact dates of Zosimos and physical recipe papyri; no Greek–Chinese lineage inferred.
6. A gold **solidus** is not proof of **chrysopoeia**; no analytical element-change result in period.
