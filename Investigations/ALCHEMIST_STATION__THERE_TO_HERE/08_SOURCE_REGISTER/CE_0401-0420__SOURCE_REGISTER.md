# Window 31 — Source and Witness Register, 401–420 CE

**Investigation date:** 2026-10-18. **Rule:** distinguish an ancient text's approximate composition, the later surviving manuscript, modern translation, and the alleged date of actions it mentions. Primary authored letters are strong evidence of what the writer **said/requested** but not independent proof that the requested object was built. Near-contemporary historical narration is not an original 415 court transcript. **No original 401–420 Alchemist's employment register, contemporaneous alchemical manuscript with a known 410 copying date, authenticated chrysopoeia product, or alchemy-specific Hypatia prosecution identified.**

**Grades:** ATTESTED AUTHORED LETTER (later manuscript); CONTEMPORARY/NEAR-CONTEMPORARY TEXTUAL CLAIM; LATER HISTORICAL REPORT; SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION; CONTESTED DATE; NO SECURE EVIDENCE.

## W31-P01 — Synesius, *Letter 15* to “the Philosopher” (identified as Hypatia), *hydroskopion*

- [Fitzgerald English translation of original ancient letter](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Letters_of_Synesius_of_Cyrene/Letter_15); [Livius primary-letter translation and **402** date](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/synesius/synesius-letter-015/); [Roman Letters full text with date `c.402` and transparent note that original Greek text is not yet included](https://scholarly.romanletters.org/letters/synesius_cyrene/15/).
- **Specific request:** Synesius asks former teacher Hypatia to arrange for a **brass cast and assembled `hydroscope`**—a floating **cylindrical fluted tube** marked with **graduations**, fitted with a cone (`baryllium` in English rendering), used to compare weights/specific gravities of waters or liquids. The letter is a **detailed specification sent to a person with technical/intellectual authority**.
- **What it actually establishes:** **documented technical communication, proposed instrument design, manufacturing request and trust in Hypatia to organize production**. **It does NOT independently establish that Hypatia invented the hydrometer, designed this specific one, personally cast brass, delivered it, demonstrated accuracy, or conducted an alchemical metal-transmutation experiment.** Nor does `hydroscope` here mean modern optical equipment.
- **Date problem:** date **402** follows Livius's modern editorial chronology; **no independently engraved 402 issue date in the supplied translated letter**. Fitzgerald footnotes discuss uncertain purpose/type; some modern letter collations place a cluster of Synesius's distressed letters **c.413**, so **DATE CONTESTED WITHIN 401–413**, not securely exactly 402. A modern chronological label is not an original dated autograph. **Original ancient Greek composition; later Greek MSS, 20th-century printed/translated witness**.
- **Experiment distinction:** comparison of **density/specific gravity** is a quantitative phenomenon accessible to actual measurement; the text gives mechanism, **not recorded observations/calibration, buoyancy graph or chemical assay**. [ST-111]

## W31-P02 — Synesius, *Letter 154* to Hypatia: a documented intellectual gatekeeper

- [Full translated primary-source letter, Fitzgerald/Livius](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/synesius/synesius-letter-154/); [translated source and full letter, Roman Letters](https://scholarly.romanletters.org/letters/synesius_cyrene/154/); [older complete public-domain text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Letter_to_Hypatia_VII).
- **Action claimed:** Synesius sends **two manuscripts** (generally identified as **`Dion` and `On Dreams`**), plus an earlier essay **`On the Gift`**, to Hypatia, calling for her **verdict before publication**. He says she can either endorse or reject circulation. This is a named writer seeking **evaluation from a recognized philosopher**, a strong positive **social authorization relationship** rather than imagined “secret guild”.
- **Competing dates:** some editorial site chronologies give **c.413**, while other scholarly reconstructed chronological placements are **around 404–405**. Exact issue date does **not** appear in the excerpt as a consular year; **DEBATABLE**, but 401–420 is reasonable range given Synesius's life. Do not claim Letter 154 was definitely written in 413 or that Synesius lived to see 415.
- The “if it deserves darkness/not publishing” rhetoric expresses **editorial discretion/peer review**, not demonstrable effective cryptographic secrecy, destroyed alchemical manuscripts or proof Hypatia wrote the `Synesius-to-Dioscorus` pseudonymous alchemical dialogue.
- **Other contextual letter:** [*Letter 81* Hypatia](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Letters_of_Synesius_of_Cyrene/Letter_81) recommends a young man into her intellectual care and connects friendship/patronage to teaching; exact relative date debated. [ST-112]

## W31-P03 — Synesius's *Letter 105*: philosophical reservations entering episcopal office

- [Primary letter in published translation, Livius](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/synesius/synesius-letter-105/). Synesius discusses his proposed installation as **bishop**, possible clash between received religious beliefs and **philosophical commitments**, a desire to remain married and reservations about publicly stating doctrines he cannot sincerely affirm. Explicitly puts his conditions before **Theophilus of Alexandria** through intermediaries.
- [Oxford Classical Dictionary, *Synesius of Cyrene*](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/550532814) describes bishop of **Ptolemais c.410–413**, pupil of Hypatia, corpus of 156 letters/hymns/orations. [Cameron & Long, *Barbarians and Politics at the Court of Arcadius* (1993) chronology](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/barbarians-and-politics-at-the-court-of-arcadius.html): elected **410**, consecrated **411**, died **spring 413**, a modern scholarly reconstruction, not original dated death inscription.
- **Critical quote/interpretation:** `truth ought to remain hidden` rhetoric in *Letter 105* reflects concern about **philosophical vs pastoral speaking to differently prepared audiences**. It is not a coded alchemical formula, evidence of an occult initiatory office or a physiological danger rule. **His episcopal station has independent plausibly datable evidence; the “Synesius” of later alchemical commentary is an entirely different, contested textual attribution.** [ST-113]

## W31-P04 — *Synesius, On the Gift*, astrolabe physically made in Alexandria but EARLIER

- [Cameron & Long 1993 chronological reconstruction](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/barbarians-and-politics-at-the-court-of-arcadius.html): during embassy to Constantinople **c.397–398**, Synesius gave imperial court patron **Paeonius** a **silver astrolabe** manufactured in Alexandria; the work `On the Gift / De dono` was composed then. In [*Letter 154*](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/synesius/synesius-letter-154/) written **later in this window**, he refers explicitly to this **older embassy-period** technical and rhetorical gift.
- **Chronology discipline:** don't date the original astrolabe's manufacture to **413** because it is referred to in a letter that may have been written then. This is legitimate **pre-401 cross-window technical craft evidence** recalled by a 401–413 author; separate original early manufacturing, later mention, surviving physical artifact **NOT IDENTIFIED**.
- Silver astrolabe: demonstrates a recorded **claim of commissioning, workmanship and patron gift** much more directly than alchemical claims, but none of this identifies a chrysopoeia practitioner or an elemental silver-from-copper operation.

## W31-P05 — Socrates Scholasticus, *Ecclesiastical History* VII.15: Hypatia's murder, March 415

- [Socrates *Church History* Book VII, chapter 15 primary translated narrative](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/26017.htm); [parallel primary textual translation with footnotes](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nicene_and_Post-Nicene_Fathers%3A_Series_II/Volume_II/Socrates/Book_VII/Chapter_15); [source editions referring to original Greek](https://earlychurchtexts.com/main/socrates/murder_of_hypatia.shtml).
- **Event date:** **March, during Lent, in tenth consulate of Honorius and sixth of Theodosius II**, firmly resolves **415**; the occasional secondary-site claim **413/414** conflicts with the consular dating and is **not preferred**. Distinguish event 415 from author Socrates writing his history **c.439–440**, after Hypatia's death and after Synesius's apparent death c.413.
- **Socrates says** she publicly instructed students, enjoyed public access/esteem and communicated with imperial prefect **Orestes**; some Christians accused her of obstructing reconciliation between Orestes and bishop **Cyril**. A mob headed by **Peter “the reader”** seized and brutally murdered her, destroyed/burned remains; Socrates condemns the deed as a scandal for church and its bishop. **The text does NOT demonstrate Cyril personally issued a murder order** or that the accused owned alchemical equipment.
- **Witness type:** historian's near-generation **retrospective Christian narrative** (roughly 25 years later), later extant copies; not a contemporaneous 415 autographed eyewitness confession, original court docket or proven location of every named participant.
- **Do not misclassify:** loss of Hypatia as a teacher/philosopher is real, and violent political/religious conflict is strongly sourced. Nothing in Socrates identifies her as an Alchemist, directs the attack against chrysopoeia, or proves all Alexandrian technical schools closed immediately. [ST-114]

## W31-S01 — Theon/Hypatia mathematical editing attribution: distinguish surviving textual superscription from laboratory

- [Research on Theon of Alexandria and Hypatia, Michael Lambrou (2004)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228609702_Theon_of_Alexandria_and_Hypatia); [scholarly 2025 source-critical reconstruction](https://ejournals.epublishing.ekt.gr/index.php/dianoesis/article/view/41703); [1987 study of rediscovered Theon *Almagest* commentary Book V](https://www.persee.fr/doc/antiq_0770-2817_1987_num_56_1_2208).
- Later manuscript headings of **Theon's commentary on Ptolemy's *Almagest* Book III** mention revision/reading with his daughter Hypatia (`paranagnostheisa`); precise editorial role debated and text's copied headings are not Hypatia's original personally signed manuscript. Hypatia's Diophantus/Apollonius/Ptolemy titles often come from later sources (e.g., tenth-century *Suda*). They are **mathematics/astronomy, not evidence of alchemical metallurgical work**; source-critical uncertainty required.

## W31-S02 — Synesius/Dioscorus alchemical manuscript attribution boundary from Window 30

- [Window 30 alchemical original-witness discussion](CE_0381-0400__SOURCE_REGISTER.md); [Martelli and Dorandi 2011 critical text notices, original later Greek manuscripts](https://pinakes-dev.irht.cnrs.fr/notices/bibliographie/J8SKCZW8/); [Cameron and Long 1993](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/barbarians-and-politics-at-the-court-of-arcadius.html).
- The work addressed **to Dioscorus, priest of Serapis** is a disputed **alchemical commentary** of unknown original date, later Greek medieval transmission; the well-documented **Hypatia pupil and bishop Synesius of Cyrene** must **not automatically be identified as that alchemical author**. No independently dated 405 alchemical autograph, apprentice claim, or original material workshop receipt.

## Source-quality summary and necessary remainder

1. **Earliest named technical request to Hypatia:** **Synesius Letter 15**, material/mechanics form described; modern date **~402 contested**; actual instrument's finished state, inventor and delivery unknown.
2. **Earliest named explicit evaluation relationship:** **Letter 154**, two works sent to Hypatia for judgment/publication discretion; date **~404–413 debated**, **not an alchemical peer-review case**.
3. **Two stations in one real individual:** **Synesius philosopher/pupil and bishop c.410/411–413** with authorial reservations in his own Letter 105; that bridge is **genuine biographical evidence**, but **not** a bridge to a named Alchemist.
4. **415 murder:** Hypatia a public philosopher and teacher, murdered amid Cyril/Orestes political conflict, source Socrates c.440. **A tragic documented station-level attack**, **not shown to be caused by an alchemy decree**.
5. **No observed original period alchemy worker with registered authorization or verifiable transmutation**. No evidence the pseudo-Synesius dialogue was written because Hypatia died, because Serapeum was destroyed (391), or to hide a technique.
6. **The Outside:** the same teacher could remain authoritative to philosophers/pupils amid religious changes; state/church politics could threaten individual scholarly figures. **Counterfactual leap prohibited:** “Hypatia was killed” does not show “science/alchemy ended.”
