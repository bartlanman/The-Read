# Window 12 historical sources — 21–40 CE

**Pass date:** 2026-10-18. Distinguish the event date, author date, manuscript transmission and modern interpretation. No evidence of a named Greek-Egyptian Alchemist within this precise window.

## [P01] Hou Hanshu — Du Shi biography
- Chinese text: https://www.njmuseum.org.cn/files/wshl/rbbook/gb/25/houhanshu/hhs35.html
- The biography credits Du Shi in the seventh year of Jianwu (**31 CE**) as Nanyang governor with making a `水排`, water-powered bellows for casting farm tools. It claims less labor and greater benefit: `造作水排，鑄為農器，用力少，見功多，百姓便之`.
- The explanation of water driving a furnace blower (`冶鑄者為排以吹炭，今激水以鼓之也`) is from a **later commentary**, not an engineer's dated 31 CE notebook.
- The transmitted history was compiled mainly by Fan Ye in the **fifth century CE**, and later copied.
- **Supports:** a historical report of a concrete mechanical device, public-use output, official credit.
- **Cannot establish:** intact original machine, exact mechanism, experimentally measured benefit, names of foundry workers or Du Shi's sole personal invention.

## [C01] Rafe de Crespigny — Later Han chronology
https://backendstaging.chinesereferenceshelf.brillonline.com/dictionary-qin-and-han/prelims/sim-200005
- Wang Mang's Xin dynasty falls **23 CE**; Gengshi claims rule 23–25; Guangwu establishes Eastern Han **25 CE**; political consolidation continues to **36 CE**.
- **Not** a primary testimony of craft-worker continuity.

## [C02] Nanyang Municipal Government — Du Shi public history
https://lyj.nanyang.gov.cn/2024/04-08/402876.html
- Secondary retelling linking Du Shi's 31 CE office with water-powered metallurgy. Local public-history source, not independently recovered 31 CE archaeological machinery.

## [E01] Philo of Alexandria, In Flaccum — events in 38 CE
https://www.posenlibrary.com/entry/riots-alexandria-38-ce
- A Jewish Alexandrian author wrote around **39–40 CE** about anti-Jewish violence in **38 CE** and the role of Roman prefect Aulus Avilius Flaccus.
- **Warrants:** near-contemporary politically situated witness of religious/civic exclusion.
- **No warrant:** a persecution of metal-transmuting practitioners.

## [E02] Background on Alexandrian crisis
https://www.posenlibrary.com/entry/background-riots-alexandria
- Political rivalry, visit of Agrippa, and legitimacy of Alexandrian Jews disputed. This is **not** technical alchemical evidence.

## [E03] Richard Alston, Philo's In Flaccum (Greece & Rome 44:2, 1997)
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/greece-and-rome/article/abs/philos-in-flaccum-ethnicity-and-social-space-in-roman-alexandria/632A6C528EB03BF3778B360A26B4FDE3
- Examines social space and the political construction of ethnicity. Modern analysis; the article's later online publication date does not replace **1997** as its original publication.

## [G01] Matteo Martelli, The Four Books of Pseudo-Democritus (2014)
https://www.routledge.com/The-Four-Books-of-Pseudo-Democritus/Martelli/p/book/9781909662285
- First-century CE broadly; a more restrictive scholarly dating places origin **after its first half, potentially during Nero (54–68 CE)**.
- Scholarly review: https://brill.com/previewpdf/journals/esm/21/4/article-p354_5.xml
- **Guard:** broad first-century date is not proof of composition in **21–40 CE**.

## Source gaps
Search excavated Nanyang furnace remains, administrative records of actual workers, dated Roman-Egyptian craft contracts, and independent near-contemporary witnesses to the technology. A report of practical competence is stronger than untested wonder claims but is not itself modern experimental replication.
