# Source Register — Window 13 (41–60 CE)

**Evidence check:** 2026-10-18. **Source-critical rule:** Distinguish the dated 41 CE papyrus from the *undated* original Greek alchemical corpus preserved in significantly later manuscripts and citations. Literary concealment in a narrative is not proof of a historical hiding place.

## [W13-P01] P.Lond. VI 1912 = British Library Papyrus 2248 — Claudius' letter to Alexandria
- **Ancient event:** Roman emperor Claudius writes to the Alexandrians after the civic and anti-Jewish conflict of 38 CE. **On 10 November 41 CE**, the prefect Lucius Aemilius Rectus ordered publication of the letter; the date is assigned by the source's public-prefectural instruction.
- **Material witness:** Greek papyrus manuscript from the period, **P.Lond. VI 1912 / British Library Papyrus 2248**, with tax records on the other side from **37–38 CE**.
- **British Library primary digitization:** https://www.bl.uk/manuscripts/FullDisplay.aspx?ref=Papyrus_2248
- **Text and translated primary source excerpt:** https://www.attalus.org/docs/other/claudius_letter.html
- **British Library historical account / School Collection:** https://www.bl.uk/collection-items/letter-of-claudius-to-the-alexandrians
- **Warrants:** Claudius’ actual civic intervention as copied in Egypt and prefectural decision to display/disseminate it; material example of authorized public writing and reused papyrus.
- **Does not warrant:** a coded alchemical recipe, an alchemical guild under Claudius, general consensus on the letter's political justification, or an alchemical classroom.
- **Editorial note:** some BL page addresses and access modes have changed; inspect material witness / catalogue separately if original web listing is unavailable.

## [W13-P02] Roman Egypt primary and modern context of Claudius' letter
- Published as **P.Lond. VI 1912**; available in ancient Greek in documentary papyrus repositories (Duke Databank of Documentary Papyri, papyri.info).
- **Papyri.info identifier query:** https://papyri.info/ddbdp/p.lond;6;1912
- **Subject:** settlement of Alexandria civic petitions and disputes involving Jews and Greek inhabitants after 38 CE.
- **Limit:** this is a 41 CE state authorization; it is not direct corroboration for first-century alchemical claims.

## [W13-S01] Matteo Martelli, *The Four Books of Pseudo-Democritus* (2014; Routledge/Maney critical work)
- Edition/bibliography: https://www.routledge.com/The-Four-Books-of-Pseudo-Democritus/Martelli/p/book/9781909662285
- **Warrants:** a reconstruction of Greek alchemical recipe corpus known as *Four Books*, covering gold, silver, precious stones and purple textiles; **original text not intact**; later Greek/Syriac witnesses, excerpts and reception must be collated.
- **Dating:** broadly first century CE; later-first-century hypothesis, **possibly during Nero 54–68 CE**, is not exact year attestation, and neither an original manuscript nor a colophon proves 41–60.
- **Does not warrant:** actual ancient Democritus (c.5th–4th c BCE) as author, historical Ostanes' actual mentorship or a 54 CE original document.

## [W13-S02] *Early Science and Medicine* review of Martelli (2016)
- Link: https://brill.com/previewpdf/journals/esm/21/4/article-p354_5.xml
- **Warrants:** summary of Martelli's argument for later-first-century origins (possibly Nero), as opposed to falsely dating original treatises to early Roman decades merely because of generic “first century.”
- **Limit:** a review is secondary and does not independently prove one of the candidate years; check Martelli's full critical Greek/Syriac edition.

## [W13-S03] Olivier Dufault, *Early Greek Alchemy, Patronage and Innovation in Late Antiquity* (2019)
- University of California open access: https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ks0g83x
- **Warrants:** source-critical overview of early Greek alchemical authorial traditions, pseudonymity, reception and later patronage; ask whether the later literary constructed master corresponded to a demonstrable institution.
- **Limit:** a general historiography does not prove a named state-licensed alchemist in 41–60.

## [W13-S04] *Physika kai mystika* / Democritean initiation passage, later transmitted
- The passage about the master who dies, inquiry by pupils, recovered temple-hidden books and maxims about **nature** is contained in later Greek alchemical text tradition, associated with Pseudo-Democritus.
- Relevant modern critical discussion: Martelli *Four Books* [W13-S01] and Dufault [W13-S03].
- **Specific caution:** some reconstructions identify the unnamed/implicit master with Persian magus **Ostanes**. The teacher's name and wording vary by witness/reception. Treat the **temple pillar story as a textual claim**, not evidence a physical column containing a manuscript was discovered.
- **Interpretation options:** initiatory prestige, staged revelation of authority, technical interpretation of natural processes. **No direct evidence** of actual ingredient cipher, safety secrecy, fear of persecution or institutional temple monopoly in 41–60.

## [W13-S05] Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* XXX — Ostanes and Democritus
- Latin: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/L/Roman/Texts/Pliny_the_Elder/30*.html
- **Composition:** about 77 CE, **outside this window**.
- **Warrant:** a later Roman natural historian's association of Ostanes and Democritus with exotic magical authority; a background for how such prestigious names circulated.
- **Cannot warrant:** direct eyewitness testimony about a Greek alchemist's craft performed in 54–60 CE.

## Essential next tests
1. Compare Martelli's critical edition with actual Greek and Syriac variants of the *nature–nature* maxim: extract original word order and whether Ostanes is named in each witness.
2. Establish independently supported terminus post quem and ante quem for the *Four Books*; do not force 54–60 CE as proven composition.
3. Read Greek papyrus P.Lond. VI 1912 and its prefectural promulgation in full, distinguishing date of text, physical copy and later publishing history.
4. Identify specific chemical recipes that are demonstrably recoverable from ancient witnesses, and compare with the later legendary hidden-book narrative rather than assuming a common technique.
5. Preserve China–Greek Egypt non-genealogical relationship and do not treat the Egyptian imperial papyrus as an alchemical technical artifact.

## Primary papyrus catalog verification (2026-10-18)
- **British Library official catalog:** https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002105459 — Papyrus 2248, recto *tax register* from **37/38 CE**, verso **Claudius' letter plus prefect Rectus' publication edict dated 10 November 41 CE**. The catalog specifies columns 2 (edict) and 3–6 (emperor's letter), ancient Greek writing on papyrus, original place of the earlier register Philadelpheia in Arsinoite nome. **This is the preferred authoritative current catalog URI**, superseding older `bl.uk/manuscripts/FullDisplay.aspx` references which may be retired.
- **Edict Greek text:** https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Edicta/Aegypti33_.gr.html — prefect explains that a first public reading could not be attended by all and therefore orders display so each person may read.
- **Translated ancient text:** https://attalus.org/docs/select2/p212.html — P.Lond. VI 1912 and the public-edict paragraph.
- **Important:** the dated edict and copied imperial letter are the **opposite of a purported secret alchemical book**, and their shared century does not establish any historical connection.

- Australian National University scholarly source (chronological table): https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/fcf6cff2-9d85-4b28-add3-f9b4baddc9fd/content
