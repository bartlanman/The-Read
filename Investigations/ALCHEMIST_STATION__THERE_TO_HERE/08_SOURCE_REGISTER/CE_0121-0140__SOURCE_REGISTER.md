# Source Register — Window 17 (121–140 CE)

**Audit:** 2026-10-18. Original 130 CE monument and 133 CE papyri weighted above much later memoir/letter; Greek–Han parallel no presumed technical connection.

## [W17-P01] Julia Balbilla's four Memnon inscriptions, original 130 CE carvings
- University of London Press chapter, with epigraphic history/dates: https://read.uolpress.co.uk/read/open-data-in-ancient-and-byzantine-studies/section/985b9b47-8660-41dc-b436-1119656df982
- Oxford Classical Dictionary entry, Patricia A. Rosenmeyer (rev.2024): https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/549657039
- Four Greek epigrams **54 lines** attributed to Julia Balbilla, carved onto left leg of **Colossus of Amenhotep III**, 130 CE, during Hadrian/Sabina's Egyptian visit.
- **Exact date:** 25th Hathyr = **21 November 130 CE** on one poem; sites and calendar conversion require edition for the others.
- **Warrants:** poet's name, Greek Aeolic imitation of Sappho, testimony she and emperor heard or failed to hear a sound, political/mythic interpretation, durable contemporaneous written object.
- **Does not warrant:** god's literal voice, divine control of emperor, exact physical cause of sound, chemical transmutation or an alchemist.

## [W17-P02] P. A. Rosenmeyer, *The Language of Ruins* (Oxford UP 2018)
- https://academic.oup.com/book/9966/chapter-abstract/157323160
- Contemporary Greek/Latin inscriptions on monumental Egypt; suggests thermal stone expansion as mechanism for dawn sound, but physical mechanism remains interpretation.
- **Ancient original building:** Amenhotep III's enormous sandstone pair dates far earlier, about 14th c BCE; the *'Memnon'* re-identification was Greek/Roman rather than original Egyptian dedicatory identity.
- **Caution:** a modern physics interpretation does NOT falsify the actual Greek poet's sensory experience, only challenges her claimed sacred explanation.

## [W17-E01] Hadrian, Antinous and Egypt in 130 CE
- Historical synthesis: https://www.hermopolis.gwi.uni-muenchen.de/antinoopolis_eng/
- Antinous died while Hadrian traveled Nile. Causes accident vs deliberate sacrifice in ancient/later stories **UNVERIFIED**; no evidence involving alchemical occult initiation.

## [W17-E02] Antinoopolis foundation **30 October 130 CE**
- Mary T. Boatwright, *Hadrian and the Cities of the Roman Empire*, chapter 'City Foundations, New and Renewed': https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv346svn
- Academic historical synthesis from Heidelberg/LMU: https://www.hermopolis.gwi.uni-muenchen.de/antinoopolis_eng/
- Academic University of Basel city research: https://romegyptcities.philhist.unibas.ch/en/cities/antinoopolis/
- *Warrant:* historical city foundation during visit, urban and civic-status change; date well accepted. Not evidence of registered alchemical guild.

## [W17-E03] Early Antinoopolis settlement papyri, April/May **133 CE**
- University of Eichstaett ancient map text with precise IDs: https://www1.ku.de/ggf/ag/tabula_peutingeriana/trefferanzeige_en.php?id=2838
- **P.L.Bat. 6,29 = P.Tebt. fam. 29**, dated **1 April 133 CE**; **P.L.Bat. 6,30 = SB 5.7603 = P.Tebt. fam. 30**, **1 May 133 CE**. These are original-period documentary evidence of settlement/new civic privileges.
- Later documented label **'Antinoites New Greeks'** (`Ἀντινοεῖς Νέοι Ἕλληνες`) appears in subsequent papyri (not all in 133). **Do not present exact later labels as if carved in 130.**

## [W17-E04] Antinoopolis town council source and 2nd-century civic evidence
- Society for Classical Studies papyrology presentation: https://classicalstudies.org/pmich-inv-975-and-papyri-involving-town-council-antinoopolis
- H.I. Bell, *Antinoopolis: A Hadrianic Foundation in Egypt*, *JRS* 30 (1940):133–147: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-roman-studies/article/abs/antinoopolis-a-hadrianic-foundation-in-egypt1/A2DF6A2DC4238FB2329D17B7323213FD
- Documents show civic institutions, but often from **later 2nd-century/after 200 CE**; do not backdate all offices to exact foundation day.

## [W17-H01] `Hou Hanshu` Zhang Heng biography (`候風地動儀`), 132 CE
- Henan Museum transcription: https://www.chnmus.net/clzl/2006-03/23/content_50595.htm
- Later `Hou Hanshu` chiefly compiled **5th century CE** describes `陽嘉元年` (132 CE) construction, bronze vessel, eight dragons holding balls, toads, obscured internal mechanism, subsequent directional earthquake report.
- **Crucial source limit:** *original apparatus has not been recovered.* A later description is not an engineer's own notebook dated 132 CE.

## [W17-H02] K.-H. Hsiao and H.-S. Yan (2009), Review of reconstruction designs
- DOI https://doi.org/10.5610/jaee.9.4_1
- Different proposed architectures: suspended pendulum, inverted pendulum, direct contact mechanisms, etc. **Multiple plausible modern variants ≠ recovery of original**.

## [W17-H03] R. Feng (2009) on reengineering
- https://www.cpsjournals.cn/article/doi/10.3321/j.issn:0379-4148.2009.07.004
- Uses further textual witnesses and claims the **Longxi event was 13 December 134 CE**, original mechanism likely suspended pendulum. Treat precise success/date as **scholarly argued retrospective chronology**, not a recovered 134 CE operational log. Another paper discusses possible variants.
- **No gold, chemical transmutation or Greek link**.

## [W17-F01] Historia Augusta, *Quadriga Tyrannorum* 8, purported Hadrian letter to Servianus
- Full Latin/English with scholarly notes: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Historia_Augusta/Firmus_et_al%2A.html
- Supposed writer **Hadrian** talks of Alexandria's **glass-blowers**, papyrus and linen workers, Serapis/Christian intermixing and **variegated cups whose colors change** given by a priest.
- **SOURCE DATE:** `Historia Augusta` is a late-fourth-century-ish pseudohistorical compilation; ancient attribution of the letter to Hadrian/Phlegon is **not authenticated**.
- **NOT a source:** It cannot on its own establish a 130 CE glassmaking technique, color-changing cup, actual imperial encounter with artisan, the market composition of Alexandria in this window, or 130 CE 'secret cup' technology.

## [W17-F02] J. Schwartz, 'La lettre de l'empereur Hadrien à son beau-frère selon l'Histoire Auguste' (1992)
- https://www.persee.fr/doc/efr_0000-0000_1992_act_159_1_4224
- Review of modern philological studies by Syme, Schmid, Schwartz concluding the purported Hadrian letter is a **literary forgery**, reflecting late-antique concerns.
- **Excellent control for historian:** A prestigious imperial voice can be placed on a later false document just as Pseudo-Democritus' ancient philosopher name can be attached to a much later work. **Do not thereby equate authors or source histories.**

## [W17-G01] Olivier Dufault, *Early Greek Alchemy* (2019)
- https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ks0g83x
- Distinguishes pseudoepigraphic ancient recipe authors and later commentators; surviving earliest Greek commentary author Zosimos near c.300 CE, Maria mostly indirectly preserved and own lifetime uncertain.
- No evidence of 121–140 named alchemical state station.

## [W17-G02] Raphael Patai, *The Jewish Alchemists* (1994), Maria section
- https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7zvb6p.11
- Maria's life cannot be precisely dated to Hadrian; citations by Zosimos establish an earlier textual reputation, not contemporary 130 CE manuscripts.
