# Window 01 source register — 200–181 BCE

**Research pass:** 2026-10-18  
**Scope:** Greek/Egyptian textual evidence and modern scholarly reconstruction relevant to the *conditions of station*, not proof of the first alchemist.  
**Evidence format:** Primary (P) and secondary/catalog (S). Stable URLs and details are provided so later researchers can inspect or correct every claim.

## Primary witnesses / translations

### [P01] Memphis Decree of 196 BCE / Rosetta Stone

- **Issuer:** assembly of priests at Memphis under Ptolemy V.
- **Date of inscription:** 196 BCE. **Object:** British Museum inventory **EA24**.
- **Text:** Egyptian hieroglyphic, Egyptian Demotic and Greek; the English Demotic translation on Attalus is attributed to R. S. Simpson, *Demotic Grammar in the Ptolemaic Sacerdotal Decrees* (Oxford, Griffith Institute, 1996), pp. 258–271; hieroglyphic translation to S. Quirke and C. Andrews, *The Rosetta Stone: Facsimile Drawing* (British Museum, 1988).
- **Access:** https://www.attalus.org/egypt/rosettastone1.html
- **Museum identification:** https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/image/16456004
- **Exact relevant passages:** Demotic lines 4–5, 9–10, 16–18, 22–27, 29–32.
- **Establishes:** priestly categories; House of Life scribes; fine-linen production and fiscal obligations; crafted cult images and gold diadems; extension of priests' titles; triple-script replication requirement.
- **Does not establish:** alchemical techniques, a named alchemist, or the birth of priestly stations.

### [P02] Second Philae Decree / Alexandria Decree (186 BCE)

- **Date:** 186 BCE. **Text inventory:** Trismegistos **TM 48339**.
- **Location:** inscription at the Ptolemaic temple on Philae.
- **Translation:** excerpt attributed to L. Török and W. Müller, *Translations of Hellenistic Inscriptions* 260.
- **Access:** https://www.attalus.org/docs/other/inscr_260.html
- **Exact relevant passages:** preamble and lines/excerpts identifying office categories, proclamation following rebellion, royal statues, directive for public inscription.
- **Establishes:** a subsequent formal priestly decree under altered political conditions; named priestly categories and explicit directions for commemorative statues and inscription.
- **Does not establish:** unmediated neutral military history or alchemical station. **Caution:** excerpt marked with omissions.

### [P03] First Philae Decree / later Memphis Decree (185/184 BCE)

- **Date:** 185/4 BCE in the source; do **not** artificially resolve ambiguity.
- **Text inventory:** Trismegistos **TM 48335**.
- **Location:** Philae temple inscription.
- **Translation:** excerpt attributed to W. Müller, *Translations of Hellenistic Inscriptions* 261.
- **Access:** https://www.attalus.org/docs/other/inscr_261.html
- **Relevant sections:** names priestly and House of Life personnel; tax and fine linen; royal gifts of gold, silver, precious stones to cult images.
- **Establishes:** later royal-priestly negotiations, specifically material and ritual forms of legitimating authority.
- **Does not establish:** a workshop producing synthetic gold, any named alchemist, or a complete text of the decree in the online excerpt.
- **Dating caution:** 'First' and 'Second' in conventional names are *not chronological*.

### [P04] Ptolemy V stela from Naukratis-region, 183–182 BCE

- **Catalog record:** British Museum Naukratis project **X__3907**.
- **Owner noted in record:** Egyptian Museum Cairo, **JE22264 / CG22188**.
- **Date:** 183–182 BCE, year 23 of Ptolemy V, per catalog.
- **Description:** limestone stela, 50 lines of hieroglyphic inscription, royal and sacred scene, unfinished cartouches.
- **Access:** https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/X__3907
- **Establishes:** another physical witness to Ptolemaic sacred-political inscription during the window.
- **Does not establish:** a direct continuation of any specific decree or alchemical writing.

## Secondary scholarship / critical dating

### [S01] University College London, Digital Egypt — Ptolemy V Epiphanes
- **Access:** https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/chronology/ptolemyv.html
- **Claims supported:** king's reign, political pressures, Memphis coronation in 196 BCE and the Rosetta inscription.
- **Does not establish:** alchemical roles.

### [S02] Brian Muhs (2016), 'The Ptolemaic Period (332–30 BCE)'
- **In:** *The Ancient Egyptian Economy 3000–30 BCE*, Cambridge University Press, pp. 211–252.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781316286364.008
- **Access:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/ancient-egyptian-economy/ptolemaic-period-33230-bce/029A98BAB6F458BA138E6E028C71CAB3
- **Relevant discussion:** Ptolemaic temple redistributive networks, commodities, fine linen, state-temple shares, Rosetta Decree lines 9–10 and 16–18, and revolt of 205–186 BCE.
- **Claims supported:** economic/institutional context and explicit linen fiscal clauses.
- **Does not establish:** named alchemist or exact workshop genealogy.

### [S03] Furley and Vallance (2016), 'Bolus', Oxford Classical Dictionary
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/acrefore/9780199381135.013.1135
- **Access:** https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/548697825
- **Relevant extract:** an Egyptian from Mendes, author associated with magic, pharmacology and naturalistic writings; fragments survive indirectly; broad chronology in/around third century BCE.
- **Claims supported:** identity and attribution difficulty.
- **Does not establish:** activity in 200–181 BCE.

### [S04] Matteo Martelli, *The Four Books of Pseudo-Democritus* (2014)
- **Publisher:** Maney / Society for the History of Alchemy and Chemistry (reprint and later catalog records also exist).
- **Institutional bibliography:** https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/625234
- **Publisher description:** https://www.routledge.com/link/link/p/book/9781909662285
- **Critical review:** https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2013/2013.02.55/
- **Relevant claim:** alchemical Four Books are first-century CE, and identification of their author with Bolos of Mendes is untenable in current critical dating.
- **Does not establish:** existence of Four Books in second century BCE.

## Research cautions
- A catalog date for an inscription is not the date of invention of its social institution.
- Priestly texts record royal and temple interests; distinguish proclaimed victories and interpretations from corroborated political events.
- Passage references depend on translations and editorial conventions. Obtain facsimiles and critical editions for close philology.
- No standalone alchemical literature was securely found for this 20-year window in this first pass; that is a **limits-of-evidence** statement.
