# Window 30 — Primary Witness and Scholarly Source Register (381–400 CE)

**Audit:** 2026-10-18. **Evidence grades**: ORIGINAL PHYSICAL INSCRIPTION/OBJECT; ANCIENT LAW IN LATER COMPILATION (original law date vs compilation/copy); ANCIENT RETROSPECTIVE NARRATIVE (composition later than described event); LATER ALCHEMICAL MANUSCRIPT/UNCERTAIN ATTRIBUTION; SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION; CONTESTED; NO SECURE EVIDENCE. This window tests both the **real Serapeum's transformed position** and a **manuscript dialogue depicting a Serapis priest interpreting goldmaking texts**. These facts must **not be fused** into a proven 391 Alchemist.

## W30-T01 — Laws 381, 382, 385: ritual prohibition, preservation of art and divination

- [*Codex Theodosianus* XVI.10.7–9, source Latin and dated subscriptions](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh16.html); codex **published 438**, preserved through later manuscript copies. **These are attributed fourth-century laws, not original surviving 381 or 382 stone.**
- **CTh XVI.10.7 — 21 December 381**, to Florus: `Si qui vetitis sacrificiis diurnis nocturnisque` punishes unauthorized temple/sacrifice/divinatory activity, advocates `castis deum precibus` rather than `diris carminibus`. Source grade TRANSMITTED NORMATIVE LAW, not prosecution of goldmakers.
- **CTh XVI.10.8 — 30 November 382**, to Palladius, duke of Osrhoene: a **specific temple** once dedicated to public gatherings, with statues `artis pretio quam divinitate metienda` (“to be valued for artistry rather than divinity”), is **to remain open to public view**, but permission does **not** allow prohibited sacrifices. **A building and its craftsmanship can retain public value even as its ritual authority becomes illegal.** Neither the text nor the official indicates an alchemical atelier or goldmaking priest.
- **CTh XVI.10.9 — 25 May 385**, to Cynegius: condemns sacrifice to inspect entrails for predictions (`inspectione iecoris extorumque praesagio`) and seeking the future. Such divination is NOT chemistry and a legal penalty is not an observed conviction.
- **Status:** actual named legal categorization as later copied; *not a personal diary of practitioner response*. [ST-105]

## W30-T02 — Egypt targeted by two dated 391 laws; the third law 392 expands private rites

- [Original Latin CTh XVI.10.10–12 in Theodosian Code, edited corpus](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh16.html) (published **438 CE**).
- **24 February 391, CTh XVI.10.10** to Albinus, Praetorian Prefect: `Nemo se hostiis polluat`, prohibits killing sacrificial victims, visiting shrines/temples to venerate idols; sets **gold-weight fines on officials** for involvement/negligence. This is **normative state pressure on ritual station**, NOT a general bill to destroy every book.
- **16 June 391, CTh XVI.10.11**, issued at Aquileia **to Evagrius, Egyptian Augustal prefect, AND Romanus, count of Egypt**: `Nulli sacrificandi tribuatur potestas, nemo templa circumeat, nemo delubra suspiciat`. It therefore **directly addresses Egyptian religious administration and public access**. The preserved statute itself does **NOT command the Serapeum to be pulled down**, nor mention Dioscorus, `chēmeia` or an alchemical manuscript.
- **8 November 392, CTh XVI.10.12**, issued at Constantinople to Rufinus; forbids public and private sacrifice, household rites, incense and wine/libations, and spells penalties by property/land and officials, including gold fines (25 or 30 Roman pounds for certain violations). **Use exact clauses for exact penalties**. Law targets cult acts and administrative compliance, not dyeing, gold leaf or chemically purifying metal.
- **Status:** date-bearing transmitted laws, not original 391 Egyptian inscription or court dockets. They offer a **direct documented external force on Egyptian temple ritual**, but cannot independently prove all Egyptian priests actually changed their behavior. [ST-106]

## W30-T03 — Serapeum, Alexandria: destruction of an identifiable sanctuary ca.391/392; no proven chemical purge

- [Michael Sabottka, *Das Serapeum in Alexandria* (IFAO/Centre d'Études Alexandrines, 2008)](https://www.cealex.org/publication/das-serapeum/), combines archaeology of real sanctuary and narrative sources; scholarly synthesis situates end **391**.
- **Rufinus of Aquileia**, *Historia Ecclesiastica* XI.22–23, authored **ca.402**, is the earliest surviving **narrative account after the event**, describes bishop **Theophilus**, conflict, statues, temple and demolition; [context and translated excerpt](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/~grout/encyclopaedia_Romana/greece/paganism/serapeum.html). He is **NOT a dated 391 original court transcript**.
- **Eunapius**, *Lives of the Philosophers and Sophists*, writing late fourth / early fifth century, presents a **hostile alternative pagan literary voice** about the demolition; [translation](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/pearse/morefathers/files/eunapius_02_text.htm). His interests and chronology differ from Christian narrators.
- **Socrates Scholasticus**, *Church History* **V.16**, authored mid-fifth century (~440), and **Sozomen** *Church History* VII.15 (~440s), offer later Christian narratives of bishop Theophilus and the conflict. [Socrates V.16](https://royalarchives.org/socrates_scholasticus/ecclesiastical_history_of_socrates_scholasticus/chapter_16_5/). **They are NOT independent 391 eyewitness records**; potential dependence and contradictory detail matter.
- **Date**: convention **391**; alternative **392** noted, tied partly to late chronicle chronology; cannot claim exact day of temple destruction. The **June 391 Egyptian anti-sacrifice law** is NOT independently proven the **direct, sole order** for destruction of the site; the disturbance and imperial response must be studied separately. [Scholarly debate](https://ebrary.net/252954/history/destruction_serapeum).
- **Library issue:** **Orosius** writing early fifth century (~417) describes library cupboards/books in temples but is interpreted differently; later scholarship e.g. Dirk Rohmann, [Klio 2022](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/klio-2021-0021/html), argues Serapeum's library likely destroyed, **but whether an active library survived until destruction remains CONTESTED**. **No primary confiscation list of alchemical writings, named goldmaking instructor, or original burnt alchemical papyrus located**.
- **Material technique:** surviving sanctuary archaeology, statue/architectural accounts and gold/silver/bronze surfaces are actual **craft materials**, not proof that a temple priest performed elemental transmutation. [ST-107]

## W30-T04 — 395/396 legal shift: privileges of named priests/hierophants abolished

- [CTh XVI.10.13–14 full Latin](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh16.html).
- **7 August 395**, Arcadius and Honorius to Rufinus: reiterates prohibitions on entering sanctuaries or performing sacrifices; sanctions negligent governors and officials. Original surviving only through **later Theodosian Code**.
- **7 December 396 — CTh XVI.10.14**, to **Caesarius**, Praetorian Prefect: `Privilegia si qua concessa sunt antiquo iure sacerdotibus ministris praefectis hierofantis sacrorum sive quolibet alio nomine nuncupantur, penitus aboleantur`; **withdraws legal privileges of priests, attendants, ritual prefects and hierophants** whose profession now stands condemned. **Unlike vague hostility, this is a specific legally recognizable prior ritual STATION and a state attempt to remove its protected status.**
- The law **does not** identify any alchemical priest by personal name, specify how many institutions complied, or declare chemical recipe learning illegal. [ST-108]

## W30-T05 — 399 differentiates building preservation, rural demolition, and public festivals

- [CTh XVI.10.15–18 Latin and dated text](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh16.html).
- **29 January 399, XVI.10.15** requires public ornaments maintained for civic ornament and use, while forbidding sacrifices.
- **10 July 399, XVI.10.16**, to **Eutychianus**: `Si qua in agris templa sunt, sine turba ac tumultu diruantur` — certain **rural temples are to be torn down without disorder**. This is a **specific demolition prescription**, not proof every temple actually was razed or that specific alchemical writings burned.
- **20 August 399, XVI.10.17** (to **Apollodorus**, proconsul Africa): public festivals and common civic enjoyment may continue **without sacrifice**; `festos conventus civium ... non patimur submoveri`.
- **20 August 399, XVI.10.18**, same official: `Aedes illicitis rebus vacuas ... ne quis conetur evertere` — **buildings freed from illicit practice should not be demolished**; preserves buildings while sanctioning sacrifice and removing idols. **Legal sorting changes a site from ritual office → civic property/art/community gathering** in ways not reducible to blanket persecution or universal tolerance.
- Contrast XIX years earlier: **382** art-valued shrine kept open without cult rites; **399** explicit alternative pathways—demolition of some rural structures and protection of other non-sacrificial buildings. **Neither text is a personal account of an alchemical specialist responding.** [ST-109]

## W30-O01 — Constantinople's Theodosian obelisk pedestal, original physical inscription and engineering

- [Livius, original Greek pedestal inscription, CIG IV 8612](https://www.livius.org/pictures/turkey/istanbul/constantinople-hippodrome/constantinople-hippodrome-first-obelisk-pedestal-nw2-inscription/) and [Latin EDCS-27100012](https://www.livius.org/pictures/turkey/istanbul/constantinople-hippodrome/constantinople-hippodrome-first-obelisk-pedestal-se2-inscription/); [monument/context](https://www.livius.org/articles/place/constantinople-istanbul/constantinople-photos/constantinople-hippodrome/constantinople-hippodrome-obelisk-of-theodosius/).
- Original monumental base **c.388–392**, often dated erection **390**, under Constantinople prefect **Proclus**, with texts saying installation took **30 days (Latin)** or **32 days (Greek)**. These are **original public inscriptions with discrepant numbers**, not two independent completed construction diaries. Greek `ΚΙΩΝ` etc. praises emperor's erection; Latin `ter denis ... diebus`.
- The earlier Egyptian obelisk, partly truncated, was physically transported and raised in Byzantine capital; **clear evidence for considerable engineering, labor organization, material capacity and imperial command**, but not creation of an element or alchemical station. The difference between 30 and 32 days reveals **rhetorical/technical witness tension**, not automatically “ancient secret construction knowledge.” [ST-110]

## W30-A01 — Synesius → Dioscorus, pseudo-Democritus alchemical commentary: contested actual historical audience

- **Text:** `Synesius the philosopher to Dioscorus, priest of great Serapis in Alexandria`, *Commentary/Notes on the Book of Democritus* (`Synesii ad Dioscorum in librum Democriti`, **CAAG II 56.20–69.11**): **a transmitted literary dialogue** about interpreting a text attributed to **Democritus**, with references to Ostanes, Egyptian priestly expertise and coloring of metals, stones, purple. [Berthelot and Ruelle French version](https://www.alchemy-texts.com/book/democritian-treaties/).
- **Primary source critical edition:** **Matteo Martelli and Tiziano Dorandi, *Pseudo-Democrito. Scritti alchemici con il commentario di Sinesio* (2011), Textes et Travaux de Chrysopoeia 12**, Greek critical text + Italian translation (Synesius at pp.224–253 according to [CNRS *Pinakes* critical bibliography and witnesses](https://pinakes-dev.irht.cnrs.fr/notices/bibliographie/J8SKCZW8/)). [Bryn Mawr Classical Review 2013](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2013/2013.02.55/).
- **LATER PHYSICAL WITNESSES:** **Marcianus gr.299 10th–11th century; Parisinus gr.2325 13th; Parisinus gr.2327 1478 (15th c); Vaticanus gr.1174 14th–15th** plus other copies. These witness **transmission**, NOT a 390 original correspondence/teacher-student contract.
- **Interpretation of date:** Addressing **Dioscorus as a priest of the Serapeum** has been argued to locate the *represented social world* **before destruction ca.391**. **Conditional only**: a later author might set a dialogue in a reconstructed/idealized past; neither author nor addressee has an independently attested 381–400 Egyptian job contract. It can be a **retrospective literary staging**, not a dated 390 eyewitness. Attribution to the named fourth-/fifth-century **bishop Synesius of Cyrene (born c.370; bishop around 411)** is contested and **must not be presumed**.
- **Opposing scholarship:** Alan Cameron and Jacqueline Long, [*Barbarians and Politics at the Court of Arcadius* (1993), pp. approximate final chapters/notes](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/barbarians-and-politics-at-the-court-of-arcadius.html), reject identifying the work as genuine writing of known bishop Synesius, and challenge even an “earlier homonym” assumption. Others (Oxford Handbook discussion) infer a likely pre-391 original from the dedication, but the inference **depends on taking interlocutor setting as contemporaneous**. [Modern analytic article on priestly circles](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366408526_On_DeConstructing_an_Authoritative_Narrative_The_Case_of_The_Letter_of_Isis).
- **Positive but restricted:** A **later-preserved alchemical literary text portrays expert commentary crossing a named priestly station and instruction**, not a proven state-recognized fourth-century Alchemist. The source exhibits **how an alchemical writer represented authorization/lineage**; the original person-and-date link remains **CONTESTED**. [ST-111]

## W30-A02 — Olympiodorus the Alchemist is not securely a 381–400 practitioner

- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Olympiodorus,” §4](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/olympiodorus/) reports **four** surviving manuscripts of a commentary ascribed to “Olympiodorus the Alexandrian philosopher” on Zosimos's *On Activity*; alchemical author citations Maria, Hermes, Petasius, Pelagius; textual contents/style differ from some philosophical works and attribution **DISPUTED**.
- **Actual well-attested Alexandrian Neoplatonist Olympiodorus the Younger** was active **sixth century**, taught **after 565**; see [Cambridge History of Philosophy in Late Antiquity, Olympiodorus](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-philosophy-in-late-antiquity/olympiodorus/A1251E8F5D8B7862B2C41B21C94FFF4E), [Cambridge 2026 study](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/classical-quarterly/article/olympiodorean-neoplatonism-in-alexandria-and-constantinople-new-evidence-in-theophylact-simocattas-history-and-letters/96A5345D98CBD714A04F416B068A4797). Another **Olympiodorus the historian** of Thebes belongs to **early fifth century**, also distinct.
- **Identity theories:** wholly pseudonymous author, otherwise unknown alchemist homonym, or partially authentic sixth-century Olympiodorus commentary later expanded (Cristina Viano proposal); none supports a specifically dated **390** alchemical professor. **NO SECURE 381–400 PERSON/MANUSCRIPT**, do not create an ST record treating Olympiodorus as period practitioner.

## W30-N01 — Secrecy and loss tests

- No original **391 inventory of burned alchemical manuscripts**, chemical guild persecution decree, workshop confiscation list, or proof **Synesius's words were composed to hide a recipe from Theophilus** discovered.
- Synesius text's apparent learned ambiguity and Democritus/Ostanes lineages need their own **literary provenance audit**; Democritus is pseudonymous technical attribution, not an established physical student of Ostanes.
- **Strong counterexample to blanket destruction**: later medieval copying **did preserve** alchemical literature, but cannot prove every Alexandrian temple library was safely preserved.

**Closing source verdict:** Real Egyptian anti-sacrifice rule **391** and original imperial construction monument **c.390** securely anchored; the **Serapeum's destruction** historically grounded around **391/392**, not an established alchemy purge. A Greek alchemical dialogue **places a priest of Serapis in a didactic exchange** yet survives only in later copying, author, original composition and interlocutor historicity **UNVERIFIED**. The later alchemical Olympiodorus cannot responsibly be assigned to Window 30.
