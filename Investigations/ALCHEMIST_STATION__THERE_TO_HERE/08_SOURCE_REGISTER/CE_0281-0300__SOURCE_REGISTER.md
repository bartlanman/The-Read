# Window 25 — Historical Source Register: 281–300 CE

**Audit: 2026-10-18. Scope:** Roman Egypt, Diocletian's tetrarchic state, the 297–298 rebellion, the later literary story that books on gold/silver preparation were burned, and Zosimos of Panopolis near c.300. **Evidentiary rule:** a *primary witness produced in or for this window* is different from a *later primary text narrating this window*. Textual witnesses are valuable evidence for their own author's period even when they fail to prove the narrated event. This audit is not exhaustive of all Egyptian documentary papyri.

## [W25-O01] Original Alexandrian coins for the rebel Domitius Domitianus: 297/298 CE

- [Roman Provincial Coinage X, type ID 76414](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/type/76414), year two **297/298**, tetradrachm average **6.66 g**, Greek legend **ΔΟΜΙΤΙΑΝΟϹ ϹΕΒ** and reverse **L Β**, Nike advancing left; cataloged multiple actual ancient specimens.
- [RPC X type ID 76417](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/type/76417), **297/298**, average **7.49–7.50 g**, reverse Nike facing and holding wreath/palm, year **L Β**. [Ashmolean HCR32848](https://hcr.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coin/hcr32848) is an actual specimen of the 297/298 ruler, named under the [Ashmolean provincial-coin catalogue](https://hcr.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/collection/1?page=829).
- **GRADE: ATTESTED physical monetary objects.** The current authority claim was stamped on an actual product, not simply remembered by a future chronicler. **Greek `Σεβαστός` means Augustus-like imperial status claim**, which is not proof the entire Roman empire recognized him.
- **Limits:** original billon/silver-debased metal and numismatic appearance do not demonstrate an unrecorded alchemical crucible, minted gold, or successful conversion of an element. The issuer's name is not the artisan's name. Coins of the same type are not different political changes.

## [W25-P01] Original Greek Egyptian documentary papyri naming Domitius Domitianus in 297

- [Prosopography of the Later Roman Empire, vol. I — Domitius Domitianus, indexed documentary original witnesses](https://connectinglateantiquities.org/text.html?id=plre-1-domitianus-6).
- The prosopographical entry gives **original dated papyri** including **P.Isid. 139** (24/28 August 297, regnal year one; entry's “Aug. 24/8” should be read as a scholarly uncertainty interval rather than a specific day), **P.Isid. 99** (29 August 297, year two), **P.Isid. 62** (5 September 297, year two), **P.Thead. 26** (13 September 297), **P.Isid. 80** (19 October), **P.Isid. 100** (20 October), **P.Isid. 38** (7 November), **P.Isid. 39** (13 November), **P.Isid. 104** and **P.Mich. 1029** (20 November), **P.Isid. 105** (23 November) and **P.Mich. 24** (2 December).
- **GRADE: original period documentary witnesses as *reported and indexed in a specialist prosopography***; exact Greek readings and physical storage of each papyrus have NOT been re-inspected for this dossier. Therefore do not claim first-hand diplomatic readings or reproduce invented original Greek quotations. The date sequence makes usurper-regnal recognition in Egypt much more secure than the later alchemical suppression tale.
- **Caution:** the specialist entry notes **all main literary sources** assign rebellion leadership to **Achilleus**, contrasting **Domitianus on actual coins and papyri**. This is not a source conflict to erase; it may reflect successive/simultaneous roles or narrative compression, and political reconstruction remains debated.

## [W25-T01] Sixth/seventh-century narrative of Diocletian burning “alchemy” books: John of Antioch → Suda

- [Suda On Line, Chi 280, **Χημεία** (“alchemy”), Greek transliteration and vetted English translation](https://cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/chi/280).
- [Suda On Line, Delta 1156, **Διοκλητιανός** (“Diocletian”)](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/delta/1156) (the latter also embeds later events including **303** persecution and **305** retirement; **these are NOT events inside 281–300**).
- **Witness genealogy according to Dufault (2019, p.94 and n.5):** **John of Antioch, *Chronography* fragment 191 Mariev, c.610–626 CE**, preserved in *Suda* **Δ 1156** and duplicated/abridged at **Χ 280**, *Suda* compiled approximately tenth century. The exact extent of later adaptation/quotation is uncertain. The ***Suda*** is **an ancient/medieval primary witness TO A LATER STORY**, **NOT an original 297 imperial edict**.
- **Specific allegation:** angry at Egyptian rebellion, Diocletian searched for and burned the ancients' books about preparing **gold and silver** so that Egyptians could not use such expertise to acquire wealth and fund future revolt. The original Greek in Chi 280 explicitly uses **`χημεία`**, **`ἀργύρου καὶ χρυσοῦ`**, **`βιβλία`**, and **`ἔκαυσε`** (alchemical preparation; silver and gold; books; he burned).
- **Source conclusions:** **ATTESTED = a later writer transmitted this *claim* and understood the practice in political-economic terms.** **NO SECURE EVIDENCE here = Diocletian's actual order, original text of a law, date/place of particular burning, named book destroyed, manuscript find-site, accused individual, or practitioners' identifiable response**. An actual revolt **does not upgrade** the literary burn-story to a verified decree.
- **Beware category crossing:** John Malalas c.550 independently reports *cheimeutēs* John Isthmeus in **Anastasius' reign 491–518**, not a 297 Alchemist; Dufault shows that an alchemist-related social designation outside Greek corpus is much later than 297. **DO NOT merge the Malalas anecdote with the John of Antioch / Suda burning story.**

## [W25-S01] Olivier Dufault (2019), *Early Greek Alchemy, Patronage and Innovation in Late Antiquity*

- [Peer-reviewed open-access monograph, California Classical Studies 7, PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt2ks0g83x/qt2ks0g83x_noSplash_bd08d6316153f6cf32b8279c7ebfa658.pdf). Especially **Introduction pp.1–5**, chapter **5.1 pp.94–100** (John of Antioch/Suda, Greek `chēmeia`, Malalas), and chapter **6 pp.118–142** (Zosimos's patrons/competitors).
- The Diocletian book-burning passage is attached to **John of Antioch c.610–626** rather than treated as an extant Diocletian law. **Zosimos c.300** is **first known author of Greek alchemical commentaries**, operating in a scholarly-patronage field, engaging/dismissing rivals. He was not necessarily a publicly titled *alchemist* in his own terms. Dufault argues he presented himself as **scholar, possibly a Christian writer** and client of **Theosebeia**, a patroness in his transmitted letters.
- **Critical uncertainty:** Zosimos can be bounded broadly **after Julius Africanus** and **before destruction of the Serapeum 391**, not personally pinned to 290–299. A third/fourth-century **floruit around 300** is not a year-specific evidence event for 281–300.
- Dufault identifies main **medieval** original physical compilation witnesses: **Marcianus graecus 299 c.1000**, **Parisinus gr.2325 13th c**, **Parisinus gr.2327 15th c**. These witness transmission, not original near-300 autographs.
- **Earlier author “Democritus” pseudonymous**, and quoted **Maria** lacks independently verified dated biography; Zosimos's citations do not establish a personal meeting or teacher/pupil relation.

## [W25-S02] Lawrence M. Principe, *The Secrets of Alchemy* (2013), excerpt via Science History Institute

- [Science History Institute, “Zosimos: At Alchemy’s Foundations”](https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/the-secrets-of-alchemy/) and [Warwick hosted book excerpt (pp.14–17)](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/arts/ren/jhu/summerschool2019/reading/principe_secrets_1.pdf).
- Zosimos active **about 300**, references earlier authors, *rival schools* in his writing, and writes technical/artistic works incl. *On Apparatus and Furnaces*; some passages to **Theosebeia**. Principe: whether patron/“pupil” was a real historical person versus epistolary construction **not fully established**. **Near-300 commenter** does not equal **actual gold transmutation** or precisely dated 297 political victim.

## [W25-S03] Specialist documentary prosopography: the Egyptian 297 rebellion

- [PLRE Domitius Domitianus entry](https://connectinglateantiquities.org/text.html?id=plre-1-domitianus-6) records document dates, counterclaims: literary **Achilleus** vs documents **Domitianus**, later narratives report Alexandria siege approx **eight months** and defeat c.298. **Retrospective siege accounts are not contemporary lab records.**
- Original coins: [RPC X ID 76414](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/type/76414); [RPC X ID 76417](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/type/76417). **Two types and series** supply direct contemporaneous material evidence for issuer standing; they do not supply an alchemical profession.
- Precise times for conquest and fate of each rebel disputed; the mere number of coins does not independently locate a 298 burned book.

## [W25-S04] Manichaean-edict comparison: real legal text, different target, uncertain date

- [William Seston, “De l'authenticité et de la date de l'édit de Dioclétien contre les Manichéens,” *Mélanges* / École française de Rome, reprinted 1980, pp.551–560](https://www.persee.fr/doc/efr_0000-0000_1980_ant_43_1_1329).
- **Imperial directive against Manichaeans** survives via **Collatio legum Mosaicarum et Romanarum 15.3** (text preserved in later manuscripts, not original rescript). Scholars debate **297 versus 302** dating. Whether a particular reconstruction puts it before/after **300** must remain **CONTESTED/CROSS-WINDOW**. This is **not** the same as the Diocletian gold/silver-book-burning account.
- **Distinct Outside pressures:** a legal action directed at a **religious movement** may be attested by a transmitted legal compilation; it cannot be turned into a documented alchemical persecution unless a relevant named actor/craft is identified.

## [W25-S05] Greco-Egyptian recipe manuscripts and material technique

- [Dufault 2019, pp.1–3, 94–95](https://escholarship.org/content/qt2ks0g83x/qt2ks0g83x_noSplash_bd08d6316153f6cf32b8279c7ebfa658.pdf) and **Robert Halleux (1981), *Les alchimistes grecs I: Papyrus de Leyde, Papyrus de Stockholm, fragments de recettes***.
- **Leiden X and Stockholm** surviving physical papyri are generally **late third/fourth-century or fourth-century** recipe witnesses; operations include metal coloring/imitation, dyes, stones/glass. **Neither physical manuscript has been pinned with a secure in-document 297 date**. Gold/silver appearance is **not proof of elemental transmutation**.
- Counterevidence against *universal secrecy* is the existence of written procedural recipes, irrespective of their medieval/modern reception. Do not suggest a “burning” destroyed ALL copies or that surviving texts prove they escaped an order.
- Differentiating original manufacturing skill from **claims of authentic gold production** requires independent chemical and archaeological evidence; none obtained for an identified 297 alchemical workshop.
