# Window 11 — 1–20 CE source register

**Pass date:** 2026-10-18. Physical numismatic witnesses and Chinese primary-source excerpt supplemented with National Museum of China chronology; Roman-Egyptian temple evidence critically examined. Status: FIRST VERIFIED SOURCE PASS, not full edited corpus.

## [W11-O01] British Museum, Wang Mang gold-inlaid knife money
- Object **1996,0217.239**. **Date assigned: 7 CE**; bronze with gold-inlaid characters; inscription valued `一刀平五千`, 'one knife worth five thousand', 71mm, 22.99g.
- URL: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1996-0217-239
- **Warrants:** actual metal fabrication and official nominal valuation; **NOT** base bronze being chemically transmuted into gold.
- Curator Wang 1997b: Wang Mang introduced multiple reforms, this was one version of 7 CE knife money.

## [W11-O02] British Museum, Wang Mang huoquan coin
- Object **1908,0212.15**, **14 CE** production-date attribution; copper alloy, diameter 23mm, weight 3.21g.
- URL: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1908-0212-15
- Warrants: material monetary production under official authority; independent of goldmaking elixir claims.

## [W11-C01] National Museum of China, 'Wang Mang coins'
- Official museum overview: https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/hb/202008/t20200824_247119.shtml
- **7 CE:** big cash, inscribed knife 500, one gold-inlaid knife worth 5000; prohibits common elite holding gold; **9 CE** dynastic proclamation and new cash; **10 CE** five kinds material, six named currencies, 28 sorts; **14 CE** fourth reform `huobu` and `huoquan`; **20 CE** restrictions on old big cash.
- **Warrants:** museum's numismatic chronological reconstruction and public/legal versus material value distinction.
- **Does not warrant:** every coin type physically cast in the same year listed, transmutation or persecution of alchemists.

## [W11-P01] Hanshu 24B, Food and Currency Treatise, later retrospective original
- Primary Chinese selected section with parallel passages: https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=en&node=63385&show=parallel
- Other transmitted source reprinting reform: https://ctext.org/tongdian/8/zh
- Original `私鑄作泉布者，與妻子沒入為官奴婢`: penalty to person and family for privately casting new coinage.
- Composition: ***Hanshu*** written primarily first/second century CE **after** early Xin reforms; `Tongdian` is a much later Tang encyclopedia, not independent contemporary reporting.
- **Warrants:** the source's coercive and penal legal framework; exact enforcement magnitude would need external administrative objects rather than accepting later historian's numbers uncritically.
- **Not:** state law against all alchemy.

## [W11-E01] UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Egyptian nomination of Pharaonic temples (2003)
- https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1824/
- Covers Dendera, Edfu, Kom Ombo and Esna under Ptolemaic/Roman periods. Tiberian/other emperors' names in reliefs, broad **14–37 CE** Tiberius span; continue to distinguish Roman ritual continuity from specific 1–20 CE dated work.
- This is Egypt's **tentative nomination document**, not a final independent UNESCO scientific authentication of every claim.
- **Limit:** text says Roman priestly land was annexed; this generalized language is disputed by Connor's papyrus studies, below.

## [W11-E02] Andrew Connor, *Confiscation or Coexistence: Egyptian Temples in the Age of Augustus* (Michigan UP 2022)
- University press summary: https://press.umich.edu/Books/C/Confiscation-or-Coexistence
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3998/mpub.12220891
- Examines papyri **P.Tebt. II 302**, **BGU IV 1198/1200** and **P.Berl.Leihg. I 5**, challenging presumed province-wide Roman confiscation of temple property and the associated deterministic collapse of Egyptian religion.
- **Warrants:** an active scholarly challenge to broad confiscation narrative; not proof every local priest retained all former resources or privileges; documents' specific ancient dates must be checked individually before attributing a change to exactly 1–20 CE.

## [W11-E03] Stefan Pfeiffer, 'The Imperial Cult in Egypt', *Oxford Handbook of Roman Egypt* (2012)
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199571451.013.0007
- Institutional reorganization of imperial cult/priestly roles; timing of **high priest of Alexandria and all Egypt** itself contested, with clear named papyrological evidence in later centuries.
- **Cannot assign** later high-priest regulations and credentials automatically to a secure 1–20 CE institution.

## [W11-G01] Matteo Martelli, *The Four Books of Pseudo-Democritus* (critical Greek/Syriac edition; 2014, reissued 2026)
- https://www.routledge.com/The-Four-Books-of-Pseudo-Democritus/Martelli/p/book/9781909662285
- Source: historian places texts generally first century CE, **not a precise early 1–20 date**.
- Later manuscripts represent a long tradition of epitomes, commentary and translation.

## [W11-G02] Review of Martelli, *Early Science and Medicine* 21 (2016), 354–356
- https://brill.com/previewpdf/journals/esm/21/4/article-p354_5.xml
- More specific reading of Martelli's chronology: **after first half of first century, perhaps Nero's reign**. This excludes 1–20 CE assignment.
- Ancient Bolos of Mendes has independent BCE dating; he and the later pseudo-Democritean author cannot simply be identified.
