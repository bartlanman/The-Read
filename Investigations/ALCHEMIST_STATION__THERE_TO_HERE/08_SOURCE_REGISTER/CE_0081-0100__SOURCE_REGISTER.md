# Window 15 historical sources — 81–100 CE

**Examined:** 2026-10-18. Physical inscription with explicit regnal date is the strongest evidence, distinguished from Roman literary texts written decades later, a fifth-century Chinese compilation and modern philological interpretation.

## [W15-P01] Twin obelisks of Beneventum — original granite Egyptian inscriptions, 88/89 CE
- **Academic open-access edition / translation and inscription photographs:** Luigi Prada, '"To Isis the Great, Lady of Benevento": The Egyptian Inscriptions on the Twin Obelisks of Roman Beneventum', *Egypt and the Classical World: Cross-Cultural Encounters in Antiquity* (J. Paul Getty Museum, 2022). https://www.getty.edu/publications/egypt-classical-world/07/
- **Dating:** explicitly **eighth regnal year of Domitian, 88/89 CE**, as carved. Two actual extant objects, partly restored; A Piazza Papiniano; B Museo del Sannio. Patron's name **Rutilius Lupus** carved.
- **Historical statement:** Isis 'Lady of Benevento'; emperor honored; differing but related wording between the two stones; question whether sanctuary was first built or refurbished remains partly ambiguous.
- **Philological reconstruction:** likely **Egyptian priestly composer** of monumental Middle Egyptian, perhaps working via a Latin/Greek commissioning intermediary, with Roman artisans carving. **NOT** a named witnessed individual or secure provenance chain.
- **Material condition:** neither obelisk is evidence of a gold-transmutation or alchemical apparatus.

## [W15-E01] Getty Museum, Egyptian Obelisks and Their Afterlife in Ancient Rome
https://www.getty.edu/news/egyptian-obelisks-and-their-afterlife-in-ancient-rome/
- Original monuments, dedicator, dates, cartouches honoring Domitian. Good independent museum summary, but largely based on overlapping research.
- **Crucial:** 96 CE senatorial erasure in Roman literary source compared to **visible surviving Egyptian script**; do not infer that the 88 CE writers intentionally anticipated this.

## [W15-E02] Pirelli, The Imperial Iseum in Benevento and its Zoomorphic Gods (2023)
https://unora.unior.it/handle/11574/223000
- Sanctuary's cultic statue/iconographic assemblage and Roman Egyptian religious legitimacy. No complete surviving 88/89 CE temple foundations or securely identified alchemical school.
- **Not:** independent evidence that a specific temple priest wrote chemical recipes.

## [W15-P02] Suetonius, *Life of Domitian*, ch. 10.3
https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Suetonius/12Caesars/Domitian%2A.html
- Suetonius, **early second century CE**, describes Domitian having Junius Rusticus executed for praising senatorial opponents and **expelling philosophers from Rome and Italy**.
- Historiographical status: real danger to philosophical/aristocratic life, NOT a contemporaneous decree that surviving evidence shows applied to alchemical artisans.

## [W15-S01] Clément Bady, 'L’expulsion des philosophes de 93–94 p.C.' (2020), *Revue des Études Anciennes* 122(1):107–125
https://www.persee.fr/doc/rea_0035-2004_2020_num_122_1_6943
- Critical reconstruction **93–94 CE** and social/political motives, and discrepancies among sources. Some later writers extend persecution to astrologers; earlier sources emphasize **philosophers**.
- Scope of affected people uncertain. **Do not** call this an empire-wide anti-alchemy expulsion.

## [W15-P03] Suetonius, *Life of Domitian*, ch. 23.1
https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Suetonius/12Caesars/Domitian%2A.html
- Reports **96 CE** Senate ordered erasure of Domitian's inscriptions and memory after his assassination. Written early second century CE, not direct Senate minutes.
- **Material comparison:** at least the Benevento obelisks' **Egyptian hieroglyphic cartouches survived**. Luigi Prada suggests their relatively inaccessible script protected the ruler's representation from eradication.
- **Competing alternatives:** partial enforcement, specific local conditions, indifference or unreadability may explain survival. Avoid claiming intentional design or perfect erasure elsewhere.

## [W15-G01] Matteo Martelli, *The Four Books of Pseudo-Democritus* (2014, reissued 2026)
https://www.routledge.com/The-Four-Books-of-Pseudo-Democritus/Martelli/p/book/9781909662285
https://dacalbo.hpdst.gr/sites/dacalbo.hpdst.gr/files/Pseudo-Democritus%20Martelli.pdf
- Textual reconstruction: first-century CE Greek alchemical author under Democritus pseudonym, with tighter modern argument possibly **Nero 54–68 CE**.
- **Date warning:** No original 81–100 CE manuscript or named historical author. A corpus broadly 'first-century' may or may not have existed by 100, but the evidence does not demonstrate a specific event in this period.
- Later Greek and Syriac textual layers require source separation; authors like Zosimos are centuries later.

## [W15-G02] Leiden Papyrus X and Stockholm Papyrus, actual extant chemical recipe manuscripts
- Stanton J. Linden ed., *The Alchemy Reader*, chapter 6, 'Anonymous (late third century AD): From Leyden Papyrus X and the Stockholm Papyrus', Cambridge UP 2003/2014 online. https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/alchemy-reader/anonymous-late-third-century-ad-from-leyden-papyrus-x-and-the-stockholm-papyrus/B9648991C8B475894FB7F48982B151F4
- **Extant physical Greek manuscripts dated late third century CE**; recipe style may preserve earlier practices but texts **cannot be assigned as original 81–100 CE documentation**.
- Do not equate later manuscript with direct evidence of its writer's practice two centuries earlier.

## [W15-H01] *Hou Hanshu*, Western Regions / Ban Chao and Gan Ying mission, 97 CE
- Chinese account composed chiefly by Fan Ye in **fifth century CE**, preserves events in **97 CE**. Translation and interpretive essay by Sally K. Church, 'Interplays and Interactions on the Maritime and Overland Silk Roads in the First Millennium CE' (2024): https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09719458241236253
- Ban Chao sends Gan Ying seeking **Da Qin**; he reaches **Tiaozhi**, a far-western maritime frontier, likely in or around Persian Gulf, but cannot safely be assigned an exact modern city with certainty. He **does not reach Rome**.
- There is no stated exchange of Greek chemical treatises or named alchemists.

## [W15-H02] Imperial Rome and China: Communication and Information Transmission
- Scholarly modern treatment (book PDF): https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/16/04/56/05/2016045605/2016045605.pdf
- Confirms historical reconstruction of **failed 97 CE Roman-contact mission**, the role of intermediary reports and later compilation.
- **Not evidence of any unbroken 1st-century BCE–CE alchemical lineage linking Han and Greek Egypt**.

## Next research remainder
1. Use Prada critical synoptic hieroglyphic editions to compare exact content and divergences of the two monuments, including wording of Rutilius Lupus and calendar.
2. Trace how broadly the 96 CE damnatio affected regional scripts and whether any evidence shows direct failure to read hieroglyphic names rather than incomplete enforcement.
3. Distinguish Domitian's c.93–94 expulsion of philosophers from later assertions that astrologers/diviners were included; never extend to alchemists without individual evidence.
4. Identify actual first-century Greek alchemy original manuscripts, if any, rather than backdating later Leyden X or Stockholm.
5. Compare the early *Hou Hanshu* report of Gan Ying with later translations and geography to test whether **actual** movement of people/books to Egypt occurred. No source yet proves it.
