# Window 04 sources — 140–121 BCE: Li Shaojun, Emperor Wu, Liu An

**Review date:** 2026-10-18  
**Evidence levels:** Direct translated reading of the *Shiji* Chinese passage as transmitted; specialist scholarship on date and alchemical context; critical secondary source on Liu An's authorship. No contemporary laboratory notebook, original Li formula, bronze object matching his reputed demonstration, or complete lost alchemical book has been verified.

## [W04-P01] 司馬遷, 史記 卷二十八 封禪書 (Shiji 28, Fengshan shu)
- **Text in Chinese and English:** https://deltoi.com/zh/twenty-four-histories/shiji/chapter-28
- **Parallel translated excerpt:** https://warringstates.day/library/shiji/28-feng-chan-shu-part2
- **Chinese Text Project excerpt and variants:** https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=en&node=5772&show=parallel
- **Date represented:** activity under Emperor Wu, Li Shaojun conventionally placed around 133 BCE.
- **Date of composition:** Sima Qian's work compiled late second to early first century BCE; surviving transmitted witnesses are later still. **Not a contemporaneous stenographic court record from 133 BCE.**
- **Relevant text:** around chapter 28's paragraphs 56–58: Li introduced to ruler for 祠灶 / 穀道 / 卻老; biography and concealed age/origin; bronze vessel anecdote; 祠灶則致物 / 丹沙可化為黃金 / 黃金成以為飲食器則益壽; imperial rituals and searches; Li's death and emperor's reinterpretation 化去; imperial order to receive/retain his 方.
- **Warrants:** historian represents social recognition, claims, patronage, imperial response, and posthumous transmission attempt.
- **Cannot warrant:** historical truth of Li's extraordinary lifespan, successful transmutation, immortals, the claimed vessel identification without independent material proof, or an actual encoded ingredient lexicon.
- **Textual caution:** number/identity of individuals in 黃錘史寬舒 is disputed among commentators.

## [W04-S01] Fabrizio Pregadio, *Great Clarity* (2006), chapter 1, pp. 23–34
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.11126/stanford/9780804751773.003.0002
- **Warrants:** modern scholarly treatment of Li, Han court rituals/elixirs, Yellow Emperor, the mid-2nd-century context, and *Hongbao yuanbi shu* as a lost candidate elixir text.
- **Does not warrant:** an exact surviving mid-2nd-century manual, an Egyptian transmission, or successful elemental conversion.

## [W04-S02] *The Encyclopedia of Taoism*, ed. Fabrizio Pregadio (Routledge, 2008), entry on **fangshi**
- **Accessible bibliographic excerpt:** https://online.flipbuilder.com/onjg/hxji/files/basic-html/page446.html
- **Warrants:** Li's 133 BCE convention and discussion of court *fangshi* as a broad category; some did not enjoy formal official posts.
- **Does not warrant:** everyone labelled fangshi having one stable alchemical profession or a laboratory credential.

## [W04-S03] "History and Lore: Interpretive Emplotment and 'Empty Writing' in the 'Hereditary House of Zhao'" (*Early China*, online 2024)
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1017/eac.2024.1
- **Full article:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/early-china/article/history-and-lore-interpretive-emplotment-and-empty-writing-in-the-hereditary-house-of-zhao/AF770563B52D52663D5630D6B69B19D0
- **Warrants:** Li appears in *Shiji* among multiple types of court experts and serves a narrative of Emperor Wu's Yellow Emperor-modelled immortality program.

## [W04-S04] Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Philosophy in Han Dynasty China"
- **URL:** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/han-dynasty/
- **Warrants:** Liu An's dates (179–122 BCE), sponsorship and compilation of Huainanzi; political-philosophical program.
- **Limit:** does not establish a particular alchemical formula in surviving Huainanzi.

## [W04-S05] Judson B. Murray (2015), "A Study of 'Yao Lue': the Huainanzi from the Point of View of the Postface"
- **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/early-china/article/abs/study-of-yao-lue-a-sumary-of-the-essentials-the-huainanzi-from-the-point-of-view-of-the-postface/AC3513083FE6783A037A2D98CA93AE02
- **Warrants:** scholarly conventional 139 BCE presentation and interpretation of the *Huainanzi* as cosmological/political program.
- **Caution:** later scholarship [W04-S06] disputes how definitely to use that date as compositional boundary.

## [W04-S06] Zhang Hanmo (2016), "The Lore of Liu An and the Authorship of the Huainanzi"
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/02549948.2016.1259836
- **Accessible original:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02549948.2016.1259836
- **Warrants:** major uncertainties about Liu An's single-author status, 139 BCE presentation of exact surviving text, the late provenance of authorial lore; cites *Hanshu* 44.2145 on patrons of specialist arts.
- **Does not warrant:** treating Liu An as personal author of all later alchemical books, or his 122 BCE political downfall as an anti-alchemy purge.

## [W04-S08] Cambridge Early China, "The Soundscape of the Huainanzi"

## Future tests
1. Read critical annotated Shiji 28 and variants in Shiji 12/Hanshu 25A side by side. Look for the exact year of Li's appearance in each.
2. Locate, date and interpret the bronze-vessel tale as a crafted narrative; no object has been matched to the anecdote here.
3. Determine whether imperial transmission of 方 has any identifiable trace in later alchemical prescriptions.
4. Distinguish rituals at stove, chemical heating, forged metals and edible/utensil elixirs.
5. Compare secondary analyses of Liu An and lost *Hongbao* texts; an authorial attribution centuries after the event is not direct evidence of authorship.
6. Investigate any actual practitioner testimony from contemporary Ptolemaic Egypt before asserting contacts across traditions.
