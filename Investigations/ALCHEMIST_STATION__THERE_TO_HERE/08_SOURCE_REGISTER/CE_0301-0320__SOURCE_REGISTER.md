# Window 26 — Primary and Scholarly Source Register, 301–320 CE

**Audited:** 2026-10-18. **Claim grades:** ATTESTED ORIGINAL MATERIAL (surviving original-period inscriptions/objects); REPORTED BY NEAR-CONTEMPORARY AUTHOR (composition shortly after events, original autograph lost); RETROSPECTIVE / TRANSLATED DOCUMENT; SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION; CONTESTED; NO SECURE EVIDENCE. Distinguish **issue of imperial law**, **local stone copy**, **later quoted law** and **modern edition**.

**Central boundary:** Real 301 price regulation of precious-metal workers is not Diocletian's alleged confiscation of goldmaking books; 303 burning of **Christian scriptures** is described in near-contemporary Christian accounts, not evidence that **alchemical recipes** were burned. A named Greek alchemical commentator, **Zosimos**, remains broadly **c.300**, with exact 301–320 activity UNVERIFIED.

## [W26-O01] Aphrodisias Civil Basilica: ORIGINAL 301 Edict of Maximum Prices, Latin stone inscription

- [Oxford University Aphrodisias Excavations: inscriptions](https://aphrodisias.classics.ox.ac.uk/inscriptions.html); [PHI edited original Latin inscription, Aphrodisias 77](https://epigraphy.packhum.org/text/256955); [University research epigraphy bibliography](https://aphrodisias.classics.ox.ac.uk/p_epigraphy.html).
- **Original document/object:** imperial Latin **maximum-price edict, AD 301**, inscribed across the **north facade** of the Aphrodisias Civil Basilica in Caria. This is an actual surviving **ancient stone public text** rather than a 4th-century autograph law carried forward only in a medieval chronicle. It is one **local promulgation/copy of an imperial directive**, not a signed Diocletian autograph and not necessarily an original Egyptian document.
- **Important modern critical publication:** Michael H. Crawford et al. (2023), *Diocletian's Edict of Maximum Prices at the Civil Basilica in Aphrodisias*, Aphrodisias 13, DOI [10.29091/9783752002362](https://doi.org/10.29091/9783752002362). [2024 Bryn Mawr Classical Review by Eleanor Dickey](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2024/2024.07.21/) reports **roughly 1,400** categorized **maximum** prices for goods/services, **more than 40** fragmentary copies survive across the East, with the Aphrodisias copy about **40%** preserved. Do NOT treat all 1,400 as preserved at Aphrodisias alone. Some Greek translations of price lists accompanied Latin preambles at other sites.
- **Metal and skilled-craft anchor:** The reconstructed **chapter 30, “Concerning Gold”** and later **metal-thread** wage listings price **actual gold, gold-beating/leaf preparation and gold-worker labor** within a broad catalog that also treats textile, dye and other crafts; consult precise 2023 edition for the exact line/price before computing margins. **A tariff acknowledges commercial goldworking expertise, NOT proof that gold was synthesized from base metal.** No category in this inspected text identifies a publicly licensed **Alchemist** or a clandestine guild. Distinguish tax/maximum tariff from professional certificate.
- **Physical testimony:** true local inscription, with **modern archaeological reconstruction** of damaged panels. Missing lines must not be supplied as pristine text; beware transposing another site's edition to Aphrodisias without labeling it reconstruction.
- **Verdict:** **ATTESTED regulation of economic/material stations**, not regulation or suppression of alchemical transmutation. [ST-086]

## [W26-O02] Aphrodisias 301 Currency Dossier: inscribed monetary authority, related but DIFFERENT text

- [Bryn Mawr review of 2023 Crawford edition](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2024/2024.07.21/) specifies a roughly contemporaneous **Currency Dossier** on the same Civil Basilica; [Oxford epigraphy research references](https://aphrodisias.classics.ox.ac.uk/p_epigraphy.html), Erim/Reynolds/Crawford (1971), *JRS* 61, 171–177.
- Karl Strobel (2015/2016), [“Die Aufwertung des Jahres 301 ...”](https://tyche.univie.ac.at/index.php/tyche/article/view/5003), DOI 10.15661/tyche.2015.030.14, interprets an edict and letter about currency revaluation **1 September 301**, including the doubling of certain denominations and the link with price restrictions. **Interpretive reconstruction CONTESTED in details**; this is not a separately dated ancient recipe for making gold.
- **ATTESTED** original stone material traces of imperial monetary intervention; dating of particular phases subject to epigraphic reconstruction.
- **Avoid false provenance:** the **Currency Dossier** and **Price Edict** are distinct originally inscribed administrative texts at the same location, not interchangeable with the **later Suda book-burning anecdote**. [ST-086]

## [W26-T01] Lactantius, *De mortibus persecutorum* 12–15: Nicomedia 23–24 February 303

- [Lactantius chap.12 English translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Ante-Nicene_Fathers/Volume_VII/Lactantius/Of_the_Manner_in_Which_the_Persecutors_Died/Chap._XII); [Lactantius Latin text of broader work](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/lactantius/demort.shtml).
- **Described event:** **23 February 303**, armed officials dismantled the Christian church of **Nicomedia**; Lactantius reports **Christian scriptures found and burned**, and building smashed. **24 February 303** is conventionally attached to first edict's public promulgation; verify from chapter 13, not from simply substituting the church demolition date. The edict reportedly ordered churches destroyed, worship scriptures burned and rank/status penalties.
- **Witness chain:** Lactantius wrote *De mortibus persecutorum* **after these events**, usually dated in early fourth century, reporting/claiming inside knowledge. The modern Latin and English texts derive from **later surviving manuscripts**, not a discovered 303 original order or burned original Bible fragment independently linked to this episode.
- **ATTTESTED NEAR-CONTEMPORARY LITERARY CLAIM** for demolition/burning, strengthened by distinct Eusebius [W26-T02], though they share community interest and are not archaeologically independent confirmations of each line. **Target was Christian books**, not documented alchemical manuscripts. [ST-087]

## [W26-T02] Eusebius, *Ecclesiastical History* VIII.2.1–5: edicts on Christian books, offices, assembly

- [Book VIII chapter 2, translated edited text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nicene_and_Post-Nicene_Fathers%3A_Series_II/Volume_I/Church_History_of_Eusebius/Book_VIII/Chapter_2).
- **Original composition:** after 303, with revisions and completion during early fourth century; physical surviving manuscripts are later copies. Eusebius **claims to have witnessed** churches demolished and sacred scriptures burned in public spaces (VIII.2.1) and describes edicts targeting Christian worship, social honor and household servants (VIII.2.4), followed by instructions against ecclesiastical leaders (VIII.2.5).
- **SOURCE CHRONOLOGY CAVEAT:** Eusebius's **month of Dystrus** is presented in older English notes as Roman March; Lactantius gives Nicomedia February details. These are **regional/publication chronology and textual differences**, not proof two independent attacks on alchemy. Do not harmonize day/month uncritically.
- **Event narrated:** church suppression/Christian scriptures/official eligibility; **not** state suppression of goldmaking manuals. **Author's theological editorial agenda and selective narrative acknowledged.** [ST-087]

## [W26-T03] Eusebius, *Martyrs of Palestine* (shorter recension), chapter 1: Procopius, Caesarea, 7 June 303

- [Shorter Eusebian text, chapter 1](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nicene_and_Post-Nicene_Fathers%3A_Series_II/Volume_I/Church_History_of_Eusebius/Martyrs_of_Palestine/Chapter_I).
- Reports Procopius appeared before governor, refused sacrifice/libation for emperors, cited Homeric verse on monarchy and was executed **7 June** early in persecution, generally **303**. **Narrated named individual**, Christian service/ritual station, **NOT an alchemist**. Eusebius near-period eyewitness environment, but the quoted dialogue is an interested account, not extant Roman court minutes.
- **Version caution:** long recension exists through later Syriac manuscript; the shorter Greek text in *Ecclesiastical History* and longer Syriac are related versions **NOT independent case attestations**. [ST-087]

## [W26-T04] Lactantius, *De mortibus persecutorum* 34: toleration edict of Galerius, 311

- [Latin text at Early Church Texts](https://earlychurchtexts.com/main/lactantius/galerius_edict.shtml) and [translated source description](https://diadoche.org/document/galerius-edict); [Eusebius *HE* VIII.17](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250108.htm), Greek transmitted imperial version.
- **Date:** publicly promulgated at Nicomedia, conventionally **30 April 311**. **Actual legal wording** transmitted in **Lactantius Latin** and in **Eusebius Greek** (translated/edited), both through later manuscripts; **no original 311 posted bronze/stone tablet examined here**.
- The text grants Christians renewed permission to practice and restore meeting places subject to public order and asks that they pray for the rulers/state. **Distinct from the 313 restoration/circular letter.**
- **ATTESTED legal-change witness in near-contemporary texts**, not an original 311 inscribed artifact and not an alchemical profession's recovered license. [ST-088]

## [W26-T05] Lactantius *De mortibus persecutorum* 48 and Eusebius *HE* X.5: Licinius circular letter of 13 June 313

- [Lactantius English 313 text collection](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/edict-milan.asp); [Eusebius HE X.5](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250110.htm); [Lactantius chap.48 Latin/translation comparison](https://bkv.unifr.ch/en/works/cpl-91/compare/von-den-todesarten-der-verfolger-bkv/49/de-mortibus-persecutorum).
- **Historical distinction:** Constantine and Licinius discussed policy in **Milan early 313**, but the surviving textual proclamation is **Licinius's administrative/circular letter publicly posted at Nicomedia on 13 June 313** (as Lactantius reports), implementing worship liberty and restitution. “**Edict of Milan**” is a conventional *modern umbrella label*, not proof of a surviving separately signed original edict issued at Milan.
- **Transmission:** Latin excerpt in Lactantius, Greek translation/other version in Eusebius; *two literary witnesses to policy*, **not two independently surviving imperial autographs**. Restitution of confiscated Christian property/social rights is the focus; no alchemical craft license.
- **ATTESTED near-contemporary transmitted legal reversal**; exact local application varied, archaeological proof of each returned property not provided. [ST-089]

## [W26-S01] Olivier Dufault, *Early Greek Alchemy, Patronage and Innovation in Late Antiquity* (2019)

- [UC Berkeley Press / California Classical Studies open-access monograph](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ks0g83x), introduction and chapters on **Zosimos**, pp.118–142.
- **Zosimos of Panopolis c.300** is earliest identifiable writer of Greek alchemical commentary; **he did not present himself as `magos` or `alchemist`**, and Dufault sees a Christian scholarly author and a client addressing **Theosebeia**. Polemic against **Neilos** and other rivals identifies a textual/social competition, not independently surveyed registered professional roles.
- **Chronological boundary:** later medieval witnesses (e.g. **Marcianus gr.299 c.1000**, **Parisinus gr.2325 13th c**, **Parisinus gr.2327 15th c**) cannot locate a specific Zosimos teaching session in 303, his religion's precise institutional status, or any personal persecution under Diocletian/Maximinus. Broad date overlaps Window 25 and Window 26. [ST-085 already registers positive cross-window candidate; do not duplicate it as a new 303 event.]

## [W26-S02] Olivier Dufault (2022), “Was Zosimus of Panopolis Christian?”

- [Peer-reviewed `ARYS` paper PDF](https://e-revistas.uc3m.es/index.php/ARYS/en/article/download/6795/5727/12978).
- Investigates transmitted **`On the Letter Omega`** and letters to **Theosebeia**, challenging simple labels through Zosimos's views of Jesus and anthropological teaching. **Source-supported interpretation:** author could self-position within Christian intellectual traditions while attacking other specialists. This is scholarly argument, **not independently dated 305 confession, city office, or persecution record**.
- Internal polemic/epistolary relationship with Theosebeia does not authenticate reported adversaries without separate witnesses; even author's theological self-positioning may have multiple interpretations.

## [W26-S03] Scholarly text-dating guard on Zosimos, later compilations and recipe papyri

- [Cambridge *Alchemy Reader*, Zosimos of Panopolis (fl. c.300)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/alchemy-reader/zosimos-of-panopolis-fl-c-300-ad-of-virtue-lessons-13/0FD98882E6321A144B49317EFDB33647), [Cambridge *Alchemy Reader*, Leiden X and Stockholm](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/alchemy-reader/anonymous-late-third-century-ad-from-leyden-papyrus-x-and-the-stockholm-papyrus/B9648991C8B475894FB7F48982B151F4).
- **Leiden X / Stockholm** preserve ancient practical recipes, broadly late 3rd / early 4th century manuscript horizon, not an explicitly dated **307 original**. Only “overlaps” is valid, without exact scribe/author/workshop identity.
- **Zosimos's `Visions`, apparatus and furnace discussions** survive through later translation and compilation; distinct **symbolic/witness** layers not a single contemporary physical book. No verified elemental transmutation specimen.

## [W26-N01] Positive test against the Diocletian gold-book story

- Earlier **Window 25's** retrospective *Suda* `chēmeia` destruction story [source audit](CE_0281-0300__SOURCE_REGISTER.md), drawn from John of Antioch c.610–626 and tenth-century compilation, **must not** be promoted to a specific 303 event just because **some Christian scriptures really were burned**.
- **301 Aphrodisias inscription** concretely regulates goldworking and metal-related labor. This is not proof every legal craft was safe, but **it is positive public evidence of a material craft station at nearly the time when a separate narrative later alleged secret book-burning**.
- Specific unsupported bridge: “Diocletian burned Christian scriptures in 303” → “therefore ordered the destruction of Egyptian alchemical works in 303” = **NO SECURE EVIDENCE**. Different texts, places, materials, periods and officials.

## Remaining source tasks

- Direct epigraphic reading of specific **goldworker chapter/numbered lines** in Crawford's 2023 reconstruction and Aphrodisias surviving panel; avoid inventing prices from later composite translations without marked text-reconstruction.
- Manuscript provenance of Lactantius's transmitted 303, 311, 313 accounts and the sequence of Eusebius revisions; avoid treating printed translation as an original-period inscription.
- Whether any **original 301–320 alchemical recipe manuscript colophon, patron appointment, dated workshop account or persecution notice** can actually identify a named local specialist. **NONE confirmed by inspected sources**.
- China's and other alchemical traditions remain **independent** until documented contact.

**Window verdict:** authentic original 301 regulation of a **craft/economic station**; near-contemporary literary source chains for **303 attacks on Christian texts** and **311/313 restoration**; a broadly near-300 named **alchemical commentator (Zosimos)** with exact 301–320 status **NOT VERIFIED**.
