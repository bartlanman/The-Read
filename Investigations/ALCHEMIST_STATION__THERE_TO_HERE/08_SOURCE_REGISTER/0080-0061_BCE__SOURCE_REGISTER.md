# Window 07 evidence register — 80–61 BCE

**Research pass:** 2026-10-18. Source-critical distinction between dated contemporary speech, retrospective Latin natural history, later Chinese dynastic history, and modern reconstruction. Do not equate loss of a manuscript with intentional censorship.

## [W07-E01] Ptolemy XI Alexander II — Chris Bennett / Trismegistos chronology
https://www.trismegistos.org/calendar/Bennett/ptolemies/ptolemy_xi.htm
- **Claims supported:** Ptolemy XI was co-ruler with Berenice III, had her killed, and was murdered by Alexandrian crowd approximately before 22 April 80 BCE after 18–19 days; Ptolemy XII succeeded.
- **Primary object base:** papyri and classical narratives referenced in the scholarly notes.
- **Does not support:** court alchemical guild, technical treatises, universal secrecy.

## [W07-E02] Ptolemy XII Auletes — Chris Bennett / Trismegistos chronology
https://www.trismegistos.org/calendar/bennett/ptolemies/ptolemy_xii.htm
- **Claims supported:** accession around April 80 BCE, Memphis coronation in 76, royal cult with Cleopatra V, and documentation including **oPr Joachim 1**, 17 January 79 BCE. Modern reconstructions debate details of parentage, co-regency and documents. Egyptian royal station survived Roman attention through 61 BCE.
- **Does not support:** exact artisan hierarchy, an early alchemical master.

## [W07-P01] Cicero, *De lege agraria* 2.41–44
- **Delivered:** 63 BCE in Rome (CONTEMPORARY to political controversy).
- **English and Latin:** https://www.attalus.org/cicero/agraria2.html
- **Bilingual sections 41–44:** https://cicero.hermitsh.ai/en/works/de-lege-agraria-2/
- **Warrant:** a royal will and Roman entitlement to Egypt are openly disputed; Cicero criticizes hidden powers delegated to commissioners. Terms *occulte, recondita, furtim* describe political concealment, not lab nomenclature.
- **Limit:** Cicero is an advocate with rhetorical interests; his accusations are not necessarily neutral facts, and he neither attests an alchemical workshop nor secret scientific recipe.

## [W07-E03] Wjatscheslaw K. Chrustaljow, 'Sic est (non) iusta causa belli?' *Hyperboreus* 24(2) (2018; published online 2019), 244–264
https://bop.unibe.ch/Hyperboreus/article/view/12201
- **Claims supported:** proposed Roman annexation in **65 BCE**, Crassus and surviving fragments of Cicero's *De rege Alexandrino*; Roman legal and moral arguments about possible annexation.
- **Limits:** incomplete surviving fragments prevent reconstruction of every viewpoint; annexation did **not** occur in 65 BCE.

## [W07-M01] Duane W. Roller, *Empire of the Black Sea*, ch.13, 'The End of the Mithridatic Kingdom' (Oxford University Press, 2020)
https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780190887841.003.0014
- **Claims supported:** Pompey's command in 66 BCE, Mithridates VI's withdrawal, his death in **63 BCE**, and the end of his Pontic kingship.
- **Limit:** does not independently corroborate original medicinal archives or an actual secret recipe.

## [W07-P02] Pliny the Elder, *Naturalis historia* 25.1 and 25.5–8
- **Composition:** around **77 CE**, MORE THAN A CENTURY AFTER the 63 BCE events.
- **Latin primary-text witness:** https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/L/Roman/Texts/Pliny_the_Elder/25%2A.html
- **English translation:** https://falsafa.ai/works/pliny-the-elder-naturalis-historia-631445/25/translation/
- **Relevant Latin phrases:** *in auctoritatem scientiae* (authority from being the sole possessor of knowledge), *scrinium commentationum... in arcanis suis* (Mithridates' notes in his private holdings), *transferre... sermone nostro* (translated into Latin).
- **Pliny 25.7 warrants:** Pliny claims Pompey obtained personal medicinal material and commissioned his freedman Lenaeus to translate it into Latin. This is a **retrospective account**, original notes and Lenaeus translations not inspected here.
- **Pliny 25.1 warrants:** the writer's own concern that withholding useful knowledge maintains intellectual prestige. **This is Pliny's attitude in 77 CE, not Mithridates' motive established for 63 BCE.**
- **Pliny 25.8 warrants:** botanical images alone can fail because coloring and copying are unreliable and the plant changes through seasons; difference between picture/text and living practical competence.
- **Does not warrant:** medicinal effectiveness proven by modern study, secret formula deliberately hidden by Mithridates, direct pupilship to Pompey or Pliny, Egyptian alchemical lineage.

## [W07-M02] Alexandra Schultz, 'Translation as Conquest: Mago, Mithridates, and the Origins of Roman Science in Pliny's Natural History', Society for Classical Studies
https://classicalstudies.org/translation-conquest-mago-mithridates-and-origins-roman-science-pliny%E2%80%99s-natural-history
- **Supports:** Modern close reading of Pliny 25.5–7 as a Roman story of political conquest leading to **translation and credit appropriation**. Useful interpretive counterweight to simplistic story 'open science defeats secrets.'
- **Limit:** conference abstract, not verified full monograph or direct witness.

## [W07-H02] Huan Kuan, *Yantielun* 鹽鐵論 — source and text history
- **Library of Congress facsimile/catalog:** https://www.loc.gov/item/2021666477
- **Scholarly sourcebook:** https://fanhan.org/chapter-2/yantie-lun/
- **Text:** https://ctext.org/yan-tie-lun/fu-gu/ens
- **Dates:** 81 BCE event; compilation/embellishment under Emperor Xuan **74–49 BCE**; original manuscript not extant and surviving copies are later.
- **Supports:** a *later written reformulation* of an earlier court debate; practical arguments about real iron/salt production.
- **Does not support:** specific composition 74–61 vs 60–49 BCE, or faithful verbatim participant speech.
