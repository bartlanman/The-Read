# Window 23 — Critical Source Register, 241–260 CE

**Checked 2026-10-18.** Source identifiers are local to this window. **GRADE:** ORIGINAL PERIOD OBJECT/WRITING; NEAR-CONTEMPORARY LETTER IN LATER COPIES; LATER AUTHOR QUOTING EARLIER DOCUMENT; MODERN CRITICAL STUDY; COMPARATIVE BROAD-DATE ONLY. Records **do not** confer an Alchemist profession merely by illustrating a different social station.

## [W23-O01] Roman Provincial Coinage VIII 3329 — Philip I, Alexandria, year 6 (248/249 CE)

- **Critical database:** [RPC VIII 3329](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coins/8/3329). Modern *type* catalog; 72 known specimens, 35 core collections. The original coins, not the web page itself, are the ancient material witnesses.
- **Original object:** Roman Egyptian, Alexandria, **debased silver tetradrachm**, mean 24 mm / 12.66 g across cataloged specimens. **Obverse** `Α Κ Μ ΙΟΥ ΦΙΛΙΠΠΟϹ Ε(Υ)(Ϲ)(Ε)` (imperial titulature); **reverse** `L - Ϛ` (year six), eagle with wreath; issued **248/249 CE**.
- **ATTESTED:** struck metal, official regnal dating, alloy category, imperial image/issuer, mint attribution.
- **NOT SHOWN:** elemental gold production; goldmaking recipes; actual alloy formula from this object; named worker, workshop head, or officially named Alchemist. Coin's material composition classification should not be turned into an untested artisanal recipe.
- **Station use:** comparison between *publicly authenticated object* and *unnamed producing artisans*. [ST-075]

## [W23-P01] P.Oxy. IV 658 / Yale P.CtYBR inv. 65 — Oxyrhynchus, 14 June 250 CE

- **Edition:** [DDbDP Greek transcript and metadata](https://papyri.info/ddbdp/p.oxy;4;658); [Egyptian Inscriptions transcription mirror and HGV links](https://egyptianinscriptions.org/object/142907-certificate-of-sacrifice-of-aurelius-l-thion-and-his-children).
- **Object catalog:** [Yale P.CtYBR inv. 65 / APIS](https://egyptianinscriptions.org/object/175775-certificate-of-pagan-sacrifice); Yale University Library, *Yale Papyrus Collection* finding aid, P.CtYBR inv. 65 = P.Oxy. 658 / IV pp.49–50.
- **ORIGINAL DOCUMENT:** Greek papyrus, original first year of Decius, Egyptian Pauni 20 = **14 June 250**. Invokes the city officials in charge of sacred rites and sacrifices; applicant **Aurelius L[…]thion** (name damaged) identifies his children **Aurelius Dioskoros and Aurelia Lais**. Requests an annotation/certification after reporting sacrifice, libation and tasting.
- **Precision:** retain lacuna in adult's name; it is a **certificate/petition**, not a baptismal register, secret society roster, metallurgy permit, or an original copy of Decius's decree.
- **Same artifact:** Yale inv.65 and P.Oxy. IV 658 are two identifiers of *one* papyrus; separate metadata entries do not constitute independent evidence. **Different artifact:** P.Mich. III 157 [P02].
- **Station use:** public proof-of-ritual, witness apparatus, applicants versus certifiers. [ST-076]

## [W23-P02] P.Mich. III 157 — Theadelphia, 17 June 250 CE

- **Original critical edition:** [DDbDP Greek transcript](https://papyri.info/ddbdp/p.mich;3;157); [Papyri.info / Egyptian Inscriptions entry with Greek](https://egyptianinscriptions.org/object/139265-certificate-of-sacrifice-of-aurelius-sakis-and-his-children).
- **ORIGINAL DOCUMENT:** libellus dated Egyptian **Pauni 23, year one of Decius = 17 June 250 CE**. Applicant **Aurelius Sakis** from **Theoxenis**, with children **Aion and Heras**, residing at **Theadelphia**. The petition reports sacrificing, pouring libations and tasting offerings; officials **Aurelii Serenos and Hermas** state they *saw* the sacrificers.
- **Original wording examples:** `τοῖς ἐπὶ τῶν θυσιῶν ᾑρημένοις` (to those selected over sacrifice); `ἐθύσαμεν καὶ ἐσπείσαμεν ... ἐγευσάμεθα` (we sacrificed, poured libations and tasted); `εἴδαμεν ὑμᾶς θυσιάζοντας` (we saw you sacrificing). Translation checked against Greek; machine translation is convenient but not a substitute for edited papyrus.
- **ATTESTED:** documented named applicant, named endorsers, administrative compliance claims, day and place.
- **NOT SHOWN:** respondent's private conviction, a personally witnessed chemical operation, an Alchemist title, empire-wide uniformity of procedure, the verbatim lost edict.
- **Station use:** a clear originally dated example of authorized witnessing and proof distinct from practical craft. [ST-076]

## [W23-T01] Eusebius, *Ecclesiastical History* VI.39.1–5 — Origen in Decian persecution

- [Book VI chapter 39 (English translation)](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250106.htm); compare historical Greek text edition Dindorf 1871 and later editions.
- **Event narrated:** under Decius (249–251); **author reporting:** Eusebius, early fourth century; **surviving text:** later manuscript tradition.
- Reports Origen restrained and tortured and Roman church succession. **RETROSPECTIVE** theological narrative; no original 250 eyewitness papyrus for these particular sentences. Source may draw on earlier Origen correspondence now incompletely preserved. **No Alchemist nexus**.
- **Provenance discipline:** Eusebius's motives assigned to emperors and his martyr rhetoric are a writer's interpretation, not independently certified imperial intentions.

## [W23-T02] Cyprian of Carthage, Epistula 80.1–2 — Valerian rescript reported in 258

- **Latin textual record / critical reference:** [Presbyters Project, Epistula 80, I.2, based on Diercks 1996 pp.626–627](https://presbytersproject.ihuw.pl/index.php?SourceID=1108&id=6).
- **English:** [New Advent, numbered *Epistle 81* in this edition](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050681.htm). **NUMBERING NOTE:** Ep.80 in CSEL/Diercks-based schemes appears as Ep.81 in some older translated collections. It is the *same letter*, not independent witness.
- **COMPOSITION / ORIGINAL POSITION:** Cyprian, 258 CE, near-contemporary church leader writing while trying to ascertain Roman reports; quotation reports Valerian's instruction to senate concerning **bishop, presbyter, deacon**, and rank/property sanctions. It also mentions **Xystus/Sixtus** dying on August 6, 258 (Roman VIII Ides August). **Earliest physical exemplar** not this 258 autograph; letters reach us through later Latin manuscript editing.
- **ATTESTED as original-period composition through transmitted text / NOT an ORIGINAL rescript:** The underlying imperial dispatch is reported, **not extant verbatim independently**. Sanctions described relate to Christian identity/office, not chemical trade or "alchemists."
- **Station use:** explicit legal and reputational pressure on a named clerical station. [ST-077]

## [W23-T03] Dionysius of Alexandria against Germanus via Eusebius, *HE* VII.11.1–11 — c.257–258

- [Eusebius Book VII, chapter 11](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250107.htm).
- **Quoted subject:** Dionysius' first-person rebuttal to **Germanus**, describing interrogation before **Aemilianus** with clergy, compelled worship demand and removal to **Cephro**.
- **WITNESS CHAIN:** mid-third-century Dionysius's recollection/letter → Eusebius's fourth-century edited quotations → medieval textual transmission → modern translation. Though first person appears, the copy before us is *not* the original administrative transcript; dialogue may have reached him by surviving notes.
- **ATTESTED in an interested period source as transmitted:** a named bishop contests office/exile pressure. **NOT** evidence that Dionysius was an Alchemist or that a metal workshop was banned.
- **Station use:** the clearest direct claimed encounter between legal pressure and person's acknowledged office. [ST-077]

## [W23-T04] Gallienus's restoration instructions via Eusebius, *HE* VII.13.1–3

- [Eusebius Book VII, chapter 13](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250107.htm). The transmitted rescript names **Gallienus** and bishops **Dionysius, Pinnas, Demetrius**, orders free religious assembly/access; Eusebius explicitly notes it is a **translation from Latin**.
- **EVENT DATE:** often placed **260 CE or slightly after** the capture of Valerian. **NO SECURE YEAR/MONTH ASSIGNED** solely from the surviving passage; may cross boundary into 261–280, so treat as **260/after horizon**, not a confidently completed 260 event.
- **TYPE:** imperial-document quotation translated into Greek by a later Christian historian; not the emperor's Latin autograph; separate order on cemeteries also alleged, not physically produced here.
- **ATTESTED in transmitted document; language/editorial fidelity and precise dating open.** Shift applies to **church privileges, not an alchemical title**. [ST-077]

## [W23-T05] Dionysius via Eusebius, *HE* VII.10.3–6 — the "Egyptian Magi" accusation

- [Eusebius Book VII chapter 10](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250107.htm).
- **Source chain:** Dionysius's Letter to Hermammon (written retrospectively about Valerian) → Eusebius (early 4th c) → later copies.
- States, in a highly hostile religious frame, that a "teacher/ruler of the synagogue of the Magi from Egypt" influenced Valerian to persecute Christians; passage also names Macrianus. No contemporary legal, metallurgical or institutional witness independently confirms an "alchemical magus" profession or the details of alleged ritual atrocities.
- **RETROSPECTIVE, INTERESTED & POLEMICAL:** must distinguish alleged practices from verified practices, contested political causation from verifiable court offices, and label `magoi` from `chēmeia` and from actual recipe practice.
- **Station use:** a labeled rival figure can be an artifact of polemic; NOT a securely identified alchemical guild. [ST-078]

## [W23-S01] Olivier Dufault (2019), *Early Greek Alchemy, Patronage and Innovation in Late Antiquity*

- [University of California eScholarship HTML / full-text book](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ks0g83x), especially **Introduction pp.1–8, Julius Africanus pp.84–91, Zosimos pp.93 onward**.
- **MODERN HISTORICAL INTERPRETATION:** Africanus is a named author of some recipes later recognized as alchemical, **not an alchemical commentator**; **Zosimos of Panopolis c.300** is first known such commentator and **did not call himself an Alchemist**. Greek `magos` denotes a contentious social stereotype with rhetorical uses, not a cross-century chemical profession. Zosimos's references to older masters do not securely date or prove literal teacher-student encounters.
- **LIMIT:** historical synthesis and later witnesses; does not supply an excavated 250 CE alchemical license or prove Maria's exact life years.

## [W23-S02] Philip I and Decius — coins, regnal dating and chronology

- **Direct period anchor:** [RPC VIII 3329](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coins/8/3329) physically anchors **Philip I year 6** to 248/249.
- **Historical change:** Decius becomes emperor 249; do not translate contested exact dates in later chronicles into a contemporary Egyptian chronological event without documents. **No original complete Decian empire-wide edict** available in this audit. Cross-check original **Decius year-one** formulas in P01/P02.

## [W23-S03] Decian certificate structure and geographically limited survival

- [Papyrus Stories, *Imperial Decrees, Animal Sacrifices, and Christian Persecution* (2019)](https://papyrus-stories.com/2019/10/30/imperial-decrees-animal-sacrifices-and-christian-persecution/).
- Scholarly explanatory essay compares P.Oxy IV 658 and P.Mich III 157; notes many surviving libelli are clustered in June 250, while explanations of why they cluster (administrative delays, survival bias, enforcement) remain hypotheses, not verified.
- **Use:** explanatory comparison only. **No alchemical persecution** proved by sacrificial certificates.

## [W23-S04] Matteo Martelli (2022), “Transmuting Tinctures” and recipe-horizon cautions

- [Martelli, *Transmuting Tinctures*, *Technai* (2022), published PDF](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/e80dea99-da60-40df-a5a5-589724690433/Martelli_2022%20%28Transmuting%20Tinctures%29.pdf). Scholar describes Leiden and Stockholm **3rd–4th-century CE** collections of more than 250 recipes on coloring metals, stones and wool, with explicit relation to Pseudo-Democritus; **Halleux, *Les alchimistes grecs I: Papyrus de Leyde, Papyrus de Stockholm, fragments de recettes* (1981)** provides a critical edition.
- **Modern comparative evidence**, NOT physically dated to June 250. Experimental reconstruction of an alum-based silvery look does **not** prove elemental silver transmutation: [Ferrari et al., JAS Reports 36 (2021), 102742](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352409X20305332).
- **Caveat:** secondary writers sometimes call these “3rd century”; a **third-century date range** may include 241–260 only as a possibility. No exact contemporary named technician demonstrated by material papyrus date.

## [W23-S05] Preservation / transmission risk (continuity from Window 22)

- [British Library Papyrus 2040](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002105408), **P.Oxy III 412** Book 18 recto broadly **225–275/276**, will verso **276**.
- The ingredient geometry / musical sign substitutions occur **elsewhere in transmitted Kestoi recipe fragments**, not physically on Book 18. Use [Window 22 source register](CE_0221-0240__SOURCE_REGISTER.md); the broad date overlaps multiple windows, without independent replication.

## Open research leads deliberately NOT promoted to 241–260 facts

1. Search cataloged **original metalworking/occupational petitions** and exact papyri with dates 241–260; none in the identified present corpus demonstrates a named alchemical practitioner. Candidate leads require stable siglum / inventory / reading before attaching.
2. Exact **Maria the Jewess** early witness genealogy and archaeology of her reported apparatus: quotations ascribed by Zosimos do not provide a 250 autograph or secure meeting date.
3. Gallienus's **restoration rescript date** may belong strictly in 261–280 depending chronology; it remains marked **260/after**.
4. Chinese technical specialists deserve independent documentary analysis; no Greek–Chinese recipe transmission was established by this window.
5. Contextually impressive demonstrations, later legends and polemical “Magi” cannot substitute for documented chemical transformations or station recognition.

## Explicit research grade

**Ten-layer evidence-controlled synthesis**, not a claim to have exhaustively surveyed all excavated papyri or discovered the historical first Alchemist. No positive proof of a 241–260 Alchemist title or successful elemental transmutation. **Next window 261–280:** prioritize a datable copy or workshop witness and firm date of Africanus verso reuse; preserve open uncertainties.
