# Window 03 source register — 160–141 BCE

**Research status:** One retrospective early Chinese primary-history textual pass, specialist modern interpretation, Egyptian chronology, and an Edfu epigraphic study summary. This is not a critical edition of the full relevant corpora.

## [W03-C01] Hanshu, juan 5, Jingdi ji — clause on counterfeiting
- **Primary tradition:** *Hanshu* (History of the Former Han), *Jingdi ji* (景帝紀); compiled by Ban Gu and others **in the first–second centuries CE**, describing the 144 BCE Jingdi edict.
- **Original wording:** 定鑄錢偽黃金棄市律.
- **English parallel / Chinese original:** https://ctext.org/han-shu/jing-di-ji/ens
- **Alternative edition including commentaries:** https://www.donglishuzhai.net/chapter/817.html
- **Dating:** event 144 BCE (Jing's sixth middle year); extant written literary witness is later than that event.
- **Establishes:** a state law against illegally manufactured currency and false/counterfeit gold with execution in the marketplace.
- **Does not establish:** prohibition of alchemy, making alchemical elixirs, an actual transmutation event, or all private metalworking.
- **Philology:** the law has a term for counterfeit gold; record variant translations but distinguish private minting and elixir preparation.

## [W03-C02] Fabrizio Pregadio, The Early History of Chinese Alchemy and the Way of the Great Clarity
- **In:** *Great Clarity: Daoism and Alchemy in Early Medieval China* (Stanford University Press, 2006), ch. 1, pp. 23–34.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.11126/stanford/9780804751773.003.0002
- **Abstract:** https://academic.oup.com/stanford-scholarship-online/book/20643
- **Partially indexed full passage:** https://www.abebooks.com/9780804751773/Great-Clarity-Daoism-Alchemy-Early-0804751773/plp
- **Establishes / argues:** The 144 law is against counterfeiting coins and gold; interpreting it as an anti-alchemy law conflates metallurgy/forgery with elixir preparation, ritual and transcendence. Discusses lost *Hongbao yuanbi shu*, mid-second century BCE in broad reconstruction, and later testimony.
- **Limits:** general 'mid-second century' textual dating does not prove any precise 20-year slot; consult book edition directly before relying on commentary details.

## [W03-C03] Historical interpretation / contested narrative
- **Earlier interpretation:** Homer H. Dubs, "The Beginnings of Alchemy," *Isis* 38 (1947), pp. 62–86. Dubs linked the 144 BCE edict to suppression of alchemy.
- **Access (partial):** https://www.scribd.com/document/381452461/The-Beginnings-of-Alchemy-Article
- **Later Chinese glosses:** Ying Shao (c.140–206 CE) and Meng Kang (third century CE) comment on the 144 decree as reproduced within Han textual tradition; see full Chinese annotated edition above.
- **Why it matters:** we have not only an ancient law but a later historiographical conflict over its meaning.
- **Limits:** commentarial and modern claims are not the contemporary event itself.

## [W03-C04] Li Shaojun timing — chronological control
- **Scholar-curated Oxford project:** https://www.cabinet.ox.ac.uk/chinese-alchemy
- **Claim supported:** first more securely assignable narrated Chinese alchemical court event involving Li Shaojun and Emperor Wu around **133 BCE**, not in 160–141 BCE.
- **Dating caveat:** early account appears in *Shiji*, a later historical witness; exact contemporary inscription of the event not supplied here.
- **Use:** window boundary control; never put Li Shaojun in 144 BCE as victim of the counterfeiting law.

## [W03-E01] UCL Digital Egypt — Ptolemy VIII
- **Access:** https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/chronology/ptolemyviii.html
- **Supports:** 145 BCE return of Ptolemy VIII to rule Egypt following his brother's death; dynasty and relationship to Cleopatra II.
- **Limits:** no alchemy practices inferred.

## [W03-E02] Chris Bennett / Trismegistos — Cleopatra II
- **Access:** https://www.trismegistos.org/calendar/Bennett/ptolemies/cleopatra_ii.htm
- **Supports:** dynastic chronology, Cleopatra II's joint rule after 145 BCE; dating of royal-cult claims and names.
- **Limits:** regnal and cultural authority, not documented alchemical practitioner station.
