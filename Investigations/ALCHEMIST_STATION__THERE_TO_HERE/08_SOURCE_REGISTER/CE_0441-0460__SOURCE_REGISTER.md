# Window 33 — 441–460 CE — Source Register and Historical Witness Audit

**Investigated:** 2026-10-18. **Core result:** period-original public inscriptions document named civic-technical capacity; ecclesiastical decrees and late-Roman laws document selection and restriction of textual, ritual, or architectural authority. **No securely dated 441–460 professional Alchemist, verified elemental transmutation, licensed alchemical school or prosecution for making gold is identified.**

**Evidence grades:** ATTESTED ORIGINAL MATERIAL INSCRIPTION; AUTHORED HISTORICAL LITERARY NARRATIVE with later copy; LATER-CODIFIED ANCIENT LAW; RECONSTRUCTED/CONTESTED DATE; SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION; NO SECURE EVIDENCE.

## W33-O01 — 447 Constantinople wall repairs: original Greek and Latin building inscriptions

- [The Byzantine Legacy, Rhegium Gate, surviving Greek and Latin transcriptions/photos](https://www.thebyzantinelegacy.com/theodosian-walls); [Columbia University Istanbul Documentation Project original inscriptions and walls](https://mcid.mcah.columbia.edu/istanbul-documentation-project/land-walls-constantinople); [Van Millingen, full bilingual quotation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/61475/old/61475-h/61475-h.htm).
- **Date reconstruction:** severe earthquake **447**, threat from Attila and Hun forces; imperial leadership under **Theodosius II**. **Physical original Greek inscription** on Rhegium/Mevlevihane Gate `Ἥμασιν ἑξήκοντα ... Κωνσταντῖνος ὕπαρχος ἐδείματο τείχει τεῖχος` claims **prefect Constantine added wall to wall in 60 days**; original Latin `THEODOSII IUSSIS GEMINO NEC MENSE PERACTO / CONSTANTINUS OVANS HAEC MOENIA FIRMA LOCAVIT` similarly claims rapid work and names **Constantinus**. The Greek and Latin inscriptions are **two original public statements on one project**, not independent time sheets, original construction contracts or invoices.
- Third original inscription formerly at Xylokerkos Gate survives **as literary copy**, [Greek Anthology 9.690](https://anthologiagraeca.org/passages/urn%3Acts%3AgreekLit%3Atlg7000.tlg001.ag%3A9.690/); a **transmitted quotation of a lost-period stone** is different evidence class from two surviving inscribed surfaces. The older inner wall dates **412–414**, sea-wall extensions **439**, and **447** earthquake repair/outer wall not interchangeably one building operation.
- **Provenance contradiction:** a later Byzantine tradition associates the 447 rebuilding with **Cyrus of Panopolis**, while the **actual inscriptions name Constantine**, also named by earlier chronicle **Marcellinus Comes** (authored **early sixth century**). [Alan Cameron, *Wandering Poets* excerpt](https://www.scribd.com/document/1071376400/Alan-Cameron-Wandering-Poets-and-Other-Essays-on-Late-Greek-Literature-and-Philosophy-Oxford-University-Press-2015) and [historian close study](https://paperzz.com/doc/7937851/kyros-panopolites--rebuilder-of-constantinople) distinguish **Cyrus's 439 sea-wall contributions** from **Constantine 447 land-wall repairs**, against identifications of a fictitious “Cyrus Constantine.” The later attribution **CONTESTED**, not authority to replace inscriptional name.
- **Station outcome:** imperial official named and real monumental repair attested; makers/workers and methodology largely unrecorded; **no alchemical materials or elemental synthesis**. The 60-day duration is a **commemorative claim**, not independently verified from daily work logs. [ST-122]

## W33-L01 — 448 prohibition of writings attributed to Porphyry and Nestorius

- [Roman Law **Codex Justinianus 1.1.3**, law to Hormisdas, translated](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Anglica/CJ1_Scott.htm); [specific 448 source and legal recension discussion](https://tertullian.org/rpearse/porphyry/hoffmann.htm); [Clarence Forbes, “Books for the Burning,” TAPA 67 (1936)](https://www.tertullian.org/articles/forbes_books_for_the_burning.htm).
- **Original-law dating:** generally **448**, under emperors **Theodosius II and Valentinian III**, directed to praetorian prefect **Hormisdas**. A **parallel full Greek version in Acts of Councils (ACO I.1.4)** and **later sixth-century Justinianic Code** differ in wording; a clause extending the command to **other writers against Christianity** may be a **later interpolation**. Keep issue date (**448**) separate from **sixth-century code compilation** and from surviving manuscript copies.
- Contents order **Porphyry's works arguing against Christianity burned**, alongside Christian heretical/anti-Nicene doctrinal writings and renewed restrictions associated with **Nestorius**. **435** prior Nestorian burning law belongs **Window 32**, not a newly enacted event of 448 even when 448 repeats or revisits it.
- **Crucial guardrail:** this is a **specific source-supported order to burn identifiable writings**; it is **not** proof that every copy was burned, that Porphyry personally lived in 448 (he was a third-century author), or that any technical alchemy manuscript was named or suppressed. [ST-123]

## W33-C01 — Council of Chalcedon, October–November 451, authorized offices and textual adjudication

- [Council of Chalcedon Acts and canons in English, Fordham Medieval Sourcebook](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/basis/chalcedon.asp); [Greek/Latin parallel text of 451 canons with translation](https://earlychurchtexts.com/public/chalcedon_canons.htm); [New Advent Chalcedon proceedings, named bishops and legal disputes](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3811.htm).
- **Council period:** **8 October – 1 November 451**, convened under **Marcian and Pulcheria**; recorded doctrinal definition and adjudicated church office. The original sessions are fifth-century historical events; available acts are **later copied/edited literary/documentary textual witnesses**, not original stenographic 451 parchment in this audit.
- **Canon 2** forbids purchase of episcopal/clerical appointments, including stewards/advocates; condemns sellers, purchasers, intermediaries. **Canon 3** limits clergy managing secular estates/business (specified exemptions for guardianship/charity), **canon 4** requires episcopal permission to establish monasteries. These concretely demonstrate **authorization over who holds an office, what payment can legitimize it, and what economic activities are compatible**. No reference to chemical transmutation.
- **Named Dioscorus danger:** council deposed **Dioscorus, bishop of Alexandria** (an identifiable **fifth-century Christian ecclesiastical actor**). He is NOT independently identified as “Dioscorus, priest of great Serapis” in the **undated medieval alchemical “Synesius to Dioscorus” heading**: different titles, century/source problems; same name alone **cannot establish identical person or alchemical lineage**. [ST-124]
- **Council doctrinal dispute** must not be reduced to “all Egyptian artisans forbidden,” and no evidence of alchemical canon or alchemist's actual hearing was found.

## W33-L02 — Majorian's *Novella* 4, *De aedificiis publicis*, issue date disputed 458/459

- [Clyde Pharr, English translation of Novellae Maioriani 4 reproduced in law collection](https://www.scribd.com/document/938465019/1TheTheodosianCodeandNove-20), [Rome in Late Antiquity pp. analysis of public buildings and **11 July 458**](https://www.scribd.com/document/990843898/dokumen-pub-rome-in-late-antiquity-everyday-life-and-urban-change-ad-312-609-9781474469975); [study of Novella IV and preservation of cultural heritage](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339600524_La_Novella_Maiorani_IV_Piccolo_antico_esempio_di_tutela_del_patrimonio_culturale). **Date uncertainty:** sources variously say **11 July 458**, **11 July 459**, and a Latin consular dating analysis has been rendered **10 July 458**. The period assignment **late 450s within Window 33 is secure**, exact day/year **CONTESTED pending critical subscription collation**. Do not pretend one modern entry silently resolves disagreement.
- Imperial order in name of **Majorian and Leo** to **Aemilianus, urban prefect of Rome** condemns authorities and private citizens removing stone and other materials from public buildings to repair or construct private/smaller structures; preserves civic monuments and regulates permission to remove material. Extremely severe penalties are prescribed, **but no specific actual mutilation conviction is attested by the enactment**.
- Technically significant: **physical stone reuse/recovery is an actual practicable material technique**; a law can condemn the *unauthorized transfer of worked building material* while the material itself remains usable. Preserving monumental status and appropriating labor/resources are **economic/civic authorization questions**, not alchemical transmutation or elemental conversion. [ST-125]
- **Witness chain:** proposed enactment 458/459 → late-antique/western law collections including later **Lex Romana Visigothorum 506** traditions → modern printed translation. The text of a law does not alone demonstrate every Rome building physically preserved.

## W33-S01 — Alchemical manuscript chronology and claims of lineage

- [Olivier Dufault, *Early Greek Alchemy, Patronage and Innovation in Late Antiquity* (2019)](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ks0g83x); [Martelli/Dorandi (2011) alchemical Pseudo-Democritus/Synesius critical edition catalog](https://pinakes-dev.irht.cnrs.fr/notices/bibliographie/J8SKCZW8/); [Stanford Encyclopedia, Olympiodorus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/olympiodorus/).
- The Greek alchemical dialogue attributed to **Synesius addressed to priest Dioscorus** has **undated original composition and medieval physical witnesses**. It does **NOT** gain a date of **451** because a Christian **bishop Dioscorus** is documented at Chalcedon. Olympiodorus the alchemical commentator has an uncertain identity/date and must not be silently assigned to the same 447 building context. **Zosimos c.300** earlier author, no 450 original dated working text.
- Leiden X/Stockholm technical recipes of broad late-third/early-fourth-century papyri do not produce a direct **448–458** teacher-to-pupil link. No identified metal recipe/workshop in the documents naming Constantine, Porphyry, Chalcedon bishops or Majorian. **NO SECURE ALCHEMIST IDENTIFIED.**

## Evidence-quality summary

**Most direct artifact:** original bilingual 447 wall inscriptions and built architecture; actual technical repair with publicly assigned official, **not an Alchemist**. **Most explicit targeted text suppression:** 448 edict on named theological/philosophical writings, preserved by later compilations, not evidence an alchemical book was burned. **Most explicit professional authorization dispute:** 451 Council offices and payments; bishop Dioscorus NOT undated alchemical priest. **Most explicit material custody rule:** Majorian late 450s prohibition on stripping worked stone from civic buildings. **No verified natural-element conversion or socially identified Alchemist**, despite several documented alternative station classes.

**Next Window 34 — 461–480 CE:** distinguish western imperial collapse 476 from continuing eastern schools and manuscripts; identify sources rather than project a sudden end to all Roman technology or Greek alchemical literature.
