# Window 19 — 161–180 CE Source Register

**Audit 2026-10-18.** Weight original **P.Amst. I 27** proclamation as direct period documentary material; Galen as a real ancient first-person author, but his own accounts of prestige as **interested autobiography**; Roman conflict in Egypt from Cassius Dio/later literary source with 2014 reappraisal; pathogen claims from recent biological/historiographical scholarship. No Alchemist primary dated 161–180 identified.

## [W19-G01] Oxford Handbook of Galen (2024), chronology
https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/57517/chapter/467334321
- **162–166** first Roman stay, public debate and animal demonstrations, diagnoses of elites, writing for senator **Flavius Boethus**, competition with Methodist school.
- **166** epidemic in Rome, return to Pergamum; **168–169** return to Italian campaign area with emperors Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus, Aquileia plague; **169** Lucius Verus dies.
- **176–180** regular preparation of **theriac** for Marcus Aurelius; **180** emperor dies. **192 CE** fire with Galen's stored medicine, instruments and manuscripts **outside present window**.
- **Source critique:** chronology constructed mainly from Galen's own ancient corpus and secondary biographical research, not original day-by-day personal documents.

## [W19-G02] Debru, 'Medical demonstrations in Rome at the time of Galen' (1995)
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7705054/
- Galen's own descriptions of public anatomy/live animal experiments and elite sophistic audience; demonstration includes rhetoric, improvisation and rivalry. **No independent lab register** proving precise 163 setup.

## [W19-G03] Luis Alejandro Salas, Oxford Handbook of Galen (2024), Anatomy and Physiology
https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/57517/chapter-abstract/467341385
- Galen uses analogical animal anatomy, practical demonstrations, physiological causal theories and display culture for reputation. Later academic study, not a proof all ancient claims were sound.

## [W19-G04] Gaetano Arena (2024), Roman public vivisections
https://www.iris.unict.it/handle/20.500.11769/635171
- Argues particular spectacular live-animal sessions held in **163 CE**, audience elite; constructed from `De anatomicis administrationibus`. Specific date is **modern source argument**; broad 162–166 stronger.
- Recurrent laryngeal nerve research: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19023621/
- **No modern reenactment of animal vivisection recommended or needed**.

## [W19-G05] New York University Institute for Study of the Ancient World, 'The Empire's Physician'
https://isaw.nyu.edu/exhibitions/galen
- Museum/scholarly exhibition places Galen's writings, medical work, Roman epidemics, compounding and patrons in historical context. Original texts preserved in later manuscripts.

## [W19-D01] Haas, 'The Antonine plague' (2006)
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17195627/
- Places epidemic onset **165/166**, Roman/Aquileia events, and argues disease likely smallpox. **That conclusion is a diagnosis, not ancient identification**.

## [W19-D02] Rebecca Flemming, 'Galen and the Plague' (2018), Brill
https://doi.org/10.1163/9789004383302_011
- Distinguishes ancient term **loimos** (epidemic) from a specific modern disease; critiques diagnostic inference and missing typical smallpox scarring; describes Galen's limited and purposive epidemic passages.

## [W19-D03] Flemming and others, 'Smallpox's antiquity in doubt', *Journal of Roman Archaeology* 35 (2022)
https://doi.org/10.1017/S1047759422000290
- Paleogenomic and ancient epidemiological debates cast substantive doubt on firm VARV smallpox identification for Antonine pandemic. **No independent pathogen specimen** from the 166 epidemic in these sources. Do not say definitive smallpox.

## [W19-D04] Flemming, 'Galen, aDNA and the Plague' Society for Classical Studies
https://classicalstudies.org/galen-adna-and-plague
- Explains the ancient texts' silence on classic scarring and the challenge to modern smallpox consensus. Interpretive scholarly criticism, not proof a specific alternative disease caused the outbreak.

## [W19-E01] Katherine Blouin, 'The Boukoloi Uprising' (2014)
https://academic.oup.com/book/26827/chapter-abstract/195835401
- Reconstructs revolt in the northern Nile Delta c.172–173 and long-term **socio-hydrological and agro-fiscal pressures**, challenging hostile ancient outsider tropes. Source-critical comparative account, **not an alchemy sect**.

## [W19-E02] Robert K. Ritner, 'Egypt Under Roman Rule', Cambridge Ancient History
https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/oi.uchicago.edu/files/uploads/shared/docs/Research_Archives/Ritner%2C%20R.%20-%20Egypt%20under%20Roman%20Rule%20%28HQ%29%20-%20CHE.pdf
- Places Boukoloi unrest broadly **171–175** in Egyptian social conditions, notes **Isidorus** as a priestly leader in ancient account, Cassius suppression and temporary 175 usurpation. **Do not turn 'priest' into alchemical experimenter**.
- Dates disputed by sources; record **c.172–173 revolt peak** with broader trouble.

## [W19-P01] P.Amst. I 27 = SB XII 10991, Greek accession edict April 175 CE
https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Edicta/Aegypti41_.gr.html
- Prefect **Gaius Calvisius Statianus** proclaims/celebrates accession of **Gaius Avidius Cassius**, the short-lived **175 CE** imperial claimant. Direct **original period papyrus**, **badly damaged**; some words/dates restored by editors.
- This is **state recognition** but not sustained legitimacy, not evidence of chemical practice.
- Publication reference Sijpesteijn ZPE 8 (1971), Amsterdam papyri edition and Duke Databank.
- The regnal formula in restored Greek must never be passed off as every word surviving unbroken.
