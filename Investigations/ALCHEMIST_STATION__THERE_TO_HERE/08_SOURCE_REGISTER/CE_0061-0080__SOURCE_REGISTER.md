# Historical Source Register — Window 14 (61–80 CE)

**Prepared 2026-10-18.** Major source groups: first-century Roman Latin contemporary natural history (Pliny), first-century Greek medical work with later manuscript witnesses (Dioscorides), hypothetical narrower dating of lost early Greek alchemical Four Books, and later Latin stories about an emperor in Alexandria. **A date assigned to the event is not the writing date.**

## [W14-P01] Pliny, Naturalis Historia 33 — metal finishing, gold testing, fraud
- Author wrote in the 70s CE; conventional preface and publication date **77 CE**, died 79.
- **English primary translation/book division:** https://www.attalus.org/pliny/hn33a.html and https://www.attalus.org/pliny/hn33b.html
- **Latin / Perseus:** https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0137%3Abook%3D33
- **33.20/64–65:** application of gold to other surfaces, copper gilding with quicksilver/mercury and other materials.
- **33.42/125:** current gilding of silver and worries about deceptive inferior substitutes.
- **33.43/126–27:** `coticula` touchstone, expert judgment of relative gold/silver/copper. Author boasts extremely fine resolution—**not independent instrument validation**.
- **33.44:** other silver tests and deliberate ways to deceive those tests.
- **Warrant:** practical craft, alloy appraisal, economic value and fraud perceived by a first-century Roman writer.
- **Does not warrant:** making elemental gold, a demonstrably accurate modern assay value, first-century profession titled Alchemist, safe instructions for mercury operations.
- **Safety / research:** Mercury is hazardous; do NOT reproduce high-temperature mercury-gilding or vapor isolation recipes as a historical experiment.

## [W14-P02] Pliny, Naturalis Historia 30.1–11 — magic, Ostanes, Democritus, debated attribution
- **Latin primary text:** https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/L/Roman/Texts/Pliny_the_Elder/30%2A.html
- **English classic translation:** https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0137%3Abook%3D30%3Achapter%3D2
- **30.1** Latin `magicas vanitates` and `fraudulentissima artium`: Pliny's *hostile evaluation* of magical art.
- **30.8–11:** Ostanes linked to Persian rulers and Greek reception; Democritus and alleged travel/study of magic, supposed **tomb-held writings of Dardanus**, and claims of magic's ancient founders.
- **30.10** says some admirers of Democritus **denied** he wrote these offensive works, yet **Pliny himself insists they were by him**. Record as an **ancient contested attribution**; cannot identify with *Pseudo-Democritus Four Books* unless independent textual link is proven.
- **Warrant:** a contemporaneous scholarly *discourse* on transmission, magic's prestige, disputed attribution and the framing of its social appeal.
- **Does not warrant:** verifiable Democritus–Ostanes apprenticeship, documented excavation of the reported ancient tomb, proof of magical effects.

## [W14-P03] John F. Healy, Pliny the Elder on Science and Technology (Oxford UP, 2000)
- DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198146872.001.0001
- **Warrant:** methodological study of Pliny's technical language, Roman material processes, later reputation and manuscript transmission.
- **Limit:** secondary; does not transform Pliny's accounts into recovered original experiment notebooks.

## [W14-S01] Matteo Martelli, The Four Books of Pseudo-Democritus (2014, later reprint)
- Academic edition: https://www.routledge.com/The-Four-Books-of-Pseudo-Democritus/Martelli/p/book/9781909662285
- Additional lecture PDF: https://dacalbo.hpdst.gr/sites/dacalbo.hpdst.gr/files/Pseudo-Democritus%20Martelli.pdf
- First-century date broad; argument suggests **second half**, plausibly Nero **54–68 CE**, overlapping 61–68.
- Original four-book text LOST. Greek fragments, Syriac materials and later commentator layers must be distinguished. Four themes: gold-looking, silver-looking, gem production/imitation, purple textiles.
- **Warrant:** a historically significant *candidate* textual authority.
- **Limit:** no surviving 61–80 CE colophon or named original author; ancient atomist Democritus not actual proven author.

## [W14-S02] *Early Science and Medicine* book review of Martelli (2016)
- https://brill.com/previewpdf/journals/esm/21/4/article-p354_5.xml
- Emphasizes narrowed later-first-century dating, possibly Nero. Review does not itself date the original document precisely.

## [W14-S03] Olivier Dufault, Early Greek Alchemy, Patronage and Innovation in Late Antiquity (2019)
- https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ks0g83x
- Source-critical study of the textualized creation of Greek alchemical authority, pseudonymous writers and later reception.
- **Limit:** later figures like Zosimos are not contemporaries of Nero.

## [W14-P04] Dioscorides, De materia medica I.72 and V.95 (Wellmann ed.)
- Ancient medical/pharmacological composition **broadly c.50–70 CE**, not securely 61–70 within this window.
- Greek-science translated passages including mineral mercury/cinnabar recovery: https://www.originalsources.com/Document.aspx?DocID=A6NW93NDP3S4AYE
- Smithsonian institutional historical context: https://collections.si.edu/search/detail/edanmdm%3Afsg_F1932.22
- Original first-century autograph LOST; much later manuscript and translations preserved.
- **Warrant:** first-century medical author records a thermally driven separation/collection operation involving mercury from cinnabar. **No gold synthesis**.
- **Limits:** modern source's chemical labels may differ from ancient ambiguous names (`minium`, `cinnabar`), and modern identification of species needs philological care. Mercury operations are toxic; not a recipe to attempt.

## [W14-S04] Research history of mercury/gilding, *ISIJ International* 54(5) (2014)
- https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/isijinternational/54/5/54_1106/_html/-char/ja/
- Identifies Dioscorides *Materia Medica* as an early extant description of mercury recovery by distillation from cinnabar. **Research source**, not 70 CE physical condenser.

## [W14-P05] Tacitus, Histories 4.81–82
- Historiographical account of **Vespasian's time in Alexandria c.69–70 CE** and alleged healing cures; **Tacitus wrote about 105–110 CE, decades later**.
- English/Latin: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Tacitus/Histories/4D%2A.html
- Describes physicians consulted on whether proposed recovery was physically possible, and crowd acclamation of result. **Warrants:** a retrospective claim of miracle and public legitimation, not independent biomedical evidence.

## [W14-P06] Suetonius, Life of Vespasian 7
- Composed **early second century, usually after 120 CE**, event related c.69–70 CE.
- https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Aabo%3Aphi%2C1348%2C020%3A7
- Corresponding narrative of Serapis-backed cures, with differences (hand versus leg, etc.). **Distinct literary witnesses sharing cultural narrative** do not prove miracle or independent measurement.

## [W14-P07] Pliny writing date
- Academic study of Pliny: Healy [W14-P03].
- Traditional prefatory Titus titulature indicates **77 CE**, at least completed by Pliny's death **79 CE**.
- Historical transcription/preface context: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/57493/57493-h/57493-h.htm
- Warrant: actual contemporary Roman discussion in the 61–80 interval, though surviving ancient manuscripts are later.

## Open source questions
1. Find precise passages in Martelli's Greek/Syriac witness stemma demonstrating whether the Ostanes–Democritus works mentioned by Pliny overlap *Four Books*, rather than assuming identity.
2. Assess Roman touchstone tests against physical residue, museum specimens and known limits of appraisal (no modern quantitative precision assumed).
3. Collate Dioscorides Wellmann V.95 and modern translation with mineral nomenclature and manuscript variants before any reconstructive chemistry claim.
4. Determine if late-first-century Egypt has *named* workshop personnel making gold-like alloys or purples in contemporary papyri.
5. Keep Tacitus' / Suetonius' healing reports as literary sources about imperial authority and perceived power; seek independent dated testimony before declaring the healing event verified.
