# Window 29 — Source Register and Witness Chronology, 361–380 CE

**Research pass:** 2026-10-18. **Evidence scale:** ORIGINAL PERIOD OBJECT; LATE-ANTIQUE AUTHORED TEXT (later physical copies); TRANSMITTED IMPERIAL LAW (later legal code and physical copies); SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION; CONTESTED DATE; NO SECURE EVIDENCE. **No 361–380 original autographed alchemical treatise, alchemy guild license, metal-to-gold laboratory result, or independently named gold-transmuting practitioner was established in this audit.**

## W29-T01 — Julian's 17 June 362 teacher approval law, CTh XIII.3.5

- Ancient legal source in much later **Theodosian Code (published 438)**: [CTh 13.3.5 Latin and translation](https://readancienttexts.com/theodosius/liber_xiii/1); [Latin critical presentation of Book XIII](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh13.html).
- Subscription: `Dat. XV Kal. Iul.` = **17 June 362**, with later **receipt at Spoletium** (different date, often **29 July 362**). Teaching candidates, `magistros studiorum doctoresque`, must be approved by **municipal council/decurions** (`iudicio ordinis probatus decretum curialium`) with imperial review. **This is a legal rule governing teachers, not original 362 stone or a chemistry licensure notice.**
- [Julian's separate Greek explanatory rescript, translated as *Letter 36* (Wright) / 61c (Bidez)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Works_of_the_Emperor_Julian/Letters/Letter_36), likely **after June 17, 362**; connects educators' sincerity and belief in classical authors' gods to legitimacy to teach Homer/Hesiod. Different physical later literary witnesses; **do not treat it as the exact Latin text of the June 17 law**, and the explanatory letter is not itself securely dated to June 17.
- [Edward J. Watts, *City and School in Late Antique Athens and Alexandria*](https://www.scribd.com/document/657043325/WATTS-Edward-Jay-City-and-School-in-Late-Antique-Athens-and-Alexandria), and [*A Companion to Julian the Apostate*](https://studylib.net/doc/25971649/--a-companion-to-julian-the-apostate----libgen.li) distinguish city approval from anti-Christian implication. **No named alchemist appears in the law**. A municipality could vet a **teacher** without testing elemental transmutation. [ST-101]

## W29-T02 — Themistius, Oration VII, pp.145.12–146.11; 366/367 CE

- **Late-antique authorial text, not modern invented attribution:** Themistius's **Oration 7**, Greek `Περὶ τῶν ἠτυχηκότων ἐπὶ Οὐάλεντος` (“Concerning those who suffered under Valens”). Original public speech about **Valens's handling of Procopius's defeated followers**, generally dated **367**, but some specialists allow **366 or 367**. It follows Procopius's revolt defeated **366**. [Themistius Greek-work catalogue, Dindorf 1832 edition](https://catalog.perseus.org/?f%5Byear_facet%5D%5B%5D=1832) lists `Them. Or. 7` / `tlg2001.tlg007`; [19th-c historical biographical witness](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ADictionary_of_Greek_and_Roman_Biography_and_Mythology_%281870%29_-_Volume_3.djvu/1037) dates to 367.
- [Olivier Dufault, *Early Greek Alchemy, Patronage and Innovation in Late Antiquity* (2019), chapter 5, pp.100–102; direct research PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt2ks0g83x/qt2ks0g83x.pdf). Dufault quotes/paraphrases **Oration 7 145.12–26, 145.26–146.1, 146.1–11**, featuring transformations as analogies: **Circe, Medea, Xerxes, Autolycus**, then a **HYPOTHETICAL** technique to change **bronze to silver / silver to gold**; Themistius praises Valens for turning enemies to friends through **mercy** rather than supernatural or rivalrous power.
- **Original text's composition period is plausibly 366/367; extant Greek text is transmitted in later manuscript/modern critical printed editions**. The modern author's English summary is **not the ancient verbatim Greek**, and we do **not** claim we have collated the complete Dindorf Greek critical apparatus here.
- **Critical logic:** Dufault identifies the notion of possible transmutation **in non-alchemical public rhetoric**, not observed laboratory success, named alchemist, guild, teacher or recipe. The analogy's counterfactual wording must be preserved; it is not a testimonial that a contemporary professional actually possessed the technique. **ATTESTED AUTHORIAL REFERENCE** with **SOURCE-SUPPORTED** reconstructed date, **NO SECURE ALCHEMIST RECOGNITION**. [ST-100]

## W29-T03 — Valentinians' regulatory differentiation of divination and maleficium, 364 and 371

- [Theodosian Code Book IX, CTh IX.16.7–10 Latin](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh09.html) and [full clause text](https://www.forum.thelatinlibrary.com/theodosius/theod09.shtml).
- **IX.16.7 dated 9 September 364:** `ne ... nocturnis temporibus ... nefarias preces aut magicos apparatus aut sacrificia funesta` forbids nighttime harmful prayers, magical preparations or funeral sacrifices. Does not name alchemical laboratory practice.
- **IX.16.8** prohibits `mathematicorum tractatus` (astrology in legal divinatory context), with **DATE ANOMALY**: transmitted consular subscription suggests **12 December 365**, but modern editors propose **370? or 373?**; **never assign one exact 371 date uncritically**.
- **IX.16.9 addressed to Senate, 29 May 371**, `haruspicinam ego nullum cum maleficiorum causis habere consortium`; `nec haruspicinam reprehendimus, sed nocenter exerceri vetamus`. The legal voice explicitly says ordinary **haruspicy** is not inherently identical to punishable **harmful magical acts**. **This is a broad normative distinction, NOT proof all private succession-divination was immune from treason prosecution.**
- **IX.16.10 dated 6 December 371** sends inquiries against senatorials accused of `maleficia` to the urban prefect, with some cases reserved to imperial review. More explicit procedure is not proof acquittal. These are legal sections reproduced in **438 code and later copies**, not separate original autographs. [2025 *Journal of Roman Studies*, legal exemption for temple cults](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-roman-studies/article/legal-exemption-for-the-temple-cults-of-rome-and-alexandria-in-the-fourth-century/8FCF6B2789837BED8D95874E77605DC5) analyzes 371 senatorial context. [ST-102]

## W29-T04 — Ammianus Marcellinus, *Res Gestae* XXIX.1–2, Antioch political-divination investigations

- [Ammianus original Latin XXIX](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/L/Roman/Texts/Ammian/29%2A.html) / [English Loeb Rolfe translation](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Ammian/29%2A.html). Ammianus wrote **LATER IN FOURTH CENTURY** about **Valens's regime**, **not a surviving 371/372 court transcript**; original autographs lost, manuscripts later.
- **Anxiety over who would succeed Valens**, allegations of a ritual device (tripod/dish carrying letters of Greek alphabet with suspended ring) allegedly spelling `THEOD-`; investigation of **Theodorus** (imperial notary), Hilarius, Patricius and associates, brutal interrogation, convictions and executions. Physical ritual device described in an author's narrative, **not independently excavated in this investigation**. Also presence of poison/veneficium accusations, which are **not evidence these persons were chēmeutai**.
- **Chronology:** usual historical placement **late 371 to early 372**, sometimes compressed to **371** in older translations, other scholarship argues different datings including **374**. [Scholarly close chronological reassessment, McGill *Hirundo*](https://www.mcgill.ca/files/classics/2002-04.pdf) reviews 371/372 vs 374; original literary text order is an interpretive rather than documentary day-level date.
- [Attilio Mastrocinque, “The divinatory kit from Pergamon and Greek magic in late antiquity,” *Journal of Roman Archaeology*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1047759400013891) discusses the ring-letter mechanism alongside documented ancient artifacts in different contexts. **Do not equate a comparable archaeological artifact with the actual seized Antioch device.**
- Source grade: **named ancient people in a retrospective but near-generation historian**, likely real political crackdown; direct accusation and written dialogue filtered through Ammianus's political/ethical interpretation; **no named Alchemist, no demonstrated chemical repression**. [ST-103]

## W29-T05 — Valens et al., restrictions on exporting gold, CJ IV.63.2, approximately 374?

- [Original Latin law as transmitted in sixth-century *Codex Justinianus* 4.63.2](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Corpus/CJ4.htm); [alternative transcription](https://www.ancienttexts.org/library/latinlibrary/justinian/codex4.html).
- `Non solum aurum barbaris minime praebeatur` = gold must not be supplied to barbarians; directive even calls for recovery of gold already held, and punishment of merchants transporting gold outside imperial territory for slaves or goods. The heading lists **Valentinian, Valens and Gratian**; addressed to **Tatianus, count of the sacred largesses**. Editorial date **`<A 374 ?>`** — **CIRCA 374 UNCONFIRMED, NOT A SECURE 374-DATED ORIGINAL OBJECT**.
- [Recent scholarly numismatic review on late Roman metal supply](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-roman-archaeology/article/supply-of-metals-in-the-later-roman-empire-framing-the-question/D5964EA36165F3B079B177D59098A661) reads this as significant state concern to retain precious metal; this is economic and strategic material control, **not fear of anyone synthesizing gold**.
- The **legal source survives a sixth-century compilation and still-later manuscripts**, not an original period merchant account or a 374 actual seizure. [ST-104]

## W29-O01 — Real struck Valens gold solidus (367–375 broad)

- [Berlin Münzkabinett ancient solidus, object **18202344**](https://ikmk.smb.museum/object?id=18202344), **Trier mint**, **gold 4.22g / 21mm**, **Valens obverse**, `VICTORIA AVGG` reverse and mint `TROBS` indicating Trier mint and gold-standard/mint-office designation in the museum interpretation. Broad catalog date **367–375 CE**, Depeyrot more narrowly **mid-373–375**; **not** a coin securely made in 367.
- Original ancient metal object attests striking already-existing gold, official issue and precious metal value. No recipe converting copper to gold, mint artisan identity or link to Themistius. Do not assert alchemical production from metallic portrait or standard mark.

## W29-S01 — Later alchemical texts are not authors of Themistius's speech

- [Dufault 2019](https://escholarship.org/content/qt2ks0g83x/qt2ks0g83x.pdf) discusses near-300 **Zosimos** commentarial tradition separately. No evidence Themistius **read** him, was his student, ran an alchemical workshop or employed gold transmuters.
- **Leiden X/Stockholm** original Greek recipe papyri are broadly late-third/early-fourth-century, not evidence a 367 writer made real gold. Pseudo-Democritus / Maria authorial traditions cannot supply a direct oral chain to Themistius.
- **Proclus's commentary around 450 CE** and later Olympiodorus interpretations are **later witnesses**; Dufault compares them but **not 367 contemporary historical events**.

## W29-S02 — Thematic comparative secondary research

- [Themistius Greek Oration 7 edition record](https://catalog.perseus.org/?f%5Byear_facet%5D%5B%5D=1832); oration 366/367 public clemency context.
- [Julian's Greek rescript original-author text in later editions](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Works_of_the_Emperor_Julian/Letters/Letter_36), **distinct from the June 17 Latin law**; privilege of teacher approval vs true technical proficiency.
- [Ammianus Latin and modern scholarship on 371/372](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/L/Roman/Texts/Ammian/29%2A.html) articulate actual repression of **succession divination**; no false bridge to targeted alchemical persecution.
- Original gold [Berlin 18202344](https://ikmk.smb.museum/object?id=18202344) plus gold trade ban as **material economic controls**, not laboratory witness.

**Window verdict:** A named public intellectual **Themistius** invokes *hypothetical* metallic transmutation **366/367**, making the **idea publicly intelligible outside alchemical recipe manuals**. Explicit authorities **licensed teachers** and differentiated **haruspicy vs harmful magic**, yet there is **NO SECURE NAMED 361–380 ALCHEMIST as socially independently authorized profession, prosecuted specialist, or successful goldmaker.**

**Next window 30 — 381–400 CE:** assess later anti-sacrifice/divination laws, actual alchemical-commentary manuscript dates, and competing evidence for **Olympiodorus/Synesius**; do not assume traditional dating proves original c.390 physical autograph.
