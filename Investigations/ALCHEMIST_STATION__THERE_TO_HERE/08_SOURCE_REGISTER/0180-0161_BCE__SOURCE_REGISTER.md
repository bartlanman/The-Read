# Window 02 primary and scholarly sources — 180–161 BCE

**Research pass:** 2026-10-18  
**Evidence rule:** date of source text, modern copy, edited publication and broad object dating are distinct. Quotes and interpretations must remain tied to their actual witness.

## [W02-P01] Memphite Sacerdotal Decree of 161 BCE
- **Object:** Cairo CG 22184 / TM 89794.
- **Composition/issue date:** July/August 161 BCE, according to translation/commentary.
- **Physical text:** Hieroglyphic; fragmentary; later erasures of cartouches.
- **Online translation/editorial introduction:** https://attalus.org/egypt/memphis161.html
- **Scholarly critical study:** Maxim Panov and Eddy Lanciers, "The Memphite Sacerdotal Decree of 161 BCE," *Journal of Egyptian History* 16(1), 30–82 (2023), DOI 10.1163/18741665-bja10017.
- **Warrant:** a later Ptolemaic priestly decree, inscription of royal station, changes to cartouches.
- **Does not establish:** direct alchemical secrecy, that originally three scripts were used, or a securely dated chemical recipe.
- **Dating/interpretive caution:** editors infer probable royal coronation/re-coronation from text, do not turn it into an unchallenged fact.

## [W02-S01] Fischer-Bovet, Ptolemy VI, Oxford Classical Dictionary
- **URL:** https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/548069872
- **Use:** broad chronology of rule in 180–164, 163–145 BCE, regency and disruptions.
- **Does not establish:** individual crafts or alchemy.

## [W02-S02] University College London Digital Egypt, Ptolemy VI
- **URL:** https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/chronology/ptolemyvi.html
- **Use:** dynastic and broader temple-building contexts; note page's own chronological shorthand.
- **Does not establish:** particular early alchemical practice.

## [W02-S03] British Museum catalogued coins of Ptolemy VI
- **Copper alloy:** https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1926-0116-951
- **Alexandrian copper alloy:** https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1994-0915-95
- **Dating:** both cataloged to broad royal reign 180–145 BCE (not necessarily produced in 180–161).
- **Use:** actual coined metal objects in broader period with royal iconography.
- **Does not establish:** specific technique of manufacturing, mint privilege, or alchemical transmutation.

## [W02-S04] Bettineschi et al. (2018), Ptolemaic inlay workshop in Tebtunis sanctuary
- **Title:** "Crafts in the temple: the Ptolemaic inlay workshop in the Soknebtynis sanctuary".
- **URL:** https://iris.unive.it/handle/10278/5020357
- **Use:** temple's artisan/productive functions in Ptolemaic period as archaeology-informed context.
- **Caution:** no narrow dating to this 20-year window from the accessed abstract.

## [W02-S05] Panov and Lanciers (2023), peer-reviewed decree study
- **Publication:** *Journal of Egyptian History* 16(1), 30–82, https://doi.org/10.1163/18741665-bja10017
- **Abstract:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371819822_The_Memphite_Sacerdotal_Decree_of_161_BCE_Plates_I-V
- **Use:** 161 decree is a distinctive later member of priestly-decree corpus, not a simple copying of 196 and 185 texts; possible coronation ceremony; reports later erasures.
- **Limit:** abstract and accessible introduction, not full article close reading yet.

## Outstanding philology

- Record precisely which parts of the hieroglyphic text have erased names and evaluate archaeological context of alteration.
- Seek contemporary workshop records and native terminology for craft training; don't infer technical encryption from sacred script.
