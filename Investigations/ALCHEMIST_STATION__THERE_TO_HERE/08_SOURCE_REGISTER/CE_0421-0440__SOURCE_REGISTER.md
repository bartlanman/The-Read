# Window 32 — Source and Witness Register, 421–440 CE

**Source audit:** 2026-10-18. **Verdict:** strong period evidence for **state-authorized public teaching, comparative coin-weight measurement, selection of binding textual authority, and explicitly targeted destruction of certain theological writings**, but **NO named alchemical professional, certified chrysopoeia, or evidence of an alchemical manuscript burning in 421–440**.

**Grades:** ATTESTED original-period object; dated ancient law TRANSMITTED in later code; dated ancient Roman Senate minutes preserved in later manuscript; historical AUTHORIAL narrative composed c.439–440 about earlier events; SOURCE-SUPPORTED interpretation; CONTESTED; NO SECURE EVIDENCE. A late antique law in a sixth-century manuscript is **not** an original fifth-century autograph. Source labels in square brackets are *research identifiers*, not invented manuscript inventory numbers.

## W32-L01 — 9 April and 8 June 423: pagan ritual enforcement with express guard against assaults

- [CTh XVI.10.22–24 original Latin as later compiled](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh16.html). **9 April 423**, XVI.10.22 to Asclepiodotus, says any remaining pagans should be constrained by existing measures. Its rhetoric `quamquam iam nullos esse credamus` (“though we believe none remain”) is an **official claim**, not evidence that actual pagans had disappeared.
- **8 June 423**, XVI.10.23 prescribes **confiscation and exile** for pagans convicted of sacrifices even though it says they would have deserved a capital penalty; 16.10.24 includes an explicit prohibition against Christians attacking **quiet Jews and pagans** not violating the law (`iudaeis ac paganis in quiete degentibus ... non audeant manus inferre`). **This is differentiated legal treatment**: condemned ritual practices vs protection against extralegal violence. Later code excerpts, not an original 423 trial transcript.
- Original **legal enactments were in Window 32**; publication of the code housing them belongs to **438**, so event date and later selection/copying must be separately recorded. No named Alchemist, laboratory, recipe or prosecuted metalworker in these paragraphs. **[ST-115]**

## W32-L02 — 27 February 425: authorized public professorships, CTh XIV.9.3

- [CTh XIV.9.3 (Latin text, Sections pr. and 1)](https://www.forum.thelatinlibrary.com/theodosius/theod14.shtml); [alternate full-text version](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/theodosius/theod14.shtml). The law of **Theodosius II and Valentinian III (then Caesar)**, at Constantinople, **27 February 425**, forbids unauthorized teachers `usurpantes sibi nomina magistrorum` from collecting students in unauthorized **public school rooms**; sanctions include disgrace and exclusion from the city, yet **private tutoring within domestic walls remains permitted** for those without special Capitoline appointment.
- Prescribed public faculty: **3 Latin orators + 10 Latin grammarians + 5 Greek sophists/rhetorical lecturers + 10 Greek grammarians + 1 teacher of `philosophiae arcana` + 2 legal teachers = 31 positions.** `philosophiae arcana` is the “deeper matters/mysteries of philosophy” as part of officially sanctioned philosophical education; **not an imperial chrysopoeia certification**, secret metal recipe or demonstration of effective alchemical technique.
- State-authorized education **sets boundaries between public recognized office and allowable private instruction**. Statute does not provide a list of **31 actually hired or named teachers**, evidence that all chairs were filled, or an alchemical professor. Transmitted in **438 code**, later physical manuscripts. [ST-116]

## W32-O01 — actual 425–430 `EXAGIVM SOLIDI` coin standard; and separate 425–435 Alexandria copper coin

- [Pondera scholarly weight catalogue #8094](https://pondera.uclouvain.be/artifact/8094/), British Museum original object **1987,0703.24**: exagium **solidi** standard, iconographic emperors **Theodosius II and Valentinian III**, marked `EXAGIVM SOLIDI`, `CONS` for Constantinople, and figure with **scales**. **Modern Asolati 2022 assigns c.425–430**, whereas museum broader dating **early fifth century**. Metal, exact weight and workshop operator are not specified in the Pondera excerpt used here; **DO NOT INVENT WEIGHT MASS OR ASSAY RESULTS**. This is **original-period physical evidence of an officially represented benchmark for coin weight**, not proof of laboratory synthesis.
- [Berlin Münzkabinett original gold solidus, object 18213856](https://ikmk.smb.museum/object?id=18213856), Theodosius II **Thessalonica**, RIC X 362 (typically **424–425**, alternative **425–430** catalog dating); **gold, 4.37 grams, 20 mm**. The official weight and real gold coin are different objects with different provenance and chronology; no verified test showing the specific catalogued coin weighed with that exact weight.
- [British Museum 1995,0201.83](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1995-0201-83), original **Alexandria copper alloy nummus**, **425–435**, **1.19 g (fragment)** and **11 mm max**, Theodosius II portrait and cross within wreath. A copper coin and gold solidus show currency material production, **not copper-to-gold transmutation**. [ST-117]

## W32-L03 — 26 March 429 and 20 December 435: lawful editorial power over inherited texts

- [*Codex Theodosianus* I.1.5 (Latin original transmitted), dated **26 March 429**](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/theodosius/theod01.shtml): emperor orders aggregation of **general constitutions from Constantine onward**, assignment to legal topic titles, with chronology and textual comparison used to rank apparently contradictory rulings. First commission names Antiochus and other court experts; projected second ideal code in proposed programme **NOT independently completed as originally envisaged**.
- [CTh I.1.6, **20 December 435**](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/theodosius/theod01.shtml): revised/new committee and explicit power to **remove unnecessary words, add necessary words, alter ambiguities and correct inconsistencies** (`demendi supervacanea verba et adiciendi necessaria et demutandi ambigua et emendandi incongrua`). This is **deliberate official redaction**, openly authorized, NOT accidental scribal corruption or a hidden cipher.
- [Caroline Humfress, legal compilation assessment](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/10023/8865/Humfress_2016_Ordering_FinalPublishedVersion.pdf%3Bsequence%3D1), specifically separates 429 selection purpose, 435 editorial regime, and later authoritative adoption. **Explicit difference:** writing a legal statute, selecting/excerpting it into corpus, receiving authoritative copy, and modern observing surviving manuscript are four distinct events. [ST-118]

## W32-L04 — 3 August 435: targeted theological manuscripts ordered searched out and burned

- [CTh XVI.5.66.1–2, Latin](https://www.ancienttexts.org/library/latinlibrary/theod16.html) **3 August 435** in standard critical subscription (`III Non. Aug.`); [Justinianic redaction CJ 1.5.6 alternate modern date **5 Aug 435**](https://www.uwyo.edu/lawlib/blume-justinian/ajc-edition-2/books/book1/Book%201-5rev.pdf) (discrepant dating/conversion preserved, not silently overwritten). The law to **Leontius**, urban prefect, stigmatizes followers of condemned **Nestorius** after **431 Ephesus** and **explicitly commands searching for and publicly burning Nestorius's theological writings** (`quos diligenti studio requiri ac publice conburi decernimus`).
- **Important direct vs inferred:** we have an **actual legal command to find and burn specifically attributed doctrinal books**, but not a list of every book located, an original confiscation inventory, proof of perfect enforcement, or a statute naming `chēmeia` or goldmaking authors. **Do not generalize it to an alchemical book purge**.
- Historical witness 435 law → Theodosian compilation promulgated 438 → later surviving manuscript copies; and parallel Justinianic sixth-century redaction, with textual/version/date differences. [ST-119]

## W32-L05 — February 438 promulgation, Roman Senate reception 25 December 438, force 1 January 439

- [University of Cologne, *Bibliotheca legum*, Codex Theodosianus](https://www.leges.uni-koeln.de/en/lex/codex-theodosianus/): editors compiled **429–438**, promulgated **February 438**, **effective January 1, 439**. A **sixteen-book compilation** organizes general imperial law from Constantine on; books **1–5 largely fragmentary**, **6–16 more substantially survive**, **not 16 complete original autographs**.
- [`Gesta Senatus Romani de Theodosiano publicando` original Latin as edited from **later Ambrosian manuscript C.29 inf**](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/public.htm) records **Western Roman Senate 438** reception/public recognition, naming **Faustus**, **Anastasius**, **Martinus** and formal acclamations. John F. Matthews, [*Laying Down the Law*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt211qwgm), specifies **25 December 438** for Rome Senate promulgation/reception. This is **a transcribed record of proceedings surviving in a medieval manuscript**, not an original carved 438 legal stone. Details of dates differ between **eastern February promulgation, western December Senate action, and Jan 439 general entry into force**.
- [Library of Congress original surviving **sixth-century parchment copy**, Codex Books VI–VIII](https://www.loc.gov/item/2021668234/). It is an **early surviving physical manuscript** (sixth century), **not** the **438 master imperial copy**, and proves significant later textual custody. [*The Christianization of Knowledge in Late Antiquity* manuscript discussion](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/christianization-of-knowledge-in-late-antiquity/new-texts/8EC8446C48B045AE5EFE73BE6B6CFE9B) says earliest surviving witness **Vat. Reg. lat. 886** late fifth / early sixth, with margins/private-copy signs, and sacred original authority. Distinguish Library of Congress sixth-century *a* manuscript from earliest surviving witness overall; the source claims are compatible.
- **Public corpus station:** named authorized compilers and rules for officially disseminated authoritative copies, without any new **Alchemist accreditation**. [ST-120]

## W32-H01 — Socrates Scholasticus writing ca.439–440 about 415 Hypatia, and reception

- [*Ecclesiastical History* VII.15 primary text of Hypatia murder](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/26017.htm); [Church historian introduction and scope to 439](https://royalarchives.org/socrates_scholasticus/ecclesiastical_history_of_socrates_scholasticus/introduction/); [older scholarly edition introductory commentary](https://www.catholicculture.org/culture/library/fathers/view.cfm?recnum=2884).
- Socrates's history covers through **439**, scholarly reconstruction **completion c.439 or shortly thereafter (~440)**. His **narration of March 415 murder** is therefore **an early fifth-century EVENT re-described in a c.440 textual composition**, not a new 439 death. Socrates represents Hypatia as **public philosophy teacher** and criticizes violence; he **does not identify her as an Alchemist, original bronze-to-gold maker, or teacher of alchemical text**.
- Additional church historians **Sozomen and Theodoret** have partially overlapping historical horizons but different composition/reception chronologies and agendas; should not be counted automatically as independent 439 eyewitnesses to Hypatia. [ST-121]

## W32-A01 — Later alchemical Greek commentaries are not securely 425–440 texts

- [Olivier Dufault, *Early Greek Alchemy* (2019)](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ks0g83x) Zosimos **c.300**, alchemical patronage and commentaries mostly in **later manuscript** tradition.
- [Martelli/Dorandi 2011 pseudo-Democritus/Synesius manuscript critical notices](https://pinakes-dev.irht.cnrs.fr/notices/bibliographie/J8SKCZW8/): “Synesius to Dioscorus, priest of Serapis” remains an **undated/contested authorial attribution**, not automatically bishop **Synesius of Cyrene** or 425 alchemical school.
- [Stanford Encyclopedia, Olympiodorus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/olympiodorus/): alchemical commentary author **disputed**, not securely 430; known Neoplatonic Olympiodorus the Younger **sixth century**; other namesakes must not merge.
- **No original 425 alchemical equipment described in the 425 school law**, **no Greek/Latin alchemist term in legal chair roster**, and no documented alchemical offender in the 435 book prohibition. **Station verdict NO SECURE 421–440 Alchemist**.

## Historical remainder

- Did all **31** Constantinople school positions attract named teachers, and how did instruction proceed? The law defines **positions**, not observed appointment roster.
- Did all Nestorius-associated books get found? No actual manuscript seizure list present in these sources.
- Original 438 master code's physical fate unknown; early sixth-century copies attest transmission, not that all versions are perfectly identical.
- What original working role did the **425–430 exagium solidi** have at a specific mint? Weight's marked purpose clear, individual usage/assay not demonstrated. No precise mass supplied.
- Hypatia's **415 event** and Socrates's **c.440 narration** are different milestones; witnesses cannot make the dead teacher alive in 439.
- No demonstrated period **professionally titled Alchemist**, elemental production of gold, or historical bridge between 425 pedagogy, 435 censorship and a Greek alchemical author.

**Next — Window 33 (441–460 CE):** examine securely dated fifth-century original craft/material or manuscript copying, new laws and cross-border transmission; never treat 438 Codex's **later physical manuscript** as a 450 original event or make contested Olympiodorus a 450 professor without independent evidence.
