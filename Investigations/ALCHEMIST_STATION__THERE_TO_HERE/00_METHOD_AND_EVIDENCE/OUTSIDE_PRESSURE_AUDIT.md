# OUTSIDE PRESSURE AUDIT — Station response protocol

Required for every historical 20-year interval in the *Alchemist — THERE → HERE* project.

## Ten-field frame
Historical conditions → material practice → language/allegory → secrecy/access/danger → perception → station → lineage → texts → HERE and remainder → **Outside pressure / station response**.

## Four compulsory research moves
1. **Identify a force outside the station** before describing a shift: institution, regulation, market, evidence, technology, audience, language, public sentiment, rival or patron.
2. **Identify its encounter with the station**, including exact textual/material witnesses and dates. A general history of science is not automatically evidence for a local actor.
3. **Demonstrate the reaction**: adaptation, adoption, resistance, loss of office, loss of funding, critique, new credential, pseudonymous concealment, public disclosure, reinterpretation, or no demonstrable change.
4. **Track survival vs loss**: What technique survives, what explanatory claim is rejected, what term is changed, what texts remain, and who now bears authority?

## Demarcation/labeling test
- Who says 'alchemy', 'chemistry', 'science', 'fraud', or 'pseudoscience', in which language, at what date, about what specific operation?
- Is it the actor's own category, a contemporary rival's attack, or a later historian's judgment?
- Was disagreement based on evidence, ontology, legal standing, religion, prestige, patronage, or institutional demarcation?
- What source would falsify our account of 'a station being pushed out'?

## Status vocabulary
DIRECT / RETROSPECTIVE / SCHOLARLY INFERENCE / INTERPRETATION / NO SECURE EVIDENCE.
No 20-year timestamp should be invented for a slow shift. Where no clear dated cause exists, mark a broad or undetermined window.

## Contemporary guardrail
**After 2020 the current 2021–2040 window is incomplete as of 2026-10-18.** It must not assert events after the current date; leave the 2027–2040 portion explicitly FUTURE / NOT YET OBSERVABLE.
