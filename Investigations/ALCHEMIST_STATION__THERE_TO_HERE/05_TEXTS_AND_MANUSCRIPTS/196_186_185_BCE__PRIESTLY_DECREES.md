# Primary-text case study — Memphis and Philae priestly decrees

**Purpose:** Track texts that demonstrably came from an attested *priestly station* during Window 01, without misclassifying them as alchemical literature.

## 196 BCE — Memphis Decree (Rosetta Stone), BM EA24

- **Origin:** assembled Egyptian priests at Memphis; named ruler Ptolemy V.
- **Genre:** politico-religious decree, not alchemical treatise.
- **Language:** Hieroglyphic Egyptian, Demotic Egyptian, Greek.
- **Physical witness:** surviving granodiorite stela, British Museum EA24, dated 196 BCE.
- **Evidence-bearing sections:** Demotic lines 4–5 (priestly categories and House of Life scribes); 9–10 (tax and temple linen); 22–27 (royal images and gold diadems); 29–31 (ritual honors and additional priestly titles); 31–32 (three scripts, public inscription).
- **Warrant:** formal religious titles/duties + temple craft resources + royal-priestly authority in one document.
- **Does not warrant:** metallurgical transmutation or a self-identifying alchemist.

## 186 BCE — Second Philae Decree (TM 48339)

- **Origin:** priestly assembly in royal setting following suppression of rebellion.
- **Genre:** political-sacerdotal decree and commemoration.
- **Witness:** temple inscription at Philae; online translation/extract by L. Török and W. Müller.
- **Evidence-bearing sections:** opening identifies various priestly and scribal stations; later orders new royal cult statues and public copies.
- **Warrant:** repeat public formulation of priestly authority under changing conditions.
- **Caution:** hostile descriptions of rebels are royal-priestly claims; available online translation contains omissions.

## 185/184 BCE — First Philae Decree (TM 48335)

- **Origin:** priests gathered at Memphis; copied on a temple wall at Philae.
- **Genre:** political-sacerdotal decree, chronologically *later* than so-called Second Philae.
- **Witness:** Philae text; excerpt translation attributed to W. Müller.
- **Evidence-bearing sections:** names temple offices, fine linen and fiscal privileges, silver/gold/precious stones donated for goddess statues and cult practice.
- **Warrant:** rearticulation of temple privileges and material/sacred object value.
- **Caution:** source dates this 185/4 BCE and the excerpt is partial; do not collapse either uncertainty.

## 183–182 BCE — Naukratis-region limestone stela

- **Catalog:** British Museum Naukratis Project X__3907; ownership and collections records identify Cairo JE22264, CG22188.
- **Date:** 183–182 BCE (year 23 of Ptolemy V), per catalog.
- **Object:** limestone stela with hieroglyphic lines and king depicted with deities; decoration described as unfinished.
- **Warrant:** material witness to this period's political-sacred inscriptions.
- **Does not warrant:** that this is a copy of the earlier decrees or that it pertains to alchemical techniques.

## Documented miniature textual lineage

196 BCE Memphis decree → 186 BCE Second Philae decree → 185/184 BCE First Philae decree.

**Arrow definition:** repeated and revised *institutional form*, with text explicitly reiterating privileges and honors; **NOT** a line of alchemists. The 183–182 BCE stela is a separately cataloged witness, not inserted in that direct sequence.
