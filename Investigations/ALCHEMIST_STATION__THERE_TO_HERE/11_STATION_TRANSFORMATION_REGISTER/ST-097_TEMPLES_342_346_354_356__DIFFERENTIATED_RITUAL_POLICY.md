# ST-097 — 342/346–356: Conflicting Temple Preservation and Sacrifice-Closure Prescriptions

**Scope:** comparative civic-ritual station; chronology contested. **Dates:** XVI.10.3 **1 Nov 346 [or 342]**; XVI.10.4 **1 Dec 346 [or 354?]**; XVI.10.5 **23 Nov 353**, XVI.10.6 **19 Feb 356**. **Witness:** texts collected in *Codex Theodosianus* (438), later copies. **Grade:** ATTESTED prescriptions with CONTESTED dates and **NO SECURE EVIDENCE alchemy**. [Window 28](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0341-0360_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Civic games, traditional temples, ritual offices and emperor-driven religious policy persist in a contested religious field. The law compilation contains differently targeted instructions that cannot be homogenized.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
Temples hosted festivals/games, and worshippers offered sacrifice. No documented craft operation, named alchemist, specific apparatus or changing metals appears.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
**XVI.10.3** preserves extramural temples associated with games, to remain `intactae incorruptaeque`. **XVI.10.4** commands temples shut and sacrifice forbidden; **XVI.10.5–6** add more restrictive measures. Differences may concern time, setting, or later chronology; not every traditional ritual became instantly physically impossible.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Imperial authority at once recognizes usefulness of some civic religious structures and restricts specific rites. Temple building and a sacrificial act can have different status; any link to an **Alchemist's priestly office** remains unwitnessed.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Penalties create conceivable fear for ritual specialists, **not proof** of clandestine alchemical laboratory, encrypted metal recipes or guild migration. Actual enforcement unknown.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`intactae incorruptaeque` means buildings preserved, **not** a grant of unrestricted sacrifice. `templa`, `sacrificia` are religious legal categories. The date pair **346/[342]** and **346/[354?]** is printed with scholarly corrections in the source; keep all options.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
342/346–356 changing official policy pressures sacred places and rites. Precisely identifying its chronology is prerequisite to inferring an institutional response.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
The state prescribes preservation of designated sites and separate closures/penalties. No original field observation of uniform destruction, executed priests or persecuted alchemical commentators is presented.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
