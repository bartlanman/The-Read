# ST-114 — March 415: Hypatia's Philosophical Station Becomes Vulnerable to City Conflict

**Scope:** historically recognizable teacher/philosopher murdered in political-religious conflict, **NOT independently documented as an Alchemist**. **Event:** **March 415** by consular date. **Textual witness:** Socrates Scholasticus *Ecclesiastical History* VII.15, composed approximately **c.440**, preserved in later MSS; ancient Greek historiography and modern editions. **Grade:** **SOURCE-SUPPORTED RETROSPECTIVE HISTORICAL WITNESS**, not a 415 autographic court record. [Window 31](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0401-0420_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Hypatia publicly taught mathematics/Neoplatonic philosophy at Alexandria, was associated with prominent students (including Synesius), and in Socrates's history interacted openly with prefect **Orestes** amid growing conflict with bishop **Cyril**.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
Authentic pupil correspondence shows her recognized teaching and possible coordination of instrument production and text review. No 415 source demonstrates Hypatia fabricated a hydrometer or transformed base metals into gold.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Socrates praises her philosophical learning and public esteem; his narrative says some people suspected she obstructed reconciliation between Cyril and Orestes. This accusation is **reported contemporary hostile perception**, not verified actual act or scientific controversy.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Students and civic elites treated Hypatia as a teacher/thinker, but a Christian mob led by **Peter the reader** (in Socrates's account) attacked her. Her status **did not prevent lethal extralegal violence**. The text does not independently prove Cyril personally ordered the killing.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
A deadly threat to a philosopher is documented retrospectively; do **not** infer she hid recipes, belonged to secret chemical society, or was murdered for pursuing alchemy. The event predates modern “science vs religion” categories and cannot be collapsed into them.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
Greek author Socrates's designation **Hypatia philosopher** is a real historical-teaching identity, not a profession of Alchemist. Some websites' **413/414 date** conflicts with his consular year and Lent/March **415**; resolve event and later composition dates.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Alexandrian civil/church political conflict around **415** between prefect Orestes and bishop Cyril made a publicly visible intellectual a target of communal hostility.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Socrates records her capture and murder and condemns it as scandal for church and bishop. **Actual Hypatia self-authored response at the attack is unavailable**. Synesius appears to have died c.413, so no 415 reaction by him can be attributed.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[Socrates Scholasticus HE VII.15](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/26017.htm); composed **c.440 about 415**, later physical manuscript texts. Synesius Letters 15, 154 from prior years verify teacher relationship independently, not murder details.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
A real individual may enjoy extensive **recognized social/intellectual standing** yet lack practical protection when political/religious struggle transforms the institutional field.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Full causation/culpability, original court proceedings, competing histories, precise involvement of civic/religious leaders, material fate of Hypatia's writings, impact on specific schools, any Alchemist connection.
