# ST-092 — 334 CE: The State Protects Training for Scarce Architects

**Scope:** legal scholarly/craft capacity comparator (NOT an Alchemist). **Named legal date:** **27 August 334** in standard transmitted subscription (date and regional applicability subject to editorial control). **Witness:** **CTh 13.4.1** in later Theodosian compilation and manuscript transmission, **NOT an original 334 stone**. **Verdict:** **ATTESTED TRANSMITTED LEGISLATION**, real apprenticeship outcomes unverified. [Window 27](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0321-0340_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Urban construction in Africa required more trained architects than imperial authorities claimed were available. Architecture was an identifiable technical role requiring literacy/education, skills and practiced experience, not merely a supernatural status.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
The law requests candidates around eighteen with some prior education who wish to learn architecture, offers privileged status to them/parents, and provides financial aid. It does **not** identify names of actual students or demonstrate that particular buildings were built because of the law.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
The ruler presents specialized knowledge as **scarce and worth cultivating**: `Architectis quam plurimis opus est; sed quia non sunt`. This is evidence for a governmental value claim about trained competence, not a survey of every region's actual workforce.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
The emperor addresses the provincial administration through **CTh 13.4.1**; state can create material conditions for learning. It does not confer an alchemical initiatory grade or direct a named chemist to teach.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Protective public policy helps capacity and transmission; **NOT a secret craft monopoly**. No hazards or deliberate code are named; no persecution avoidance can be inferred.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`architecti` = architects; `liberalibus litteris` describes education, not alchemical masters; `opus est` is expressed labor demand. Original language survives through fifth-century codification and later physical copies, not a near-334 original object.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Perceived shortage of architects by 334 drives an imperial educational exemption/allowance. The administrative prescription is the documented response; implementation, population trained and skill improvement unmeasured.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Legal exemptions and payments specified to support training. **No actual completed scholarship, apprenticeship contract, named architect or alchemist** documented by this law itself.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
**CTh XIII.4.1** [Latin in later compilation](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Codex_Theodosianus/Liber_XIII), [translated source with annotation](https://readancienttexts.com/theodosius/liber_xiii/1). Code published **438**, later surviving manuscripts, not contemporaneous physical inscription.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
Institutional authority explicitly **makes room for becoming capable**, not just for possession of existing expertise. This supplies a concrete comparative mechanism for transmission of skills.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Exact candidates and placements; enforcement, funding, regional actual scarcity; relations to any goldmaking expert or alchemical texts.
