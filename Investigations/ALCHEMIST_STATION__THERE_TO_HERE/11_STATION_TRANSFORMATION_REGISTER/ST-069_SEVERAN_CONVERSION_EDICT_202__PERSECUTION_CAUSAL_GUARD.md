# ST-069 — 202–203 CE: Martyrdom Is Documented; a Universal Severan Conversion Ban Is Disputed

**Historical event:** persecution and martyrdom of Christians including **Perpetua and Felicitas in 203 CE**.
**Source conflict:** late `Historia Augusta` attributes to Septimius Severus a ban on conversion to Judaism and Christianity; legal historians doubt the existence or wide enforceability of **one empire-wide specifically anti-Christian conversion edict**.
**Evidence grade:** source-critical CAUTION record. **This is not testimony to persecution of alchemical practitioners.**

## THERE
A mixed Roman religious environment, imperial local administration and changing attitudes toward Christians and other religious groups.

## AUTHORITY / OUTSIDE
Some Roman authorities punished Christian believers. Separate historical witnesses give differing descriptions of the emperor's personal directions and provincial policies.

## SECRECY / LANGUAGE
**Witness to persecution** does not automatically identify a universal statutory basis; neither establishes a secret Greek goldmaking sect was targeted. Do not infer the motive for pseudonymous alchemical writings simply from proximity to Christianity and Roman persecution.

## RESPONSE / HERE
**Recognizing a documented victim and event is separate from proving a single universal law that caused it.** This is a stringent parallel to any future claim an Alchemist concealed a formula from danger.

## REMAINDER
Exact local legal charges, dates of edicts, regional variation, independent documentary proof of conversion prohibition, whether any chemical practitioner was threatened (NONE known here).
