# ST-036 — Dioscorides, First-Century CE: Matter Prepared for Medicine Is Not Gold Made

**Region:** Greek-language medicine across the Roman world.
**Date:** `De materia medica` conventionally **c.50–70 CE**, a span crossing **41–60** and **61–80** windows; exact composition year not independently secured.
**Witness:** first-century physician/writer, surviving text through much later manuscripts and recensions.
**Status:** documented **medical mineral-processing literature**. Not a securely named alchemist station.

## THERE
Drug preparation includes plants, minerals and animal substances. Greek medical writers have traditions of describing substances, qualities and methods of preparation.

## CAPACITY
`De materia medica` records a method described by historians as obtaining mercury from cinnabar with heat and enclosed equipment. The existence of the technical passage is stronger evidence of a transmissible operation than a claim of miraculous gold production. Original apparatus not recovered. **Mercury exposure is hazardous**; reconstructing the heating procedure is not recommended outside professionally controlled conditions.

## PERCEPTION
The skill of a medical preparer and the authority of a writer on medications are different from an emperor's grant of a transcendent title or later authorial claims of chemical goldmaking.

## AUTHORITY
Dioscorides' work becomes authoritative as a medical corpus via copying and reception. This is **textual and medical prestige**, not proof that he practiced the Greek `Four Books` transformation arts or held formal 'Alchemist' office.

## SECRECY AND LANGUAGE
Mineral identification and names can vary across ancient languages and later translations. Obscurity from manuscript transmission is **not proof of deliberate ancient encoding**. Mercury/cinnabar nomenclature needs philological checking.

## THE OUTSIDE
Need to prepare medicinal substances and systematize technical information for readers who may be geographically separated from practitioners.

## RESPONSE
A collection transmits operations and materia medica names that later readers can study and adapt, but a manuscript does not itself confer trained practical judgment.

## HERE
**A working material technique can be described by a recognizable professional writer without establishing the more ambitious claims of metal transmutation or supernatural authority.**

## REMAINDER
Exact year of composition, physical tools contemporary to author, wording in earliest Greek layers, reader/apprentice network and efficacy of every recorded drug.
