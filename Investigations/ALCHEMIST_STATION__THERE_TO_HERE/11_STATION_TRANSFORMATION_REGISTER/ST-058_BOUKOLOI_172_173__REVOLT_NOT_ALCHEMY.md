# ST-058 — c.172–173 CE: Northern Nile Delta Revolt Is Not Evidence of an Alchemist Purge

**Location:** Roman Egypt's northern Nile Delta, especially communities conventionally called **Boukoloi** ('herdsmen/cowherds') in Greco-Roman sources.
**Witness:** Cassius Dio/later literary accounts; modern socioeconomic and environmental reconstruction by Katherine Blouin. Dates c.172–173 with broader unrest through mid-170s.
**Status:** serious political and military unrest, detailed motives and group identities disputed. **No documented Alchemist persecution.**

## THERE
Rural communities experience changing watercourses, agricultural pressures, taxation, Roman and local power contests.

## CAPACITY
Rural work, social organization and insurgency involve actual skills and people, yet later narratives frequently reduce them to animal-herders and strange rites.

## PERCEPTION / AUTHORITY
Roman historians shape a threatening outsider image; some ancient reports include priestly figures (notably **Isidorus**). Neither an Egyptian priest nor rural rebel should be renamed an Alchemist without texts showing chemical techniques and occupational identity.

## LANGUAGE / SECRECY
`Boukoloi` is a social/ethnographic label susceptible to stereotyping. References to extraordinary or gruesome rites in some later accounts must be treated as hostile narratives, not independent evidence of secret experimental metallurgy.

## OUTSIDE / RESPONSE
Environmental/agricultural-fiscal pressure and armed conflict meet Roman suppression associated with general **Avidius Cassius**.

## HERE
**Political vulnerability of rural occupations and priestly reputations is real, but it is not proof of a separate persecuted alchemical profession.** Historicizing secrecy requires an actual link, not mere proximity of rebellion and occult stereotypes.

## REMAINDER
Named local participants, date and exact causes, evidence from their own perspective, effects on workshops and any actual technical documents.
