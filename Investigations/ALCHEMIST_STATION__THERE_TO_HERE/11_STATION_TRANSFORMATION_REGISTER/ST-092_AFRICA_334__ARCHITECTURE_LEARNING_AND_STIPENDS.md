# ST-092 — 334: Roman Law Incentivizes Architectural Training Instead of Merely Buying Output

**Scope:** documented technical-education station comparator, **not a Greek alchemical school**. **Event:** Constantine to Felix on African architects, posted Carthage **27 August 334** by transmitted subscription (minor date uncertainty in editions). **Witness:** *Codex Theodosianus* 13.4.1 (**fifth-century compilation**, later manuscripts). **Grade:** ancient legal directive preserved in later text, not original 334 stone. [Window 27](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0321-0340_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Imperial administration sought skilled architects to serve construction needs in African provinces. The law states that architects are needed but insufficient in number.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
The policy targets literate youths around eighteen as prospective students of architecture. It mentions **salary for trainees** and exemptions from some burdens for students and parents. No names of students, examinations or built projects survive in this wording. Craft competence is a stated objective, not verified output.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Imperial authority believed education plus relief from obligations might produce experts. This recognizes **capacity can be made through learning**, but cannot certify each participant mastered skills.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
`Imp. Constantinus a. ad Felicem` in the later legal code, addressed to an imperial official, creates a protected **training pathway**, not a specific named job appointment.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
No apprenticeship secrecy, ingredient cipher, exclusivity based on danger or hidden alchemical rite. Public incentive program, not clandestine schooling.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`architectis quam plurimis opus est; sed quia non sunt` articulates shortage; `liberales litteras` foundational education, `salarium` allowance. **`Architectus` is not `chēmeutēs`.**

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and date it entered
Public works demand meets insufficient number of technical specialists **334**, prompting governmental training support.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
State explicitly orders educational encouragement and burden relief. **Actual enrollment/results not documented**; no 334 alchemist's patronage contract.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[*CTh* 13.4.1 Latin](https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/theodosius/theod13.shtml) dated 334 via subscription, conserved in **438 compilation/later MSS**; not excavated contemporary posting.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
A technical station may be **built institutionally by support for training**, an important mechanism for future comparison to whether alchemical competence became recognized.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Actual beneficiary names, number of architects produced, broader enforcement, exact African provincial impact, connection to Greek alchemical scribes/teachers.
