# ST-007 — Luan Da, 113–112 BCE: Prestige, Withholding and Collapse of a Station

**Date:** honors c.113 BCE; execution conventionally 112 BCE.  
**Source:** *Shiji* 28, close-reading reference; later duplicated in reconstructed *Shiji* 12.  
**Verdict:** exceptionally strong evidence **within historical narrative** of direct patron-created office and reversal; promised goldmaking unverified.

## THERE
Imperial court recruiting *fangshi*, with unmet promises of gold, immortality and flood relief; Shaoweng already executed and death concealed.

## CAPACITY
Luan **claimed** a master who could make gold, control floodwater, obtain life-extending medicine, and summon immortals. The emperor saw a minor moving-object demonstration, not proof of the stated outcomes.

## PERCEPTION
Luan's eloquent claims impressed an emperor concerned by an actual Yellow River disaster and lack of goldmaking success.

## AUTHORITY: The station was constructed by the ruler
- **Court entry** through Jiaodong queen and Marquis of Lecheng.
- **Previous position** as Jiaodong royal *shangfang* (court workshop official), meaning of exact duties to verify.
- **New titles** military/spiritual offices including 五利將軍 and 天道將軍, multiple seals, a marquisate of 2,000 households, estate and numerous servants.
- **Marriage** to imperial Princess Wei and guest-like honors: an unusual *officially materialized* station, not merely secret personal identity.

## SECRECY AND LANGUAGE
Luan invokes an **alleged master**, social hierarchy of immortals, restrictive access to powerful methods, and explicit fear of execution: 方士皆奄口. He says masters do not seek patrons; rulers must seek and honor the master or envoy. Also after Luan's rise, coastal practitioners reportedly claimed **禁方**, restricted arts.

These are **explicit secrecy-and-authority claims**, not proof of deliberately encrypted usable chemical formulas or universal fear among practitioners.

## OUTSIDE PRESSURE
- Flooding / demand for practical remedy.
- Failure to produce the promised gold.
- Desire for immortality and legitimation.
- Risk of execution for court fraud.
- Emperor's secret surveillance and repudiation when claims fail.

## RESPONSE
Luan is richly elevated before the central claims succeed. When he alleges a trip east to a supernatural master, court agents follow and find no corroboration. The emperor orders execution (c.112 BCE). Luan's *personal official station* collapses; demand for other specialists continues.

## HERE
**A court can create high station by conferring titles, marriages and seals on a claim, then dissolve it by moving the test of legitimacy from patron belief to the unacceptable failure of promised performance.**

This directly engages our question whether secrecy is an instrument of protection or creation of the station: Luan's account uses both positions strategically.

## REMAINDER
Independent reality of alleged teacher, actual recipe, physical mechanics of moving-objects demonstration, historical authenticity of speeches, exact chronology of execution, any general anti-*fangshi* prohibition.
