# ST-040 — 96 CE: The Emperor's Name Survives Erasure in Egyptian Hieroglyphs

**Date of erasure attempt:** after Domitian's assassination, **96 CE**.
**Original material:** Benevento Isis obelisks **88/89 CE**, Domitian names in still extant cartouches.
**Literary witness:** Suetonius, *Domitian* 23.1, compiled decades after event.
**Verdict:** MATERIAL PRESERVATION CONFIRMED; exact reason for survival **INTERPRETATION / UNKNOWN**, not intentional original cipher.

## THERE
Domitian had been honored in Egyptian monumental script on Italian obelisks, patron Rutilius Lupus. Names and political station were visible even to viewers unable to read them.

## THE OUTSIDE
Senate's hostility after 96 CE murder and order to erase imperial inscriptions and memory, according to Suetonius.

## RESPONSE
Many public Latin/Greek honorific inscriptions and images were removed/recut, but Domitian's hieroglyphic cartouches on Benevento obelisks survived.

## PERCEPTION AND AUTHORITY
Different audiences possess different powers of recognition: rulers might order a name removed, carvers might not know which signs spelled it, local authorities might not fully enforce edict, or the monument might be exempt/overlooked. **Which mechanism prevailed is not demonstrable from survival alone.**

## SECRECY / LANGUAGE
This is **public but specialized writing**, not intentional concealment. Luigi Prada notes the irony that an Egyptian script could preserve the name Roman authorities wanted erased. No evidence the 88/89 CE author anticipated 96 CE events.

## HERE
**Language can preserve evidence because it lies beyond a later institution's practical interpretive reach, without being written to keep a secret.** This is a powerful counterweight to the assumption that all opaque alchemical allegory exists to avoid persecution.

## REMAINDER
Local compliance with 96 edict, who could interpret royal cartouches, whether stone location or lack of enforcement explains survival, comparisons across Egyptian inscriptions in Rome/Italy.
