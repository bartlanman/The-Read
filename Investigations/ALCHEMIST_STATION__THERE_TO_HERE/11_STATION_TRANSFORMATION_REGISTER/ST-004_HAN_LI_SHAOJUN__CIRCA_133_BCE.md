# ST-004 — Li Shaojun, c.133 BCE: Recognition, Secret Biography and Posthumous Custody

**Date:** conventional circa 133 BCE, within Window 04 (140–121 BCE). Not exact year proved by the primary passage.  
**Source:** *Shiji* 28 (compiled after event), paragraphs 56–58, not an eyewitness transcript.  
**Scope:** **CHINESE** court *fangshi* tradition, not Greek-Egyptian descent.  
**Verdict:** Strong narrative documentation for claimed transformation, patron recognition, identity concealment and attempted transmission; **claimed transmutation unverified**.

## THERE
Emperor Wu begins reign in 141 BCE, with court interest in ritual specialists, dietary methods, long life, Yellow Emperor models and extraordinary claims.

## CAPACITY
Li **claimed** techniques of stove sacrifice, aging resistance, and transforming cinnabar to gold. The historian recounts a bronze vessel attribution that impressed onlookers. No actual successful transformation or extraordinary longevity is independently established.

## PERCEPTION
Patrons interpreted his concealed age and origins, reputed memory and bronze vessel identification as signs of extreme age/exceptional standing. Sima Qian simultaneously records ordinary death, contrasting the emperor's insistence on transformation.

## AUTHORITY
Li was reportedly retained by a marquis before imperial audience, received substantial gifts, and gained the emperor's respect. This supports recognized **specialist patronage**, not a named formal alchemical office.

## SECRECY
- **Attested within transmitted narrative:** Li withheld biographical details.
- **Inferred purpose:** it may have enhanced aura and credibility; historical writer explicitly says mystery increased others' trust.
- **Not evidenced:** escape from persecutors, controlled chemical guild initiation, a secret reagent name, or legally imposed silence.

## LANGUAGE
- 方士 (*fangshi*) = method specialist, not exact 'alchemist.'
- 祠灶 (*ci zao*) = ritual at stove; not necessarily a coded lab furnace.
- 丹沙 (*dan sha*) = cinnabar; 黃金 (*huang jin*) = gold; both presented in claimed transformation.
- 蓬萊 (*Penglai*) = sacred-legendary site, not established as a coded reagent.
- 化去 (*hua qu*) = emperor reads death as transformed departure; contradicts historian's description of death.

## THE OUTSIDE
Demand from powerful patron for immortality and ritual legitimacy, court competition, ruler's capacity to fund attempts and declare continuity.

## RESPONSE
Imperial ritual and gold-making attempts reportedly followed Li's advice. After Li died, emperor treated him as having departed through transformation and ordered others to receive his methods. Sima Qian reports additional coastal experts entering court thereafter.

## TEXTS
Shiji 28 primary witness (late second/early first-century BCE composition); repeated/edited parallels Shiji 12, Hanshu 25A are not independent confirmations.

## HERE
**A claimed extraordinary capacity becomes an institutionally consequential role because a patron credits and acts upon it. Its association with a method may persist beyond the death of its promoter.**

This is a **socially recognized station of a specialist of methods**, not proof of the chemical result or a formal profession of Alchemist.

## REMAINDER
Actual recipe; material demonstrability; motive for hiding identity; details of transmitted formula; subsequent user and impact; direct Chinese-to-Egyptian contact remains unproved.
