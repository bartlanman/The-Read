# ST-111 — Early Fifth Century: Synesius Requests Hypatia's Assistance With a Brass Hydroscope

**Scope:** named philosopher/technical communication station; **NOT a demonstrated Alchemist**. **Event:** writing of *Synesius Letter 15* to “the Philosopher” (commonly identified Hypatia), often assigned **c.402** by modern editorial chronology; **precise date CONTESTED**, original ancient author letter survives only through later Greek manuscripts/editions. **Verdict:** **ATTESTED AUTHORIAL TECHNICAL REQUEST**; actual making, delivery, invention and elemental transmutation **NOT DEMONSTRATED**. [Window 31](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0401-0420_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Synesius, philosopher and pupil/correspondent of Hypatia at Alexandria, sought an instrument during his early-fifth-century career. Hypatia was trusted as an advanced philosophical/mathematical teacher; no independent technical workshop registration is present.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
Synesius's **Letter 15** specifies a **brass, graduated, floating cylindrical hydroscope** for comparing properties/relative densities of waters or liquids and requests that Hypatia **arrange casting and assembly**. This is a concrete *description and request*, **not evidence of manufacture, inventor credit, test measurements or a laboratory conversion of metals**.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Synesius treats Hypatia as capable of directing technically knowledgeable manufacture. His **trust and request** are direct evidence of a named pupil's recognition of her authority, not independent proof of the specified instrument's successful delivery.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Hypatia appears as the request's intellectual addressee/organizer. Neither an emperor nor professional guild formally licensed her as a transmuter. No named brass craftsman is evidenced.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Technical design is **explained in a letter**, not concealed as a cipher. Presumed craft skill does not prove a secret initiation, hazard-based gate, state persecution or proprietary gold recipe.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`hydroskopion` / hydroscope here is a **floating liquid-measurement instrument**, not a modern optical hydroscope. `Hydrometer` is a functional comparison rather than proof of identical standard scales. Approximate date **402** is an editor's inference rather than original consular subscription.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Need for a means of comparing liquids led to the actual written request; no evidence imperial religious policy targeted the instrument or its makers.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
**Observed response:** Synesius wrote specifications and asked Hypatia to coordinate creation. **Her reply, actual manufacture, delivery, calibration and use are unknown.**

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[Synesius Letter 15](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/synesius/synesius-letter-015/) (editorially c.402); [Fitzgerald translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Letters_of_Synesius_of_Cyrene/Letter_15). Ancient author, later MSS, modern translation.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
A **named socially recognized mathematical teacher** was trusted with coordinating a material instrument for a named former pupil. Positive capacity **through an actual instructional relationship**, without assuming physical result.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Dating of letter, manufacturer's identity, actual surviving device, completion, experimental purpose/measurements, invention credit, any gold-production method.
