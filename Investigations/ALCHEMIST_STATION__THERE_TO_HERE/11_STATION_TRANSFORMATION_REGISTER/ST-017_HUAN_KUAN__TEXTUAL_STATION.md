# ST-017 — Huan Kuan: From 81 BCE Manufacturing Policy Dispute to an Enduring Book

**Place:** Western Han China.  
**Event:** 81 BCE policy debate, *previous* Window 06.  
**Text composition:** Emperor Xuan's reign, **74–49 BCE**, exact date unresolved; can overlap both 80–61 BCE and 60–41 BCE.  
**Verdict:** literary/textual lineage, NOT an alchemical profession.

## THERE
Government salt/iron production was fiercely debated at court under Emperor Zhao in 81 BCE. Earlier direct dialogue records are not preserved as independent verbatim speeches.

## CAPACITY AND AUTHORITY
Technical iron smelting, tool production and salt extraction require manufacturing capacity. *Yantielun* does not manufacture iron; it gives an authorizing account of who may profit from and regulate its production.

## LANGUAGE
A later author compiles/elaborates speeches into structured persuasive dialogue, combining moral ideals and technical-economic facts. This creates a text-based *station* of policy interpretation beyond the original debate.

## THE OUTSIDE
A new emperor's government and differing attitudes toward war costs, private monopolies, and official welfare lend continuing relevance to older controversies.

## HERE
**An earlier station dispute can be inherited as a text, and the redactor can shape how later readers understand legitimacy.** The date of the book's compilation cannot be forced into this interval.

## REMAINDER
Original participants' exact words, first manuscript copy, precise completion year and effect on the actual productivity of iron offices.
