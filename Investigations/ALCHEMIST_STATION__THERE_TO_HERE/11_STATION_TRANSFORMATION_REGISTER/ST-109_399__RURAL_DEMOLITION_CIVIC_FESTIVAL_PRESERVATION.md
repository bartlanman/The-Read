# ST-109 — 399: The State Separates Temples, Buildings, Objects, Festivals and Ritual Actions

**Scope:** different legal transformations of ritual and civic/material stations. **Laws:** **29 Jan**, **10 Jul**, **20 Aug 399**, CTh XVI.10.15–18, compiled **438**, copies later. **Grade:** DATED TRANSMITTED NORMATIVE LAW; no original specific observed compliance or Alchemist. [Window 30](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0381-0400_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Traditional sacred buildings contained material art and supported public festivals, while sacrifice had become condemned under several imperial orders.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
A building can physically support gathering, art and civic festivities. Ritual professionals may perform rites, but **none of these legal categories supplies an alchemical recipe or measured material transmutation**.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Government differentiates rural temples suitable for demolition from buildings that can be reused without illicit practice, and public festivals that need not end when sacrifice is prohibited.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
`Si qua in agris templa sunt ... diruantur` **10 Jul** orders rural demolition without tumult; `festos conventus civium ... non patimur submoveri` **20 Aug** protects festivals absent sacrifice; `Aedes illicitis rebus vacuas ... ne quis conetur evertere` **20 Aug** protects former ritual buildings when illegal practices removed.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Some site custodians could lose premises; other artistic/material functions might be preserved. No evidence that an Alchemist was secretly practicing in a protected building, or that a closed shrine contained chemical books.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`templa` building and `sacrificia` cult act are not synonyms; `festos conventus` community festivals are not proof of continuing priestly legal permission; `aedes illicitis rebus vacuas` emphasizes the structure after prohibited activity ceases.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Imperial laws in **399** deliberately distinguish effects by site/type of activity; the state both threatens demolition and mandates preservation.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
These are **prescriptions**, not counts of buildings demolished or verified festival events; no identifiable alchemical response shown.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[CTh XVI.10.15–18 Latin](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh16.html). Original issue dates in 399, 438 codification, later manuscripts.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
The same cultural infrastructure can move from **licensed ritual → publicly retained civic material** or **be ordered removed**, depending on place/acts. A station is a set of permissions, not just a building.
