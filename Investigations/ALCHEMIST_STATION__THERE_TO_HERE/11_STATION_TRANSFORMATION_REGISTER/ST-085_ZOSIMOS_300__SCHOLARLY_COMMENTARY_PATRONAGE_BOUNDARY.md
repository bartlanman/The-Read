# ST-085 — Zosimos of Panopolis, circa 300: A Named Commentator Competes for Alchemical Authority

**Scope:** Greek alchemical **scholarly authority formation**, a **cross-window c.300 candidate** (281–300 / 301–320). **Composition/event date:** Zosimos usually placed around **300 CE**, no secure year within 281–300; **surviving witnesses:** especially **Marcianus gr.299 c.1000**, **Parisinus gr.2325 thirteenth century**, **Parisinus gr.2327 fifteenth century**. **Evidence verdict:** **SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION** for authorial identity/social rivalry; **NO SECURE YEAR-SPECIFIC 281–300 EVENT**, no formal state professional title. **Window:** [25 (cross-boundary context)](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0281-0300_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
An earlier Greek recipe tradition includes pseudonymous Democritus, Hermes, cited Maria and dyeing/coloring operations; named author Julius Africanus transmitted different technical materials earlier third century. Near 300, an interpretive-scholar role becomes visible in Zosimos's writing as studied by modern scholars.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
**Zosimos authored commentary** about alchemical books, apparatus and furnaces, and compared competing readings of technical procedures. This is textual evidence of **scholarly interpretation and organized procedure discourse**, not independent modern proof of successful gold-element transmutation or exact original apparatus produced in 299.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Within Zosimos's transmitted letters, **Theosebeia** is addressed as a learned, socially influential woman; rival practitioners were attacked as `magoi` and demon worshippers. A **Neilos** portrayed as **Theosebeia's priest** is criticized for rival authority. Because these are the polemicist's words, their depiction cannot be accepted uncritically as independent accounts of opponents' technical achievements.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
[Olivier Dufault (2019)](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ks0g83x) argues that **Theosebeia's patronage** and the higher value accorded Greek learned interpretation enabled alchemical commentary to compete with temple-associated ritual claims. A patron-client relationship is source-supported, though independent salary, contract, public title, city appointment or actual household original documents not located.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Zosimos claims mastery requires **reading, interpretation, ethical/scholarly formation**, not simply an elite secret key. He contests rival ritual practice. No documented 297 suppression, state-approved “licensed alchemist,” chemical-hazard secrecy rule or personally persecuted alchemical guild. Some textual opacity is caused by selection and loss during medieval copying, not demonstrated original intent.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`magoi` is Zosimos's label for **rivals**, not his self-title; **`paideia`** designates education/cultured competence (modern historians emphasize it analytically). `baphai` are dye/tincture operations; **`chēmeia`** appears in later context and textual interpretation, but cannot retroactively furnish a job title in 297. “Democritus,” “Maria,” “Hermes” in citations demonstrate **textual authority claims**, not proven direct apprenticeship.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and date it entered
Elite patronage and rival claims from specialists pressed the status of alchemical knowledge **around 300**; not assigned specifically to 297. Zosimos's writings, as preserved, describe a competitive intellectual environment. A political Diocletian-burning explanation for his commentary or loss of manuscripts is **unsupported**.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Zosimos **writes to Theosebeia**, disparages priest Neilos/other `magoi` and makes reading/interpreting Greek material writings a claim to authority. This is a **documented authorial position preserved through later witness**, not proof rivals were actually deceivers or that Zosimos possessed a government credential.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
Technical treatises/epistles attributed to Zosimos including apparatus/furnaces, material on magnesia and alchemical teaching. **Author ~300**, surviving chiefly **later Byzantine compilations**; actual wording and authorship vary by manuscript family. Dufault **chap.6 pp.118–142**, editions Mertens (1995), Halleux (1981). No surviving 297 autograph. Unlike Suda story, this is *named alchemical commentary* rather than modern inference from monetary objects.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
**Positive emerging station candidate:** competition over interpretation of alchemical texts under **patronage** becomes historically describable, introducing recognizable scholarly *authority*, apart from anonymous craft practice or legendary ancient authors. **Not** evidence that the formal Alchemist profession was state-recognized or that an experiment worked.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Precise birth/death/floruit years, actual Theosebeia biography and patronage contract, whether rival named Neilos independently documented, degree of workshop practice, original autograph manuscripts, chronology of attributed sections, specific modernly verifiable gold conversion.
