# ST-061 — 191–192 CE: Commodus Claims Hercules in Material Form

**Actual Roman artifacts:** British Museum **R.15104**, copper alloy `as` dated **191–192 CE**, Commodus wearing lion-skin, club on reverse; **1846,0910.247**, copper-alloy medallion dated **192 CE** showing ruler as Hercules.
**Status:** PHYSICAL PERIOD-WITNESSED MINTED AUTHORITY, **not actual supernatural change or an Alchemist station**.

## THERE
Commodus's late reign displays increasingly personal divine associations. Money and prestige medallions are produced by skilled metalworkers under political authorization.

## CAPACITY / PERCEPTION
Die preparation, metalworking and mint output are physically evidenced. Images present emperor as Hercules. An observer may recognize the mythic claim without concluding human identity was materially converted into a deity.

## AUTHORITY / LANGUAGE
Minted imagery and official imperial inscriptions alter perceived public standing; political power selects the narrative. An emperor's portrait is not evidence of a successful biological, elemental or spiritual transformation.

## SECRECY
Symbolic costume, lion skin and Hercules' club are publicly communicated, **not a hidden chemical cipher**.

## THE OUTSIDE
Political fragility, competing memories of legitimate leadership and desire for renewed/refounded imperial identity. The emperor dies **31 Dec 192**; the material claim survives him.

## HERE
**A physical product can substantiate that an institution *claimed* an extraordinary identity without substantiating that the identity was materially true.** Makers are less visible than the named ruler.

## REMAINDER
Who cut dies, chose iconographic design, public reactions, geographic extent of issues.
