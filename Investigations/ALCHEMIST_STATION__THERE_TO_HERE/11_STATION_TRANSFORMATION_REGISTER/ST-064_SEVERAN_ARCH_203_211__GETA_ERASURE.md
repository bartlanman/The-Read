# ST-064 — 203 CE Arch, after 211 CE Geta Erasure: The Name Removed, the Work Remains

**Physical object:** Arch of Septimius Severus, Roman Forum, dedicated **203 CE**; inscription subsequently altered after Geta was murdered in **December 211 CE**.
**Evidence grade:** ORIGINAL ROMAN MONUMENT and material inscription-recutting; not a speculative hidden text.

## THERE
Septimius Severus and sons Caracalla and Geta share a dynasty whose public authorization appears as monumental Latin dedication.

## CAPACITY
Skilled stoneworkers and bronze-letter installers create an official textual surface; after the political break, other craftsmen remove a name and adjust the inscription. **The cut-back surface and letter sockets remain**, physically revealing historical revision.

## PERCEPTION AND AUTHORITY
The emperor's legal/political status is presented through monument and titles. Geta's name could be officially denied after his death, even though the text originally recognized him. Editing the record is not the same as undoing his former existence.

## LANGUAGE / SECRECY
No alchemical ingredient lexicon. The material operation of erasure is **visible through traces**, despite an intention to suppress public commemoration.

## THE OUTSIDE
Caracalla kills Geta and state actors reshape public dynastic memory.

## RESPONSE / HERE
**An institution may revoke a name's public authority without erasing every material trace of previous recognition.** The material record can rebut the finalized political claim.

## REMAINDER
Exact day and responsible official of recutting, how fully Geta images were erased beyond the Forum; no reason to infer a chemical guild.
