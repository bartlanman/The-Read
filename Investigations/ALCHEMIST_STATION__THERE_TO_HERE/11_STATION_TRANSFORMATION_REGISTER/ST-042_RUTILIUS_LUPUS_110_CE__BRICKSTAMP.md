# ST-042 — Rutilius Lupus, 110–116 CE: Dated Brick, Unnamed Maker

**THERE:** Roman urban building and brick manufacturing.
**CAPACITY:** Clay sourcing, forming, firing and stamping. Actual bricks survive; individual artisans unnamed.
**PERCEPTION:** A stamp permits date/provenance or owner recognition without reproducing kiln knowledge.
**AUTHORITY:** The `figlinae Brutianae` are tied to **M. Rutilius Lupus**, a known proprietor and official; **CIL XV 18 110 CE** consular stamp, plus an extant **116 CE** example. He is not shown personally firing bricks.
**SECRECY/LANGUAGE:** ordinary industrial abbreviations, not a cipher for making gold.
**OUTSIDE:** demand for traceability and output in the building industry.
**RESPONSE:** consular-stamp dating enters Roman urban industrial practice; specific first-in-all-Roman-world claim would be false.
**HERE:** The material artifact authenticates a manufactured product and associated ownership while leaving worker skill anonymous.
**REMAINDER:** foremen and workers, mechanics of workshop, independent proof of the owner's personal training. Not Alchemist.
