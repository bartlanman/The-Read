# ST-063 — 193–194 CE: Five Imperial Claimants, One Provincial Coin-Mint Legitimacy Claim

**Historical period:** Commodus killed **31 December 192**, multiple imperial claimants in **193**, Severan military/political consolidation in **194–197**.
**Period physical witness:** **RPC V.3 type 65128** original Roman Egyptian tetradrachms issued at **Alexandria, 193/194 CE**, under Septimius Severus's **year 2**; Greek imperial name and Athena reverse; debased silver.
**Evidence grade:** ORIGINAL materially dated provincial minted recognition plus later Roman historians. No chemistry guild.

## THERE
Commodus's assassination ends his particular imperial station, ushering in military, Senate and provincial competition over sovereignty.

## AUTHORITY / PERCEPTION
The 193 competitors **Pertinax, Didius Julianus, Pescennius Niger, Clodius Albinus, Septimius Severus** are **five claimants**, not five universally recognized consecutive sovereigns. Physical coin from Alexandria explicitly uses Severus's titulature and regnal year **2 (193/194)**, showing one successful provincial public representation.

## CAPACITY
Real coin workers produce alloy money and dies. The coin does not record the mint-worker identities or their religious beliefs. Their craft competence is not replaced with every new emperor's claim.

## LANGUAGE
Greek `Αὐτοκράτωρ Καῖσαρ ...` and `L Β` are public political monetary speech. A coin can announce a valid local regnal-calendar claim while regional loyalty remains contested.

## SECRECY / OUTSIDE
Dynastic instability and civil war shift external authorization to rulers and mint administrations. No attested 193 anti-Alchemist statute or master–pupil shift. The state may rename objects and offices faster than productive workers change their tools.

## HERE
**Imperial names can change quickly while material crafts persist, and the public mark of a new regime is evidence of *claimed recognition*, not a new material transformation.**

## REMAINDER
Complete chronology of local Egyptian loyalty, every rival coin issue, named mint artists and operators, whether any craft institution was materially disrupted.
