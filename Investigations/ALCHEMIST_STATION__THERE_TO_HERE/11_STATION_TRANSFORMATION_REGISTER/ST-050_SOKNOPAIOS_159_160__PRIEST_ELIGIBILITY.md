# ST-050 — 159–160 CE: The Priest Whose Eligibility Was Examined

**Original witness:** **BGU I 16 (Berlin P.6889)**, Greek original papyrus from Soknopaiou Nesos, Fayum, dated to **30 August 159–28 August 160 CE** (Antoninus Pius regnal year 23).
**Attested persons:** priest **Panephremmis**; complainant **Pasis son of Neilos**; strategos **Hierax**; royal scribe **Teimagenes**; five senior priests of temple of **Soknopaios**.
**Evidence status:** strong contemporary documentary testimony; fragmentary text and **unknown final verdict**. The Berlin summary mentions a 1,000-drachma penalty in the procedural background, not proof of final payment.

## THERE
Egyptian temple office carries recognizable institutional requirements under Roman administration and fiscal oversight by the Idios Logos bureaucracy.

## CAPACITY
The case concerns **priestly grooming/dress**—allegedly wearing long hair and woolen clothes—rather than a technical procedure. The papyrus documents a sworn inquiry, not whether Panephremmis possessed knowledge of a material art.

## PERCEPTION / AUTHORITY
An allegation triggers officials' instruction to five elder priests to investigate compliance. What a priest **may be seen to wear** can affect whether others recognize the station as properly occupied.

## SECRECY / LANGUAGE
This is public administrative oversight of ritual qualifications, not a secret laboratory curriculum, coded reagent, or persecution of alchemists.

## THE OUTSIDE
Imperial fiscal and provincial controls meet inherited Egyptian rules about priestly identity and purity.

## RESPONSE
The surviving text records oath/proceedings; its lower/ending section is lost, so **no conclusion about guilt or final penalty may be invented**.

## HERE
**Permission to hold a religious station can be regulated independently of the specialist's practical productive abilities.** This helps explain how institutional and operative authorization might later intersect—but their intersection is not proved here.

## REMAINDER
Full missing elder declaration, actual judgment, any later dispute, and whether this person ever practiced specialized nonritual craft (unknown).
