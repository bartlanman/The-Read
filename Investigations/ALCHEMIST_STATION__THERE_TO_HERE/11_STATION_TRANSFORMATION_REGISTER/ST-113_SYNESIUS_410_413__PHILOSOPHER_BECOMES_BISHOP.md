# ST-113 — c.410–413: Synesius Negotiates Movement From Philosopher to Bishop

**Scope:** identifiable individual's transition between **philosopher/pupil** and **Christian episcopal office**, not Alchemist. **Dates:** election **c.410**, consecration **c.411**, death **c.413** scholarly reconstruction (Cameron/Long; letter-based); no original contemporaneous dated grave inscription inspected. **Witness:** Synesius *Letter 105*, ancient-authored letter transmitted later, historical biography. **Grade:** **ATTESTED PERSONAL NEGOTIATION + SOURCE-SUPPORTED OFFICE HISTORY**. [Window 31](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0401-0420_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Synesius of Cyrene was educated in Greek philosophy and linked to Hypatia, with public writings and elite networks. Christians around Ptolemais chose him for episcopal leadership under archbishop Theophilus of Alexandria's authority.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
Letters show rhetorical, philosophical and diplomatic abilities, not successful elemental gold creation or priestly transmutation. His known public role as bishop required pastoral governance; exact activities are to be established from letters, not inferred from alchemical pseudonyms.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Religious authorities thought his social and learned standing made him a candidate for bishop; he expressed serious reservations about what he could conscientiously teach, about marriage and philosophical doctrines.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Church institutional recognition (election/consecration in historical reconstruction) coexisted with his **negotiation** of acceptable public doctrine and domestic life. Neither a formal chemistry qualification nor a Dioscorus-alchemist apprenticeship is documented.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Letter 105 discusses the difference between what is philosophically believed and publicly taught to a religious audience. **Audience-calibrated withholding** is an **explicit historically contextual philosophy/pastoral claim**, not an effective cipher for dangerous metal reagents.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
Bishop and philosopher are historical occupations/social roles; **“Synesius” alone in a medieval alchemical heading cannot identify the same person**. The later text to Dioscorus has contested author and original date; letter 105 is from a genuine Synesius correspondence corpus.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Ecclesiastical election/ordination demands (~410–411) pressured Synesius to reconcile religious office with philosophical conscience and domestic commitments.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Synesius argues conditions in Letter 105 and eventually held episcopal office. Full details/timing of agreement unknown; his later letters show actions as bishop. He likely died **before** Hypatia was murdered March **415**, so no claimed response.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[Synesius Letter 105](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/synesius/synesius-letter-105/); [Cameron and Long 1993](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/barbarians-and-politics-at-the-court-of-arcadius.html). Later copies of original letters.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
**Actual same individual's station transformation** between two historically evidenced roles, unlike the fictional reconstruction of a goldmaking priest into a bishop.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Precise election/consecration/death dates, exact terms accepted, later parish reception, direct alchemical activities or connection to pseudo-Synesius corpus.
