# ST-032 — Alexandria, 38 CE: The Coercive Power to Deny Civic Standing

**Place:** Roman Alexandria in Egypt, 38 CE.
**Witness:** Philo of Alexandria, `In Flaccum`, written around 39–40 CE.
**Verdict:** near-contemporary account of anti-Jewish civic/religious persecution. This is **NOT** a record of an Alchemist station; it is a guarded comparative case about state authorization and exclusion.

## THERE
Alexandria housed different ethnic, religious and civic communities under Roman prefectural government. Aulus Avilius Flaccus served as prefect.

## CAPACITY / PERCEPTION
Philo describes exclusionary naming, attacks and efforts to remove the Jewish community's recognized protections. These concern real people, homes and religious institutions, **not** a technical procedure or craft competency test.

## AUTHORITY
Roman prefectural power, civic hierarchies and local political disputes shaped who could claim status and protection. Philo is a near-contemporary Jewish author with a strong participant viewpoint; other causes and roles remain historically debated.

## SECRECY AND DANGER
Violent civic discrimination must not be recast as symbolic persecution of an occult guild. No source ties these events to chemistry or secret metal-making.

## THE OUTSIDE
Political and religious conflict, civic rights controversies, and the exercise or failure of official protection.

## RESPONSE / HERE
The transmitted account illustrates how government and public authorities can **change recognition and protection without changing the individuals themselves**. This is a serious historical case of exclusion; its relevance to the Alchemist's station is methodological only.

## REMAINDER
Full independent evidence for the 38 CE crisis, its administrative roots and competing testimony; **no basis to infer chemical practitioners were targeted**.
