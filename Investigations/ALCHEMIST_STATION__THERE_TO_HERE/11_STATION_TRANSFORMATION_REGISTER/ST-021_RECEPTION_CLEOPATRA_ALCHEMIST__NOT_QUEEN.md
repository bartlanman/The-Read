# ST-021 — 'Cleopatra the Alchemist': A Later Name Projected Backward onto the Queen

**Status:** **RETROSPECTIVE ATTRIBUTION WATCH**, not a contemporary 60–41 BCE alchemical station event.  
**Historically documented bearer of name:** Cleopatra VII, monarch 51–30 BCE.  
**Alchemical textual witnesses:** Greek/Byzantine collections, including *Chrysopoeia* page in **10th/11th-century Marcianus gr. Z.299** and alchemical dialogue of Cleopatra and Comarius; earlier textual phases need independent dating.  
**Issue:** later authors or readers could identify an alchemical 'Cleopatra' with the famous queen; scholar Virginia Fabrizi considers works probably spurious.

## THERE
A famous queen becomes an exemplar of ancient learning, political power and Egyptian prestige long after her lifetime.

## LANGUAGE / ALLEGORY
- 'Goldmaking of Cleopatra' is a **manuscript attribution**, not contemporary proof of first-century BCE authorship.
- Death and rebirth language in the later dialogues about metals is a genuine alchemical motif within its actual textual transmission.
- **Ouroboros**, alembic diagrams and other motifs from later manuscripts do not become artifacts of the queen's reign just because they use her name.

## AUTHORITY
The name 'Cleopatra' could operate as a prestigious authorial or character-based *station*, even if no single identifiable first-century BCE technical writer behind it is proven.

## OUTSIDE
Late-antique and Byzantine interest in ancient priestly, Hermetic and Egyptian authorities; manuscript copyists and readers re-situate literary origin in an earlier epoch.

## HERE
**A powerful station in memory may be created by later attribution to a celebrated historical figure, without any demonstrated period-appropriate technical capacity.**

## REMAINDER
Actual identity of ancient alchemical Cleopatra author; possible Queen's intellectual patronage; earliest Greek manuscript archetypes; how Greek and Arabic versions interact; whether dialogues depict patronage, not authorship. **Keep Cleopatra VII, Cleopatra the Physician and Greek alchemical Cleopatra as analytically separate referents.**
