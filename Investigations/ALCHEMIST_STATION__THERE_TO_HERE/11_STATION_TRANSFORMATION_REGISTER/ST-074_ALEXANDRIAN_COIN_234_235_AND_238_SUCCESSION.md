# ST-074 — 234–238 CE: Material Imperial Claims and the Collapse of a Patron's Station

**Original object:** Roman Provincial Coinage **RPC VI 10643**, Alexandria **year 14 of Severus Alexander (234/235 CE)**, debased silver tetradrachm, reverse **Helios and Selene**.
**Political events:** emperor Alexander killed **235 CE**; Maximinus Thrax takes power; **238 CE** six imperial names rule/claim power amid civil disorder.
**Status:** dated physical state-issued metal and securely historical political transitions; **NOT evidence of alchemy**.

## THERE
Severus Alexander, dedicatee of Africanus' `Kestoi`, is emperor for 222–235; provincial Roman Egyptian mint operates in an official calendar.

## CAPACITY / PERCEPTION
Mint engravers and metalworkers produce actual dated coin; the obverse title and year 14 grant a public assertion of current office. The emperor's name is made durable even though the holder can soon lose power.

## AUTHORITY
A named political patron and an impersonal official coin-issuing institution differ from the workers' hands. After 235 the imperial patron no longer can support an author, though this **does not prove** the author's texts were suppressed.

## LANGUAGE / SECRECY
Greek legends and year marks are ordinary formal identity and time markers, not chemical ciphers. Helios/Selene imagery is religious/cosmological, not an operative transmutation recipe.

## THE OUTSIDE
235 assassination and unstable competing governments **238** create stress on administration; dated mint output can help historians reconstruct provincial allegiance.

## RESPONSE / HERE
**An authorial relationship anchored by dedication can outlive the patron's station through copying even though political support and material office may vanish.** The physical coin proves a title was minted at that date, not that every inhabitant agreed or metal was converted into gold.

## REMAINDER
When the `Kestoi` was copied relative to 235, Africanus' reaction to Alexander's fall, how Egyptian mints responded in each 238 sub-period, the identity of ancient coin workers.
