# ST-041 — 97 CE Gan Ying: The Border of Contact Is Not a Lineage

**Date:** Eastern Han, **97 CE**, ninth year of Yongyuan.
**People:** Ban Chao (commander, dispatcher); Gan Ying (envoy).
**Witness:** *Hou Hanshu* fifth-century CE history preserving an earlier report, interpreted through modern geography.
**Verdict:** SOURCE-SUPPORTED REPORTED DIPLOMATIC EXPEDITION; **no proven Greek–Chinese technical alchemical meeting**.

## THERE
Han Western Regions policy and long-distance intermediaries enabled reports of a far western entity called Da Qin, generally identified with Rome.

## CAPACITY
Gan Ying reportedly traveled far west to Tiaozhi near a sea, commonly located around the Persian Gulf, then returned without reaching the Roman government. Actual itinerary and ancient location identifications remain debated.

## PERCEPTION / AUTHORITY
The mission's authorization derives from Ban Chao and imperial diplomacy; descriptions of 'Rome' can circulate through several traders, officials and translations without direct face-to-face exchange.

## SECRECY / LANGUAGE
Chinese names Da Qin and Tiaozhi represent geographical/intermediary information, not a demonstrable recipe lexicon. Parthian sailors reportedly influenced his decision not to continue; that is not an alchemical secrecy rule.

## THE OUTSIDE
Trade routes, imperial geography, distance, maritime risk and information held by intermediary peoples.

## RESPONSE
The envoy does not meet the Roman emperor, yet his account enters later East Asian descriptions of the Roman world.

## HERE
**Expanded geographic knowledge is not evidence of transmission of material transformative craft.** We need a dated named translator, matched recipes, shared vocabulary, or document before positing a China-to-Greek alchemy line.

## REMAINDER
Precise route and modern site identifications, timing and sources of the surviving narrative, what facts Gan Ying obtained, any real manuscript exchange. No alchemical data has been shown.
