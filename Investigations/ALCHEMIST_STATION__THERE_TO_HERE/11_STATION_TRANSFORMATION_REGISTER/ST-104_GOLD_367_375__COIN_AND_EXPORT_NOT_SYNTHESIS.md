# ST-104 — c.367–375: Gold Is an Imperial Commodity and Coin, Not a Verified Alchemical Product

**Scope:** Roman economic and physical gold material comparator, not a named Alchemist. **Original-period artifact:** Berlin Valens solidus 18202344, Trier mint **367–375 broad**, 4.22g. **Regulatory source:** CJ IV.63.2, approximate **374?** date, **sixth-century compilation and later MSS**. **Grade:** ORIGINAL GOLD OBJECT + TRANSMITTED LAW (DATE CONTESTED); no transmutation. [Window 29](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0361-0380_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Rome used struck gold currency, valued retention of precious metal and maintained cross-frontier trade. A public speaker Themistius separately used hypothetical metal-changing in 366/367.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
The Trier mint **struck pre-existing gold** into a solidus with Valens's public title. The surviving coin is gold metal; it does not show formation of gold out of copper. The law restricts giving/exporting `aurum` beyond the imperial boundary.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Imperial actors value available gold as strategic resource. No evidence a court ordered gold synthesis, believed successful alchemy was a production strategy, or possessed a specific named transmuter.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Imperial mint authority and controlled trade are visibly regulated. A merchant might be penalized for export, but a **particular enforcement case is not contained in the law itself**.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
The law concerns **trade restriction**, not a recipe secret, hazard protocol, protected alchemical guild, or persecutions for claiming material transformation.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`Non solum aurum barbaris minime praebeatur` describes gold supply policy; `aurum` is gold as substance/commodity. Coins bear mint marks and imperial honor rather than experiment recipes. The phrase bronze→silver→gold in Themistius remains counterfactual.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Economic and frontier policy drove a restriction on cross-border gold transactions **c.374?** (date in modern critical code questioned). Original minted money circulated separately. No identified metalworker's response to the restriction.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
A state order and coin issuance exist; the real enforcement and effect on gold artisans/merchants are unverified in this record. Neither exhibits actual chrysopoeia.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[Berlin 18202344 gold solidus](https://ikmk.smb.museum/object?id=18202344); [CJ IV.63.2 Latin](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Corpus/CJ4.htm), edition date **`<A 374 ?>`**. Law originally fourth-century, sixth-century Justinianic compilation, later MSS.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
Gold as **actual managed material/economic object** differs from **imagined alchemical transformation**, even when both receive imperial public attention.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Actual law issue date, origin of gold, specific traders punished, methods of gold refining, alchemical practitioner link, success of any hypothetical transformation.
