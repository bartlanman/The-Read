# ST-012 — 81 BCE Salt–Iron Debate: Who May Control a Material Practice?

**Date of consultation:** 81 BCE (Window 06)  
**Composition:** *Yantielun*, edited later by Huan Kuan (reign of Emperor Xuan); recorded dialogues **not contemporary transcripts**  
**Verdict:** Strong source-supported political economic contest over material production; **not evidence of an alchemical profession**.

## THERE
Han state-controlled salt and iron production had substantial fiscal and strategic importance under Emperor Wu. The court of Emperor Zhao convenes an inquiry into previous institutions and their burden.

## CAPACITY
Producing practical iron implements or extracting salt requires actual techniques and organization. The debates dispute how these are distributed, whether quality is sufficient, whether private workers or administrators are best placed to provide tools, and who bears costs.

## PERCEPTION AND AUTHORITY
Ministers defend state centralization as a curb on powerful private interests and a means of financing frontier policy. Literati criticize harm to farmers and argue about propriety and public benefit.

## LANGUAGE AND SECRECY
*Yan tie* (salt and iron) are ordinary economic and material categories, not esoteric reagent names. The narrative dialogue is rhetorically constructed but not evidence of deliberate encrypted chemistry.

## THE OUTSIDE
Economic demands, political legitimacy of centralization, private producer complaints, material quality and access.

## RESPONSE
A formal court debate and later textual reconstruction contest prior policy. Its later redaction cannot establish exact spoken words or immediate abolition of all monopolies.

## HERE
**Authority to produce and distribute matter becomes an explicit political question: competence, public benefit and jurisdiction can be challenged independently of privileged secrecy.**

## REMAINDER
Local foundry records, actual production outcomes and links (if any) to goldmaking practitioners still needed.
