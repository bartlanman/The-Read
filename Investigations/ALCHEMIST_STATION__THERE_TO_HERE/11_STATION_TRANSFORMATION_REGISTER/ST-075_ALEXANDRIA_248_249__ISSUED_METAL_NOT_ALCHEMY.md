# ST-075 — 248/249 CE: A Minted Metal Object Carries Public Authority Without Naming Its Makers

**Scope:** Western formation **comparison case**, not a proven Alchemist incident.  
**Date of object/event:** Alexandria, Roman Egypt, year 6 of Philip I = 248/249 CE.  
**Date of witness:** Original metal coin from issue; modern RPC catalog.  
**Evidence verdict:** **ATTESTED** for object, metal and official regnal dating; **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** for Alchemist or elemental transmutation.  
**Linked 20-year window:** [Window 23 — 241–260 CE](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0241-0260_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
The Roman Egyptian mint produced publicly recognizable money under changing emperors. Philip I's provincial coins anchor a particular administrative regime within the wider third-century disruptions. This is a *mint and political authority station*, not an alchemical profession.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
**RPC VIII 3329** shows a real **debased-silver tetradrachm**, typically c.24 mm, RPC average 12.66 g, stamped with emperor and year. At least metal preparation/striking happened. The specific metallurgical formula, source ore and named artisan cannot be deduced from the iconography alone. Debased-silver production and striking are *not transmutation of one element into another*.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Obverse Greek titulature names Philip I; the eagle and regnal year `LϚ` publicly express imperial chronology and official authority. What ordinary buyers thought of coin quality or its relation to technical mastery is not directly attested by the object.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
The issuing power is represented as Philip's imperial rule. The individual workers are anonymous within this cataloged type; **there is no named Alchemist who claimed or received mint authority**.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Mint operations plausibly needed guarded skill and commercial organization, but this precise source does **not** disclose workshop entry restrictions, craft oaths, protected goldmaking formula, punitive secrecy or risks as motives. **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** of alchemical concealment.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
Greek title `Α Κ Μ ΙΟΥ ΦΙΛΙΠΠΟϹ Ε(Υ)(Ϲ)(Ε)` is an imperial inscription; `L - Ϛ` indicates regnal year six, not a coded ingredient. Eagle/wreath is public iconography, not a demonstrable tincture allegory. Modern term *debased silver* is a numismatic classification, not a contemporary claim to fabricate gold.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
**248/249:** mint output under Philip; **249** imperial replacement by Decius. Changing political office places pressure on named issuance; no primary local evidence here of a coinworker's response to the emperor's fall.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
The coin type embodies public recognition under Philip. Changing imperial authority changes the name authorized on future coins. A contemporaneous local change in technical process or named craft staff remains **UNVERIFIED**.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
**Original physical metal specimens**, dated 248/249; [RPC VIII 3329](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coins/8/3329) is a modern systematic catalog citing many specimen collections. No ancient recipe-manuscript, emperor's alchemical edict, or guild inscription attaches to this type.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
**One original object can reveal material capacity and state certification while leaving technical skill and artisan identity uncoupled.** This is a useful negative control for later claims that a material result proves its maker held an alchemical station.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Unknown worker(s), metal processing sequence, social competence framework and any link to alchemical writers. Do not confuse existing precious metal mixed or imitated with proven elemental alteration.

## SOURCES: primary, secondary, what each supports and does not support
[RPC VIII 3329](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coins/8/3329), catalog of original coins and regnal dating. [Window 23 source register W23-O01](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0241-0260__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

**Final station test:** **MAINTENANCE of public monetary office; comparative only. NOT a newly founded Alchemist station.**
