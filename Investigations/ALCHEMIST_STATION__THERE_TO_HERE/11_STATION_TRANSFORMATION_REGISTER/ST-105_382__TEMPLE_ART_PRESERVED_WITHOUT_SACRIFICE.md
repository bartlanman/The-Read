# ST-105 — 30 November 382: A Temple May Keep Its Artistic Station Without Sacrificial Authority

**Scope:** a named Roman public-art/cult-building classification, NOT an Alchemist. **Issue date:** **30 Nov 382**, *Codex Theodosianus* XVI.10.8 as compiled **438** and surviving later copies. **Grade:** DATED TRANSMITTED LEGAL PRESCRIPTION; no original 382 inscription of this order audited. [Window 30](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0381-0400_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
In Osrhoene an imperial official Palladius oversees an identifiable temple once used for civic gatherings, containing artworks. Political/religious orders oppose sacrifices but material artifacts and public meeting uses can retain value.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
The temple's sculpted art and built structure physically represent skilled craftsmen's work. The law addresses legal use/viewing, **not demonstration of metallic transmutation**, a named sculptor or a recipe.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
`artis pretio quam divinitate metienda`: statues deemed worthy through **artistic merit rather than their divine power**, an explicit state distinction. Does not mean all locals abandoned religious meanings.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Imperial authority directs public access remain to this specific site, **without license for forbidden sacrifice**. A site's material/civic station is protected while its sacrificial station is curtailed.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Law is PUBLIC preservation rule; no private chemical formulas, taboo on material transformation, trade secret, hazard key, or personal persecuted Alchemist appears.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`artis pretium` is assessed craft/art value and `divinitas` spiritual/ritual authority. “Temple” as a building is distinct from “sacrifice” as an act. Neither is a goldmaking guild.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Religious restrictions under late-fourth-century emperors challenge the cult status of an existing monument. The precise **382** policy grants limited public reuse.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
State **orders** monument open to public viewing, while rituals prohibited. Whether actual visitors attended and which rites ceased locally remain **UNVERIFIED**.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[CTh XVI.10.8 Latin](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh16.html), original purported issue **382**; *Theodosian Code* **438**; physical manuscripts later.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
Material craft value may survive a discredited religious function. This is a real **split between object, place and office**, not proof alchemical knowledge survived by renaming itself chemistry.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Temple's exact local implementation, art objects, artists, fate of ritual staff, any named alchemy practitioner.
