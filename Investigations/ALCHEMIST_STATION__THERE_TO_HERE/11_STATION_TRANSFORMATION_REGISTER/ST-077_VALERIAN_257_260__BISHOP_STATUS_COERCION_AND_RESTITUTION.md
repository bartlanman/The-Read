# ST-077 — 257–260/after: The Christian Bishop's Station Is Penalized and Subsequently Restored

**Scope:** Comparative institutional station, **NOT an alchemical persecution event**.  
**Dates:** Valerian-era measures **257–258**; Dionysius examination/exile **c.257–258**; Gallienus restoration **at or after 260, precise date contested**.  
**Witness dates:** Cyprian Letter 80 **258** (near-contemporary letter in later manuscripts); Dionysius's mid-third-century letters quoted in Eusebius **early fourth century**; Eusebius's translated Gallienus rescript likewise later transmitted.  
**Evidence verdict:** **ATTESTED/RETROSPECTIVE** for pressure on Christian office, **CONTESTED** precise restoration year and wording; **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** of a connection to alchemical practitioners.  
**Linked:** [Window 23](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0241-0260_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Christian clergy, bishops and deacons were publicly recognizable officeholders subject to political power. Valerian and Gallienus ruled together; the former's captivity in **260** shifted sole power to Gallienus.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
Dionysius of Alexandria could organize and lead worship and explain his refusal to recognize requested gods. His **HE VII.11** account records being called before prefect Aemilianus and removed to **Cephro**. Actual competence at crafts, recipe preparation and goldmaking is **not evidenced**.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Civil authority in the quoted interrogation demands acceptable worship; Dionysius sees compliance as unacceptable and defends his office. Cyprian reports rank-specific sanctions. We have distinct official/clerical interests filtered by Christian literary witnesses; not neutral contemporary state minutes.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Valerian's measures, as reported by **Cyprian Ep.80.1–2**, target bishops, presbyters and deacons, and threaten social rank/property of other Christians. Later Gallienus, in a document translated by Eusebius, reportedly restores bishops' freedom to use religious assembly places.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Documented fear and coercion exist in relation to Christian ritual affiliation and public ecclesiastical office. **No evidence** that a 250s alchemical recipe was censored, hidden for defense against these measures, or that a guild adapted its technical language in response.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
Cyprian's Latin **`episcopi`, `presbyteri`, `diaconi`** names precise offices. Gallienus's rescript in **HE VII.13** is explicitly **translated from Latin into Greek**, so we cannot confidently recover the emperor's exact original formula from Eusebius's translation. Modern edited numbering **Epistle 80** may appear as **81** in older English collections.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and date it entered
**257–258:** changing imperial religious policy imposes sanctions and local interrogation upon Christian leaders; **260/after:** captivity of Valerian and imperial reversal change conditions. Neither witness shows imperial *classification of alchemical work*.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Dionysius refuses ordered worship and is removed from Alexandria, per his quoted text. Gallienus's policy is presented by Eusebius as allowing bishops to reclaim places; precise date or uniform implementation remains open. **No practitioner of alchemy is shown responding.**

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[Cyprian Ep.80.1–2, Latin](https://presbytersproject.ihuw.pl/index.php?SourceID=1108&id=6) as transmitted, not Valerian's autograph rescript; [Eusebius HE VII.11](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250107.htm), quoting Dionysius; [HE VII.13](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250107.htm) giving a Greek translation of purported Gallienus rescript.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
There is a demonstrable **office-specific change in legitimate public practice**: bishops face sanctions and exile and later obtain renewed access to worship property. This is a powerful control case for “Outside pressure → station response” precisely **because** it does **not** prove a comparable change to alchemical technical standing.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Exact full imperial edicts, imperial motivations, documentary fidelity, Gallienus rescript's date at 260/after; consequences for unrecorded artisans; claimed alchemical persecution. **Do not force a 260/after event into a closed 241–260 timestamp.**

**Final station test:** **A clerical station is pressured and later partially restored; contextual analogy only. No demonstrable alchemical station transition.**
