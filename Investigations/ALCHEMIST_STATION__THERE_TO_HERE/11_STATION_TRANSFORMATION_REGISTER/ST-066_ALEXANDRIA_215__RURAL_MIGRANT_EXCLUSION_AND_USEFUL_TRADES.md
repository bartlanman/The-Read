# ST-066 — c.215 CE: Exclusion from Alexandria and Exceptions for Needed Trades

**Original witness:** **P.Giss. 40**, Greek papyrus copied around **215 CE**, preserving a Caracallan order about exclusion/expulsion of Egyptian rural residents from **Alexandria**.
**Evidence grade:** period documentary order with missing portions and scholarly disputes over genre/dating. NOT a list of chemical guilds.

## THERE
Roman Egypt combines a massive capital city and surrounding rural population under imperial administration, while Caracalla's citizenship grant has broadly changed the legal status of many free inhabitants.

## CAPACITY AND ACCESS
The order limits presence of categories of Egyptian rural newcomers in Alexandria but **exempts trades supplying goods/services** such as pig traders, boatmen and suppliers of reeds for heating bathhouses.

The occupations matter to the official because the city **needs their services**, not because they necessarily possess mystical knowledge or an alchemical degree.

## PERCEPTION / AUTHORITY
Administrative classification of who is useful, undesirable or legitimately present can change **access to a city** without proving anyone's practical skill has changed. The decree uses derogatory language toward nonurban Egyptians; do not adopt that judgment as neutral description.

## SECRECY / LANGUAGE
Occupational exceptions are **open legal carve-outs**, not symbolic disguised recipes. The papyrus contains distinct kinds of orders; Peter van Minnen disputes describing it simplistically as three edicts.

## THE OUTSIDE
Urban crowding/control, imperial political power and reliance on rural-to-city supply lines. Exact motives and actual enforcement contested.

## RESPONSE / HERE
**An institution can legally recognize nearly all free residents as Roman citizens while still restricting some from a particular city—except where a profession's practical service is considered necessary.**

## REMAINDER
Actual expulsions, migrants' responses, broader exemptions, effect on workers with no documented profession, precise textual layers and legal form.
