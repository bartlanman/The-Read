# ST-047 — 130–133 CE: Antinoopolis, an Imperial City Produces New Citizens

**Grounded events:** Hadrian founds **Antinoopolis 30 October 130 CE**, shortly after Antinous' death; early **133 CE** settlement/civic-status papyri **P.L.Bat. 6.29** and **6.30**.

## THERE
Roman Egypt has few Greek-style poleis, with different legal categories for towns, metropoleis, villages, Alexandrians and resident groups.

## CAPACITY
The creation of a city and organized relocation/privileges requires administrative procedures, infrastructure, urban planning and later civic councils. No chemical transformation is implied.

## PERCEPTION AND AUTHORITY
Hadrian's imperial founding confers a new institutional category. Someone can become legally recognized as part of a privileged urban community when admitted, without acquiring unusual material expertise.

## LANGUAGE
Later papyri use Greek civic titles like **Antinoites, New Greeks** (`Νέοι Ἕλληνες`). Precisely date these later forms; do not backdate every legal phrase to **130**.

## THE OUTSIDE / RESPONSE
Death of Antinous and imperial need for Greek-style provincial prestige drive commemorative urban innovation; by 133 original papyri supply tangible evidence of new settlement.

## HERE
**A sovereign can create a station of civic belonging by law and public organization; this is a demonstrable transformation in recognized status, not a material or alchemical change.**

## REMAINDER
Personal eligibility, native Egyptian role in building, economic/priestly artisan connections and the exact earliest civic regulations.
