# ST-039 — c.93–94 CE: Domitian Expels Philosophers, Not Proven Alchemists

**Place:** Rome and Italy.
**Event:** expulsions of philosophers conventionally **93–94 CE**, chronology and scope debated.
**Sources:** Suetonius *Domitian* 10.3, written early second century; later authors; Clément Bady 2020.
**Verdict:** DOCUMENTED RETROSPECTIVE TESTIMONY TO INTELLECTUAL PERSECUTION; **no evidence this targeted a defined Alchemist profession**.

## THERE
Philosophy functions as an intellectual, educational and elite moral identity in Roman society; some aristocrats associate philosophical exempla with political critique.

## PERCEPTION / AUTHORITY
Domitian regards some authors/patrons of philosophical praise as politically threatening. Literary sources recount executions of dissidents and expulsion of philosophers.

## THE OUTSIDE
Autocratic anxiety, public commemoration of senatorial opponents and a network of scholarly/aristocratic resistance.

## RESPONSE
Philosophers expelled from Rome and Italy. Scope disputed: some later sources say astrologers too, whereas earlier accounts emphasize philosophers. These groups should not be conflated.

## LANGUAGE / SECRECY
A story of **philosophical persecution** cannot be transferred onto a pseudonymous **alchemical** author merely because both are concerned with knowledge, Egypt or 'secrecy'. No anti-alchemy law, seized recipe list, or named alchemical victim identified for this policy.

## HERE
**The political danger of occupying an intellectual station is historical, but does not by itself explain why another specialized craft uses allegory.** A claim of secrecy from persecution requires a concrete link between persecution and the hidden text.

## REMAINDER
Specific list of expelled persons and professional identities, date of edicts, later additions about astrologers, any actual evidence linking Greek recipe writers to the policy.
