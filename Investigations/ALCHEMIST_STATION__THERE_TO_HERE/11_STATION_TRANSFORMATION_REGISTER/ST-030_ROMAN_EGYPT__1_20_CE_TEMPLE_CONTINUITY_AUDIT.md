# ST-030 — 1–20 CE Roman Egypt: Was the Priesthood Dispossessed?

**Place:** Roman Egypt.
**Interval:** 1–20 CE under Augustus (to 14 CE) and Tiberius (14–37 CE; only his first six years fall in this interval).
**Witnesses:** broad Ptolemaic/Roman temple chronology; papyri whose precise date must be checked; Andrew Connor's 2022 re-evaluation.
**Verdict:** COMPARATIVE STATION-AUDIT; no direct attestation of a professional Alchemist or a single universally documented confiscation event within these twenty years.

## THERE
Egyptian ritual stations, temples, crafts and local property arrangements had survived the end of Ptolemaic kingship in 30 BCE, now encountering Roman prefectural administration.

## CAPACITY
Temples produced and maintained material objects, inscriptions, cult operations and architecture. The present first-pass records do not identify one individual craftsman, furnace, recipe or chemical achievement securely dated 1–20 CE.

## AUTHORITY AND PERCEPTION
Roman emperors retain pharaonic representation at Egyptian sanctuaries. Priesthoods operate amid imperial taxation and institutional changes. Some modern scholarship has characterized this as a Roman program of extensive temple property confiscation and decline.

## OUTSIDE PRESSURE AND SOURCE CONFLICT
Andrew Connor, *Confiscation or Coexistence* (2022), reevaluates papyri previously used to support a sweeping confiscation model and disputes the generalization from local conflicts to a province-wide seizure. This is evidence of a **historiographical dispute**, not proof Roman government applied no controls. Specific papyri and dates must be verified individually before crediting an event to 1–20 CE.

## SECRECY / LANGUAGE
Sacred Egyptian titles, hieroglyphs and restricted cult procedures are not automatically an alchemical cipher. Roman legal custody of land and offices is different from the custody of secret scientific knowledge.

## HERE
**A change of ruler does not suffice to prove destruction of an institution, and modern interpretations of that transition may change when primary property records are reexamined.** This record tests pressure on priestly stations, not an already attested Alchemist station.

## REMAINDER
Actual priestly land allocations; calendar dates for Tebtunis documents; titles and work of temple personnel; specific workshop continuity, access and technical manuscripts.
