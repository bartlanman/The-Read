# ST-112 — Early Fifth Century: Hypatia Asked to Judge Whether Synesius's Books Merit Publication

**Scope:** named teacher, textual authority, patronage and intellectual station. **Event:** **Synesius Letter 154**, dated variously **c.404–405 to c.413** by modern chronologies; precise original date unverified. **Witness:** letter by documented ancient author, later Greek MSS/editions. **Verdict:** **ATTESTED AUTHORIAL SUBMISSION FOR EVALUATION; NOT ALCHEMICAL MANUSCRIPT CUSTODY**. [Window 31](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0401-0420_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Synesius moved through a culturally educated Greek philosophical network associated with Hypatia of Alexandria. Authorship and public circulation carried social stakes; he sought a trusted teacher's verdict.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
Synesius sends manuscripts of **`Dion`** and **`On Dreams`** to Hypatia and discusses the older `On the Gift` / silver astrolabe in a letter. The request attests a real writing/evaluation workflow, **not proof the recipient edited each surviving edition**.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
A known intellectual explicitly recognizes another's **judgment over the prospective publication/withholding of his books**. This is more securely dated to a real social relationship than an undated pseudo-author title alone.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Hypatia exercises **social/personal recognized editorial authority according to the letter**; no government appointment to run a chemistry academy or control reagent access. Synesius remains responsible for his own works.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
The possibility of keeping a manuscript unpublished if unworthy is **reputational editorial discernment**, not secret alchemical initiation, threatened persecution, professional economic monopoly or evidence of a hazardous recipe.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
Terms such as verdict, review, suppression, circulation in translations refer to **manuscript evaluation**, not cryptographic concealment. `On the Gift` recounts **an earlier ~397–398 gift**, not a newly manufactured silver astrolabe in a 413 workshop.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Literary critics and reputation exert pressure on an author; he sought a trusted teacher's judgment. No identifiable 405 law required alchemical writers to submit manuscripts to Hypatia.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Synesius **asks** Hypatia to judge the writings. We do not have a verified direct response, editing notes, or original publication certificate from her.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[Letter 154 in original-author translation](https://www.livius.org/sources/content/synesius/synesius-letter-154/), [Roman Letters editorial date note](https://scholarly.romanletters.org/letters/synesius_cyrene/154/), ancient composition; later physical manuscripts.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
A real **teacher's station of interpretive/publication authority** is visible through a named pupil's explicit request, without state licensing or technical alchemy being involved.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Precise dating, final manuscript revisions, direct reply, extent of readership, any personal overlap with disputed alchemical “Synesius”.
