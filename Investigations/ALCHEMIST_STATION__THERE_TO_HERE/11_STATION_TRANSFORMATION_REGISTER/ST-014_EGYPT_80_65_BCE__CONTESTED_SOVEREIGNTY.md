# ST-014 — 80–65 BCE: The Ptolemaic Royal Station Survives Contested Sovereignty

**Place:** Egypt and Rome.  
**Dates:** 80 BCE Ptolemy XII accession; 76 coronation; 65 Roman annexation proposal; 63 Cicero's debate about Egypt's legal ownership.  
**Status:** political institution and its legal contest attested. **No alchemical practitioner**.

## THERE
Following succession violence in April 80 BCE, Ptolemy XII assumes kingship and adopts cultic legitimation within Egyptian institutions.

## AUTHORITY AND PERCEPTION
Alexandrian crowd forcibly eliminates Ptolemy XI, Ptolemy XII takes throne, and priestly coronation confirms legitimacy. Roman politicians claim a royal will might already have granted Egyptian sovereignty to Rome; Cicero points out the existence of contradictory claims.

## OUTSIDE PRESSURE
Foreign Roman power can contest ownership of the entire royal station without conquering the country. Legal rhetoric extends beyond Egypt and affects perceptions of the king's title.

## SECRECY / LANGUAGE
Cicero's terms for hidden transfer (*occulte, recondita, furtim*) are rhetorical about the **political transfer of a kingdom**, not proof of secret alchemical jargon.

## RESPONSE / HERE
The king stays in office through the 65 BCE proposal and 63 BCE controversy. The Roman Senate does not conquer Egypt in this window. Political station remains but its legitimacy is disputed abroad.

## REMAINDER
Specific changes to Egyptian workshops and the ability of named specialists to practice remain unattested.
