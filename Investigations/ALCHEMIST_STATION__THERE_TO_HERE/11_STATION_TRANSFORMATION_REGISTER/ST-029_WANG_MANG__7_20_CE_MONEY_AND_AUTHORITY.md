# ST-029 — 7–20 CE Wang Mang: A State Can Change the Value of Metal Without Changing the Metal

**Location:** Western Han / Xin China.
**Dates:** reforms in 7, 9, 10, 14, and 20 CE. Dynasty Xin begins 9 CE, remains in existence beyond 20 CE.  
**Evidence:** National Museum of China dated numismatic synopsis; surviving physical objects British Museum 1996,0217.239 (7 CE) and 1908,0212.15 (14 CE); Hanshu 24B compiled later.  
**Verdict:** ATTESTED material currency and source-grounded monetary laws. **Not an alchemical profession, nor an anti-alchemy law**.

## THERE — What prior station exists?
Sovereign monetary control and skilled coin casting exist before Wang Mang. An authorized minting station is not identical to the technical capacity to cast, plate, inlay, counterfeit or refine metal.

## CAPACITY — What could someone do?
The 7 CE bronze knife coin has **physically gold-inlaid characters**. It shows precise craft; **the artisan did not turn bronze into gold**. A 14 CE copper-alloy huoquan coin establishes continued casting activity. Neither object tells us the identities, training or official titles of individual makers.

## PERCEPTION — What would viewers believe?
The gold-inlaid inscription **一刀平五千** assigns nominal value of five thousand units. Officially assigned worth is a political claim and exchange rule, not physical evidence of five thousand units' worth of precious metal in the object.

## AUTHORITY — Who may perform or recognize it?
A new Xin dynasty under Wang Mang claims authority to issue legal money and punish private casting. A maker may possess the craft yet lack legal authority to put money into circulation.

## SECRECY / LANGUAGE
Public legends and official terms `貨泉` and `貨布` change monetary categories. These are neither alchemical code names nor a private glossary for a transmutation recipe.

## THE OUTSIDE — What pressure enters?
Dynastic legitimation, monetary standardization, economic disruption and the challenge of policing privately manufactured currency.

## RESPONSE
Reforms in 7,9,10,14 and 20 CE repeatedly alter the form, denominations and legal status of circulating objects. Laws restrict unauthorized production and some older currencies. Actual regional enforcement remains to be checked against excavated texts.

## TEXTS AND PHYSICAL WITNESS
- British Museum bronze/gold-inlaid knife 1996,0217.239.
- British Museum copper-alloy huoquan 1908,0212.15.
- National Museum of China summary of Wang Mang reform years.
- *Hanshu* 24B (later written account): private casting criminal penalties.

## HERE
**The same power to shape metal can be a legitimate state skill or illegal manufacture depending on sovereign authority.** Social value and material composition must not be collapsed into one operation.

## REMAINDER
Specific foundries, licenses, molds, assays, personal craftsman identities, fines actually imposed and any actual transmutational worker's life. **No source here proves a goldmaking Alchemist was licensed or persecuted as such.**
