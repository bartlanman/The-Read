# ST-056 — 176–180 CE: Galen Compounds Theriac for Marcus Aurelius

**Evidence:** Oxford scholarly chronology dates regular theriac preparation for Marcus Aurelius to **176–180 CE**, based significantly on Galen's written corpus and later transmission. Original formula/batch records for these precise years not independently recovered.
**Station:** expert **court physician and pharmaceutical compounder**, not a demonstrated Alchemist.

## THERE
Roman high-status medicine values trained judgment, specialized ingredient access, compound recipes and imperial patronage.

## CAPACITY
Theriac (`θηριακή`) is a multi-ingredient medicinal preparation associated with antidotal claims. Preparation entails choosing substances, combining them, and controlling dosages and handling. That is a real material-compounding occupation, **not conversion of copper or lead into elemental gold**.

## PERCEPTION / AUTHORITY
The emperor's confidence can give the physician exceptional recognition and access to costly ingredients. Court favor does not independently verify theriac's therapeutic effectiveness, antidote value or protection during an epidemic.

## LANGUAGE / SECRECY
A detailed pharmaceutical recipe may require trained knowledge and custody. No direct evidence here that 176–180 theriac was concealed through an alchemical cipher to avoid persecution or chemical hazards.

## OUTSIDE / RESPONSE
Epidemic, poisoning fears, and prestige pressures around imperial medicine sustain demand for specialist compounds; Galen's medically recognized station is amplified by imperial service.

## HERE
**The practitioner who actually mixes a substance and the institution that trusts its claimed effect can be identified; efficacy still requires evidence independent of prestige.**

## REMAINDER
Actual formulas used in each year, ingredients/source suppliers, dose, patient outcome, medical efficacy, and who assisted preparation.
