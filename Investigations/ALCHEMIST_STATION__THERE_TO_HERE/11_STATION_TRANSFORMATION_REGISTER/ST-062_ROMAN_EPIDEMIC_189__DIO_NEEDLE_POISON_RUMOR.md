# ST-062 — c.189 CE: The Epidemic and Alleged Secret Poisoning in Cassius Dio

**Source:** Cassius Dio, `Roman History` Book 73.14, written **after the events**, historically situated around **c.189 CE** late in Commodus's reign.
**Status:** WITNESS TO ROMAN EPIDEMIC NARRATIVE; actual death totals, alleged needle attack and medical cause **NOT INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED**.

## THERE
Rome endures prolonged disease and political/urban tension after earlier Antonine epidemics.

## CAPACITY / PERCEPTION
Dio reports that at times **2,000 people died each day**, alongside tales of criminal poisoners using tiny needles. We can recover his **statement**, not a contemporary daily hospital log, preserved forensic specimens or verified operation of a large secret toxin gang.

## AUTHORITY / LANGUAGE
Poisoning reports offer an ancient way to make frightening, unexplained deaths intelligible as intentional hidden agency. This may express real anxiety, malicious practices, moralizing narrative or literary exaggeration; **no independent proof chooses among them**.

## SECRECY / DANGER
**Do not reclassify alleged poisoners as 'Alchemists'**, derive dangerous technical recipes from the narrative or pretend that the story proves protected poison-making knowledge. No occupational record or apparatus accompanies Dio's account.

## THE OUTSIDE
Epidemic uncertainty and strained public trust make fear of covert human action socially powerful.

## HERE
**A frightening event may generate an explanation about invisible dangerous expertise that is far more specific than the evidence warrants.** The historian must separate real illness from a source's causal accusation.

## REMAINDER
Epidemic pathogen, actual scale, what Dio directly witnessed, whether alleged poison attacks occurred at all.
