# ST-055 — c.165–180 CE: The Epidemic Tests What Even an Expert Knows

**Scope:** Roman empire from c.165–166 CE onward. Medical witness: Galen's scattered disease descriptions; modern epidemiological and paleogenomic dispute.
**Historical grade:** major epidemic established; **exact pathogen disputed** (smallpox is a hypothesis, not a proven identification).

## THERE
Imperial military movement, densely connected cities and medical practitioners create conditions for large-scale epidemic spread.

## CAPACITY
Galen observes actual disease and offers ancient medical interpretation within the knowledge of his period. He cannot supply a modern microbiological assay. Specific symptoms in writings are purposefully selected and incomplete.

## PERCEPTION / AUTHORITY
Even a respected imperial physician has limited observational and explanatory access during an unfamiliar catastrophe. Later readers sometimes treat traditional 'Antonine smallpox' as verified fact; modern scholarship emphasizes uncertainty.

## LANGUAGE
`loimos` means an epidemic/plague, **not inherently Variola virus**. Historical words describe observed syndromes through available concepts.

## OUTSIDE PRESSURE AND RESPONSE
Widespread illness pressures institutions, military forces and physicians; Galen observes the epidemic during Roman activity and again at Aquileia winter 168–169.

## HERE
**A station may hold real expertise and still fail to know the full cause, extent or remedy of what it observes.** A later historian should not make a stronger disease diagnosis than the evidence permits.

## REMAINDER
Pathogen, precise scale, case rates and transmission routes, treatment success, consistent clinical series. No connection to a recognized Alchemist office in this interval.
