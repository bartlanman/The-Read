# ST-011 — 89 BCE Luntai Edict: A Later Story of Rejecting the Fangshi

**Date described:** 89 BCE (Emperor Wu)  
**Transmission:** *Hanshu* 96B edict vs. substantially later *Zizhi tongjian* (11th c) with material possibly derived from *Han Wu gushi*  
**Verdict:** Edict and strategic decision SOURCE-SUPPORTED; total purge of immortality specialists **CONTESTED**.

## THERE
An emperor who long patronized ritual specialists had sustained major military expenses and faced political/military failures.

## CAPACITY
The Luntai edict discusses border colonization, disastrous campaigns, divination and burden on agriculture, **not** chemical procedures.

## PERCEPTION
The transmitted edict criticizes costly misjudgments and favors limiting harmful exactions.

## AUTHORITY
Imperial power issues an instruction rejecting a major expenditure; the ruler's evaluation of strategy changes.

## LANGUAGE
The later *Tongjian* says immortality-seeking specialists were all dismissed; that assertion does not appear as such in the earlier *Hanshu* edict. 'Pseudoscience' is an anachronistic label here.

## SECRECY / OUTSIDE
Bad predictions and claims can lose patronage under war/fiscal pressure. A record of alleged broad dismissal is a **retrospective story**, not direct evidence of a new law criminalizing alchemy.

## RESPONSE
A specific expansion proposal is refused. Total reversal on immortality practices remains UNVERIFIED; modern historians dispute whether Sima Guang's enlarged repentance narrative is historically trustworthy.

## HERE
**A later historian's account of a station's disappearance can itself be an act of boundary-making.** The story of a ruler withdrawing recognition is not proof of the actual disappearance of a practitioner community.

## REMAINDER
Find contemporaneous *fangshi* personnel records and exact source genealogy for the dismissal story.
