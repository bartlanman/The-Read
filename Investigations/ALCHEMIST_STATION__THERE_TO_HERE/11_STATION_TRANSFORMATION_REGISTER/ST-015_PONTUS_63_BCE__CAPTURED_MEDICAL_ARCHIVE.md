# ST-015 — Mithridates VI, 63 BCE: Conquered Royal Medicine Translated into a New Authority

**Region:** Pontic kingdom → Rome  
**Event date:** Mithridates dies in 63 BCE; Pompey's acquisition of records after conquest described later, exact dates not independently established.  
**Text date:** Pliny, *Naturalis historia* 25.5–8, about 77 CE.  
**Verdict:** historically documented collapse of king; **retrospective textual claim** about medical notes' seizure and Latin translation. Not an 'Alchemist'.

## THERE / CAPACITY
Mithridates was associated with botanical research, management of poison risk and study of antidotes. Pliny describes a habit of collecting medicinal observations from subjects across his realm. Actual efficacy, detailed experiments and personal record contents cannot be inspected from surviving notes here.

## PERCEPTION AND AUTHORITY
In Pliny's Roman framing, Mithridates was an extraordinary royal student of nature; Pompey's victory and Lenaeus' reported translations provide Roman readers access to a captured corpus of knowledge.

## SECRECY
Pliny locates these notes in Mithridates' **private collection**; no evidence directly demonstrates a formal prohibition on publication or deliberate encoded chemical nomenclature under Mithridates. His antidotal celebrity does not establish universal immunity to poisons.

## THE OUTSIDE
A war removes the station supporting the medicinal archive. Conqueror's possession and translation turn the archive into Roman intellectual and political capital.

## RESPONSE / LANGUAGE
The alleged texts change audience and language, from the king's realm to Latin readership through Lenaeus. Original texts and translation dates unverified.

## HERE
**An expert's data and practices may be appropriated by a victor, surviving the demise of the patron who had collected them.** Custody of texts and demonstrated technical competence are two distinct capacities.

## REMAINDER
Who physically performed medicine? Did the royal court enforce secrecy? How did experiment reports become translated or changed? No secure gold-making formula or alchemical succession linked to Egypt.
