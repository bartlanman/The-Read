# ST-049 — The Forged 'Hadrian' Letter: A False Second-Century Craft Witness

**Historical author represented:** Emperor Hadrian (117–138 CE).  
**Actual source:** `Historia Augusta`, *Quadriga Tyrannorum* 8, a **late-fourth-century or late-antique pseudo-biographical compilation**. Philologists including Schmid, Syme, Schwartz conclude the letter to Servianus is **not authentic**.  
**Classification:** RETROSPECTIVE ATTRIBUTION / SOURCE TRAP, **not an actual 121–140 CE station event**.

## THERE
A later author seeks to depict the supposedly tumultuous, commercially lively, religiously mixed Roman Alexandria.

## CLAIMED CAPACITY
The forged Hadrian lists **glassblowers, papyrus makers, linen weavers** and describes **variegated or color-changing cups** supposedly gifted to the emperor by a priest. These claims are **not genuine eyewitness evidence** from Hadrianic Egypt.

## AUTHORITY / LANGUAGE
The emperor's supposed first-person voice lends apparent royal authority to vivid trade detail, much as the Greek recipe corpus invokes ancient Democritus. **The resemblance is analytical, not proof of a shared author or technique.**

## SECRECY
Nothing in the forged letter authenticates a secret priestly cup recipe or a persecuted alchemical worker. Do not cite 'Hadrian personally observed magical Alexandrian glass' as a second-century fact based on this witness.

## THE OUTSIDE
Late antique religious polemics and characterizing stereotypes about Egyptians/Alexandrians feed the pseudohistorical composition.

## RESPONSE
The text enters subsequent historical discussion, and readers may mistake its imagined Hadrianic first-person report for original administrative evidence.

## HERE
**A famous name can manufacture apparent age and credibility for a text written much later. Identifying the actual source time prevents a false technical lineage.**

## REMAINDER
Which late-antique sources the forger used, whether any earlier accurate workshop observations underlie literary tropes, earliest manuscript layers and relationship to other `Historia Augusta` forgeries.
