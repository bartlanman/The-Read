# ST-110 — “Synesius to Dioscorus, Priest of Serapis”: A Later Text Portrays Alchemical Authority, Not a Verified 390 Event

**Scope:** source-critical **alchemical literary station claim**, NOT securely dated 381–400 practitioner. **Putative represented setting:** priest of **great Serapis in Alexandria**, suggested **pre-ca.391** by some scholarly assumptions; **original composition UNKNOWN**. **Physical witnesses:** medieval Greek manuscripts (Marcianus gr.299 ~10th–11th c; Parisinus gr.2325 13th; Parisinus gr.2327 15th) and other later copies. **Modern critical edition:** Martelli & Dorandi **2011**. **Grade:** ATTESTED MEDIEVAL TRANSMISSION / LITERARY REPRESENTATION; real author, date, interlocutor, actual apprenticeship **CONTESTED/NO SECURE EVIDENCE**. **Window 30** [381–400 research horizon](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0381-0400_CE__WINDOW.md), **NOT A YEAR-SPECIFIC STATION TRANSFORMATION**.

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Greek-Egyptian alchemical texts attributed to Democritus, Hermes and other names circulate in later copied collections. An actual Serapis sanctuary existed at Alexandria and was destroyed ca.391/392, giving the later title a historically evocative setting.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
The *transmitted text* contains **exegesis of pseudo-Democritean alchemical teachings**, dyeing/coloring and transformation discussions. It does **not** provide independently witnessed successful elemental goldmaking nor an original experiment performed by a named 390 writer.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Within the dialogue, authorial voice positions a **philosopher** writing to **Dioscorus, priest of Serapis**, suggesting learned and ritual authority could be invoked to legitimate technical explanation. **This is a statement about the text's portrayal**, not the true beliefs of a 391 Egyptian audience.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
The heading **claims** an instructional/priestly relationship. No independent **381–400** inscription or employment record corroborates Dioscorus, no original dated letter corroborates Synesius, and attribution to later **bishop Synesius of Cyrene** must not be taken for granted.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Learned technical complexity and claims to ancient authors create interpretive gates. **No evidence the manuscript was encoded to evade Theophilus**, created after sanctuary destruction, or that a destroyed Serapeum library lost the key. The dedication is not an apprenticeship contract.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`Synesius`, `Dioscorus`, `Serapis`, `Democritus` are author/address/attribution names; none alone guarantees original author, historical encounter or ancient Democritean biological lineage. “Priest” is a portrayed station, not proven rank/date.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Actual **391 legal restriction and Serapeum sanctuary destruction c.391/392** are independently contextual. **No documented encounter** between those events and the writer/priest of this undated text.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
A later manuscript preserves the literary dialogue; **no observed 390 authorial response** to legal change, person migrating, hidden technique, or teaching school established.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
`Synesii ad Dioscorum in librum Democriti`, **CAAG II 56.20–69.11**; [Martelli & Dorandi 2011, *Pseudo-Democrito. Scritti alchemici con il commentario di Sinesio*](https://pinakes-dev.irht.cnrs.fr/notices/bibliographie/J8SKCZW8/). Physical MSS later medieval; original work's exact date UNKNOWN.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
Greek alchemical literature **represents the prestige of a Serapis priest as relevant to interpreting inherited technical texts**. This is potentially revealing for the station's *literary authorization* even if original author/date remain unverified.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Real Dioscorus identity and priest date; bishop Synesius versus namesake/pseudonym; original composition, exact manuscripts' relationships, actual goldmaking, any response to Serapeum destruction.
