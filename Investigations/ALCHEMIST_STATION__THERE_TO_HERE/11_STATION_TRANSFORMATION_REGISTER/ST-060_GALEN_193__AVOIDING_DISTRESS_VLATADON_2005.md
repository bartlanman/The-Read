# ST-060 — Early 193 CE Text, 15th-Century Copy, 2005 Recognition

**Work:** Galen, *On Avoiding Distress* / `Περὶ ἀλυπίας`, probably composed **early 193 CE** in wake of **spring 192 fire** and death of Commodus **31 December 192**.
**Surviving manuscript:** **Vlatadon 14**, copied **15th century CE**, recognized by researchers in **2005**; modern critical edition/translations (2023).
**Status:** DOCUMENTED HISTORICAL TEXT AND A WELL-DATED LATER WITNESS. **2005 is the modern recognition date, not ancient restoration**.

## THERE
Galen loses instruments, precious compounds, historical books and personal editions in urban Rome. A correspondent requests an account of Galen's ability to withstand distress.

## CAPACITY
He writes on philosophy and practical moral discipline—**alypia**, avoidance of excessive distress. The later copying chain preserves a meaningful account of the loss, though not every original object the fire destroyed.

## PERCEPTION
Galen constructs an image of disciplined resilience; this is an ancient **self-authored moral performance**, not independently verified absence of every moment of grief.

## AUTHORITY
The known practitioner signs his own argument. Galen's title and citations in medieval inventories existed before researchers recovered a coherent later Greek witness, so a **known name/title did not guarantee recoverable full content**.

## LANGUAGE, SECRECY, TRANSMISSION
| Historical threshold | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **192** | Event he describes |
| **early 193** | Scholarly date of composition |
| **15th century** | Greek codex Vlatadon 14 was copied |
| **2005** | Researchers recognized full Greek text in codex |
| **2023** | New critical Greek/English edition |

An **obscured manuscript due to miscataloging and transmission** is not a text deliberately encrypted to avoid persecution.

## THE OUTSIDE / RESPONSE
Copyist chains, libraries and scholarly classification both preserve and obscure a work; modern recognition adds public availability but **does not retrieve the 192 lost exemplars**.

## HERE
**Historical knowledge can disappear from the known corpus yet survive materially, unnoticed, in another chain. The station of a text depends not merely on survival but on legibility and recognition.**

## REMAINDER
Exact post-193 early reception, all copies lost in the fire, reconstructing Galen's lost annotations from later quotations.
