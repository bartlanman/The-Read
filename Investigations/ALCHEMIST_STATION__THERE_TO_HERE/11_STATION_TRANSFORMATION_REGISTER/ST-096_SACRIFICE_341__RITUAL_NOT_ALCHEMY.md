# ST-096 — 341: An Imperial Law Threatens Sacrificial Practice, Not Alchemical Technique

**Scope:** comparative ritual institution, not Alchemist. **Date:** 341 consular receipt; precise day unknown. **Witness:** *Codex Theodosianus* XVI.10.2 compiled 438, later copies. **Grade:** ATTESTED transmitted normative law; independent implementation not demonstrated. [Window 28](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0341-0360_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Roman traditional sacrifices and priestly practice had changing status after Constantine. This law names the ruler Constantius and an imperial official Madalianus.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
Targeted **sacrificial acts** (`sacrificia`) are cult practice; no recipe, metallurgy, alchemical apparatus, chemical hazard or gold product accompanies the statute.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Legal voice expresses hostility: `Cesset superstitio, sacrificiorum aboleatur insania`. This is an **imperial perception** labeling rites irrational/superstitious, not a modern scientific assessment of all technical practice.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
The state threatens those offering sacrifices contrary to laws, claiming earlier father's restrictions. The alleged earlier rule is referred to, not fully preserved here. **No named Alchemist or metalworker is targeted**.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
A threatened ritual performer might conceivably become discreet, but this text has **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** of an actual practitioner's concealment, special alchemical code, protected formula, or alchemical fear.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`superstitio` and `sacrificiorum insania` are official evaluative rhetoric; neither labels chrysopoeia. Consular `Accepta Marcellino et Probino conss.` dates receipt to **341** without certain month/day.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
341 imperial anti-sacrifice instruction imposes **legal pressure on a ritual station**. It cannot be projected onto a craft station by shared ancient religious imagery.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Prohibited sacrifices are condemned by instruction; a specific priest's compliance or trial requires further documentation and is not established here.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[CTh XVI.10.2 Latin](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh16.html), edited 438 code, later manuscript tradition, NOT an original 341 found tablet.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
A public authority contests the legitimacy of a defined ritual action, without demonstrating that alchemical material operations were either included or banned.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Actual penalties/enforcement, regional exemptions, precise earlier law, local practitioners, any alchemical guild link.
