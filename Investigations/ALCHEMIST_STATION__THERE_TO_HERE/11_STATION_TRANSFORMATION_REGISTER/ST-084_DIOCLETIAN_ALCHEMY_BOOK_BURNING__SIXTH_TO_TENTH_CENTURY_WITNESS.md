# ST-084 — Diocletian's Alleged Burning of Goldmaking Books: A Later Claim, Not an Original 297 Decree

**Scope:** Later reception of alleged late-third-century **alchemical suppression**, not established original event. **Narrated time:** Diocletian and Egyptian revolt around **297–298 CE**. **Text composition/witness:** John of Antioch ***Chronography* c.610–626 CE**, fragment **191 Mariev**, as transmitted in tenth-century ***Suda* Δ 1156 / Χ 280**. **Evidence verdict:** **ATTESTED LATER NARRATIVE; NO SECURE EVIDENCE ACTUAL 297 BOOK BURNING.** **Window:** [25 (claimed incident), 281–300 CE](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0281-0300_CE__WINDOW.md); actual **witness period later**.

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Egyptian political rebellion in 297 has **contemporary papyrological and numismatic evidence**. Greek/Egyptian material-coloring and precious-metal imitation were practical traditions, and later commentators attached different ontological claims to them. Neither automatically proves a politically organized guild of Alchemists.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
**Contemporary:** no named practitioner from this episode is independently attested preparing metal, book, or gold. **Later author's narrated claim:** old Egyptian books contained expertise on making gold and silver. That is a **textual allegation**, not a performed experimental result or a book inventory.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
The *Suda* portrays the emperor viewing the capacity to create wealth from material transformation as a political threat. This is **evidence for later Byzantine perception of potentially dangerous wealth-producing technical knowledge**, not independent testimony of Diocletian's mind or of Egyptians' actual material success.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
The emperor supposedly ordered searches and burning; **no original Diocletian edict naming `chēmeia`, no confiscation receipt, no named person punished, no text identified as burnt** has been established. Documentary repression of rebel political authority cannot be used as a substitute.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
**Political suppression** is *claimed* by much later writers. There is **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** that secrecy arose because this action occurred, that priests hid a guild from Diocletian, that the destroyed works contained effective goldmaking, or that any particular 297 teacher changed writing styles. An allegation that books were *publicly searchable* also complicates any claim of universal secrecy.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
[*Suda* Χ 280](https://cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/chi/280) defines **`χημεία`** as **`ἡ τοῦ ἀργύρου καὶ χρυσοῦ κατασκευή`** (preparation of silver and gold); uses **`βιβλία`** (books), **`ἔκαυσε`** (he burned). **The late word does not prove an earlier administrative title “Alchemist”**, and gold/silver production may denote preparation/imitation, not elemental transformation.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and date it entered
**Historical context:** political revolt 297–298 **ATTESTED**. **Specific alchemical Outside pressure:** suppression of precious-metal books **only RETROSPECTIVELY ASSERTED** by John/Suda and cannot be dated precisely. A separate anti-Manichaean directive in *Collatio legum* 15.3 has debated 297/302 dating, targets a different religious station and is **NOT** this alleged book-burning edict.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
The late narrative says the emperor **burned books** so that Egyptians could not fund rebellion. **NO DOCUMENTED alchemical practitioner response**: no verified hidden text, migration, changed technique, new title, apprenticeship, preserved confiscation list or burned material specimen directly connected to Diocletian.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[**Suda Χ 280**](https://cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/chi/280); [**Δ 1156**](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/delta/1156) are **not independent primary events**; Dufault 2019, pp.94–95 and n.5 links to **John of Antioch fr.191 Mariev, c.610–626**. Neither is a 297 Roman legal autograph. [*Suda* Δ 250](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~raphael/sol/sol-entries/delta/250) offers a separate later **Golden Fleece as goldmaking book** allegory; not proof of an actual classical manuscript.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
**Documented later reputation**: Byzantine historiography cast material knowledge as economically and politically potent enough that rulers might suppress its textual transmission. **Not** proven actual third-century suppression. If an original dated anti-alchemical directive were found, reclassify; until then maintain the causal firewall.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Earliest independent witness to original incident, exact nature of ancient Egyptian writing if any, presence of real books, economic and military causes of revolt, interpolation between John of Antioch and Suda, actual 297 alchemical actor or institutional response.
