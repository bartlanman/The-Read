# ST-099 — Alexandria 351–355: A Struck Copper Alloy Is Not Evidence of Gold Transmutation

**Scope:** original-object material/mint station comparator. **Date:** 351–355 CE range, NOT single specific 353 year. **Witness:** original copper-alloy **British Museum B.13591**. **Grade:** **ATTESTED PHYSICAL OBJECT**, NO SECURE EVIDENCE Alchemist or chemical transmutation. [Window 28](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0341-0360_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Alexandria minted money under the emperors and Caesars of mid-fourth-century Rome. Metal craft and political money authority persisted amid imperial religious/technical policy changes.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
The **1.41g, 18mm** nummus is struck **copper alloy**, with bust of **Constantius Gallus** and soldier attacking fallen horseman. This proves prepared metal and minting, NOT an industrial method for synthesizing gold from copper.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Military reverse is public imperial victory imagery. The ruler's presentation is a claim of authority, not proof of any given battle, gold-making success or magical effect.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Ruling field in BM lists **Constantius II**; **Constantius Gallus** is named/portrayed on the coin as Caesar. Do not collapse them into one person. The actual die-cutter and metal preparer remain unnamed.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
No named secret workshop, alchemical oath, hazard concealment, political persecution or initiation in the object/catalog.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`nummus` = coin category in modern museum record; **copper alloy** is materially distinct from elemental gold. Public imperial portrait is not ingredient/allegorical transmutation instruction.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Changing imperial monetary and political situations provide broad context for issuing official copper-alloy money; no direct 357 anti-divination regulation is shown to have affected this particular coin.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Mint produced an original money object in Alexandria c.351–355. An individual mint worker's response to changing ritual laws remains unverified.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[Original British Museum B.13591, 351–355](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_B-13591), described and weighed by catalog. Not an original technical recipe manuscript or alchemical certificate.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
Direct physical capacity to process and strike copper alloy in the same political era as restrictions on divination, without evidence connecting **material work** to prohibited **ritual** or the officially protected engineer's role.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Names of mint staff, actual workshop alloy formula, ore source, actual trust of coin recipients, any link to Zosimos tradition or identified transmuter.
