# ST-019 — c.55–54 BCE: Rabirius Postumus Takes a Foreign Royal Financial Station

**Region:** Ptolemaic Alexandria / Roman Republic.  
**Sources:** Cicero, *Pro Rabirio Postumo* §§25–29 (defense in 54 BCE), modern Ptolemaic chronology.  
**Verdict:** historically contemporaneous speech identifies a named royal finance office, but the account of coercion is ADVOCACY, not a neutral fact.

## THERE
Ptolemy XII driven out in 58 BCE and restored by Aulus Gabinius in 55 BCE after borrowing resources from Roman financiers.

## CAPACITY
Rabirius is a lender/financial organizer. **No known material alchemical process**.

## PERCEPTION / AUTHORITY
A Roman creditor, seeking repayment, is installed or accepts role as **dioikētēs** (royal fiscal treasurer/revenue administrator). Cicero stresses that a foreign office has its own *name*, clothing and public recognition, and a claimant must accommodate the local rules to occupy it.

## SECRECY / LANGUAGE
Cicero's report is *public rhetorical defense*, not an esoteric language. The garments and office title communicate legitimate station; local public perception may still be hostile.

## OUTSIDE
Debt, Roman war-making power, restoration of Egyptian monarchy.

## RESPONSE
A foreign financier inhabits inside royal fiscal station. Later Roman prosecution and disgrace expose how differently Egyptian and Roman audiences judge the same role.

## HERE
**Outside dependency can open a station to an outsider without transferring its members' craft capacities or creating a new profession.**

## REMAINDER
Which Egyptian officials were displaced? Did workshop financing or temple craft authorization change? No close-dated evidence connecting him to an Alchemist.
