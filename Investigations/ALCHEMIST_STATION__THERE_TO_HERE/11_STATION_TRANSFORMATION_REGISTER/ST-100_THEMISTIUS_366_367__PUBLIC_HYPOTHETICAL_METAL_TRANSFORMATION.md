# ST-100 — Themistius 366/367: Public Concept of Bronze → Silver → Gold, Not an Alchemist at Work

**Scope:** positive **public perception** evidence, NOT proved independent Alchemist occupation. **Event:** *Oration 7* usually **367**, alternatively **366/367**, after Procopius's 366 defeat. **Witness:** named Greek author Themistius, transmitted by **later manuscripts**; modern Dufault 2019 pp.100–102 analyzes *Or.7* 145.12–146.11. **Grade:** ATTESTED AUTHORED LITERARY REFERENCE / HYPOTHETICAL PROCESS; NO SECURE EVIDENCE successful transmutation. [Window 29](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0361-0380_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
After the defeat of Procopius's rebellion, the philosopher/orator Themistius sought to praise **Valens's mercy and reconciliation**, using philosophical and mythological models for transformation. He was a public philosophical intellectual; the empire knew ordinary precious-metal manufacture and traded actual gold.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
Themistius could **compose and publicly deliver persuasive rhetoric**. His comparison includes the imaginary discovery of a technique changing **bronze to silver** and **silver to gold**, but he supplies **NO recipe, sample, instrument or named performer**. It cannot establish physical or elemental conversion.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
The metaphor works because **the extraordinary possibility of converting metals was intelligible to a courtly audience**; belief that it actually happened is NOT demonstrated. Transformation of enemies into friends by mercy is the speaker's political comparison, not an alchemical institutional case.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Themistius spoke as an authoritative orator/philosopher to power; this is **his own station**, not social recognition of an independently titled Alchemist. A rhetorical contrast is no license, patron contract or recognition of material success for a transmuter.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
The public speech itself complicates any thesis that merely **mentioning** transformation was taboo. No evidence of secrecy oath, hazardous instruction, persecutions or hidden practical recipes associated with Themistius.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
Dufault analyzes **Or.7 145.12–146.11**, comparing **Circe/Medea/Xerxes/Autolycus** and hypothetical metallurgy. Distinguish the orator's **counterfactual bronze→silver→gold** from **social/political enemy→friend**. Modern scholarly English paraphrase is not claimed as exact quoted Greek original, which survives in later texts.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
**366 political civil war** and the need to establish Valens's legitimacy pushed the orator's rhetorical argument. The 366/367 reference shows **public conceptual reception**, not external persecution/regulation of a specific goldmaker.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Themistius **responded by praising political clemency in public speech**. No metal transmuter's response observed.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
*Themistius Oration VII*, usually dated **367**, date **366/367** plausible; Greek written/speech text later manuscript transmission, Dindorf/other modern editions. [Dufault 2019 pp.100–102, passage citations](https://escholarship.org/content/qt2ks0g83x/qt2ks0g83x.pdf).

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
**The IDEA of transmutation has a named, plausibly dated fourth-century PUBLIC witness outside technical recipe writing.** No actual skill or socially authorized practitioner follows automatically.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Precise delivery date; complete Greek critical collation; source of comparison; audience beliefs; contemporary practitioners; laboratory results; no direct Zosimos citation.
