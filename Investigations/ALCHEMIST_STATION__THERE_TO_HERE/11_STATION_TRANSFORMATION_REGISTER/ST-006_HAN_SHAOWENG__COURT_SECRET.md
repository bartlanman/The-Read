# ST-006 — Shaoweng: A Recognized Court Specialist Executed for a Claimed Sign

**Date:** before Luan Da's appearance c.113 BCE; precise year disputed / not assigned without primary chronological apparatus.  
**Witness:** *Shiji* 28 and later reconstructed *Shiji* 12 (overlapping, not independent).  
**Status:** NARRATIVE OF PUBLICLY BESTOWED OFFICE + DEATH; claimed supernatural technique unverified.

## THERE / CAPACITY / PERCEPTION
Shaoweng presents ghost/spirit methods, displaying to the emperor the supposed image of a deceased favored consort, leading to honor and a named military title. Court acknowledged claimed expertise, but no independent successful conjuring is demonstrated.

## AUTHORITY
Emperor Wu appoints Shaoweng **General of Accomplished Literature** (文成將軍), gives gifts and honors as guest. He also commissions ritual décor, imagery, palatial apparatus and objects to attract spirits, showing resources flowing from recognition.

## SECRECY AND LANGUAGE
The emperor reportedly identifies Shaoweng as author of an alleged message concealed in an ox's body; the historian reports a forgery. The emperor then orders his execution **but hides it** (隱之). This is evidence of **court management of embarrassing news**, not of a scientific text being deliberately allegorical.

## OUTSIDE PRESSURE
Court demands results, and recognition becomes precarious when wonders fail to appear. A purportedly mysterious written omen is falsified by detecting handwriting.

## RESPONSE
Execution destroys Shaoweng's individual official station. Luan Da later uses this precedent to warn that other *fangshi* would keep silent rather than risk his fate.

## HERE
**A specialist could be elevated by extraordinary claims, then destroyed when the patron withdrew belief.** This is a historical narrative of an actual office, not proof of the paranormal claim.

## REMAINDER
Exact calendar year; original silk; exact role of technical craft; a shared master with Luan Da is narrated but not independently verified; no proof of general persecution of legitimate goldmakers.
