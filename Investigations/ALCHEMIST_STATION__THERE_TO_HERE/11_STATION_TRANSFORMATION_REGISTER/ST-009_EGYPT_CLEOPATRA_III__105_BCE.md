# ST-009 — Cleopatra III c.105/104 BCE: Who May Occupy a Station?

**Date:** 116–101 BCE rule changes, especially c.105/104 BCE personal assumption of the eponymous priesthood.  
**Geography:** Ptolemaic Egypt.  
**Verdict:** Supported royal/ritual station change; **no alchemical practitioner link** established.

## THERE
Cleopatra III ruled with her sons, initially Ptolemy IX, then Ptolemy X after 107 BCE. The royal cult already had named eponymous priestly offices, often connected to ruling legitimacy.

## CAPACITY
Political/ritual office not a documented technique for transmutation. Do not mistake a cultic symbol for a hidden chemistry.

## PERCEPTION / AUTHORITY
The kingdom's authority is expressed via office-holding, royal names, cult titles and contemporary dating practices. A specific female ruling actor occupied an office otherwise associated with male incumbents.

## THE OUTSIDE
Succession conflict, royal sovereignty, social eligibility and priestly institutional customs. The same station is not necessarily open to every person under inherited norms.

## RESPONSE
Cleopatra III assumed eponymous priesthood herself in year 13/10, broadly 105/104 BCE (as documented by modern chronology and papyrological evidence). This is an unusually explicit case of **the station's customary occupant changing under dynastic pressure**.

## HERE
The criteria for legitimate office-holding can shift as the political pressures on an institution change. This is a *comparative station mechanism*, not proof of an alchemical mastership.

## REMAINDER
Exact primary papyrus PColon.2.81 and specialist study needed for office wording, date, whether 'unprecedented' is true in all regional practices, and the public reaction.
