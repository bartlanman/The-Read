# ST-087 — 303 CE: Near-Contemporary Witnesses Describe Destruction of CHRISTIAN Scriptures, Not Alchemical Books

**Scope:** comparative **Christian institutional station** under external coercion, not alchemical persecution.  
**Narrated event dates:** **23 February 303** Nicomedia church demolition; **24 February** posting of first restrictive edict per Lactantius; **7 June 303** Procopius of Caesarea per Eusebius.  
**Witness dates:** Lactantius *De mortibus persecutorum* composed following events; Eusebius *Ecclesiastical History* VIII and *Martyrs of Palestine* composed/revised early fourth century; surviving exemplars later.  
**Evidence verdict:** **NEAR-CONTEMPORARY LITERARY REPORT**, strengthened by multiple same-community sources; **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** alchemical books/workshops targeted. [Window 26](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0301-0320_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Christian churches, their reading/publication of scriptures, worship and clergy roles were socially recognizable despite earlier varied regional sanctions. Diocletian's government entered a particularly coercive phase after 303.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
**Lactantius DMP 12** reports officials forcing entry into the **Nicomedia church 23 February 303**, finding **scriptures** and burning them, dismantling the structure with iron tools. **DMP 13** reports posting the associated edict the next day. Eusebius **HE VIII.2** separately describes churches torn down and sacred books burned. This is an attack on **religious texts** and a recognized congregation; the ancient reports are not independently recovered 303 burnt pages or mint workshop ledgers.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Lactantius and Eusebius portray a hostile state effort to degrade Christians' public standing and remove texts through which their religious station was preserved/transmitted. Both write as Christians with strong interpretative interests; their agreement supports a common persecution history but is not neutral official transcript or proof of all reported conversations.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Rulers and administrators allegedly order closing/tearing down of churches, destruction of scriptures, rank restrictions and sanctions. Procopius in Eusebius **Martyrs of Palestine I** is a **named Christian individual** whose religious refusal led to execution on **7 June 303**, not a named scholar of gold transmutation.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
**Real reported religious pressure** could create concealment of worship/books; **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** a recipe-key hiding practice arose from persecution or that *Leiden X, Stockholm* or Zosimos's writings were listed for confiscation. A Christian author active near 300 is not automatically a documented victim of this policy. Physical hazards of reagents are irrelevant to the stated religious target.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
Lactantius: `scripturae repertae incenduntur` (scriptures found and burned). It does **not** say `chēmeia`, `aurum` or alchemical manuals. The *Suda* story about Diocletian's gold/silver books is a **7th–10th-century related transmission** narrating the **late 290s** and must remain separate. Eusebius's dating/calendar `Dystrus` and Lactantius's **February Nicomedia** sequence are distinct textual witnesses, not equivalent exact local timestamps.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
**303 state policy** challenges precisely Christian worship and textual custody, with unequal implementation by region. It changes the church's permission to assemble and preserve books; an alchemical community's encounter is **not evidenced**.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Church destruction and loss of privilege are the responses described by Christian sources; individual Procopius's refusal/execution dramatizes religious standing. No **documented response by an identified alchemical technician**: not proven Zosimos encrypted recipes, fled, lost an appointment or protected goldmaking methods.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[Lactantius DMP 12–13 Latin original in later copies](https://www.ancienttexts.org/library/latinlibrary/lactantius.html); [Eusebius HE VIII.2](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nicene_and_Post-Nicene_Fathers%3A_Series_II/Volume_I/Church_History_of_Eusebius/Book_VIII/Chapter_2); [*Martyrs of Palestine* I](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nicene_and_Post-Nicene_Fathers%3A_Series_II/Volume_I/Church_History_of_Eusebius/Martyrs_of_Palestine/Chapter_I). Independent 303 imperial original issued scroll absent in audit; these are near-contemporary testimony.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
A **directly targeted institution**, **named textual genre**, concrete destruction claim and changing public status can be linked by near-contemporary records. This is much more grounded than attributing Diocletian's alleged suppression of `chēmeia` to an actual 303 executive order.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Exact wording of all imperial edicts, geographical implementation, physical lost books, original witness autographs, date/identity of any alchemist who was also Christian, any alchemical text destroyed with Christian scriptures.
