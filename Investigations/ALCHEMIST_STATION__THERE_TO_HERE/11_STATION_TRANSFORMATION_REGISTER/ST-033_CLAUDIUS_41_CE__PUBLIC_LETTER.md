# ST-033 — Claudius, 41 CE: Public Authority Through a Published Letter

**Event:** Prefect orders publication of imperial correspondence on **10 November 41 CE**.  
**Place:** Roman Alexandria, Egypt.  
**Primary material:** **P.Lond. VI 1912 = British Library Papyrus 2248**, actual Greek papyrus copy; other side holds earlier **37–38 CE tax records**.  
**Station status:** CONTEMPORARY AUTHORIZED PUBLIC WRITING, **not alchemical profession**.

## THERE
A Roman prefect governs the contested multi-community city Alexandria after the dangerous violence of 38 CE. The emperor Claudius responds to petitions and claims from the civic community.

## CAPACITY
Writing, copying, reading and publishing a legal-political message. This is **administrative communication**, not an experiment with metals.

## PERCEPTION
The emperor's letter has authority through the offices attached to it. The prefect's dated instruction specifically calls for dissemination for personal inspection, creating public access to official wording.

## AUTHORITY
Named institutional sequence: **Claudius → prefect Lucius Aemilius Rectus → public posting → copies and readership**. The actual papyrus gives a materially grounded chain of document transmission.

## LANGUAGE / SECRECY
Greek official writing is publicly distributed by order of the prefect. This is a **counterexample** to assuming all consequential elite knowledge relies on restricted initiation. The message does not contain a hidden recipe or specialized chemical lexicon.

## OUTSIDE
The previous Alexandrian civic conflict places pressure on the governing station to clarify protection, honor and public political roles.

## RESPONSE
Official communication promulgated to a public audience. The reuse of a document-bearing papyrus illustrates that copied matter could have different institutional uses across years.

## HERE
**An institution can acquire legitimacy by permitting people to inspect its wording, not merely by restricting access to a privileged few.** Publicly dated surviving document versus an imagined hidden-column secret is the crucial methodological contrast.

## REMAINDER
How many citizens actually read the document? To what extent did policy change conditions on the ground? Not evidence of Egyptian Alchemist training.
