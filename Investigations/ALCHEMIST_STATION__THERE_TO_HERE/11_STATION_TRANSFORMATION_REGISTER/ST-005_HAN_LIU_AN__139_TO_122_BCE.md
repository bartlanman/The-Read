# ST-005 — Liu An, 139–122 BCE: Patronage, Text, and Political Vulnerability

**Date:** Huainanzi conventionally presented to Emperor Wu in **139 BCE**, and Liu An dies in **122 BCE**. Composition/author attribution disputed in modern source criticism.  
**Scope:** Chinese intellectual court / philosophical collection and later esoteric attribution.  
**Verdict:** well-attested institutional patronage and broad literary production; distinct personal alchemical office and gold-making authorship **not demonstrated**.

## THERE
Prince Liu An of Huainan (179–122 BCE) presided over a princely court drawing writers, scholars and specialists in methods under Western Han political conditions.

## CAPACITY
The *Huainanzi* is a sophisticated surviving cosmological and statecraft text. **It is not itself a direct technical recipe-book for the production of gold**. Later evidence ascribes additional now-lost esoteric or metalmaking compositions to Liu An's circle.

## PERCEPTION
A princely patron could associate himself with encyclopedic knowledge, cosmology and sage rule. Later accounts make Liu An an especially prolific writer and an esoteric authority; these must be distinguished from contemporaneous composition.

## AUTHORITY
A prince's wealth, court and political position facilitate compilation, employment and transmission, independently of demonstrated personal chemical skill.

## SECRECY
Titles such as 'treasure' / 'hidden' in lost-text traditions require manuscript witnesses and source-critical date tests. The current evidence does not show a 139 BCE secrecy oath or a political ban on alchemical knowledge.

## LANGUAGE
Multiple specialists' cosmological and political writing; later technical attribution; a collective text should not be silently reduced to one author's secrets.

## THE OUTSIDE
Centralized Han state power and accusations of treason culminated in Liu An's death and loss of political station (122 BCE). No evidence this was persecution *for alchemy*.

## RESPONSE
The princely court's specific patronage setting ended, while the surviving *Huainanzi* and later traditions associated with the court circulated. Mechanism and identity of direct alchemical technical transmission remain unverified.

## TEXTS
*Huainanzi* philosophical compendium; lost *Hongbao* works named in later sources; do not collapse these into one original 139 BCE document or assign sole authorship to Liu An.

## HERE
**Patronage can sustain and confer authority on collective writing without identifying the patron as the skilled performer.** Loss of royal standing does not imply disappearance of a text.

## REMAINDER
Disputed 139 BCE boundary; contributors; exact relation of later reports of 'Hongbao' to actual texts; evidence of practical method lineages.
