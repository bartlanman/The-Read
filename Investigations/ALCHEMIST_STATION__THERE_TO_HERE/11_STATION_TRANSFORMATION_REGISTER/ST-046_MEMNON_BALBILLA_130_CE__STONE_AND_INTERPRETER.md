# ST-046 — 130 CE: Julia Balbilla Hears and Names the Stone

**Evidence:** actual Greek poems carved November **130 CE** onto the northern Memnon Colossus at Egyptian Thebes. One dates **21 November 130 CE**.
**Roles:** ancient Amenhotep III sculptural object, poet **Julia Balbilla**, Roman emperor **Hadrian**, empress **Sabina**, sacred-tourism visitors.

## THERE
A colossal Egyptian statue damaged earlier produces a reported morning sound; Greek visitors had reidentified the pharaonic statue with mythical Memnon, the hero mourned at dawn.

## CAPACITY
A physical acoustic phenomenon is widely reported, its exact cause still proposed rather than independently reconstructed. Writing/inscription carving is visibly real; divine causation not established.

## PERCEPTION
The poet records hearing a sound and presents it as mythical/emperor-honoring speech. Her Sapphic Aeolic learning gives additional literary prestige.

## AUTHORITY
The emperor's presence amplifies what the sound is taken to mean; the **poet** receives a named station as interpreter. The ancient sculpture's material date differs by more than a millennium from its newly inscribed Roman poetic meaning.

## SECRECY
Publicly carved Greek verse, not a restricted occult formula or chemical cipher.

## THE OUTSIDE / HERE
Roman imperial cultural encounter transforms a preexisting monument into a claimed speaking hero; **a public written interpretation of matter can outlast the ability to determine exactly what materially occurred**.

## REMAINDER
Physical acoustic mechanism, which words represent sensory testimony vs fiction, actual stoneworkers carving her verses, effect on local priests, no alchemical link.
