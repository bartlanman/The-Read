# ST-079 — 263/264 to 270/271: Original Coins Materialize Changing Public Authority

**Scope:** Roman Egyptian money/authority **COMPARATIVE CASE**, not positive proof of an Alchemist.  
**Documented material dates:** Gallienus **263/264**, Aurelian with Vaballathus **270/271**.  
**Date of witnesses:** surviving actual coins minted in each interval; modern catalogues describing originals.  
**Evidence verdict:** **ATTESTED** names, regnal marks, struck billon coins and dual portrait; **SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION** on political accommodation; **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** Alchemist, specific workshop or gold-transmutation event.  
**Linked window:** [Window 24, 261–280 CE](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0261-0280_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Roman Egypt has a provincial mint and regnal calendar, with imperial changes amid wider third-century instability. On an original coin it is possible to identify a **public issuer** without recovering the exact coinworker's social standing. Gallienus's sole rule followed Valerian's capture, but a quoted Gallienus religious-restoration order is **260/after** and unrelated to authorization of alchemy.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
**Gallienus HCR31547**, Alexandria year eleven = **263/264**, billon tetradrachm **9.35g**, **24.3mm**, struck, with `L ΙΑ` and a reverse warrior; catalog describes identity as **Ares OR Gallienus OR soldier**. **Aurelian/Vaballathus HCR31960**, **270/271**, struck billon tetradrachm **8.98g**, **21.8mm**, faces of Aurelian and Vaballathus. These establish historical production of shaped/stamped alloyed metal; neither reveals a chemist's identity, ore-source, workshop process or elemental conversion.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
The physical issuer claims Gallienus's imperial rule in 263/264. Aurelian and Vaballathus later appear on two faces of the same coin but with **unequal titulature**—a political statement whose actual general acceptance is not measured by the coin itself. The warrior could be an artistic convention, not eyewitness proof of a specific battle.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
The named rulers have publicly inscribed ranks. Mint artisans are **anonymous** in these specimens; attributing the emperors' legal status, alchemical capacity or metallurgical authorship to workers would be improper. Political power over Egypt changes around 270.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
No identifiable secret recipe, guild oath, danger-based access rule, deliberately obscured material manual, protected initiation or persecution of Greek “alchemists.” Organizational and metallurgical craft skills are **reasonable general inference**, not documented secret training at this specific mint.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
Gallienus's obverse `ΑΥΤ Κ Π ΛΙΚ ΓΑΛΛΙΗΝΟϹ ϹƐΒ`; regnal `L ΙΑ` = year eleven. Aurelian's obverse `[ΑΥΤ Κ Λ Δ ΑΥΡΗ]ΛΙΑΝΟϹ ϹƐΒ L Α`; Vaballathus reverse `[Ι Α Ϲ] ΟΥΑΒΑΛΛΑΘΟϹ ...`. Bracketed signs are restored/lost, not certainty. `L` indicates Egyptian regnal dating, not coded chemical reagent ratio. Roman ranks versus native titles are distinct.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and date it entered
After Gallienus in 268 and Aurelian's 270 accession, Palmyrene military/political influence in Egypt changed the issuer/naming framework. Evidence from **270/271** is on the original coin, not a later historian's invented symbol.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
The mint demonstrably produced an issue displaying **Aurelian and Vaballathus** together in 270/271. This is a **documented reconfiguration of public political claims on minted metal**. **No proven single continuous worker cohort, no recorded chemical innovation, no particular craftworker's personally recorded response.**

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
**Original coins** catalogued in Ashmolean and RPC, not literary compositions. [HCR31547, 263/264](https://hcr.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coin/hcr31547); [HCR31960, 270/271](https://hcr.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coin/hcr31960); [RPC X 75751](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/type/75751). No surviving 263–271 named Alchemist shop/account identified in this audit.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
A socially recognized public **issuing station** can change what it stamps onto existing material practice. We have a direct example of **authority shift not requiring evidence of a changed metallic technique**.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Mint personnel, metallurgy recipe, exact date of workshop response to change, acceptance by population, any alchemical professional title; alternative readings of the Gallienus reverse figure.

## SOURCES: primary, secondary, what each supports
[Source register W24-O00–O01](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0261-0280__SOURCE_REGISTER.md); [Palmyra cultural heritage synthesis](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/palmyre/en/conflict-aurelian). Historical synthesis supplies interpretive military context, **original coins** supply dates/images/materiality.

**Final station test:** **Change in state/monetary authorization; comparative only; not establishment of an Alchemist occupation.**
