# ST-068 — c.215/216 CE: Caracalla and the Reported Loss of Aristotelian Privileges

**Historical report:** **Cassius Dio, Roman History 78**, writing retrospectively, on Caracalla's treatment of Alexandria, including termination of some privileges for local **Aristotelian philosophers**.
**Date:** Caracalla's Egyptian visit generally **215/216 CE**; sequence and exact details of violence vary across ancient witnesses.
**Evidence grade:** ANCIENT LITERARY TESTIMONY, not directly corresponding original-period legal writ.

## THERE
Greek philosophical communities in Roman Alexandria depend on status, patrons and recognizable educational traditions. Caracalla's hostility to parts of Alexandria is documented by hostile ancient histories.

## CAPACITY AND AUTHORITY
Dio reports suppression of common meals and certain benefits enjoyed by Aristotelian philosophers, linking the grievance rhetorically to Caracalla's devotion to Alexander the Great and his alleged resentment toward Aristotle. Whether each motive or sanction is correctly explained is **Dio's reported interpretation**, not direct imperial confession.

## PERCEPTION
A philosophical expert's skill may remain while a ruler revokes the legal/social support and privileges under which that intellectual station operates.

## LANGUAGE / SECRECY
`Aristotelian philosopher` refers to a scholarly school, **not another name for an Alchemist**. The emperor's quarrel with Aristotle in Dio's narrative is not evidence every Egyptian technical writer was expelled or forced to encrypt recipes.

## THE OUTSIDE
Imperial political violence and arbitrary recognition/revocation of urban scholarly benefits.

## RESPONSE / HERE
**An institutional station's privileges can be withdrawn even without any demonstrated loss of intellectual competence.** The documentary link to a particular alchemical professional remains absent.

## REMAINDER
Independent original 215 legal record, actual people expelled or deprived, duration of privilege loss, direct relation to any material technical school—unknown.
