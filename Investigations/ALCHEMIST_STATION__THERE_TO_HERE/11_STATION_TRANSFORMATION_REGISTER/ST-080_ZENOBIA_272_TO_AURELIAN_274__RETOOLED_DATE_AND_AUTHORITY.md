# ST-080 — Zenobia 272 and Aurelian 273/274: A Change of Imperial Name; a Coin Later Recut

**Scope:** Egyptian public political authority and post-original material intervention, **COMPARATIVE** to alchemical claims.  
**Original dates:** Zenobia Augusta **272 CE** and Aurelian year five **273/274 CE**; **undated post-striking tooling** on a particular Zenobia specimen.  
**Witness:** actual coins and dated regnal inscriptions; modern [RPC X 75758](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/type/75758) and [RPC X 75719](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/type/75719).  
**Evidence verdict:** **ATTESTED** for struck coin names/dating and recorded later tooling; **SOURCE-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION** politics; **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** alchemical operation/occupation.  
**Linked window:** [Window 24](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0261-0280_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
After coins depicting Aurelian and Vaballathus together in **270/271**, political claims in Palmyrene-controlled Alexandria changed. Mint records exist as **objects** carrying titling and regnal-year inscriptions, not verified spoken declarations of every inhabitant's allegiance.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
**Zenobia Augusta RPC X 75758** is cataloged **272** (`L E`, year five under Vaballathus), average **8.90g** debased-silver tetradrachm, with **Homonoia** personification on reverse. **Aurelian RPC X 75719** records year five **273/274**, Zeus's head and star, debased silver. These specimens evidence minted objects, not a gold-making apparatus. A separate *later intervention* into one Zenobia specimen **HCR32033** altered the recorded numeral.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
`Σεπτ(ιμία) Ζηνοβία Σεβ(αστή)` asserts **Zenobia Augusta**; Homonoia pictures concord but does not document general peaceful agreement. In **273/274**, Aurelian again controls official issuer appearance on his type, but we do not know who in Egypt supported either government privately.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Imperial/political public name and monetary authority. **No named mint employee** attested as titled Alchemist; a sovereign's name on the object is not the artisan's evidence of social standing. The Aurelian reconquest is external to mint workers, affecting who can be named.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
No valid evidence of secret alchemical guild law, an initiation hidden within Homonoia image, recipe suppression, gold conversion, or an Alchemist executed by Aurelian. Numismatic **tooled** markings show physical alteration, but **neither secrecy nor ancient persecution motive is proven**.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
**This record's critical discovery:** RPC X **75758** catalogs HCR32033 (Ashmolean) as its own specimen **and expressly notes** `rev. tooled, Ɛ erased and Δ cut in field to l.`. [Ashmolean HCR32033](https://hcr.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coin/hcr32033) currently records `L Δ` and marks it **tooled**. The correct inference is **an original year-five type with a specimen whose reverse date was changed post-striking**—not an original fourth-year Zenobia type. The **tooling date, agent, and motive are not documented**. `L E` is a regnal date, `Homonoia` public concord iconography, neither a chemistry cipher.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and date it entered
Aurelian's military conflict with Palmyra climaxed **272**, with subsequent **273** revolt; source-critical historical narratives differ on context. Independently **273/274** original minted coin depicts Aurelian without Zenobia. The later coin tooling is an *additional reception-layer pressure on evidence*, not demonstrably an original-period reaction.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
**Mint-issued textual response**: Zenobia's public Augusta title appears **272**, Aurelian's title on a recovered issue **273/274**. **No documented response by particular metalcraft workers**. **Post-striking unknown actor** later reworked HCR32033's numeral—**do not assign the operation to 272, the mint or an alchemist**.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
Original [RPC 75758 Zenobia 272](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/type/75758) and [RPC 75719 Aurelian 273/274](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/type/75719); [HCR32033 altered](https://hcr.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coin/hcr32033). [Suski 2018](https://bibliotekarzpodlaski.pl/index.php/bp/article/view/131) contests an oversimple story that Aurelian destroyed Alexandria's great library; his abstract does not prove survival or link library fate to alchemical persecution.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
**Original change in *who* may be named on metal vs later physical change in *what the object appears to date*** must be told apart. The altered specimen is an instructive falsifier of naïve “object = pristine first creation.”

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Actor/date/motive for tooling, particular mint-workers, how residents received public image, actual fiscal ratio or original metal mixture, the true history of ancient Alexandrian collections. Cannot infer 272 original year-four Zenobia coin from a cut/erased numeral.
