# ST-023 — Cornelius Gallus, 29–26 BCE: Three Public Languages and the Limits of a Prefect's Authority

**Date:** 30 BCE first prefect; 29 BCE Philae stele; fall/suicide c.27/26 BCE.  
**Place:** Roman Egypt, Philae, Rome.  
**Document:** actual contemporaneous trilingual monument (OGIS 654 / CIL III 14147.5).  
**Verdict:** strong attested formal station and materially recorded public legitimation; punishment discussed in later Roman accounts. No alchemical function.

## THERE
New Roman rule over Egyptian priests and older Greek-Egyptian institutions needs social and political recognition beyond conquest alone.

## PERCEPTION / LANGUAGE
Gallus's stone monument makes claims in Latin, Greek, and Egyptian hieroglyphs. Latin/Greek and Egyptian portions are **different enough not to count as identical translations**; cultural expression of sovereignty adapts to audience. One physical object contains more than one political account.

## OUTSIDE PRESSURE
Upper Egyptian opposition and Nubian border conflict; Roman emperor's suspicion that a subordinate is presenting himself with excessive royal-style glory.

## RESPONSE
Gallus asserts an unprecedented first prefect role and military victories; later Roman histories report recall, accusations and suicide (date 27 or 26 BCE disputed). **Political authority is conferred yet personally revocable**.

## SECRECY/TECHNIQUE
The stele is public, not secret. Stone carving and three scripts are technological and linguistic crafts but **not** proof of an encoded alchemical procedure.

## HERE
**An office can be created by an external conqueror, adapted into local visual and linguistic regimes, and withdrawn when its holder competes with the patron's prestige.**

## REMAINDER
Consult 2009 critical edition for exact differences among texts and possible earlier inscriptional parallels; determine actual legal charges independent of third-century Dio's interpretation.
