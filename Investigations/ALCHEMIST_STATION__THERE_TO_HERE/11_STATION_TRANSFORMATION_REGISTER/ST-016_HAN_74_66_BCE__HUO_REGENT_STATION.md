# ST-016 — 74–66 BCE: Han Regents, Appointments and Collapse of a Ruling Family

**Place:** Western Han China.  
**Date:** Emperor Xuan succeeds in 74 BCE; Huo Guang dies 68 BCE; Huo family persecuted/eliminated 66 BCE.  
**Witness:** *Hanshu* biography of Huo Guang, compiled centuries afterward.  
**Verdict:** supported dynastic administrative changes, not persecution of alchemical practitioners.

## THERE
Emperor Wu's specialist-patronage court is gone. After Emperor Zhao, powerful regent Huo Guang decides who may occupy imperial office.

## CAPACITY / PERCEPTION
Civil and military authority is recognized through titles, regency, family influence and eventual public rejection—not through demonstrated material transmutation.

## AUTHORITY / OUTSIDE PRESSURE
Emperor Xuan gains a more independent position after Huo Guang's death. Following allegations of a family conspiracy, formerly privileged officials lose political security.

## SECRECY, DANGER, LANGUAGE
Reported plots, imperial fear and court confidences are politically dangerous. Nothing in the cited record indicates a ban on trained chemists or a hidden alchemical lexicon.

## HERE
**Institutional office can survive while the specific family custodianship of power dissolves.** That is a historical model of station transformation, not a genealogy of metallurgists.

## REMAINDER
Direct evidence for how fangshi positions and hand-on craft expertise changed between 74 and 66 BCE is lacking.
