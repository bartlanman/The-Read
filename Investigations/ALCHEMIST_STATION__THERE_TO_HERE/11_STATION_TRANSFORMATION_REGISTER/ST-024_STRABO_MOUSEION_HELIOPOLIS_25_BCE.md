# ST-024 — Strabo and the Egyptian Learned Stations: Appointment Continuity, Local Loss and Secretive Ancestors

**Travel:** Egypt in **25–24 BCE**; Greek *Geography* composed/revised later, significantly in first century CE.  
**Source:** Strabo 17.1.8, 17.1.29.  
**Scope:** Egyptian scholarly/temple institutions under Roman control; **not directly an alchemical profession**.

## THERE
Egypt possesses older temple-priestly knowledge traditions and Alexandria's Mouseion scholarly community with property, meals, and priestly leadership.

## STATION SURVIVAL
Strabo records in 17.1.8 that the **priest appointed to preside over the Mouseion** is now appointed by Caesar, whereas kings appointed him in the past. Office and community survive despite replacement of kingship with Roman administration.

## FIRSTHAND PERCEPTION
At Heliopolis, Strabo reports that he saw buildings formerly occupied by astronomically learned priests, but **did not find anyone at the time who held that old intellectual leadership**; he saw ritual officers and interpreters. This shows **an observer's perceived institutional loss**, not the exact date or cause of loss.

## REPORTED HISTORICAL STORY ABOUT SECRECY
He also recounts an older Plato/Eudoxus story portraying priests as **μυστικούς / δυσμεταδότους** (secretive / reluctant to impart) and **ἀπεκρύψαντο** (concealing most), but eventually imparting knowledge through study/translation. This refers to astronomy/philosophy. Historical accuracy of their shared travel and dating is unverified.

## OUTSIDE
Roman conquest changes patrons; new ruler controls appointment; a Greek visitor narrates the old learned station and critiques a local self-proclaimed expert.

## HERE
**An institution can continue under a new sovereign while another old specialist tradition is remembered as missing, with its most prestigious knowledge reconstructed through texts and stories of withheld expertise.**

## REMAINDER
No clear evidence Roman annexation itself caused missing Heliopolis school or that secrecy covered an actionable metalmaking recipe. The *Geography* has late composition and retrospective borrowed lore.
