# ST-088 — 30 April 311 CE: A Regime Reauthorizes Christian Assembly

**Scope:** comparator institutional **re-authorization** through transmitted imperial words, not an Alchemist license.  
**Historical act/date:** Edict issued under Galerius; publication at Nicomedia **30 April 311** according to Lactantius DMP 35.  
**Witness chain:** Lactantius *DMP 34–35* near-contemporary Latin quotation, Eusebius *HE VIII.17* Greek version, through later manuscripts. No original 311 inscription reviewed.  
**Evidence grade:** **NEAR-CONTEMPORARY DOCUMENTARY QUOTATION** and independent historian source; precise variations and enforcement remain open. [Window 26](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0301-0320_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
After edicts and persecution beginning in **303**, Christians had experienced restrictions, destroyed meeting places, penalties, and varied local enforcement, with some officials changing policy sooner than others.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
The quoted edict permits **Christian religious assemblies and existence** under certain public-order conditions, with instruction that Christians pray for the rulers and the state. Capacity here is a group **legally re-authorized to assemble**, not a laboratory technique or demonstrated use of precious-metal reagents.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
The imperial voice says policies failed to bring Christians back to traditional observance and grants toleration for public safety/order. Christian narrators present the decree as a victory/vindication. The document is *quoted*, not the original autograph; motives need not be treated as a perfect direct window into rulers' private beliefs.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Imperial rule grants this limited religious permission. Eusebius transmits the policy in Greek; Lactantius provides Latin and expressly dates publication in **DMP chapter 35**, distinct from the decree's quoted body in **chapter 34**.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Christian book/meeting restrictions now partly reversed; no 311 rule names alchemical symbols, metals, teaching capacity, protected technical initiation or licensed alchemical office. Direct legal change relates to worship, not preservation of Zosimos's alchemical works.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
Latin `conventicula` (assembly/meeting place) and insistence on public order mark religion; Eusebius gives Greek translation/variant. A Latin legal quote and a Greek translated text do **not** furnish two separate ancient signed imperial original tablets. **30 Apr 311** from Lactantius's posting statement, not a date written on excavated law.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Administrative and religious legitimacy shifts by **311** after prolonged repression; emperor's decree is the outside policy force changing a Christian institutional station.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Permission to meet/reorganize is publicly reported. Not every local authority immediately obeyed, and no named Christian community's individual property case is proved by the general letter alone. No actual alchemical practitioner's documented response.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[Lactantius DMP ch.34–35 Latin/translation](https://www.church.mcgill.ca/source/galerius-edict_of_toleration.shtml); [Eusebius HE VIII.17](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250108.htm), both transmitted later.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
**The same institution that lost some right of public assembly can regain a documented legal permission** under a new policy, but the social response of technical artisans remains a separate question. An external force visibly restores an actual identified religious station.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Region-specific effective dates and enforcement, incomplete restitution before 313, independently preserved original edict, relevance (if any) to a named alchemical writer's career, private imperial motives.
