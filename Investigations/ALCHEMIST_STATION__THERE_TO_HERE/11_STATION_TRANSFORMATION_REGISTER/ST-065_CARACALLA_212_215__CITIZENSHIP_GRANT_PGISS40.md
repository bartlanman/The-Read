# ST-065 — 212/213 CE: Citizenship Conferred in a Damaged c.215 Greek Copy

**Act:** Caracalla's **Constitutio Antoniniana**, extending Roman citizenship to **almost all free inhabitants**.
**Event date:** **212 or 213 CE**, with some scholarly variation.
**Physical witness:** **P.Giss. 40**, original Roman-Egyptian Greek papyrus *copy*, produced **c.215 CE**. Left citizenship column is damaged.
**Evidence grade:** DIRECT PERIOD DOCUMENT OF TRANSLATED LAW; exact legal exception and words remain contested.

## THERE
Free residents of the Roman empire occupy different legal statuses; emperor exercises power over what official belonging means.

## CAPACITY
The decree changes **juridical recognition**, not bodily matter or the ability to execute any particular craft. Scribes translate and preserve state action in Greek.

## PERCEPTION / AUTHORITY
The state can make someone Roman *for legal purposes*. Being a citizen does not tell a historian whether the person is a physician, metalworker, priest, or trained practitioner of transformation.

## LANGUAGE / SECRECY
Damage to Greek papyrus requires restorations. The often-invoked `dediticii` exception is a **contested textual restoration/interpretation**, not a secure intact clause we may recite unqualified. The Latin original does not survive.

## THE OUTSIDE
Imperial universalizing authority and administrative/fiscal organization. Motives (taxation, religious gratitude, military coherence) remain historiographically debated.

## RESPONSE / HERE
**A sovereign can give virtually all free subjects the same broad legal station without equalizing workshop access, social standing, or material expertise.**

## REMAINDER
Excluded categories, precise legal effect by province, implementation timing, original Latin wording, relation to 215 Alexandria exclusions.
