## Interpretative pattern at this stage
- **Authority can be institutionally assigned, revised or defended without creating an alchemical profession.**
- **A state can regulate an economically valuable material without suppressing transformative inquiry as such.**
- **The chronological story of a law must be separated from the later story told *about* it.**

## The three key false inferences we must not make
1. A law against fake gold → proof that all alchemists were persecuted.
2. A change of monarch → proof that all skilled practices changed custodians.
3. A sacred official possessing specialized knowledge → proof of an alchemical school.

## Conditions that would support a true 'Alchemist station' verdict
A historically attributable practitioner **who undertakes or claims material transformation as a definitional expertise**, a **social witness** recognizing their role (patron, recipient, critic or colleague), and a **dated primary or near-contemporary text/material witness** that grounds the claim.

**ST-004 and ST-005 are now deposited.** ST-004 documents *Shiji*'s early narrative of a court-recognized transformative specialist, **not proven practical goldmaking**. ST-005 documents a princely intellectual patron and the separate danger of equating lost alchemical attributions with the surviving *Huainanzi*. Next candidates following this chronology: **ST-010 — first-century CE Pseudo-Democritean authority**, and **ST-011 — Zosimos c.300 CE**. These are not yet verified register records.

**Quality assurance:** Do not turn broad date ranges repeated across windows into multiple confirmed station changes.


## Window 05 insight
**The court may build a station with titles, seals and marriage before the promised capacity is independently shown, and revoke it after failure.** Luan explicitly invokes the risk of Shaoweng's execution as a reason experts might keep their arts secret. But this is also a bargaining claim, not evidence of a general anti-alchemy law. See [Window 05](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/FORMATION_BCE_0120-0101_BCE__WINDOW.md).


## Window 06 audit — 100–81 BCE

[Read Window 06](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/FORMATION_BCE_0100-0081_BCE__WINDOW.md): **judging dangerous claims**, **a contested account of dismissal**, and **a public debate on the control of manufacturing** are different mechanisms. Critical distinction: the alleged 89 BCE expulsion of fangshi is in the much later *Zizhi tongjian*, not the *Hanshu*'s Luntai edict; avoid turning a historical narrative of the end of a station into evidence of its actual disappearance.


## Window 07 — 80–61 BCE

The distinctive new comparative mechanism is **political destruction or foreign conquest → seizure/possession of an expert's private knowledge → linguistic translation → reauthorization by a different audience**, visible in Pliny's **much later** account of Mithridates/Pompey/Lenaeus. Both historical and textual transmission dates must be kept separate. **Pliny himself** critiques secret-keeping for prestige and the inadequacy of copied images to carry practical botanical competence, but this **late first-century CE voice** is not direct testimony from 63 BCE. [Window 07](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/FORMATION_BCE_0080-0061_BCE__WINDOW.md).


## Window 08 checkpoint — 60–41 BCE

**First extremely concrete Han technical-office case:** *Hanshu* 36 says Liu Gengsheng/Xiang presents otherwise guarded Huainan text(s), claims gold can be made, and is given responsibility for `Shangfang` manufacturing. Substantial spending fails to verify the method; a counterfeit-gold accusation results in liability for execution, later reduced by family intervention and imperial discretion. The actual recipe is missing; c.60/c.56 dating is *conventional* rather than in the paragraph. **Chinese and Egyptian genealogies remain separate.** Roman creditor Rabirius occupies a named Egyptian revenue office after 55 restoration, and later Greek alchemical Cleopatra attribution cannot be pasted onto Queen Cleopatra VII's reign. [Window 08](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/FORMATION_BCE_0060-0041_BCE__WINDOW.md).


## Window 09 — 40–21 BCE
**The same post-Ptolemaic Mouseion office continues with a new appointing patron (Strabo), while Cornelius Gallus uses three scripts for his first-prefect monument and later loses imperial favor. In Han China Liu Xiang receives a new text-collation station in 26 BCE, decades after a gold-making commission failed. Critically the *fangji* category goes to physician Li Zhuguo.** None of these witnesses establishes a distinct Egyptian Alchemist in this window. [Window 09](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/FORMATION_BCE_0040-0021_BCE__WINDOW.md).


## Window 10 — 20–1 BCE

Three distinct access arrangements appear: **a temple's spatial threshold**, **an imperial catalogue recognizing classes of technical and immortality writings**, and **an openly circulated 3 BCE claim that wearing a writing prevents death**. The reports of the Chinese movement and surviving bibliographic classification come from a later Hanshu tradition, whereas Dendur is a surviving physical monument; do not homogenize the sources. None independently verifies transmutation. [Window 10](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/FORMATION_BCE_0020-0001_BCE__WINDOW.md).


## Window 11 — 1–20 CE
The physically surviving **7 CE bronze coin inlaid with gold** and **14 CE copper coin** show skilled fabrication, while state reforms legally specify which object circulates at which value. **Metal mastery, political issuance authority, and a claim of transmutation are three different operations.** Roman Egyptian priestly land confiscation is debated rather than securely established province-wide. Critically, **later first-century pseudo-Democritean alchemical works must not be squeezed into 1–20 CE** merely because their broad dating says 'first century.' [Window 11](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0001-0020_CE__WINDOW.md).


## Window 12 — 21–40 CE

The strongest source claim is **Du Shi's 31 CE water-powered blast-furnace bellows**, remembered by fifth-century `Hou Hanshu`: mechanical power improves foundry labor and produces agricultural tools. The **administrator receives the name** but builders and operators are unknown. This separates material competence, government implementation, and later attribution.

The **38 CE Alexandrian crisis** is a separately sourced example of civic identity and state coercion, not alchemical repression. Pseudo-Democritus's later-first-century Greek alchemical texts are not secured within 21–40 CE.

[Window 12](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0021-0040_CE__WINDOW.md) · [Sources](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0021-0040__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).


## Window 13 — 41–60 CE

The *Four Books* attributed to Pseudo-Democritus are a **major candidate for a Greek alchemical textual station**: four connected arts of gold-like metals, silver-like metals, gems and purple dyes are attributed to a celebrated ancient name. The *Physika kai mystika* temple-hidden-book episode represents restricted access to teaching in **literary form**, without an independently excavated secret manuscript or witnessed Ostanes apprenticeship. The more specific modern dating of the corpus (perhaps **Nero 54–68 CE**) **overlaps but exceeds** the window and is not proof of composition by 60 CE. The **41 CE original papyrus** of Claudius' Alexandrian letter illustrates the opposite mechanism—public circulation of authorized wording. [Window 13](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0041-0060_CE__WINDOW.md) · [Sources](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0041-0060__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).


## Window 14 — 61–80 CE

Pliny's first-century text describes a **real craft/test distinction**: gilding or gold-like appearance does not mean the object is wholly gold, and `coticula` touchstones are described as means of judgment. Pliny also **opposes magic yet rejects some contemporaneous claims that magical works ascribed to Democritus were not authentic**—an important source conflict, not proof of original author identity or contact with the Greek `Four Books`. Dioscorides gives a separate broad c.50–70 CE medical/mineral procedure. Vespasian's Alexandria miracle is recounted **decades later**, not an objectively verified healing. The Pseudo-Democritus dating hypothesis (possibly Nero's 54–68 CE) stays OPEN across 41–60 and 61–80. [Window 14](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0061-0080_CE__WINDOW.md).


## Window 15 — 81–100 CE
Original granite obelisks dated to **Domitian's eighth year (88/89 CE)** preserve his hieroglyphic names even after a later Roman account says the Senate ordered imperial memorial erasure in **96 CE**. The survivorship of difficult-to-read public script is material fact; **deliberate protection against that later order is not evidenced**. Persecution of philosophers **c.93–94 CE** must not be repackaged as anti-alchemy persecution. Gan Ying's **97 CE** aborted mission is a *contact horizon*, not a proof of transmitted alchemical recipes. [Window 15](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0081-0100_CE__WINDOW.md).


## Windows 16–17 — 101–140 CE

**101–120:** Dated industrial brick stamps and a period Greek prefectural petition both refer to M. Rutilius Lupus, while possible identification with Benevento's 88/89 sacred benefactor remains *UNVERIFIED*. The 115–117 conflict's severe harm to Jewish communities is real but is NOT evidence Maria the Jewess practiced alchemy or was persecuted at that time. [Window 16](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0101-0120_CE__WINDOW.md).

**121–140:** A first-person *poetic claim* about a stone's sound survives on physical 130 CE stone, an emperor's *legal founding* of a new polis is shown in original 133 papyri, a Chinese technical machine is *reported much later* for 132, and an alleged Hadrian letter about crafts is demonstrably *forged much later*. [Window 17](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0121-0140_CE__WINDOW.md).

**Rule retained:** Material object / date / speaker / skill / institutional permission / physical outcome / later reception must be audited separately.


## Window 18 — 141–160 CE

**Three independently useful stations are visible**: a priest's **eligibility** examined under Roman oversight (BGU I 16, 159/160; surviving oath incomplete), **access to productive premises** under Antinoopolis property law (P.Tebt II 613, 154/155), and a recognized physician's **medical competence/appointment** (Galen, 157, largely from his own later writings). The **144/145 original Alexandrian coin** anchors matter, cult symbolism and imperial issuing authority. **None** verifies a named Alchemist's profession or successful transmutation. [Window 18](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0141-0160_CE__WINDOW.md).


## Window 19 — 161–180 CE

**Practical demonstration, medical risk, compounding, and state proclamation come apart.** Galen's visible anatomy demonstrations (162–166) provide an effect for spectators, but are sourced mainly in his interested first-person narrative. The Antonine epidemic reveals severe limits of diagnosis—the supposed modern pathogen is disputed. Galen's preparation of theriac for Marcus Aurelius (176–180) is recognizable **pharmaceutical practice**, not verified universal efficacy. The damaged original **April 175 P.Amst. I 27** shows prefect Calvisius Statianus publicly proclaiming Avidius Cassius emperor, even though the claimant's station soon disappears. Boukoloi violence is **not** an anti-alchemical purge. [Window 19](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0161-0180_CE__WINDOW.md) · [Sources](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0161-0180__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).


## Window 20 — 181–200 CE

Galen's **spring 192 CE material catastrophe** distinguishes **remembered knowledge**, **technical instruments**, **compounded substances**, **unique edited texts** and **physically distributed copies**. His first-person narrative was probably composed **early 193 CE**, but the full surviving Greek witness is **15th-century MS Vlatadon 14**, recognized in **2005**. The unexpected survival of this account through a separate manuscript chain **does not recover the books Galen lost in 192**. [Window 20](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0181-0200_CE__WINDOW.md).

Original **Commodus–Hercules 192 medallions** and **Severan 193/194 Egyptian coins** establish durable minted **claims about political stations**, not actual divinity or material alchemy. Cassius Dio's later writing about **c.189 poisoned needles** is not independent evidence of poison technology or a secret Alchemist guild. **No identified 181–200 CE alchemical station.**


## Window 21 — 201–220 CE

The **203 Arch of Septimius Severus** and post-211 **physical erasure of Geta** show removal of political standing leaving inspectable material traces. **Caracalla's 212/213 citizenship decree** is recorded in **P.Giss.40**, a copied **c.215 Greek papyrus** whose citizenship column is damaged. The **same papyrus** includes a later **Alexandrian rural-migrant exclusion instruction**, making a sharp distinction between **imperial citizenship**, **urban access**, and **profession-specific useful exemptions**. Clement's `Stromata` book VI, **c.198–203 cross-window**, describes Egyptian priestly roles and 36 + 6 medical books but no securely identifiable Alchemist. Dio's Alexandrian philosopher-privileges story is retrospective, and any alleged 202 empire-wide conversion ban is disputed. [Window 21](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0201-0220_CE__WINDOW.md) · [Primary/audit sources](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0201-0220__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).


## Window 22 — 221–240 CE: First named technical author, still not a proved Alchemist

**Sextus Julius Africanus** is a substantially identifiable early-third-century **scholar, civic intermediary and author of Greek technical/medical/natural instructions**; his *Kestoi* was dedicated to **Severus Alexander (222–235)**. A **physical Oxyrhynchus papyrus** (P.Oxy. III 412 = BL Papyrus 2040) attests Book 18, copied broadly **225–275/276**, with **later 276 will on the verso**; this proves third-century circulation **not exact copying in 221–240**. Other *Kestoi* excerpts exhibit ingredient references through **geometric figures and musical notation** and refer to a now-lost explanatory appendix. Dufault notes the system was likely **educated display/puzzle rather than effective exclusivist encryption**. **The Book 18 papyrus itself does not preserve this code**. [Window 22](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0221-0240_CE__WINDOW.md) · [Sources](../08_SOURCE_REGISTER/CE_0221-0240__SOURCE_REGISTER.md).

**The station advances from pseudonymous authorial prestige toward a named technical writer whose later recipes enter alchemical literature, but no formally recognized title, actual goldmaking success or teacher guild has been shown.**


## Window 23 — 241–260 CE: A state-certified ritual is NOT a certified Alchemist

**Four new evidence-bounded comparison records (ST-075–ST-078)** bring the verified total to **78**. **ST-075:** Alexandria's original Philip I **248/249 debased-silver coin** establishes actual minted material and publicly marked issuer, not alchemical goldmaking. **ST-076:** two **independent original Egyptian papyri** from **14 and 17 June 250** attest state witnesses certifying sacrifice, not any alchemical initiation or permission. **ST-077:** **Cyprian Ep.80 (258)** and Dionysius quoted in **Eusebius HE VII.11** describe coercion against the Christian clerical station; Eusebius VII.13 reports Gallienus' restoration, whose exact date is **260/after, not safely inside this window**. **ST-078:** the hostile label “Egyptian Magi” in **Dionysius via Eusebius VII.10** does not verify an alchemical guild or physical chemical procedure.

**Station verdict:** **NO SECURE NAMED GREEK ALCHEMIST FOUND in 241–260**. None of the original papyri contains a metallurgical recipe, nor does a public mint coin certify its workers' professional standing. `magos` in religious polemic ≠ `chēmeutēs` in later technical literature. Later alchemical papyri and Zosimos cannot be fixed to 250 by overlapping date ranges.
