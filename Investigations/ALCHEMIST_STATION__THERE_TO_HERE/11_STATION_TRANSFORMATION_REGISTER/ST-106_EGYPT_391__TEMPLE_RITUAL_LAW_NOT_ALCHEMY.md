# ST-106 — 16 June 391: Egypt's Religious Administration Receives a Prohibition, Not an Alchemy Edict

**Scope:** direct Egyptian institutional pressure, NOT independently verified chemical-profession ban. **Issue date:** **16 June 391**, CTh XVI.10.11 to **Evagrius and Romanus**; also general CTh XVI.10.10 **24 Feb 391** and later XVI.10.12 **8 Nov 392**. **Witness:** later *Theodosian Code* compilation **438**, later manuscript copies. **Grade:** DATED TRANSMITTED LAW; implementation specifics remain unknown. [Window 30](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0381-0400_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Late-fourth-century Egyptian traditional shrines and imperial administration coexisted amid intensified anti-sacrifice policy. Evagrius (Egyptian Augustal prefect) and Romanus (comes) are named state recipients.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
Laws can ban ritual action; they **do not demonstrate abilities of accused priests, chemical recipes, or changed elemental materials**. No practitioner described preparing gold.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
State presents sacrifice and shrine veneration as forbidden activities. This is a real **normative challenge to temple ritual authority** and demonstrates imperial perception, not a record of alchemical practice or universal local compliance.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
`Nulli sacrificandi tribuatur potestas, nemo templa circumeat, nemo delubra suspiciat` restricts cult actions in Egyptian administration. **No Dioscorus named, no chemical manual indexed, no Serapeum demolition commanded by name**.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Legal pressure on priests might plausibly prompt responses; this text has **NO independent proof** that alchemical practitioners hid equipment or encrypted instructions in 391 to evade the law.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`sacrificandi` concerns sacrifice; `templa` structures, `delubra` shrines. No `chēmeia` goldmaking term or professional title appears. Later copied law ≠ independent contemporary destruction ledger.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
**391** state explicitly directs Egyptian administration to prohibit cult actions. This is the Outside force actually attested, rather than inference from medieval alchemical dialogues.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Law prescribes no lawful sacrifice/veneration. **Actual named priest reaction, temple-specific enforcement and destruction chronology are not secured by this text alone**.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[CTh XVI.10.10–12](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/CTh16.html), dates **24 Feb 391**, **16 Jun 391**, **8 Nov 392**; compilation **438** and later manuscripts.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
The Egyptian **ritual station** faces clearly identifiable imperial legal pressure, creating a rigorous control against which alleged alchemical-priest biographies would need to be checked.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Named affected priests, regional enforcement, actual direct order behind Serapeum destruction, books lost, goldmaking results, Dioscorus historicity.
