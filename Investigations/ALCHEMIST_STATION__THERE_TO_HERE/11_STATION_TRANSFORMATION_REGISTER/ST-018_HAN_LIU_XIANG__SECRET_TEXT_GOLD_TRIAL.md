# ST-018 — Liu Xiang, c.60–56 BCE: A Rare Book, an Imperial Workshop, a Failed Claim and a Sentence

**Scope:** Western Han Chinese *waidan* early case; not Greek-Egyptian practitioner lineage.  
**Participant:** Liu Gengsheng 劉更生, later Liu Xiang 劉向 (traditionally 77–6 BCE).  
**Dates:** under Emperor Xuan (74–49 BCE); **c.60 to c.56 BCE conventional**, not exact years in the core *Hanshu* biography.  
**Source:** *Hanshu* 36, compiled 1st–2nd c CE; later Chinese commentary; specialist review Pregadio.  
**Verdict:** unusually strong **retrospective source documentation of an authorized technical attempt and failure**; not laboratory verification of genuine elemental transmutation.

## THERE
Royal manuscript collections and family custody; growing imperial interest in immortality techniques. Earlier Liu An / Huainan affiliations form a **textual attribution**, not an identified apprenticeship.

## CAPACITY
Liu has access to a rare text purportedly describing metalmaking techniques, knows its words, and argues that **gold can be produced**. No ingredient or apparatus of the actual operation survives in this report.

## PERCEPTION
Ruler treats textual learning plus Liu's official standing as sufficiently credible to authorize an actual **Shangfang** production assignment. When nothing verifiable results, perception changes to potential wrongdoing.

## AUTHORITY
`上令典尚方鑄作事`: the ruler delegates craft/casting operation; title/office is an existing royal workshop context. He is not an 'Alchemist' by a uniquely attested vocational title.

## SECRECY
`枕中鴻寶苑祕書`: texts treated as guarded family possessions in a pillow, inaccessible to ordinary people (`世人莫見`). **What is restricted:** book custody. **Not proven:** textual encryption, safety-driven silence or persecution as the reason for the initial book's concealment.

## LANGUAGE
Possible two titles, **Hongbao** and **Yuan mishu**, per later Yan Shigu gloss; popular single-title `Hongbao yuanbi shu` may compress distinct components. **`方不驗`** gives the source's explicit result: the method was not verified / failed. `鑄偽黃金` is a *legal accusation of false gold*, not proof of the 144 BCE law's precise application.

## THE OUTSIDE
Emperor Xuan's desire to revive material/transcendent arts and access to workshop resources; later expense and unverified results prompt intervention by officials.

## RESPONSE
The court spends substantially; attempt fails; Liu is prosecuted and **faces execution**, but his brother Liu Anmin offers a large portion of his marquisate/fief and the sentence is reduced. Liu continues a scholar/official career afterward.

## TEXTS
*Hanshu* 36 (later compilation) is source of the event. The earlier Huainan book(s) are LOST. Father Liu De's alleged acquisition during prosecution of Liu An is **chronologically questioned by later commentators**, so the alleged transfer chain remains unstable.

## HERE
**Text access does not guarantee practical capacity.** Authority to spend and experiment may be awarded for intellectual possession, then revoked when the institution confronts failure or considers the product counterfeit. The phrase *method unverified* supplies a rare explicit judgement of the technical claim.

## REMAINDER
Exact regnal year, original craft recipe, physical assay of 'false gold', identity and training of craftsmen working under him, court criteria of proof, text title segmentation, possibility of politically influenced charges, source's literary shaping.
