# ST-002 — 145 BCE Egypt: A royal station changes its bearer

**Scope:** Comparative political station under external pressure; **not a history of an Alchemist's profession**.  
**Date:** 145 BCE, Window 03 (160–141 BCE).  
**Verdict:** ATTESTED royal succession; no attested consequences for alchemical specialists.

## THERE
Ptolemy VI and Cleopatra II rule within established Ptolemaic political, priestly and fiscal institutions.

## CAPACITY AND PERCEPTION
The king's authority is political and cultic; no source here identifies him as author or regulator of an alchemical teaching.

## AUTHORITY
The ruling dynasty and court recognize kingship through accession, marriage and cultic representation.

## SECRECY AND LANGUAGE
Royal names/titles and hieroglyphic inscriptions are public legitimation, not demonstrated encrypted chemical recipes.

## THE OUTSIDE
Ptolemy VI's death on campaign in 145 BCE; ensuing accession of Ptolemy VIII and marriage to Cleopatra II.

## RESPONSE
The bearer of kingship changes; dynastic/cultic positioning is renewed. There is no demonstrated causal response of an alchemical station.

## TEXT/MATERIAL
Royal chronologies and later Edfu epigraphic material; individual copies and dates require witness examination.

## HERE
The authority to hold a station can pass by political events independent of an expert's practical capacities.

## REMAINDER
Did workshop patronage change as a result? Need specific fiscal/contract and technical textual evidence.
