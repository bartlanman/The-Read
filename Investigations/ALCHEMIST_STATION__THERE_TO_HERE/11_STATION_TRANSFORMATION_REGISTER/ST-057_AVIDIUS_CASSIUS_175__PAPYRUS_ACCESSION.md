# ST-057 — April 175 CE: A Papyrus Can Proclaim an Emperor Who Will Not Endure

**Object:** Original period Greek papyrus **P.Amst. I 27 = SB XII 10991**, damaged, apparently from **April 175 CE**.
**Roles:** **Gaius Calvisius Statianus**, Roman prefect of Egypt; **Avidius Cassius**, short-lived imperial claimant; existing emperor **Marcus Aurelius**.
**Grade:** strong direct period witness to public political recognition; fragment restoration/interpretation necessary.

## THERE
Marcus Aurelius's reign faces a military/political succession scare while Cassius holds power in the eastern empire.

## CAPACITY AND PERCEPTION
The prefect issues a formal written celebration/proclamation naming Avidius Cassius emperor. Imperial-style titles may be written onto a page and publicly disseminated immediately, even though durable power and general consent are not thereby established.

## AUTHORITY AND LANGUAGE
Greek formulae of accession and imperial honor function as a **performative administrative utterance**. Some phrases in the published text are editorial reconstructions of damaged papyrus; do not equate every restored word with a visible ancient letter.

## THE OUTSIDE / RESPONSE
Political misinformation/uncertainty, army power and provincial allegiance pressure the office of governor to recognize a claimant. The usurpation ends after a few months; the document survives as evidence of temporary recognition.

## SECRECY
The proclamation is **public authoritative writing**, not an alchemical manual or hidden recipe.

## HERE
**Institutional recognition may be explicit and materially witnessed, yet fail the test of persistence.** A text's official date and title do not prove its claim is enduringly warranted.

## REMAINDER
Precise publication/circulation network, recipients' reactions, the papyrus' missing text, whether the edict changed local practice.
