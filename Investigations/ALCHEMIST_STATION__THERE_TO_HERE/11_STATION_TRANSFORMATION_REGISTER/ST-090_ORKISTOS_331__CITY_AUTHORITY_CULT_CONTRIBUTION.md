# ST-090 — 331 CE: Orkistos's Status and Cult-Tax Obligations Are Changed by an Original Rescript

**Scope:** comparative civic/institutional station, **NOT an alchemical event**. **Document date:** **30 June 331** (second imperial rescript); earlier petition **324–326** and first approval **c.329–330**; **one original inscribed dossier**, MAMA VII 305 = CIL III 352/7000 = ILS 6091. **Verdict:** **ATTESTED original legal inscription**; NO SECURE EVIDENCE Alchemist. [Window 27](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0321-0340_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Orkistos, a Phrygian settlement subordinated to neighboring Nacoleia, sought restored independent `civitas` status. Petitioners stated its roads, lodging, watermills, public facilities and commercial role supported an independent municipal authority. Original **324–326** petition and first **329/330** favorable grant appear as earlier quoted documents in one inscribed dossier.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
The inhabitants could petition, own/manage municipal structures, and arrange economic life, by statements in the official dossier. The source does not document goldmaking, a named metallurgical teacher, a transformation recipe or even an alchemist's presence.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
The town sought recognition of its former urban rank and local autonomy. The petition's claim of Christian inhabitants is an argument within interested civic writing, not a survey proving every resident's belief and not proof of a chemistry community.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Constantine and sons confirmed the town's autonomous station. **30 June 331** imperial response rejects continuing demand by Nacoleia for **`pecunia pro cultis`** (payments for civic cults). This directly alters municipal financial/civic authority and eligibility.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
The dispute involved visible formal municipal duties and religious-cult financing, **not** protected alchemical formula, secret guild, physical hazards or techniques concealed out of fear of targeted prosecution. Civic petition is fundamentally public.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`civitas` = legally independent municipal community; `pecunia pro cultis` = payments for cult expenses. Neither means `chēmeia`. A single inscribed monument reproduces different dates and texts; never assign first grant's date to 331 merely because later confirmation occurs then.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Neighboring Nacoleia continued asserting authority and financial obligations after the first favorable ruling. The second **331** rescript directly addresses the conflict and confirms independence.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Original inscribed ruling declares the payments improper and protects the newly recognized civic community. Whether collection ceased immediately and all inhabitants agreed **UNVERIFIED**.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
Original engraved Orkistos pillar, multiple documentary excerpts: **CIL III 352 + 7000 = ILS 6091 = MAMA VII 305**. [Treccani full Latin critical epigraphic discussion](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/l-epigrafia-costantiniana-la-figura-di-costantino-e-la-propaganda-imperiale_%28Enciclopedia-Costantiniana%29/); [Chastagnol, *Ktema* 6 (1981)](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ktema_0221-5896_1981_num_6_1_1858).

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
A **station's authority can be formally requested, regranted, and defended against an outside claimant**; religious-cult financial obligations can change without abolishing all traditional cults or establishing a new craft.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Timing of stone inscription relative to every earlier document, exact compliance of Nacoleia, workshop population, all religious motivations, any alchemical involvement.
