# ST-053 — 144/145 CE: Alexandria's Coin Gives an Emperor and a Local Cult Material Presence

**Original artifact:** **RPC IV.4 787**, copper-based alloy coin from **Alexandria, Egypt**, **regnal year 8 of Antoninus Pius**, **144/145 CE**.
**Depiction:** local Menelaites nome, **Harpocrates with crocodile lower body**, combined with Antonine coin conventions. Museum/catalog evidence for actual material coins, not Alchemist.

## THERE
Roman Egyptian province continues a system of minted currency linking emperor, local district identities and Egyptian gods.

## CAPACITY
Ancient artisans prepared alloy, struck/cast coinage and cut the dies. The specimen is a produced material object with date and iconography; it **does not show production of elemental gold**.

## PERCEPTION / AUTHORITY
Imperial minting confers value and recognized monetary status; cult imagery gives local sacred associations. Neither entails the actual mint workers were priestly alchemists.

## SECRECY / LANGUAGE
Regnal year `L Η` and Greek district names provide public administrative and monetary marking, not an encrypted chemical procedure.

## THE OUTSIDE
Need for regular exchange, state-issued denominational trust and continuity with local religious referents.

## HERE
**A product can carry legal value and sacred imagery while revealing almost nothing about its anonymous makers' specific training.** Visible symbols are not instructions for matter transformation.

## REMAINDER
Die engravers, mint workshop practices, contemporary metal assays and relationships (if any) to local cult officials.
