# ST-001 — 196 BCE Memphis: Public recognition of a priestly station

**Scope:** Comparative institution in Ptolemaic Egypt; **not an alchemist**.  
**Date:** 196 BCE, Window 01 (200–181 BCE).  
**Verdict:** ATTESTED institutional title/duty; not alchemical.

## THERE
Egyptian temples, named offices, cult images, production and fiscal obligations preexist the decree.

## CAPACITY AND PERCEPTION
Crafted material objects and sacred records participate in political and ritual functions. No individual technical competence of an alchemist is demonstrated.

## AUTHORITY
The royal-priestly decree affirms positions and confers extra titulature relating to Ptolemy V's cult, to be recorded in written forms and rings.

## SECRECY AND LANGUAGE
The three scripts and public display indicate deliberate dissemination of authority. They do **not** prove esoteric secrecy. Egyptian ritual language is not evidence of a chemical cipher.

## OUTSIDE PRESSURE
Crown–temple relations, political instability, economic needs and legitimation.

## RESPONSE
Existing priests acquire documented additional royal-cult titles and ritual obligations; not the foundation of the priesthood.

## TEXT
Memphis decree of 196 BCE, preserved in the Rosetta Stone; Demotic lines 29–32 in published translation.

## HERE
Station can be reinforced through *public naming, office, material sign and repetition*.

## REMAINDER
No proof that alchemical training or authority was inherited from the same office.
