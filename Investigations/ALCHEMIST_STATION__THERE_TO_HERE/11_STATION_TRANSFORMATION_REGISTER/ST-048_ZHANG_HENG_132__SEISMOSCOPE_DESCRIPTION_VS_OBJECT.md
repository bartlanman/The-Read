# ST-048 — 132 CE Zhang Heng: The Lost Instrument and Its Reported Capacity

**Independent Chinese parallel:** Eastern Han state. **Reported construction:** 132 CE, Zhang Heng. **Witness:** `Hou Hanshu`, compiled mainly fifth century CE, supplemented by other retrospective Chinese histories. **Original instrument LOST.**

## THERE
Han imperial astronomic/official expertise sought to register natural events, including earth tremors.

## CAPACITY
The biography describes a bronze vessel with ornate dragons, toads and balls and an internal mechanism sensing earth movement. It claims directional indication from the ball that fell; an anecdote about a later message of an earthquake in Longxi is traditionally assigned **134 CE**, exact corroboration and dates debated.

## PERCEPTION
Contemporary onlookers in the story reportedly first doubted the unobserved event, then accepted the claim upon later confirmation. This is **literary depiction of a test and social recognition**, not an independently recovered 134 CE measurements log.

## AUTHORITY
Zhang is a known astronomer, engineer and court figure; the written history invests his name with durable machine authority while unnamed bronze workers remain largely invisible.

## LANGUAGE
`候風地動儀` is functional title for an earthquake-indicating instrument. Dragon and toad ornament is not a coded goldmaking recipe.

## THE OUTSIDE
Desire to observe distant natural disasters rather than wait for local direct perception; court need for better records.

## HERE
**An instrument may acquire a convincing historical reputation through a specific claimed prediction and later corroboration while its original mechanism remains physically unrecovered.** Modern functioning replicas test plausibility of interpretations, not authenticity of the original design.

## REMAINDER
Original 132 mechanism, calibration, independent event log, origin of precise 134 date, identity of hands-on engineers and workshop.
