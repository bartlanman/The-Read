# ST-091 — 333–337: Hispellum's Conditional Temple and Priest/Festival Authorization

**Scope:** traditional civic-religious institution comparison, **not Alchemist**. **Original date:** **c.333–337** (not precisely 335). **Original material:** inscribed Constantine rescript **CIL XI 5265**, Spello. **Verdict:** attested permission and priest/festival station, contested interpretation of superstition. [Window 27](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0321-0340_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
The local city Hispellum organized annual imperial-cult obligations and entertainments linked to Volsinii; sought reduced travel and local dynastic honors, with annual `sacerdotes`.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
City representatives petitioned emperor for temple/festival permission; local priests were to preside over performances. No identified metalworking task, trained transmuter, operated furnace or documented goldmaking claim.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
Petitioners valued imperial dynastic prestige and the city's name. Ruler judged some traditional public acts acceptable with restrictions. Permission does not measure efficacy of religious rites.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
The imperial rescript grants name **Flavia Constans**, a temple to **Flavian dynasty**, local theatrical and gladiatorial spectacles, while keeping Volsinii festival. An expressly limited ritual recognition **complicates blanket claims all old priestly stations vanished**.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Public inscription, not a secret guild. A condition relating to undesirable `superstitio` cannot prove that metal recipes or Alchemists were forbidden. Temple ceremony and economic privilege are separate.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
Inscription names **`sacerdotes`** and prohibits temple contamination through **`contagiosae superstitionis fraudibus`**. Scholars debate whether this chiefly refers to animal sacrifice or other prohibited practices; do not turn ambiguity into a specific anti-alchemical rule.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and date it entered
Emperor's control over status/name and religious festival permissions, **c.333–337**. Local petition sought relief from traveling to Volsinii; no 335-specific chemical ban.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Temple, name and rites conditionally approved, Volsinii remains authorized too. No demonstrated participant priest's independent policy response beyond intended festival procedure.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[Actual Latin inscribed rescript CIL XI 5265, edited text](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/Hispellates_CIL.htm); [Treccani epigraphic context](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/l-epigrafia-costantiniana-la-figura-di-costantino-e-la-propaganda-imperiale_%28Enciclopedia-Costantiniana%29/). Actual ancient local copy, not autograph sent by emperor.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
Recognition of a **religious-civic office can be conditional rather than absolute**, with explicit consent despite Christianizing central rule.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Exact calendar date, effective prohibitions, local practical enactment, any alchemical activities in town.
