# ST-070 — c.222–235 CE: Julius Africanus, a Named Author of Transformative Techniques

**Identity:** Sextus Julius Africanus, Christian chronographer, editor/critic and author of the Greek `Kestoi` ('Embroideries'), dedicated to Severus Alexander, emperor **222–235 CE**.
**Status:** **HISTORICALLY NAMED TECHNICAL WRITER**, plausible literary contact with later alchemical authors. **NOT independently certified as an official Alchemist or proven gold-transmuter.**

## THERE
Roman Near Eastern Greek technical, medical, military and natural-lore writing exists alongside imperial-client and Christian scholarly institutions.

## CAPACITY
The `Kestoi` assembles practical-looking instructions—medicine, dye/color adjustment, cosmetics, agriculture, military stratagem and extraordinary effects. Some color-dye methods are **quoted by later Greek alchemical authorities**. This is documented **written procedural transmission**, not independently witnessed execution of every claim.

## PERCEPTION / AUTHORITY
A named author dedicates work to an emperor and displays scholarly capacity, worldly experience and classical education. The later survival of **P.Oxy. III 412**, a third-century copy of Book 18, makes him better anchored than a purely legendary master. But patronage, authorship, professional practice and measurable material results are separate.

## SECRECY / LANGUAGE
Part of the transmitted material uses geometric/musical symbols for words in recipes; interpretation/appendix problem addressed independently in ST-072. Africanus' editorial claim to Homeric hidden lines likewise requires literary source criticism.

## THE OUTSIDE
Interest in useful military and medicinal knowledge, imperial patronage and educated Greek textual display.

## RESPONSE / HERE
**A historically identifiable author can package and circulate techniques later appropriated into alchemical literature without ever being demonstrably recognized by contemporaries as 'the Alchemist'.**

## REMAINDER
Which recipes were in original 222–235 edition, actual workshop and pupils, precise date, results, whether any goldmaking performed, source of patronage beyond dedication.
