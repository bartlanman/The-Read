# ST-071 — The Ancient Kestoi Papyrus: A Copy With a Later 276 CE Will

**Physical witness:** British Library **Papyrus 2040**. Front: `P.Oxy. III 412`, portion of Sextus Julius Africanus' `Kestoi` Book 18. Back: `P.Oxy. VI 907`, legal will dated **276 CE**.
**Dating:** First use palaeographically **c.225–275/276 CE**; later use **276**. **This does not give exact 221–240 copying year.**

## THERE
An Oxyrhynchus reader/scribe copies a Greek learned text originally composed in a wider Roman intellectual network. The later user writes a private will on the reverse, indicating physical circulation/reuse of a manuscript.

## CAPACITY / MATERIAL
Real ancient papyrus and writing survive; Book 18 discusses **claimed omitted Homeric `Odyssey` spell/necromantic text** and assesses authenticity. Neither text demonstrates success of a ritual, elemental transmutation, nor a historically discovered 'lost Homer' composition.

## PERCEPTION AND AUTHORITY
The name Africanus and book-title enable attribution of a *portion* of a surviving work. A 276 document supplies a firm date by which the recto already existed.

## LANGUAGE / SECRECY
The Book 18 recto is **NOT** the preserved example of geometric/musical ingredient notation reported in other `Kestoi` fragments. Do not put the lost appendix on this papyrus. Homeric restoration and effectiveness of rites are **literary claims**, not verified facts.

## THE OUTSIDE
Scholarly re-use of an old roll for a legal instrument demonstrates material transmission and document economy; custody can change without a planned initiatory passage.

## HERE
**A real manuscript dates the circulation of a technical author's text, but neither confirms every component of the original corpus nor proves every reported technique worked. A reused back provides a latest possible date, not an exact front-side composition date.**

## REMAINDER
Exact recto palaeographic date, relation to authorial first edition, what was added to later `Kestoi`, manuscript owners and reader communities.
