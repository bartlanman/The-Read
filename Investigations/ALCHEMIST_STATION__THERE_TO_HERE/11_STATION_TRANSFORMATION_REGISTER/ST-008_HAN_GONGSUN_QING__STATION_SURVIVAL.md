# ST-008 — Gongsun Qing: Sustaining Court Authority After Others Fail

**Date:** active in reign of Emperor Wu, including ca.113–110 BCE, within Window 05.  
**Source:** *Shiji* 28.

## THERE
Shaoweng and Luan Da receive imperial honor yet eventually die by execution; Emperor Wu continues seeking specialists in rituals and extraordinary knowledge.

## CAPACITY
Gongsun Qing invokes writing about the Yellow Emperor and divine tripods, traces of spirits, and long-range predictions. No verified transmutation or actual spirit encounter is documented.

## PERCEPTION
The emperor credits appeals to prestigious ancient textual authority and visits reported signs, while increasingly expressing concern about repeat fraud.

## LANGUAGE
Gongsun invokes alleged inherited written wisdom and locates proof in signs and imprecise future conditions. The original master/ancient slip sequence is a textual claim, **not independently secured apprenticeship**.

## AUTHORITY
Gongsun gains official appointments and continues to receive imperial attention and dispatch, though his station differs from Luan Da's extraordinary seals and kinship.

## SECRECY, DANGER AND ACCESS
No demonstrated secrecy of a chemical procedure. The capacity to reinterpret failure through delayed prophecy is visible in the historian's account.

## OUTSIDE PRESSURE
The earlier practitioners' executions heighten the risk of promising quickly testable results. When challenged, Gongsun emphasizes that immortals cannot be commanded on a ruler's timescale.

## RESPONSE / HERE
**The imperial demand for a class of experts may persist despite the collapse of particular members.** Strategies of interpretation and timelines of proof shift under scrutiny.

## REMAINDER
True authorship and age of the bamboo text, empirical basis for signs, his methods beyond reported court narratives.
