# ST-031 — Du Shi, 31 CE: Who Deserves the Credit for a Working Transformation?

**Date and place:** 31 CE, Nanyang, Eastern Han China.
**Witness:** Du Shi biography in `Hou Hanshu`, compiled in the fifth century CE.
**Verdict:** SOURCE-ATTESTED HISTORICAL REPORT of useful iron-production technology; not independently recovered machinery, gold transmutation or a formal Alchemist's station.

## THERE
The Xin regime collapsed in 23 CE. Eastern Han rule began in 25 CE; regional officials organized production amid prolonged restoration.

## CAPACITY
The biography says Du Shi `造作水排`, creating/arranging a water-driven bellows to support casting of farm implements (`鑄為農器`). It reportedly lowered effort and improved productivity.

**Unverified:** original machine, measured output, exact engineering design and identity of component makers. A fifth-century written account is not a recovered 31 CE technical drawing.

## PERCEPTION AND STATION
The biography credits **the Nanyang administrator** with the useful technology. The actual furnace workers and engineers are unnamed. The office may have supplied organization and resources, but who invented the mechanism is unresolved.

## AUTHORITY / SECRECY / LANGUAGE
`太守`: administrative office; `水排`: practical hydraulic furnace blower. Neither is a designation of a secret gold-maker or an encrypted reagent. The government can confer public recognition while practical expertise remains distributed.

## THE OUTSIDE
Pressure for lower labor costs and improved agricultural tools during political restoration.

## RESPONSE
Later history reports water-power applied to furnace air supply and people benefiting. No source proves that the device was guarded or that craft workers were persecuted for making it.

## HERE
**Effective transformative practice can be remembered under an administrator's name while the artisans who materially realize it disappear from the written lineage.** Distinguish invention, sponsorship, operation and reputation.

## REMAINDER
What prior water technology existed, who actually designed and maintained it, what was fabricated and how benefits were evaluated?
