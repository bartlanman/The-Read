# ST-028 — 3 BCE: The Queen Mother of the West and a Publicly Circulated Promise

**Date:** 3 BCE, Emperor Ai's Jianping fourth year.
**Region:** Western Han China.
**Source:** Hanshu Emperor Ai annals and Five-Elements treatise, both composed in a later compilation rather than independent contemporaneous eyewitness testimony.
**Status:** documented report of mass circulation; no proven immortality and no identified Alchemist office.

## THERE
Chinese beliefs about extraordinary longevity and Queen Mother of the West existed alongside imperial interest in specialist arts, under conditions of drought and anxiety.

## CAPACITY — what people actually did in the transmitted report
People transmitted tally-like tokens (籌), gathered, carried lights and worshiped. A writing was quoted asserting that a person wearing it would not die. These acts document reported collective behavior, not a viable elixir or material transmutation.

## PERCEPTION AND AUTHORITY
The message derived claimed authority from the Queen Mother and spread without a single named authorized court specialist. The historians report popular fear and excitement; they are not neutral contemporary fieldworkers.

## LANGUAGE
母告百姓，佩此書者不死 — a message telling the people that wearing this writing prevents death. Here the words themselves **claim efficacy**; they are not a cipher for a documented furnace recipe.

## SECRECY
This is an important counterexample to assuming that all immortality promises were kept secret by an exclusive class. Circulation is the central reported event. The original circulated writing has not been recovered.

## OUTSIDE PRESSURE
Drought, collective anxiety and a widespread narrative of supernatural rescue; no direct cause from court alchemical patronage demonstrated.

## HERE
**A claim to exceptional power can achieve wide social presence through copying and movement, even without proven expert capacity, patronage or an official station.**

## REMAINDER
Who first wrote and distributed the message; exact physical nature of tallies and texts; social network of carriers; material outcomes and extent of elite participation.
