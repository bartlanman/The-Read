# ST-091 — c.333–337 CE: Hispellum Receives Conditional Permission for Imperial Cult and Games

**Scope:** comparative priestly/ritual municipal station, **NOT** an Alchemist. **Event and original stone witness:** **c.333–337**, **CIL XI 5265**, Spello/Hispellum. **Evidence:** **ATTESTED inscription**, **CONTESTED precise year and `superstitio` scope**. [Window 27](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0321-0340_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Hispellum traditionally shared regional festival obligations with Volsinii, involving annual priests and entertainment. Its inhabitants petitioned to stage their own religious/dynastic events rather than require their appointed local priest to travel across difficult terrain.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
The source attests scheduling **games**, organizing civic priests and planning construction of a dynastic temple. It does **not** attest secret ingredients, a chemical operation, experiment or successful creation of precious metals.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
The city asserted need for civic honor, travel relief, festival capacity and symbolic closeness to the ruling Flavian dynasty; emperor's approval validates claims but is not an unqualified religious creed.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
The inscribed imperial rescript approves a new name **Flavia Constans**, construction of shrine to the imperial family and local annual **`sacerdotes`** to organize spectacles, provided new temple is not polluted by **`contagiosae superstitionis fraudibus`**. Festival at **Volsinii** must continue. **These are real authorized ritual/civic roles**, not an authorization of technical goldmaking.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Permission is publicly carved on stone. Ritual is constrained, with source-specific `superstitio` wording, but **the exact prohibited practices are not fully enumerated**. **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** it was a targeted anti-alchemy rule or that threatened alchemists hid recipes.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
`sacerdotes` = priests; `aedes` = shrine/temple; `gladiatorii muneris` = gladiatorial event; `superstitio` = condemned ritual category rather than scientific diagnosis. Date **333–337 range**; some scholarship links wording to Christian rejection of sacrifice, but modern interpretation is not identical to an explicitly listed prohibition in original text.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Travel/burdens and imperial approval of civic cult between **333 and 337** placed conditions on existing festival stations in a world of shifting imperial religious preferences.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
Local temple/festival privilege granted subject to specified restraint; exact implementation, construction schedule, and later participants unknown. The text explicitly preserves a place for Volsinii's annual celebration.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
**Original Latin inscribed rescript** [CIL XI 5265, University of Grenoble full text](https://droitromain.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/Constitutiones/Hispellates_CIL.htm); [Treccani epigraphy interpretation](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/l-epigrafia-costantiniana-la-figura-di-costantino-e-la-propaganda-imperiale_%28Enciclopedia-Costantiniana%29/).

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
A named religious office can receive **conditional, local, public authority** in a Roman empire evolving under Christian rule, contrary to an easy story of all traditional religious practice either persecuted or fully unrestricted.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Precise year and scope of `superstitio`; temple construction/continued games' full archaeology; specific officiants; any alchemical technique or professional guild.
