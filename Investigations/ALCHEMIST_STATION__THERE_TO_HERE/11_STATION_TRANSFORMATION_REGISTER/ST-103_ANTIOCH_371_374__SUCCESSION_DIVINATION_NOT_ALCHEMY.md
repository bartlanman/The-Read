# ST-103 — c.371–374: Antioch's Succession-Divination Trials and Political Risk

**Scope:** named alleged diviners as comparative political/ritual station; NOT proved Alchemist persecution. **Events:** usually **371/372**, some scholarly placements **374**. **Witness:** Ammianus Marcellinus *Res Gestae* XXIX.1–2, **authored later fourth century**, extant through later MSS. **Grade:** NAMED RETROSPECTIVE NARRATIVE / CONTESTED EVENT DATE; no independent original court record inspected. [Window 29](../02_CHRONOLOGY_20_YEAR_WINDOWS/CE_0361-0380_CE__WINDOW.md).

## THERE: surrounding conditions and existing station
Valens's government was sensitive to political prophecy concerning succession. Ritual consultation and literary/magical accusations could carry political consequences.

## CAPACITY: what the actor demonstrably could do
Ammianus describes **ring over lettered dish/tripod** generating partial letters **THEOD-** and names alleged defendants including **Theodorus**, **Hilarius**, **Patricius**. This attests that the historian **reported** the mechanism and alleged practice, NOT that predictions succeeded or original device survives.

## PERCEPTION: what contemporaries believed or claimed
The narrative portrays a government fearing prophecy of a successor and participants whose purported consultation was dangerous. It has Ammianus's moral/political framing; no direct victim self-authored diary or 372 court transcript is examined.

## AUTHORITY: who recognized, employed, patronized or challenged the actor
Valens's officials investigate and allegedly punish divinatory practice perceived as treasonous. Named actors are not demonstrated to have held a gold-making teacher's appointment or a public Alchemist occupation.

## SECRECY: institution / fear / economic value / competence / danger / prestige / loss
Ritual/secrecy and legal risk are narratively plausible, but **NO SECURE EVIDENCE** any alchemical author concealed metal-coloring procedures because of these particular trials.

## LANGUAGE: exact terms, translation, technical idioms, pseudonyms, allegory, ambiguity
Ammianus's reported `THEOD-` is a partial successor name, not a chemical substance sign. `veneficium` and magic accusations are not automatically metallic transmutation. Date commonly **371/372**, perhaps **374**, must remain unsettled.

## THE OUTSIDE: external pressure and the date it entered
Imperial fears over succession produced coercive inquiry (as the historian recounts); the actual **targeted political diviner's station** is distinct from practical metallurgy.

## RESPONSE: changes, resistance, accommodation, continuity, or unverified
The ancient narrative describes interrogation, punishment and fear. No independent original archaeological proof of the kit or response by an Alchemist is presented.

## TEXTS MANIFESTED: work, composition date, copy date, passage and authority claim
[Ammianus *Res Gestae* XXIX Latin](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/L/Roman/Texts/Ammian/29%2A.html); [English](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Ammian/29%2A.html). Later-fourth-century author describing earlier trial, later physical manuscripts.

## HERE: what becomes newly warranted
We have **named reported persecuted consultants** in a plausible political narrative, but not evidence to rename those named persons **Alchemists**.

## REMAINDER: unknowns, false bridges, contradictory evidence
Precise chronology; source bias; ritual device archaeology; actual original accusations/verdicts; any metalmaking knowledge of accused.
